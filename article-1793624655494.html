If Beauty Is Your Only Currency, How Will You Spend It?

A woman who believes her looks are all she has to offer probably won’t behave the way men want her to.

It’s not every day that I’m watching a TV show that coincides perfectly with my sludgy trough of genderslop, but East of Eden on Netflix (based on the 1952 Steinbeck novel) somehow fits the bill. Specifically, this show answers a question which I’ve always wondered: if a woman truly believes that beauty is her only currency—as prominent manosphere voices repeatedly say—will she behave as they hope she will, by cashing in as young as possible, “knowing her place” and accepting her impending irrelevance?

Perhaps not. (This paragraph contains some light East of Eden spoilers (the TV show, not the book) so be aware.) One of the main characters, Cathy (played by Florence Pugh) is a pretty young girl (at least at the beginning of the series—we follow her over decades) who quickly realizes that her striking beauty is her only form of currency in a pre-feminism world that is stacked against women. Desperate to obtain power through any means possible, she leverages her beauty in particularly machiavellian, if not completely evil, ways. Yes, she marries (but not for long), she has no interest in being a mother, and she quickly realizes that owning her own brothel and blackmailing powerful men (who routinely underestimate her, cozy up to her and trust her because she’s beautiful) is the best use of her currency, setting her up with as much security and wealth as she ages—which she doesn’t have to hand over to a man.

Not every woman is Cathy, of course. Most women want marriage and children, even if they also want careers, and most women can’t imagine doing sex work for any reason. But if you truly believe in the cynical worldview propped up by the manosphere—that no matter what other assets you may have, the only thing that will matter is your beauty, which only exists for about ten to fifteen years—your actions may not follow an especially traditional or nurturing path.

First, let’s start with a contradiction I’ve noticed in my own tour through the genderslop trenches—the inconvenient truth (promoted mostly by manosphere men and, oddly, some radical feminists) that men are primarily attracted to much younger women (often including girls under the age of consent) and lose attraction somewhere around thirty, certainly by forty. When men are talking about this, the narrative is presented to scare women to settle down as quickly as possible (presumably to someone much older, who is not very impressive physically) so they don’t miss their supposedly brief window of desirability. But taking this belief literally—as some women actually do—will yield very different results.

Of course, as you might imagine, a woman who truly believes men will be repulsed by her after thirty, or after forty, stands little to gain from marriage, especially a marriage where she has no career or financial independence. You don’t have to be terribly bright to see where such a union might lead: he will tire of her, cheat on her or abandon her for a younger woman, and if she had no job or career prospects it will be very difficult for her to suddenly restart life at forty. Now, I disagree that most “tradwives” will find themselves in this position, and I think presenting it as an inevitable fate is pretty ridiculous, because, as I’ll get to later, plenty of men actually like their wives. But if you truly believe that a man only values a woman for her youth and beauty, it’s going to be pretty hard for you to simultaneously make a convincing case that she should hand over her entire life to a man who may treat her like an opened Amazon box—lying around in case he needs to re-use it for something, but eventually broken down and discarded.

In fact, if a woman really believes that her youth and beauty is her only asset, the smartest thing to do will be to live as independently as possible, leveraging youth and beauty to ruthlessly and coldly extract resources which will set her up for life. This could be via sex work, scams, or anything to make as much money from a depreciating asset in as short a time as possible, maximizing long-term security. Depending on one man to provide these resources makes little sense if the man actually holds all the power and has the ability (and motivation) to discard you and leave you with nothing, or close to nothing. A woman hellbent on leveraging beauty as her only asset looks a lot less like June Cleaver, and a lot more like Bonnie Blue.

Keep in mind that “men only want you for your youth and beauty” is a belief often held in conjunction with “men are not naturally monogamous.” This is held up to promote polygyny and encourage women to accept their fate, forgetting the inconvenient fact that a polygynous society would actually be a detriment to all the men left without a wife. But why on Earth, if you truly believed men only cared about your youth and beauty and had no desire to be monogamous, would you even consider trusting one? I’m about as straight as they come, and I think if I truly believed men worked this way, I would avoid them for anything other than the occasional fling.

Although I didn’t ask about age preferences specifically, I ran a survey to examine general attitudes about sexuality. Sure enough, the more cynical women, who believed men were uninterested in women in their own “league,” were less enthusiastic about marriage, not more motivated to lower their standards and find a partner. These women were also more likely to prioritize time alone. (Yes, there’s a causation/correlation issue here in that women with bad experiences with men might be more likely to hold this cynical belief, but the point isn’t about where the belief originates, just whether or not it is genuinely held.)

In fact, there is an entire ethos that revolves around these ideas: the pink pill. The pink pill has been framed as “opposition” the manosphere red pill, but these theories aren’t really at odds with each other at all. The pink pill is essentially what happens when young women absorb cynical, manipulative manosphere talking points and then apply these theories practically to their own lives. A core feature of pink pill philosophy is that your beauty or lack thereof is the only relevant factor that determines how men will treat you. But most of these women do not leverage this information to lower their romantic standards and become compliant tradwives. Instead, they congregate in communities such as r/Vindicta, where women looksmaxx not to find love and companionship, but to, in their own words, “weaponize” beauty and “make it work for us.” This is the natural conclusion of young women believing that beauty is their only currency—instead of mooning over marriage, they will treat a currency as currency, and attempt to spend it strategically. If you are fully pink-pilled, surrendering your only “weapon” to a man you believe doesn’t love you and will cheat on you at the drop of a hat is just about the stupidest thing you can do.

This also explains why so many zoomer radfems seem paradoxically focused on their looks. Granted, some of these people are mentally ill teenagers with no coherent politics, but some of them are probably viewing beauty as a practical currency for survival, assuming they will get by easier in a patriarchal world if they look the part.

Some men use the same basic information about beauty as a woman’s only currency, but take a different, more optimistic view. These men believe that men and women were in a nice symbiotic relationship where women’s only currency was looks, men’s only currency was resources, and they exchanged these things in a happy, utopian Richard Scarry-type world, until all the damn feminists and wokies shoawed up. At this point, per the theory, these people forced us to pretend women care about men’s looks and that men care about women’s jobs. The reality, of course, is that women do have physical preferences for men, which is inconvenient for a man who hopes to build great value just by things mostly within his control (his ability to provide.) Yes, men may care more about looks than women, and women may care more about their partner’s career than men, but a woman who sees her looks as a currency to exchange for resources is often doing so pragmatically as a deliberate survival strategy, not out of genuine sexual desire. (If that were the case, then you would genuinely be arguing that strippers are attracted to their clients). Women do not care about looks more in 2026 because weird horny feminists are telling them to be exactly like men. Realistically, women have always cared, but only when women have financial independence can they really entertain physical preferences as a priority. Of course, it’s not lost on me that this theory not appealing to many men, and it’s a big motivator behind getting women out of work. The ultimate cope, if you will.

A woman who believes that beauty is her only currently might still want marriage and children, of course—Cathy of East of Eden is an anomaly and fictional, and the drive to reproduce and obtain romantic companionship is pretty innate to many of us. A woman who strongly desires a husband and motherhood might seek that out even if she believes the worst about men’s true motivations. But she is unlikely to blindly trust a man. If she believes that a high-value man can get anyone of any age no matter his own, that men want a rotating door of very young women, that men are incapable of finding women over a certain age attractive, and that men aren’t “programmed” for monogamy, why on Earth would she allow any degree of personal risk, knowing what might happen if her husband were to follow this likely path? A woman who wants a husband but believes men are Like This will take measures to secure her position. She will likely be jealous and possessive. She might be controlling and paranoid. It’s highly unlikely she would allow herself to be in a vulnerable position, and might be less amenable to giving up her career. (Which, as I’ve discussed before, a lot of men don’t even want their wives to do, but that’s a topic for another day.)

Of course, the manosphere is prepared with their hand-waving explanation—Wife Goggles.

This is the belief that if you get married young, your husband will forever see you as the woman you were when you met, and your age will not matter to him as you get older as long as you make reasonable efforts to look decent.

But there are cracks in this theory, of course. The same men who extoll the virtues of Wife Goggles will still proudly admit men don’t want monogamy at all and are only forced into it by society. Wife Goggles as a concept only makes sense if a man sees value in marriage beyond easy sex and cooking. If your wife is just one of many broads you may be banging, and could be replaced by a maid, cook and prostitute, why does she specifically benefit from these goggles, which require some degree of genuine romantic love?

The Wife Goggles phenomenon is likely a bit true, at least for a significant number of men. I truly believe older men who claim to be deeply attracted to their same-age wives, and I think they are probably more attracted to these women than they would be if they were single and happened to meet one day at the age of sixty-seven. But these are also men who clearly like being married, and are not only married begrudgingly because they were dragged to the altar. Wife Goggles, as a concept, cannot coexist with being a philanderer or even an aspiring one.

And, indeed, lots of men do want monogamous marriage, because even though youth and beauty might be powerful signals for dating, marriage involves much more, and at the risk of being corny and bluepilled, I don’t think people are focused on calculating their relative SMV after twenty years of raising kids together. Sure, if one half of a couple suddenly and willingly lets themselves go or loses interest in sex and romance, it’s possible the other half will lose attraction. But I don’t believe men are losing attraction to their wives en masse solely on the basis that they are aging. One of my earliest studies (both qualitative and quantitative) was on infidelity and divorce in the postpartum period, and while plenty of women expressed self-consciousness about their changing bodies, none of the men mentioned it as an issue, and that’s including men who openly admitted to cheating on their wives. The men who cheated were more likely to cite frustration with less frequent sex post-baby. (This is not me rushing to their defense by any stretch of the imagination, but I think “men lose interest in their wives as they get older” is not a fair generalization.)

Unfortunately, there have been no longitudinal studies of attraction over decades of a marriage. The closest we’ve come is pathetically inadequate, but it actually tells the opposite story. A German study conducted in 2002 followed young couples who were dating for different periods of time, and found that women’s attraction diminished over time, but not men’s. In fact, you could easily make an argument that men are more suited to monogamy than women are: the few quasi-matriarchal societies that have existed over time have been less focused on monogamy and marriage than patriarchal societies. (And yes, I know there are segments of the manosphere who are anti-monogamy because they believe women aren’t suited for it either. Don’t even try to manosphere-splain to me.)

Realistically, neither theory is true. Men and women are pretty aligned on monogamy and marriage! Another earlier study I conducted showed that of single men and women over thirty-two, their stated goals for dating were remarkably similar. The vast majority wanted marriage and children. Granted, there are some potential errors here: monogamous people are more likely to read my work in the first place because I am very pro-monogamy, and this is, like any survey, a “stated preference.”

But I disagree that the revealed preference for men is infidelity or harems. Comedian Chris Rock famously joked that a man is “only as faithful as his options.” That’s a funny bit for standup, but it’s not really true. Where there’s a will, there’s a way, and not every man has that will. There are attractive, well-off men who are faithful to their wives, and somehow Jerry Springer is full of men who look like they’d have no options, but somehow have three women fighting over them.

That’s not to say most men don’t look at other women, watch porn, or fantasize about other women, but I also don’t think most men necessarily want to cheat, or only get married begrudgingly. Infidelity does seem fairly common among celebrity men, but celebrities are a famously (pun intended) bad sample for just about anything. Celebrities don’t behave like “regular people if they had more money.” Anyone who wants to be famous, or who gladly lives the life of a famous actor or singer, probably has a very different personality from your average person, even your average attractive and wealthy person. Celebrity behaviors in the sexual arena aren’t to be completely discounted, but rather, they should be regarded as what one type of person does, not what everyone would do if they could.

The unsexy reality is that while men and women do have some on-average differences, driven by both socialization and biology, there is also a lot of intrasexual variability, and statements that “women are like X” while “men are like X” are rarely true unless they are too obvious to be interesting in the first place. But the good news is that cold, cynical “hard truths” about the true nature of men and women are rarely that true at all—and using them to manipulate women will likely produce the exact opposite effect.
