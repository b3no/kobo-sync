The moment is here! The data from the bodies rating survey is in! Today I will cover women, and I will cover men sometime next week. I was able to get almost 3,000 responses- I would have liked more, but this was enough to get a pretty solid idea of statistically significant patterns across a variety of women’s bodies—both natural-looking ones and extreme ones.

Also, let us not forget that even if you have gripes with my sample size or the images I used to generate the women not looking realistic enough, most of the cited literature on the topic has a sample size of like 72 people and uses images like these (yes, really):

This is also better than my last stab at this type of survey, which in the interest of creating consistency, made everyone look like a headless alien. Complain about the new images all you want, but at least we are getting somewhere.

Also, for full transparency, I used a body simulator first (plugging in precise measurements) which was then uploaded to an LLM and further refined in a Photoshop-type interface by hand. So I tried! I did not use real-life images because I wanted everything to be as consistent as possible, so I understood exactly which variables I was measuring.

Anyway, I wanted to measure many different variables—not just weight, but height and six different body shapes.

The body weights represented:

Now, the shapes themselves, all shown in the “slim” body type:

So, let’s see how the different body types performed! A few questions I had going into this:

  • Does weight matter more than shape, or vice versa? Would men prefer a straight-shaped slim woman or an hourglass-shaped average-weight woman?

  • Assuming the weight is the same, which body shape is the most popular?

  • If someone does not have an hourglass body type, to what degree will weight loss or gain affect her dating prospects?

  • What “types” of men feel strongly about particular unconventional female bodies?

And more…

Note that this article will primarily cover the preferences of straight and bisexual men. However, I will briefly touch on the differences for lesbian and bi women rating women along the way, which surprisingly didn’t differ all that much from straight men’s opinions.

Onwards!

Slim Weight Dominated

Women’s weight was just one of many variables I tested. This wasn’t very surprising, but should be mentioned nevertheless: the best-performing body size was the slim one, which would translate to about a 20-21 BMI. The skinny (slightly underweight) BMI performed slightly better than the rest of the non-slim sizes, but was roughly on par with average and muscular body sizes. The real penalty was for chubby and obese body types, although chubby bodies performed far better than obese ones.

Women’s Height Didn’t Matter

I had three heights represented for women, shown in scale next to a 5’10” man. They were 5’2”, 5’6” and 5’10”.

Given that some respondents mentioned women’s head sizes becoming disproportionate if they were too short or too tall, I was concerned that this would create a fake effect where average-height women’s ratings were artificially inflated. Height turned out not to matter very much for women at all, although the short women received the highest ratings by a very small margin (4.8 average rating for short women, 4.76 for average height, and 4.55 for tall, which I suspect was an artifact of wonky head size.)

There were two exceptions: the skinny women were consistently rated higher if they were tall. Perhaps men who like very skinny women are more likely to prefer a runway model physique than an overall-petite physique, but this seemed to make a measurable impact for this body type. 5’10” skinny women scored better than 5’6” or 5’2” skinny women. Then, the opposite happened with muscular women, who were rated higher if they were short.

Size Was Much More Important Than Shape- Even Within Non-Obese Weights

While I measured women’s weight and height, I was most interested to examine their body shapes overall, because as you know, the conflation of size and shape has been a pet peeve of mine for a while. I wanted to understand to what degree body shape could trump a preference in body size, or vice versa.

As it turns out, weight matters more than shape. Weight explained about 79% of the differences in rating, while shape contributed to about 10%. This is great news for women who want actionable advice to be “hotter”—getting to a healthy, slim (but not underweight) BMI is 79% of the work, no matter what shape you were born with. (I understand this is still not attainable for many women, but weight loss is generally more attainable especially with GLP-1s than changing your bone structure.) Yes, some shapes were preferred over others, but especially once some curvature was present (see: subtle hourglass) the differences in men’s enthusiasm for the different body shapes wasn’t significant.

This emphasis on overall size was not just in terms of thin versus overweight, which is a bit obvious. Even within non-overweight sizes, men rated every slim woman, at every body shape, higher than any other woman, including average and skinny weights, which I expected to be more prevalent in men’s top eight. To avoid the same body type showing up repeatedly at different heights, I filtered for 5’6” women while evaluating shape (especially since women’s height was largely immaterial.)

Although lesbians and bi women’s top preferences differed slightly in order, they weren’t all that different from how men organized their top eight, with all of the slim bodies in the lead. However, lesbians and bi women were more interested in average-sized bodies than skinny bodies by a long shot:

To illustrate just how much more important a slim weight was than a curvy body shape to men, this chart shows how men’s ratings differed for a slim straight-shaped woman versus a skinny or average-sized woman with an hourglass figure:

As you can see, a slightly higher proportion of men rated the skinny hourglass an 8+, but were also more likely to rate it low, so the combination of the very low weight and hourglass shape was clearly polarizing. But looking at average scores and a man’s yes/no binary, the straight-shaped slim woman was overall more popular than hourglass bodies that were skinny or average.

While technically we can rank some body shapes above others within the same weight class, the difference isn’t that strong when it comes to how men would react in real life. I asked everyone what their “dating floor” from 1-10 would be, because someone can rate someone else a 4, but still find 4s totally acceptable for dating. Using men’s self-defined “dating floor” from 1-10 to determine their binary yes/no for each body, this was the percentage of men who would date all the average-weight women:

The only body type that really took a hit was the extreme hourglass, probably because it looked too ridiculous to measure as a real person (although I do see bodies like that on Instagram every day). Within the “average” weight class, there is very little benefit or drawback to whatever body shape you might be stuck with—roughly 50% of men will want to date you either way.

Same thing with slim women. In this case, the straight shape took a bit of a hit, but was still considered acceptable by 74% of men. Either way, no meaningful difference between being a pear, subtle hourglass, inverted triangle or hourglass. Certainly not enough to warrant plastic surgery! Unless you consider 6% of men worth it:

So, while weight is not 100% in our control, I do consider it reassuring that our genetic body shape and weight gain pattern (which is, outside of plastic surgery, not within our control) doesn’t actually make that much of a difference. Just because men seem to prefer proportionately small waists and big boobs, the difference doesn’t seem to actually impact who they are willing to date at the end of the day. At least not by enough to warrant freaking out about it.

Boobs Trump Waist-Hip Ratio (by a little)

Although the difference between men’s preferences in specific body type was not that different, I did notice one surprising thing: the #1 or #2 body shape for men within several weight categories was the inverted triangle (sometimes tied with, or above, the hourglass shape.)

Typically, when discussions of female attractiveness come up, waist-hip ratio is a popular topic. Inverted triangle shapes typically have broad shoulders, big boobs, and then fairly straight and narrow hips, which was how I modeled that figure in the survey. As it turns out, the narrow hips and straight waist don’t matter to men when you’re thin with big boobs! See: the beautiful (and unambiguously desired) Sydney Sweeney.

Perhaps we can go a step further—given that the exaggerated hourglass shapes had Sydney Sweeney’s boobs but also Kim Kardashian’s hips, and they performed much worse than the V shape, we could even make the argument that men who like this body type aren’t merely overlooking the narrow hips to get to the boobs—they want that whole package.

While hourglass shapes performed roughly similarly, pear shapes (which often had a more pronounced waist-hip ratio and smaller boobs) were usually in third or fourth place within their weight class. Perhaps the issue is that the angles of the pear shape images showcased their hips, but not ass, which is usually the main asset (no pun intended) for a pear shape. But either way, as far as frontal views went, the big boob-narrow hip figure reigned supreme. Anyway, another survey will cover ass versus hips, because of course.

Men Really Do Like Different Things

Some on-average patterns were obvious. However, within each body, it was clear that plenty of men would disagree.

I found three bodies in particular that would generate a lot of disagreement: the skinny pear body, the muscular inverted triangle body, and the average hourglass boddy. As you can see, their ratings were all over the place:

A sizable minority of men would consider each of them really hot, but many others consider them average or below-average. Given how different the bodies are from each other in both shape and size, I’m assuming the men who strongly like each one are a different group of men.

Looking at the “average” ratings, all are 5s. But that does not mean that men are unanimous in considering these bodies “meh.” Within all three, about 15-20% of men consider them an 8 or higher, which is really all you need if you’re looking for a partner.

This lends credence to the idea that men who disagree with each other’s preferences aren’t just “virtue signaling,” but genuinely disagree. The average-sized hourglass has the same number of men rating it a 8 versus a 2. These men will probably call each other gay while arguing about it, but they just have different preferences.

Right-Wing Men Are Pickier

This one is no surprise—I proved it a while back via a different survey, which showed that men and women became harsher when rating other bodies as they became more right-wing, although left-wing men were still harsher on women’s bodies than right-wing women were on men’s bodies.

This stayed true in this survey too. Men did not have strongly different preferences on body shape when it came to politics (inverted triangle and hourglass were the most popular among all political alignments, and all political alignments preferred slim bodies) but as women’s bodies became heavier, there was a clear difference in how they were rated between men of different political stripes. The one weight which was more harshly rated by left-wing men than right-wing men was the underweight one. This also remained true in my earlier survey, but far-right men were more interested in very skinny bodies than other political stripes of men.

Men’s Own Looks Matter…A Bit

Men were able to identify a few things about their own attractiveness: their own self-rating from 1-10, their assessment of their own body type, and their dating “floor,” ie: the lowest number from 1-10 they’d be willing to date.

Men who were overweight were slightly more forgiving of overweight female bodies:

All that said, “slightly more forgiving” isn’t doing much. An overweight man is still likely to rate overweight women low—just higher than slim men are rating them. They are also slightly less attracted to skinny women, but still consider them more attractive than chubby or obese women. I have a feeling that this tendency is what ties into weight being a sensitive topic in online discussions of attractiveness. The reality is that fat people marry each other all the time and live happily ever after, but given how overweight people tend to rate each other in these surveys, sometimes I wonder if the real frustration isn’t an inability to find a partner, but an inability to find a partner who isn’t also overweight.

With the exception of men who consider themselves a 3-4, men were all theoretically willing to date a woman who was less attractive than themselves, with the gap between one’s own perceived attractiveness and their dating floor getting broader as the men become more attractive. Assuming she can compete with the better-looking women, a 6 really can pull anyone from a 3 to a 9.

Keeping their dating floors in mind, and measuring that against the ratings they gave women in the survey, the most attractive men were the pickiest, but not by much. A 9 was willing to date 39% of the bodies in the survey while a 3 was willing to date 65%. Extreme Curves Weren’t the Ideal, But Influenced Men’s Other Ratings

I am fascinated by this detail. As part of the survey, I split men into two random groups by their birthdays. One of these groups was shown all the body types in the survey, but the other was not shown extreme variations of conventionally attractive bodies—basically, the bodies that would not exist without Photoshop or plastic surgery, which you can frequently find on Instagram or in AI porn. This included all the extreme hourglasses and the skinny hourglass. I wanted to replicate the experience of man who views AI-generated e-girls or other altered, unrealistically attractive women’s bodies, compared with men who don’t, to see if it made them less forgiving of regular women’s bodies.

The extreme hourglass figure performed well on a slim-sized body (although it underperformed compared to most other slim body shapes) but performed poorly on basically every other body type and was never once in the top two of any body weight class. When viewing them side-by-side, I also realized that the slim extreme hourglass was actually less extreme than the others, which may have contributed to the higher rating, as it looked less absurd:

Either way, I long suspected that men who prefer anime or AI porn—which basically look like the skinny extreme hourglass—were not representative of the modal preferences. It turns out things can get weird when they enter uncanny valley—extreme and unnatural curves on an otherwise underweight body will actually just look weird to a lot of guys. Meanwhile, as we can see here, it can be the ideal for 15% of guys, and as I’ve argued before, those guys are the ones who are likely to be buying sex dolls, watching anime porn or consuming other sexual content that does not involve real-life women who just don’t look that way.

However, men who were exposed to extreme hourglass figures of all sizes (and the regular hourglass figure on the skinny body) wound up being slightly harsher on all other bodies in the dataset. Again, this difference was minimal but statistically significant and consistent across slim, muscular, average and chubby bodies:

The same effect was present for lesbian and bi women, but the sample size was too small for this minor difference to be statistically significant.

Straighter Bodies are More Accepted on Muscular Women

While hourglass and inverted triangle shapes were the most popular for almost all weight classes, followed by pear shapes and extreme hourglasses, there was one exception: a muscular body was more likely to receive higher ratings if the physique was more straight or subtly curvy without large breasts. This could in part be because the extreme curves on muscular bodies just looked bizarre and ‘roided out, but even the less extreme ones did not seem to follow the same pattern as the other body sizes. Perhaps the preference for smaller breasts on a muscular physique come along with the fact that a woman with this degree of muscle rarely has natural large breasts.

Women and Men Don’t Differ Much on Skinny Bodies

For a while, I’ve been annoyed by the stereotype that straight women are totally clueless to the fact that men like curves, and have instead decided that every man wants a boyish, emaciated runway model, foolishly attempting to emulate that look. I wrote about that here, but I also wanted to use this survey to figure out whether straight women felt meaningfully different about skinny female bodies. Was I wrong? Would straight women think the skinny bodies were much more attractive than the straight and bi men?

As it turns out, not really. Nor were straight women delusional about a lack of curves being preferred. Men and women did not differ much on the attractiveness of super-skinny figures, with virtually zero disagreement over the straight-shaped skinny body, which they both rated the lowest of all the skinny bodies, and lower than average.

The “Types” of Men With Niche Preferences

Given the amount of data I asked about men’s own characteristics, I was able to identify which characteristics closely mapped onto a preference for bodies that were not the conventional slim-curvy ideal. This filtered for generous raters overall, and to be considered to have a “type” you would need to have consistently rated this particular type of body in a higher percentile than other bodies you saw, especially compared with how other men were rating them.

Overweight women (chubby + obese): most popular among leftist men, overweight men, bisexual men, and men who rate their own looks a 1-4. These men tend to be more generous raters overall but are especially not attracted to skinny women compared with other men.

Skinny women (not slim): most popular among men making over $400K, who rate themselves an 8 or higher.

Muscular women: most popular among men 50+ and bisexual men, no impact from politics or income.

How much will weight loss affect a woman’s dating odds?

In basically every female hotness guide, women are instructed to lose weight (but not too much weight.) I had a feeling this was true, but I wanted to measure exactly how true.

Using men’s self-described dating “floor,” I calculated what percentage of men would agree to date women at different weights, within different body shapes. (Only two body shapes were represented in the obesity category—I had a hard time generating body shapes and keeping them consistent at that weight.) As you can see, basically every body shape sees a dramatic improvement from the average to slim body size, but all drop in attractiveness when they get to be underweight, with straight and subtle hourglass bodies taking the biggest hit.

(I did not include the extreme hourglass or muscular body types here since they are not common or realistically found among women without significant intervention.)

The shape is mostly the same if you filter specifically for the most in-demand men: under 40, making over $100K annually, a 7+ in attractiveness and visibly physically fit:

And, for those women interested in women, the curve was slightly more forgiving when the raters were lesbians and bi women. Compared with men, they were about two times more likely to date an obese woman or chubby woman, and more likely to date average and slim women of all shapes. They were similarly interested (and not interested) in skinny bodies as men were.

Back to male raters. While all body shapes benefit from a healthy degree of weight loss from chubby to slim, the effect is most significant with pear and inverted triangle shapes.

Now, although this article is not about men’s bodies (the male version is coming) I did want to illustrate how weight loss can affect dating options for a man who is 5’10” and interested in women. Weight loss also changes things significantly for men, with major positive impacts showing up when men get to an average BMI with at least some muscle tone. Similar to women, once a man becomes underweight, his attractiveness takes a major hit, and the same is true when men become overly muscular, although regular-muscular (or “fit”) men do quite well:
