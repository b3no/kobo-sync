I don’t often feature guest posts, but today I’m doing something a bit different and having my husband guest post (yes, he is real, and no he doesn’t go to another school in Canada.) As many of the long term CHH readers know, he developed an all-consuming special interest/obsession with Paris at the age of thirty-seven (driven in part by his diagnosed OCD) and after he had a mini-feature on Parisian restaurants within my Paris fashion roundup, multiple users clamored for his master list of the best Paris restaurants. I know this is a bit out of my usual wheelhouse and oddly specific, but you guys asked for it, and I think you’ll enjoy his hilarious writing even if you aren’t singularly obsessed with Paris.

CHH and I had dinner with another couple last weekend. The guy built a multi-tab projection model to gain an edge in our 9-category salary-capped fantasy basketball league. He asked to see my Paris restaurant list, took one look, and told me I was insane.

Now you all get to see what OCD looks like in the form of a Google Sheet. No formulas, just brute force research. CHH stopped me from hatching a crypto scheme to con her readers into paying for access to my restaurant research. Apparently only she’s allowed to scam her audience.

I love Paris. The culture, the beauty of the city, the fermented food from Fermentation Générale, the honey from the Oberkampf farmers market where I once bought 5 kilos directly from the beekeeper. My OCD fixates on finding the best food and drinks at the best deals. If you’re flexible on dates and willing to fly basic economy, round-trip tickets from the US are typically under $500.

Our first trip to Paris was in 2019 before we had kids. Before the trip I created a basic spreadsheet which consisted of ten restaurants I wanted to try and we ate at seven. I sourced the ten restaurants using Eater Paris. I was only capturing the name of the restaurant, address and if we had a reservation. We found ourselves eating a lot in the eleventh arrondissement, where a lot of the cool new restaurants were serving seasonal, market-driven food.

Fast forward six years, we had kids and decided to go back to Paris. By our second trip, the eleventh had become our favorite neighborhood, and we’ve stayed there on every visit since. It’s centrally located, the food capital of Paris, not as hectic as the major tourist areas, and right next to Upper Le Marais, where CHH copped sleeves as an entire shirt at an indie pop-up shop.

I started reading more food blogs in preparation for trips, such as Meg Zimbeck, David Lebovitz, Le Fooding, and a bunch more. I now have about 100 restaurants I am planning to try and almost fifty at which I’ve eaten.

I initially was using ChatGPT to just provide the descriptions and addresses of the restaurants I found. I would then manually put the information from Chat into the spreadsheet, like some chump from 2025. However, in the last couple of months I have handed the reins to Claude Cowork (AI Agent) to directly edit my spreadsheet. I set up a project, connected my Google Sheet, and trained it on the format I’d already been using. I track:

  1. Walking distance from where we stay

  2. If they offer a lunch prix fixe. Many of the top restaurants will do a limited menu for a 3-course lunch, $26–36. A nice lunch out with kids is often better than keeping them up late for dinner

  3. If they open early for dinner. Most restaurants don’t open till 7pm and if a restaurant opens at 6 or 6:30 that is a huge win

  4. If there is a playground nearby. We can either have our kids burn energy before eating or bribe them with a fun playground if they behave during the meal

  5. If they serve fries (our son is an insanely picky eater)

I have a weekly scheduled run where Cowork sources restaurants from my favorite food bloggers to add directly to my list. It also auto moves restaurants from “want to try” to “where I’ve eaten,” based on if I’ve received an email asking me to review a restaurant on my “want to try list”.

Enough yapping. Here is my list.

This has been my most useful AI work since CHH and I used ChatGPT-3 to be the mediator in a heated dispute: what her shopping budget should be. I had a number in mind and she had a different number in mind, so we turned to GPT-3 to settle the debate. Little did she know, I added to the prompt that whatever numbers we input, the shopping budget from Chat would spit out the number I had in mind. She couldn’t believe the outcome. Two years later I finally told her what I did… JK I told her an hour later. We have a family rule that if something is funny and not inherently mean, you get a pass. I mostly got one, but now she forces me to write on her Substack every few months.

People also asked how we feed our kids on these trips. My daughter is easy. She goes ham on charcuterie platters.

My son eats roughly three foods and gags when he tries new ones (anxiety, I think). He’s broken multiple feeding therapists. My favorite was Sarah, a wide-eyed PT who introduced herself to my then-3-year-old, who immediately replied, “I’m going to call you Banandy” (he loves monkeys, the idea of bananas, etc). She said he could “call her whatever he wanted.” Banandy didn’t have a chance.

After our first trip, where he was basically hungry the whole time because he wouldn’t eat anything we offered, so we switched from hotels to apartments, mostly so I could make my infamous smoothie. We travel with a homemade seed-and-nut mix, peanut butter, hibiscus tea, and cacao nibs, then hit Picard for frozen berries and vegetables. This smoothie keeps him alive. Fun fact: you cannot carry peanut butter on a plane. The punks at TSA confiscated every jar I brought on one trip. His other safe food is fries, and surprisingly only a few of the restaurants we attend serve them, so we supplement with Mickey D’s and crepes as needed.

For breakfast I take the kids out for croissants. The day my son tried a chocolate croissant and loved it was a game changer. Especially once he stopped strategically extracting only the chocolate out of the middle. This was a bigger win than when Banady boasted about getting him to lick an apple.
