Three Dan Sullivans walk into a race for Senate

And as they duke it out in Alaska, progressives win in Pennsylvania and Florida, Trump rises and falls, Michigan confronts AIPAC, Markey faces questions about his age, and a fraud fesses up.

Pennsylvania’s progressive surprise

For the first time this century, the Pennsylvania state House district in Butler County, Pa. — the county where an assassination attempt against President Donald Trump occurred just over two years ago — will be a blue seat. Democrat Brandon Dukes, a self-described progressive who currently works in mortgages and commercial lending, beat Republican Scott Timko, an Air Force veteran, by less than 100 votes in a special election on August 18.

The 12th state House seat turned red in 1998, and nearly 30 years later, Dukes outperformed Democratic registration numbers by 16 percent in a district where there are nearly twice as many registered Republicans as Democrats, the Pennsylvania Capital-Star reports.

“I’m ecstatic. I’m shocked,” Dukes told the Cranberry Eagle. “I’m still processing this. We just flipped a Trump +18 seat, and we flipped a red seat that’s been red for almost 30 years. We made history.”

Turnout was low, with less than one-third of registered voters casting their ballots. In 2024, when Stephenie Scialabba, the incumbent who stepped down in March, was running, 43,000 people turned out and she won with 65 percent of the vote. This time, less than 17,000 people showed up. Republicans in the state, including State Representative Aaron Bernstine, cautioned against over-indexing on the result.

“Special elections aren’t typically indicative of a district. Very low turnout and those types of things,” Bernstine told WPXI.

Meanwhile, Democrats are framing the win as a bellwether for the party. “Tonight’s result not only shows what can be accomplished when Democrats are willing to fight everywhere, but it sets the stage for this November’s midterm elections,” Pennsylvania Democratic Party Chair Eugene DePasquale told the Capital-Star.

“Pennsylvanians are rising up and using their power to combat the chaos, cruelty, and corruption — and we’re not slowing down,” Governor Josh Shapiro wrote on X.

Dukes and Timko will face off again in November to compete for a full term. Until then, Dukes’s win gives Democrats a 103–100 majority in the state House.

A is for Anchorage, and D is for Dan Sullivan

Another election on Tuesday had Democrats on top. Mary Peltola led Alaska’s Senate primary with 48 percent of the vote, with 80 percent of ballots counted. The state’s nonpartisan primary system advances the top four finishers to the general election. Incumbent Republican U.S. Senator Dan S. Sullivan followed five points behind with 43 percent, and trailing him was the other Republican Dan Sullivan (Dan J. Sullivan) and Democrat David Leslie, who received 2 and 1 percent of the vote, respectively, KTUU reports.

For all the concern among Republicans, including S. Sullivan and Trump, that J. Sullivan was a conspiracy by Democrats to confuse voters, neither he nor Leslie earned enough of the vote to bridge the gap between the two leaders.

To make matters more confusing, a third Dan Sullivan waded into the race last week.

Former Anchorage Mayor Dan A. Sullivan endorsed the senator on Facebook Tuesday: “As the OG Dan Sullivan, I urge Alaskans to vote for Sen. Daniel S. Sullivan. He’s been great for Alaska,” he wrote.

Alaska has not elected a Democrat to the Senate since 2008, and Peltola became the first Democrat in 50 years to represent the state in the lower chamber when she won the state’s sole House seat in 2022. In case you need a refresher, Democrats need to win four seats in addition to holding their existing ones to flip the Senate. Alaska, for the first time in years, is a very real possibility.

Republicans are spending accordingly. The Senate Leadership Fund has pledged $17 million for advertising on behalf of S. Sullivan’s campaign. Meanwhile, the Democrats’ Senate Majority PAC has committed more than $12 million in television reservations for Peltola.

The general contest, in any case, will be a continuation of the closely watched battle that unfolded during the primary.

A state where both Trump and the D.S.A. won

A third set of primaries on Tuesday ended in every kind of result, proving the limitations of trends, waves, and bellwethers. In Florida, Trump’s pick won the Republican primary for governor while another Trump-backed candidate lost a House race, and one democratic socialist came out on top in one race while another fell short.

Representative Byron Donalds won the Republican primary for governor and will face former Representative David Jolly who, when he represented the state’s 13th Congressional District, was a Republican before he quit the party in 2018 and registered as a Democrat in 2025. He is a prominent critic of Trump, often appearing on MS NOW. Donalds, on the other hand, was endorsed by Trump. Both candidates easily beat their primary opponents.

In a less successful outcome for the president, Representative Cory Mills, whom Trump had endorsed early in the campaign, lost his primary for re-election in the state’s Seventh Congressional District. Mills is facing both a House Ethics Committee probe into sexual misconduct and allegations of campaign finance violations and a Justice Department investigation into his finances. Trump did not reaffirm his endorsement of the lawmaker after those investigations began, NPR reports. On Wednesday, after Mills lost to Ryan Elijah, a former news anchor, Trump posted on Truth Social that he had, in fact, urged Mills to drop out of the race and praised Elijah for standing for “EVERYTHING MAGA,” endorsing the candidate he said “is respected by ALL.”

Another Trump pick who came up short was Catalina Lauf, a health care entrepreneur whom Trump endorsed in the final days of the primary. She lost to Jim Schwartzel, a local radio-station owner, who is heavily favored to win the November general election in the solidly Republican district in Southwest Florida, the New York Times reports.¹

As for the Democrats, the party’s progressive wing was served a victory in the shape of a win by democratic socialist State Representative Angie Nixon, who defeated Lieutenant Colonel Alexander Vindman in the Democratic primary for U.S. Senate despite being outraised more than 16-to-1. Vindman shot to fame while testifying at Trump’s first impeachment hearing. That prominence helped him raise more than $16 million, mostly from AIPAC and corporate support, which he said he will now give away to Democratic causes, having reserved nearly all of it for the general. Nixon, who raised less than $1 million, was such an unlikely victor that the D.S.A. did not even endorse her and Vindman did not bother to campaign, the American Prospect writes.

Nixon’s campaign centered on Medicare for All, free child care, a universal jobs program with inflation-adjusted wages, and a national rent freeze. She will face incumbent U.S. Senator Ashley Moody in November.

The opposite result played out in Florida’s 25th Congressional District, where centrist Representative Jared Moskowitz staved off a challenge from democratic socialist Oliver Larkin. Moskowitz, who received support from AIPAC, is one of the House’s most vocal supporters of Israel, of which Larkin is a staunch critic, and represents a district home to one of the highest concentrations of Jewish people in the country. The lawmaker was elected to the state’s 23rd Congressional District in 2022 and has been an outspoken advocate of gun violence prevention, having made it a signature issue after the 2018 shooting at Marjory Stoneman Douglas High School, his alma mater. Moskowitz will face former Boca Raton Mayor Scott Singer, a Trump ally, in one of the most competitive House races in the country, the New York Times reports.

Meanwhile on Tuesday, Representative Debbie Wasserman Schultz won the Democratic primary for a newly redrawn seat in a race that the South Florida Sun Sentinel called brutal. After redistricting affected Wasserman Schultz’s current seat, she decided to run in a predominantly Black community — a choice she, as a white woman, received significant pushback for among leaders in the district. Still, she won more than 45 percent of the vote, beating the other four candidates, all of whom are Black. Wasserman Schultz is heavily favored to win the district, which voted for Kamala Harris over Trump by 37 points.

AIPAC concerns bleed from Michigan’s U.S. Senate primary into the general

Former U.S. Representative Mike Rogers, the Republican nominee for Senate, has reportedly asked AIPAC to withhold its public support for his campaign, according to Axios. Rogers spoke with AIPAC’s board chair last week, and the next day the organization had nixed an advertisement it had already cut targeting his opponent, Dr. Abdul El-Sayed.

El-Sayed pulled off a victory earlier this month against Representative Haley Stevens with criticism for AIPAC’s support of her at the center of his bid. The group had spent more than $30 million trying to beat El-Sayed in the Democratic primary and, according to Axios, had planned to spend millions more.

But Rogers, having lost to now-Democratic U.S. Senator Elissa Slotkin in 2024 by less than 20,000 votes despite Trump’s endorsement, cannot take any chances on one of the most animating issues of 2026.

Markey’s momentum against Moulton in Massachusetts

There is a growing campaign to unseat incumbent U.S. Senator Ed Markey, who, at 80 years old, has been in the Senate for 13 years following a nearly 20-term House tenure. The senator is facing a challenge from Representative Seth Moulton, 47, who represents Massachusetts’s Sixth Congressional District.

On Tuesday, the Boston Globe editorial board endorsed Moulton, writing that despite both candidates’ strong abilities and records, the representative promises a new era of governance for the state.

“There is much to like about Markey,” the editorial board writes, listing his congressional track record. “For a man of 80, he is the picture of sharpness and vigor. But the caveat here is justified. Even if you believe 80 is the new 70, it would be fantastical to pretend that voters shouldn’t be concerned about his ability to finish his term.”

Still, the editorial board writes, “the future must be now.”

“On his proven courage in battle and in public life, in his independent nature, in his ideas for modernizing Massachusetts, in his willingness to push back against rigid ideology on both right and left, Seth Moulton is the right person for the job.”

The editorial board joins New Jersey Governor Mikie Sherrill, among others, in endorsing Moulton.

Just two days after the editorial board made its case, Representative Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez endorsed Markey, joining Senators Bernie Sanders and Elizabeth Warren, a handful of representatives, and Boston Mayor Michelle Wu in supporting the incumbent. She campaigned with the senator during his last cycle in 2020 against then-Representative Joe Kennedy III.

Despite a very real effort being made on his behalf, it will be difficult for Moulton to overcome Markey’s mountain of support, name recognition, and incumbency advantage. Polls taken in August have the senator leading the representative by double digits, with the latest, from Public Policy Polling, showing Markey up by more than 30 percentage points. Earlier that week, Suffolk University found a narrower gap, putting Markey’s lead at 15 points ahead of Moulton. The primary is on September 1.

Election integrity

Mail voting rules

On Friday, the United States Postal Service published a 95-page rule that would require states to share voter data before it would deliver mail ballots, in an effort to comply with Trump’s executive order to limit mail voting. The rule explicitly states that it would not move forward unless the federal government received relief from two court injunctions — one from June 25 blocking the order in 23 states plus D.C. and a second nationwide injunction issued on August 11. On Monday, the Supreme Court paused the June ruling, letting the administration’s directives proceed in applicable locations. The second injunction remains unaffected by today’s ruling. The U.S.P.S.’s rules are not set to take effect unless both are lifted, so, as of now, they will not go into effect for 2026.

Don’t trust everything you read on the internet
Median Strategies, a polling website run by 21-year-old Rahil Prakash, turned out to be a sham, or, as the company put it, a “short-term social experiment examining how purported polling information could enter and spread through the political information ecosystem without independent verification.” As it turns out, it could, Prakash told The Guardian.

Among its contributions, the company most recently published a poll that had Los Angeles Mayor Karen Bass leading her challenger by more than 10 points, and it also “found” that Wisconsin gubernatorial candidate Francesca Hong was leading her opponent, David Crowley, by more than 20. Hong lost the nomination earlier this month.
