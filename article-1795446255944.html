What is happening in famously polite Canada?

By: Andrew MacDougall

What the fuck is going on in Ottawa?

That wasn’t quite the question put to me by a London wag with an interest in Canadian politics, but it was the gist of his text messages after he flagged a couple of recent incidents to my attention.

First was Conservative MP John Brassard telling a member of the media that he “didn’t give a shit” about their question (about Pierre Poilievre’s future, or possible lack thereof). And then there was Liberal committee chair James Maloney calling a meeting of the Bill C-39 committee to an end by declaring “this is fucking stupid, come on”, as MPs were trying to agree whether they should sit over the weekend.

My heavens! Parliament has suddenly gone full potty mouth. What, indeed, is going on in famously polite Canada?

Don’t get me wrong. A good swear can be therapeutic, and Ottawa is no stranger to some off-mic unparliamentary language. Maloney’s comment was certainly meant to fall into that category. But shits and fucks are not part of the normal on-the-record media show. An MP who shat on a hot mic would have been crucified in the Before Times, i.e. in the world in which the professional mainstream media was the only game in town.

Remember the furore after Pierre Trudeau issued his famous “oh, fuck off”, i.e. “fuddle duddle?” Or when the d’Artagnan-bearded Justin Trudeau called then-Environment Minister Peter Kent a “piece of shit” on the floor of the House of Commons? And lest you think it’s only Trudeaus who swear in Canadian politics, former NDP MP Pat Martin once called the Conservative government “rat-faced whores” for not inviting him to an infrastructure event in his riding.

To be fair, Martin issued his bon mots on the-then newish medium of Twitter, which didn’t have the same standards as either terrestrial TV or print media. Indeed, Twitter thrives on the odd bit of rat-faced whoreism. And even more so today than in Martin’s time.

And that’s the point.

Mainstream media is not the only game in town anymore. In today’s algorithmic antfarm, a bit of the old shit and fuck helps get you noticed. Whatever point Brassard hoped to make — in this case, about Mark Carney and his ties to satellite company Telesat — would have died on the cutting-room floor had he not been so bold in both his choice of swear word and location of swearing.

Is that why Brassard swore? I haven’t a fucking clue. For what it’s worth, it looked like genuine frustration to me at a reporter who obviously didn’t give a shit why he was there that day. But there is an argument to be made that this kind of language helps to keep the home fires burning online. To wit, Brassard’s comments are now all over social media and his intemperance toward the media is being stanned by Conservative-supporting accounts left, right and centre. For his part, Brassard is standing by his language, as well as his insistence that the mainstream media are missing the real story by focusing on Poilievre’s leadership instead of issues like Telesat and the belated conflict screen applied to its affairs with respect to Carney.

Whether any of this is helpful for Canadians who don’t get off on a bit of the ultra un-Parliamentary online is another question. And this is indeed the question for Conservatives: do they benefit from being intemperate with the Canadians who don’t currently support them? If your leader already has a reputation for being a bit of an ass, is it helpful for you to exhibit ass-like tendencies?

For what it’s worth, I have sympathy for Brassard and the colleague who went out to speak to the media after the Liberals on committee used their majority to shut down the opposition’s enquiries on Telesat and the multi-billion dollar contract with the Government of Canada awarded to it under Carney’s leadership. Just how the Prime Minister didn’t have a conflict screen in place with a company run by one of his best friends is, indeed, mind-boggling. Thankfully, the Conflict of Interest and Ethics Commissioner is looking into it.

So, yes, Telesat is a story. Just like Maloney and his fellow Liberals trying to ram Bill C-39 through committee is a story. And yes, it was rude for the reporter to essentially cop to not caring about Brassard’s announcement in favour of her own narrow pursuits. But that’s journalism. Other people have written the Telesat story. The commissioner will pronounce. Life will go on.

My fear is that life will go on in the Brassardian direction. As long as our information economy is ruled by what holds our attention, heretofore obscure politicians like Brassard will be tempted to light themselves on rhetorical fire in order to get coverage. It’s like the toddler who discovers that smearing shit on the walls gets their parents’ attention.

What we collectively need to realize is that indulging the smearing only begets more shit. Serious public-policy journalism like Telesat already has enough trouble competing with content in the attention economy.

We shouldn’t be in a hurry to add to the competition.
