Millennial Marriage

I was happy to see this topic in the discourse because this had been my beat for ages now. Some of my fellow millennial women pointed out that conversations about women of their generation “delaying marriage” are incongruent with the experiences of secular millennial women in the 2000s and 2010s: being called crazy if you wanted any commitment at all, with the understanding that men didn’t want to be married and would put it off as long as possible. Now, suddenly, women have “delayed!”

While I’m sure some women deliberately deprioritized marriage to focus on other things, my anecdotal experience of being a millennial woman was that nearly every woman wanted marriage more than her boyfriend did (a lot of time, even admitting you wanted a monogamous boyfriend made you seem “crazy”) and that anti-marriage sentiment was popular among lots of young men, not just the ones with options.

Pit Bulls (Again)

I am obviously no stranger to “aggressive dog discourse.” I was never a big pit bull person, but also didn’t care that much either way. I thought about pit bulls about as much as I thought about the Steelers, which is to say, almost never. If the topic came up, I mostly bought into the “it’s the owner, not the dog” thing because it was the line that was parroted around me constantly, and I even had people telling me it was racist—not dog-racist, but like, actually racist, somehow, to believe dog breeds had any inherently different traits despite being bred specifically for different traits, unlike humans.

Until I had kids. Suddenly, there were a bunch of pit bulls (often unleashed!) roaming around my neighborhood, and I was increasingly aware of just how badly that can go when little kids are involved. My husband was chased and nearly bitten by one on his run, and when he yelled at the owner for losing control of her dog, she burst into tears and was comforted by bystanders who thought my husband was a big ol’ meanie. Frequently, I cross the street to get away from one, and its owner yells after me that “she’s friendly,” as if I’m supposed to know that (or believe them, which, I don’t.) I’ve seen stories of pit bulls with aggressive histories erased and pushed onto families, only to kill their children. I’m aware that shelters are passing off pit bulls as other breeds to sanitize them for families who otherwise may have safety concerns. Consider me radicalized.

Now, I’m at the point where I view pit bulls more or less as domesticated tigers or assault rifles with their own free will, carrying a small chance they will randomly just snap and kill someone for no reason. Because, well, they sometimes do that, even when they’re raised by attentive, loving suburban families who would never abuse an animal. So much for “the owner.” As for chihuahuas being more aggressive, they have yet to kill anyone!

All that to say, this is basically the only Twitter hivemind topics where I think Twitter is much more sane and reasonable than the general population, who mostly, across political lines, believe that pit bulls are totally fine to allow around children because they have one and hasn’t eaten anyone yet. Well, pediatricians apparently disagree. But that doesn’t matter. Outside of Twitter, people think it’s crazy to be concerned about having your children around these things.

In case that rant somehow wasn’t enough for you, you can read more of my thoughts on the topic here.

But anyway, pit bulls are back in the Twitter discourse, because why not! They seem to connect everyone across the political spectrum, not easy to do on a place like X The Everything App, where nearly any position on any topic can have you hounded by people who are one crashout away from being sectioned.

I’ve rounded up some of the most insane/amusing takes, such as this guy who suggests that a child fleeing a pit bull should simply learn to stop being so afraid and the dog is only aggressively chasing them because they can smell fear (if this were true—and it may very well be—it’s an unintentionally great argument for “pit bulls are unsafe around children.”)
