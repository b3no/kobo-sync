Happy Sunday! Let’s start by pointing you to the latest episode of The Line Podcast, recorded on November 20th, 2026, Matt Gurney hosts a special edition of the show with three guests and three very different conversations.

And earlier in the week, Gurney talked to Scott Stinson and Alan Findlay for On The Line, to discuss hockey, inflation, politics, and whether Canada can finally get serious about building major infrastructure.

And now, on with the Dispatch.

Odds are you missed the actual Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit in Shenzhen, which took place on Wednesday and Thursday of last week. You didn’t miss much. The official gathering delivered the usual post-summit buffet of blandness, long on talk and short on action or even commitments to maybe do some action: there was a “Shenzhen Outlook” declaration promising an open, dynamic, resilient Asia-Pacific, a new “Shenzhen Initiative” on AI and green supply chains, and host Xi Jinping tossed $20-million into a cooperation fund while reminding everyone that we’re all “passengers riding the same ship”. Kumbaya, my lord, kumbaya.

But nobody cared about any of this anyway, because they were all getting ready for the real show, viz., the unprecedented 90-minute trilateral sit-down on the sidelines between Xi, Donald Trump, and Vladimir Putin. The hands-down winner, by a long shot, was Putin...
