I have no problem with her work, her endearingly geeky special interest in beauty aesthetics, or this topic in general. After all, I wrote about gorillas who can’t get ass, so who am I to judge? We also likely don’t disagree that much on the average, pooled male sexual preferences. We both agree men like youthful faces and hourglass figures, and may fantasize about an unrealistic ideal. We “disagree” in that I believe there’s more type-based and culturally influenced variety than what your average sex-doll-designer creates, and that men who like non-anime-proportioned women are not virtue-signaling when they say so.

(For example, I once had to defend—not to Aria, but to someone else—the idea that JFK Jr. was genuinely attracted to Carolyn Bessette Kennedy and not just choosing her willowy physique and narrow face to virtue signal and appear “high status.” Unfortunately, he cannot be here with us today to confirm, but I’m gonna make a wild guess and assume that JFK Jr., a man who had no need to settle, just dated who he found hot.)

All that said, my issue is not with Aria’s work or her theses, but of the framing around it (often by other people) that she (and J. Sanilac, who inspired most of her work) are presenting forbidden knowledge and busting lies. Sanilac even titles his own manifesto—also full of broadly true things, to his credit—as “dispelling beauty lies” and encourages readers to send this truth nuke to women everywhere who may use this dangerous knowledge to their advantage. They believe women have been “lied to” about feminine beauty and male preferences, and that only through these controversial, uncomfortable, facts-don’t-care-about-your-feelings truths can women be enlightened in a way that they can use to their advantage on the marriage market as some kind of secret weapon.

This is where I strongly disagree—not with the content, but with the framing this content as a big, world-shattering secret that will meaningfully change life for women. I do not believe that “men like young, pretty, slim women with hourglass figures” is forbidden knowledge, nor do I think women are oblivious to this. If women have “failed” to emulate this ideal, it’s likely not because they’re clueless about it, but because it’s nearly impossible to look that way, and most women are not interested in undergoing multiple major surgeries to do so, especially if looking like regular women is not hampering their love lives in the first place.

Enjoy the analysis if you like, but “straight men think Sydney Sweeney is hot” is not a truth nuke.

The argument goes a bit like this: society has, for some reason, lied to women about what men like, and women have been foolishly following this false ideal and making themselves look less attractive, to the dismay of men, whose voices and preferences are being silenced by…I don’t know, spins wheel the Jews?

One problem here is that people cannot agree on which lie is being peddled. Either the body positivity movement has encouraged women to gain as much weight as possible and emulate the aesthetics of a polyamorous Portland dungeon master, or the scary music fashion industry and its nefarious cabal of GAYS have conditioned women into thinking that straight men prefer stick-skinny women without boobs, hips, butts or waist definition, so we have hordes of women trying to make their boobs and butts as small as possible, and their waists as square as possible, repelling men for being too skinny and boyish.

Call me crazy, but I think it’s pretty hard to be a woman in 2026 with Internet access and not be immediately aware of what men find attractive. If anything, the most porn-brained and “politically incorrect” preferences are the easiest to find, because the Internet is wall-to-wall porn, AI girls, and thirst traps. A woman cannot open Instagram without being reminded that an impossibly small waist, gigantic boobs, gigantic butt and childlike face is the ideal for many men. If anything, too much exposure to social media will probably convince women this preference is more unanimous and rigid than it is.

Even I, in 2002 (at age twelve), with zero Internet access and forbidden from watching adult television, surmised that I was not the conventional ideal, and made this comic where I drew my ugly self contrasted with the ideal. If a twelve-year-old girl was drawing proto-Sydney-Sweeney virgin vs. Chad comics in 2002, I don’t think modern women are really that clueless:

I understand some men feel their sexual preferences are being silenced because commercials or TV shows routinely feature women they don’t find hot (perhaps arousing men is not the point of fashion shows or soap commercials for women) and they can’t go around talking about their love of big racks without people looking at them like this:

I get it, I really do, but it’s not like women have free reign to blab about their sexual proclivities to everyone in the office either. Are women’s preferences being “silenced” because they can’t walk around happy hour talking about seven-foot-tall sadistic vampires from BookTok? Discussing any sexual preference is going to be weird in polite society! Nobody would applaud a man for walking around talking about how sexy narrow hips are, any more than people would applaud a man for extolling the virtues of wide hips. It’s not that your preferences are being silenced, but rather that people don’t want to hear what makes other people horny, except perhaps on the Internet, where there is apparently a huge unmet need for this.

Now, let’s attack the argument that women’s beauty ideals are all fucked because of body positivity. The body positivity movement existed, and yes, there were a few years where it was in vogue for companies to use models who were especially unconventional-looking, ideally with multiple markers of marginalized identities, to make a point about how progressive they were. But this likely did not convince women that they should aim to be obese and gender nonconforming to attract straight men. The platitudes about how you should “rock your stretch marks” might have made women feel slightly better about having stretch marks in the first place (out of their control, anyway) but hardly convinced a bunch of women that they needed to go out and get stretch marks. And, as I wrote about before, the body positivity movement didn’t actually result in a decrease in eating disorders or self esteem issues! These issues actually got worse during this era, because the body positivity movement basically did nothing. The worst thing you can say about it wasn’t that it convinced everyone to be fat (it didn’t) but that it was completely useless. Okay, and perhaps men had to suffer through a few commercials starring women who weren’t conventionally hot, but it’s not like Instagram and porn went anywhere. Were you ever really planning to jack off to a Calvin Klein jeans commercial? (Don’t answer this one, Sean Thomas.)

Now, the second argument: that women have been tricked by the insidious gays into looking like runway models, because gay men who work in the fashion industry, despite being attracted to men, will make an exception for Kate Moss. (As a woman derided for being inadequately curvy, I will go out and try to seduce a gay man for science. Wish me luck!)

This argument is perhaps even dumber than the “everyone wants to be fat now because of body positivity” argument. When you walk around outside, do you see a surplus of women who look like runway models, and who are too thin and model-y to find a boyfriend? Are men turning away women in spades for looking too much like models? This argument relies on several false assumptions, which I’ll outline below:

  • Every woman’s ideal is to look as non-curvy and skinny as possible (curves in this case meaning boobs/waist/ass, not overall weight).

  • Most women are watching high fashion runway shows and taking beauty/body cues from them.

  • Most women believe men are watching high fashion runway shows and jacking off to runway models.

  • Despite women having access to an endless drip of social media e-girls, porn stars, Sydney Sweeney, big tiddy Goth gfs, Kim Kardashian, and other straight-man-favored feminine sex icons with curves, they obtusely believe men are attracted to androgynous models.

  • If a woman looks like a runway model, men will not be attracted to her and she will have a hard time finding a partner.

  • Men are having a hard time finding women they are attracted to, because too many women look like runway models

  • Every woman could look like Kim Kardashian or a runway model, but they are stupidly choosing to look like runway models.

All of this is obviously not true. I don’t think I need to spell out why it’s not true! Runway models often have boyfriends and have no trouble finding men who are attracted to them. There are, probably, far more men attracted to runway models than women who look like runway models, even if that’s not a common preference, because model-looking women are extremely rare. (Similarly, bodybuilders are rare enough that there are probably more women attracted to bodybuilders than actual bodybuilders who exist—even if most women don’t prefer bodybuilders!) And perhaps mostly importantly, most women do not have the genetics to look anything like Kim Kardashian or a runway model, and any woman with the genetics to look like one stands zero chance of looking like the other.)

As I’ve written before, most women are not that curvy (“curves” measured by waist-hip ratio) no matter their weight, health or age. The average bra size in the US is said to be a 34DD, but that doesn’t account for the fact that the vast majority of American women are overweight. Per my article on waist-hip ratio, even when measuring the waist-hip ratios of young women in Brazil (a population you’d expect to be full of natural Kardashians) the average waist-hip ratio was actually pretty unremarkable—just 0.73, a far cry from the sub-0.6 ratio that Kim K probably has, which almost no women have naturally. And as for boobs, it goes without saying that most women do not have the genetics to simply gain weight or lose weight and come out looking like Sydney Sweeney.

Given that most women (yes, including women of color) do not have genetics for extreme curvature, it makes sense that women who want to look hot would focus mostly on the lever that is within their control—weight and fitness. To a particularly clueless man, this could look like women are pointlessly striving to look like runway models, because their realistic endgame is being thin without much in the way of curve. But that’s because most women would not be terribly curvy at any weight, and if they have to choose between being mid-size and non-curvy, and being thin and non-curvy, they usually choose thin.

As I’ve said before via this cartoon, losing weight is at least possible for most women, but looking like an anime character is beyond the realm of reality even with plastic surgery. And, let us not forget that breast implants are pretty major surgery even if they would look better in theory. So naturally, more women will pursue the low-hanging fruit of fitness, especially given that men aren’t really that picky:

Then, there is the Taylor Swift/Zendaya argument, which is a bit like the runway model argument. These women are consistently used as examples of women who appeal to women, but not men. Then, I’m supposed to believe that women are trying to look like these people, women think men find Taylor Swift and Zendaya the hottest women alive, and are foolishly emulating them when they could emulate someone hotter. I’ll concede that if you asked everyone to rate Taylor Swift and Zendaya, women might give them higher ratings than men would. These are two women with mostly-female fanbases. But it’s getting a little ridiculous that we are now supposed to pretend that Taylor Swift and Zendaya are femcel ogres who repel every man who is unfortunate enough to stumble into their slimy paths. Both of these women are married to successful, good-looking men! And if women think they’re attractive or likable or talented, it’s not because women think they have a fanbase of men jerking off to them, it’s because sometimes women just like things. We like Taylor Swift’s music. We like Zendaya’s acting. We think they’re pretty, even if we don’t think they represent the heterosexual male ideal. None of this means we are trying to look like them to please men. And, might I remind you, most of us could not look like either of these women even if we wanted to. (I tried to will myself to shape-shift into Zendaya for the purpose of this article, but somehow I’m still me.)

Male preferences for impossibly curvy women with pretty, young-looking faces are just not a secret. This is not an uncomfortable red pill that women must take, lest we be in the dark about it. The same goes for men’s preferences for women far younger than themselves. This is repeatedly mentioned as something “nobody knows about,” but makes even less sense than the supposedly forbidden truth of hourglass body supremacy. Most women have never lived with an hourglass figure, but every woman has been eighteen, and most of those women have been hit on by men far too old for their liking. Women are fully aware that men like young women, this is not a secret, and yet it continues to be circulated as an idea that’s been so shrouded in mystery that it needs more airtime.

There is a common Internet-brained misconception that if something is rude, controversial and hurts people’s feelings, it has to be true—not just true, but secret, forbidden, hidden knowledge—and that’s why people don’t like it. But most of the time, women react negatively to this messaging because we already know it’s true and have been bombarded with it constantly, not because it “dispels lies.” If women seem to be oppositionally refusing to look the way men want them to look, it’s probably because:

  • They are already with a man who likes them and it’s fine.

  • Attracting men is not their top priority/

  • They correctly surmise most men actually aren’t picky at all.

  • Even if they wanted to look like a mix betweeen Kim Kardashian and Sydney Sweeney with the face of an eighteen-year-old, it would not be possible with their genetic makeup.

I concede that perhaps, in the 1980s or 1990s, some women might have had incorrect ideas about what men liked because social media wasn’t a thing, and if women wanted to watch porn out of curiosity, they’d have to visit a seedy porn shop to do so. But even then, breast implants were very popular! Women watched Baywatch. Music videos existed. (I have no idea why some men seem to believe the only representations of attractive women exist on highbrow fashion runways. There are other forms of media!) And in any case, if women were really under some mass delusion that men liked boyish figures, we’d see more women opting for breast reductions than implants, but generally the only women who get breast reductions are doing so because of back pain, not as part of a misguided attempt to appeal to men.

While it’s fun to examine the scientific average around sexual preferences, it doesn’t actually have much application for what an ordinary woman is to do to find a boyfriend, outside of some fairly obvious things. Aria’s and Sanilac’s guides are insanely actionable and detailed if you are designing a sex doll, creating AI porn, or if you are an escort or sugar baby looking to maximize your client base. In this case, there isn’t really much of a cost-benefit analysis of whether you should get plastic surgery, for example. It’s all about the ROI, and if you’re a sex worker, there’s probably tremendous ROI to looksmaxxing. But if you’re a regular woman just looking for a boyfriend or husband, getting lots of plastic surgery to resemble a Kardashian with the face of a college freshman (or, perhaps, younger) is probably not going to do much for you. Most regular men don’t require that, and I would venture that if you only look that way because of copious plastic surgery, it may be a turnoff. The best “revealed preference” of what a Manhattan lawyer wants to marry is who those men actually marry, not the sex dolls that other men might be designing.

Plastic surgery is another hurdle that is routinely waved away as a hurdle at all, because supposedly, if plastic surgery is done well, men really like it, much the way they like makeup but often don’t notice when it’s natural. But these are two very different things. Admittedly, I do not have data on this, but a lot of ordinary men are put off when they discover a woman has had a lot of plastic surgery. Even if plastic surgery is done well and men can’t immediately tell, they’ll eventually know, once they see old photos of you, or grab your boobs or butt, or whatever. Some men won’t mind, others will, and the “others” might not be a small minority. This matters less if you are a sex worker looking for work, but matters quite a bit if you’re a woman looking for a husband. Even if we concede that your average guy prefers big boobs (he probably does) there is no guarantee that your average guy—let alone your average high-quality husband material guy—prefers fake big boobs over real average or small ones. In this case, it probably depends greatly on the boobs and the guy, and I’d be curious to see how men actually feel about this (I also feel like there is a generational aspect to this—anecdotally, both boomer and zoomer men seem more pro-implant than Gen X or millennial men.)

So while these guides are very interesting and fun, extremely well-researched, and not actually untrue, they aren’t really actionable beyond the basics of fitness, finding a good hairstyle, doing flattering makeup, and dressing for your body—all valid things to do, by the way, if you want to be hotter! (Yes, Aria is clear about finding the right weight for your waist-hip ratio, but in practice typically this just translates to “on the slim side” which is what most women aim for anyway.) Perhaps more women would benefit from natural-looking makeup, long hair and flattering clothes, but that’s also not exactly a dangerous truth nuke either. Women who aren’t doing those things “correctly,” such as women who cut their hair super short or get tattoos, are probably aware that they are not adhering to the mainstream beauty ideal and don’t really care. They’re either not trying to attract men, or trying to attract men within more alternative niches who are into those things.

In a way, all of this is the reverse of the “nobody is saying that” phenomenon, where someone will hold a ridiculous opinion but then if you criticize it, they will claim that “nobody is saying that.” This is a bit different, where the argument is, “nobody is saying that, but they should say that and they’re being silenced!” Except, no, they are not being silenced. They are saying that constantly, and everyone is like, “Yeah, we know.” I know I usually tell people to go touch grass, but in this case, if you truly believe men’s tastes are being silenced and censored…just log on.
