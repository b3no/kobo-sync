Pumpkin

Lance Corporal Scott Bukkake
Nov 16
 
READ IN APP
 

What happened with Pumpkin was like capturing lightning in a bottle. You have a cute cat and you post pictures and videos of it. It’s just what people do. How was I supposed to know that the clicks would come in like they did? Suddenly, my Instagram and my Tiktok became Pumpkin’s. I called it Pumpkin and Friends, because sometimes my dog, Winston, would be in videos. Videos where Pumpkin and Winston were together did pretty well.

My veterinarian said that Pumpkin had some kind of feline dwarfism, and was basically a perma-kitten. He was going to be tiny forever, and people love that stuff. When his mouth didn’t have enough room for his teeth and I had to have them pulled, people loved how his little tongue hung out. And it got us a really good brand dealership with Bastet brand cat food. The pate was really soft, so it was easy for Pumpkin to eat... I posted a video of Pumpkin eating the pate and Boom! Pumpkin had a brand deal. How cool is that?

Some people think it’s just views that do it, but really it’s the brand deals. Bird toys, puzzles, brands of catnip… The robotic litterbox brand was a great partnership too, until those other cats got strangled by it. We distanced ourselves from that one: I posted a photo of Pumpkin with a caption about how sad we were that the litterbox hurt other cats and that it’s not something that Pumpkin can support in lieu of the safety concerns.

Either way, Pumpkin was great. I got to quit my job so that we had more time to do more catfluencer stuff. You’ve got to be ready on the camera to capture those little moments. Every nap where his tongue hangs out. Every funny noise he makes when he eats. Every misjudged jump, and stumble from his short little perma-kitten legs. Editing video and finding the perfect clips isn’t easy and responding to comments is practically a full time job when your cat is as popular as Pumpkin was.

Since Pumpkin passed away, it’s been pretty difficult. The crappy thing about owning a cat with genetic defects is that there’s probably ones you can’t see. Comorbidities, the vet called them. We did what we could, but it was time to put Pumpkin down eventually.

The other unfortunate thing is that, being the social media manager for your pet definitely has a timeline. I haven’t had a real job in four years. You know, the cat I had growing up lived to be fifteen! I expected at least a decade out of Pumpkin. Not five years (four of which were particularly fortuitous). And when I bought Pumpkin, I just thought they were normal. They were little! How was I supposed to know he had feline dwarfism?

Do you know how hard it is to find another cat with feline dwarfism, by the way? Even cats with Cerebellar Hypoplasia aren’t growing on trees. Wobbly cat’s do great, and with the audience I have from Pumpkin, it could be a great windfall, but I can’t even find one of those. And I’m running out of shelters here. I love Winston, but I guess he just doesn’t have the same panache that Pumpkin possessed.

In the interim, I have tried to be a Lizardfluencer, but I’ve found that the market is sorely saturated, and I am trying to phase out of that because I really hate feeding Pickle those little crickets. I hate bugs. I’m trying to get over that though, because I’ve been seeing a lot of horned beetles on my feed and those seem to do well and there aren’t too many beetle people crowding the space.

Anyway, if anyone is interested in a Chinese Day Gecko, please reach out to me at my email: Pumpkinandfriends@rocketmail.com
