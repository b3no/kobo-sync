The Bad Boyfriend

Lance Corporal Scott Bukkake
·
Apr 3
Read full story

We were going to hang out with a friend from his new job. Pat didn’t always get along with people, so when someone did seem to like him he kind of held fast and hard. He didn’t have a great time when he was younger socially and carried a chip on his shoulder about it. He once had a meltdown when a friend of ours he passed on the road didn’t wave to him, assuming it was intentional instead of the guy just not seeing him while he was driving.

Nate was one of those Fox Racing working-class guys. He just didn’t have enough money for the pick-up truck that had the decal on the window about it. He was shorter than Pat, with brown hockey hair peeking out from under his hat and jeans that were too baggy and always ripped. He lived with his parents and his girlfriend. I don’t remember much about Nate.

I do remember his girlfriend, Lita, who was Russian. With an accent which was so cool. She had grown up in foster care and kind of had a Cookie Monster Pajama Pants girl thing. You’d expect her to speak in an “urban patois,” were it not for the eastern bloc accent. Blond, thin, but with a very plain face, instead of the curated image one is used to seeing when the phrase “Russian Woman” comes to mind. No one had ever taught her to put on makeup, I figured. Foster Care, after all. But I found her to be friendly and fun. Usually with expats and immigrants who come from predominantly white countries, there is a certain level of pretension or patronization with how they carry themselves and Lita didn’t have that. She just wanted to have a good time and was pretty uncomplicated.

So we had our first play date. We took a brewery tour, got tacos and visited a park. It was not a good time of year in Wisconsin to visit the park. It was late March so the terrain was rendered into a cold, muddy mess by way of slowly melting snow. The edges of our jeans all ended up sodden and dirty and there had been many slips and near-falls as we traversed the paths, crushing cheap beer as we went.

It was so long ago, that I don’t remember the inciting incident, but I do know it was harmless. Play-fighting. Pat had said something insensitive, as he was prone to do, and Lita took mock offense and threw up arms to give him a little slap. She stumbled. I saw her stumble because of the shitty, muddy ground and the little slap she’d intended to give became a big slap. Pat was pissed, but Lita profusely apologized and everything got smoothed over. No harm, no foul…

Our next play date involved inviting Nate and Lita to Karaoke night. Pat loved Karaoke because he was an attention whore and wanted Nate and Lita to bear witness to his glory. Karaoke was the only time he really felt like he had any glory. His ability to stay on key, song choice and posturing did elicit him a lot of positive attention from drunk bar patrons. It was his thing.

Nate and Lita had pre-gamed, because a pack of PBR was cheap and you could get a good buzz and maintain instead of blowing a bunch of money at the bar. Pat just smoked weed all the time, but he got a long island iced tea at the bar. I got a gin and tonic.

Unfortunately, there would be no singing. There would be no screechy rendition of Whole Lotta Love and Pat’s shuffling feet. We didn’t even get to finish our drinks. In a repeat of what had happened in the park, Pat said something stupid and Lita feigned offense. She went in for another slap… I know I mentioned being buzzed before, but in all truthfulness Lita was already drunk. This time she kind of poked him in the eye, misjudging the distance and angle of attack thanks to being inebriated. But Pat was pissed again, and no amount of apologizing could make up for the second transgression. He was treating it like a blood offense.

I’m a big “Don’t Die On That Hill” person, and found myself grabbing my giant, angry boyfriend back so that he wouldn’t beat the shit out of a tiny, drunk Russian girl. A six-foot-tall man beating up a woman is just never a good look. I closed out our tab and without a goodbye, we went back to the car and left. Pat was in a simmering rage and monologuing: “If only people knew what I was capable of. How much anger I have…” Which was, first of all, incredibly cringy and embarrassing. Like, really bro? You’re going to have a little speech about your inner turmoil?... Second of all, what he saw as a great offense, I saw as an unfortunate convergence of two facts:

Some women don’t know how to interact with men beyond being playfully rude.

Drunk people lack coordination.

So I tried to be the voice of reason. I stated these facts, and tried to explain that it was nothing personal. We were having fun with Lita and Nate, and instead of threatening to beat the shit out of the tiny, drunk Russian Girl, which would not have yielded any positive outcome, he probably could have exchanged some words and let Lita know that when she hit him, playfully or not, that it made him feel bad. If that didn’t help, a simple “Nate, come get your girl,” would have probably sufficed. Maybe instead of drinking with Lita, we try other activities? Where he saw sin, I saw a girl who was too playful and too drunk.

“Can you just ONE TIME have my back?! You always take everyone else’s side! You NEVER support me. Why can’t you just be a RIDE OR DIE and have my FUCKING BACK, like Shelly!?”

Okay, fair point. From the outside, I suppose I did seem unsupportive. If you’re tremendously stupid. What would he have me do? Let him beat up a woman and get arrested? What did he want? For me to be the Henchman to his Villain and mindlessly twiddle my fingers and say “Mmm Nyeeeesss, Master!” to every terrible impulse he had?

And… Wait a minute?…

Who the fuck was Shelly?

As far as I know, and from what I can confirm among friends, Pat never slept with Shelly. Shelly wouldn’t touch Pat with a 10-foot pole. She was a tattoo artist and we all shared friends. If I recall correctly, she was engaged to a guy at the time and in spite of being an alt-chick with cool clothes, piercings and crazy hair, was going to convert to Islam for this fellow.

I guess that scores pretty high on the Ride or Die scale, for a hot Alt-Chick to give up her labret piercings and technicolor hair for a man of, what many of my white brother and sisters would consider to be, a contentious faith (Shelly was most certainly white and non-religious). But Shelly was also the kind of girl Pat would be enamored with. I mean, he was enamored with any woman who talked to him and was kind of slutty. Shelly might have worn a uniform that was coded as potentially slutty, but she wasn’t. Also, she was out of Pat’s league by miles.

Pat was a large, fat man who wore skinny jeans and had tits. His beard was patchy and more importantly… he was a loser. For most of our relationship, I was the one who paid for everything, going so far as covering the fines for his OWI and buying my weight in weed that I never smoked. Were Pat a Muslim, he was certainly not the type of man any woman who didn’t practice would convert for. He was a bad return on investment.

Two things were apparent: Pat remained stupid and emotionally volatile (I already knew this), and he had a crush on Shelly. And, he didn’t even know her well enough to make the Ride or Die claim. He was enamored and made shit up, as those who are enamored tend to do.

Listen, I had crushes too. But, there is a code of conduct within a relationship where you bury those things. Never once did I yell at him and say: “Why can’t you hold down a job like Jesse?” or “Why can’t you be smart like Kevin?” or “Why aren’t you faithful like Aaron is to his girlfriend?” That’s bad form. That would be an admission that I was noticing these things in other men and desiring them for it, when I should only have been desiring Pat and helping him work through the things he wanted to improve willingly.

Pat having a crush was hardly surprising. He cheated on me constantly. Every year of our relationship had been marked by some incident or transgression, where the ensuing fight would end with him crying about how he was a big piece of shit and I’d be the one comforting him. Fool me once, basically. I figured it was like cockroaches, too. Where if you see one, there’s many more. And I just kept waiting for the path to change and for Pat to realize that he owed me the grace I felt I was always giving him.

By all accounts, I would say that this certainly made me a bona fide Ride or Die. I kept on riding, in fact, even though I wanted to die!
