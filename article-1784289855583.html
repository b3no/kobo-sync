What I Wore In Greece

My fashion journey through my husband's ancestral island

For my in-laws’ fiftieth wedding anniversary, our family (including my husband’s brother and his family) all went to three locations in Greece to visit my husband’s extended family who still live in Greece. In addition to Mytiline, where they are from, we also visited Athens and Naxos just for fun! (I don’t think I’ve ever seen a smaller airport. What is this, an airport for ANTS?)

So naturally, with all this cultural, historical and family-oriented significance, my mind was on one thing: what I was wearing.

We had limited baggage space so I had to be pretty intentional about a color palette for the trip. Perhaps in an ultra-tourist, cringe way, I decided to focus primarily on bright blue to match the Greek flag, but I also threw in some red just for fun. (I also just love blue and red together.)

For the plane ride from Athens (layover) to Mytilene, I wore this coord set that I sewed from deadstock cotton jersey from Lyrical Fabrics. As soon as I saw this print, I thought, “Oh, that’ll be for Greece!” I made everything a bit looser than my usual coord styles, and I actually like this fit so much more! The cropping saves it from being too “baggy” for my taste, but the looser silhouette feels more appropriate for an island vacation, perfect to throw on over a bathing suit or wear on a plane:

I also wore this Skims ribbed maxi dress, which I had to get in a petite size only because the regular size was out of stock, so on me it’s really more of a midi, and has less of a flare than this dress style typically does. Still, easy to throw on, comfortable, withstood a bunch of ketchup stains courtesy of my two-year-old (and in case you’re wondering, her dress is a Mini Boden number that I thrifted because I will never buy Mini Boden at full price, no matter how cute it is.)

Look at the view! We had lunch here.

For a trip with my husband and father-in-law to Molyvos (a city within Mytilene, where we went on our honeymoon). I’m wearing a simple blue muscle tank from Aritzia, a printed knit midi skirt from Farm Rio, and a raffia bag from Sezane. Honestly, I would not have chosen those Vivaia shoes if I had all the choices in the world, but we were limited to one checked bag and they kind of go with everything, so deal with it.

Then and now! 12 years apart:

Now, for the Naxos outfits. If you haven’t been to Naxos (and I hadn’t, before this vacation) it’s very remote, mostly a mix of mountains and beaches with resorts strewn about, and very different from the type of vacations I usually take (walkable cities with lots of things kids can enjoy.) Entertaining my children in Naxos without getting them sunburned or having them fall into an un-gated body of water was…a challenge, and I’ll admit that travel tablets loaded with Elmo were used in abundance. I’m sure Naxos would have been the most relaxing trip ever had I been a twenty-four-year-old influencer, and I am not, but it was still fun.

Dressing for Naxos was also tricky for me because every experience was beachy and breezy, and I basically never dress for that type of environment. I actually bought this very Farm Rio linen dress ages ago specifically for this vacation, and boy did it deliver!

Husband spotted:

I also bought this cool necklace in Naxos for about twenty-five euro, it came from a store that specialized in making necklaces from recycled glass bottles!

Some of their other wares:

And for those of you who thought my jumpsuit-making days were over (did anyone really think that?) voila, here is the latest iteration of my infinity wide-leg jumpsuit, in a yellow and blue toile stretch cotton from Spoonflower! I wore it for a double date with my husband’s brother and his wife. In case anyone is curious, my husband’s set is from Maison LaBiche.

Speaking of Maison LaBiche, I got this outfit from them a few months ago, which I knew would be perfect for throwing on over a bathing suit. And it was!

In case you noticed the white sandals from that outfit, I bought them from a store in Naxos called Pagonis Greek Sandals. They are maybe the most comfortable flat leather sandals I’ve ever bought, and it looks like they ship worldwide!

Last but not least—for the flight back, I wore this homemade toile two-piece set (I used toile stretch cotton from Spoonflower, similar to the one used for the jumpsuit above.) Overall, I’m pretty happy with my packing (and sewing) choices, although I don’t think I can look at any more blue and white for a week or so.
