The Men's Bodies Women Like--And Prefer

I had thousands of women rate 14 digitally-generated male bodies. Here's what I found about the men they'd be willing to date, and the men they consider ideal.

A while ago, I ran a survey with almost 3,000 respondents and asked participants to rate digitally-generated, headless body types (Not sexy sounding enough for you? It gets better: they were blue and sans genitalia.) Respondents were given three options based on a hypothetical scenario where they were single and given the option to date someone with that body. They could say yes or no, and then say “this is my type” if that particular body perfectly aligned with their ideal preferences. When putting body preferences into practice, it’s worthwhile to understand the ideal, but also good to understand what people are likely to accept, even if not given their number one choice.

I released some of my findings on women’s bodies a while back, mostly in response to an article about looksmaxxing for women. The Internet has failed to give me a viral jumping off point to discuss men’s bodies, but I just figured it was time. Men’s bodies may not be as hotly debated as women’s, but people still debate them, especially in the context of whether or not women are lying about preferring dad bods to bodybuilders, or whether a skinny guy like Timothee Chalamet would appeal to more women than a more muscular, broad physique like that of Jason Momoa. And of course, I hear from women all the time that most women’s “ideal” man is Jack Black—but is that just because he’s funny? Would they find him hotter if he was funny and fit?

First, the most attractive male body in the dataset was this one:

You might be surprised that this is the body of someone who is just normal-fit as opposed to using steroids. This was one limitation of this particular generator—I was unable to generate bodies that did not really occur in nature, which is the same reason that my attempt at creating anime-tier curves on a female body didn’t really work either (that body type was included in the dataset and was moderately well-rated, while scoring lower than more subtle-looking slim/curvy physiques. I was told the uncanny nature of the generator skewed the results and that most men would have preferred an anime-style body if it had been generated better.)

All that being said, I feel like if you truly believe that the ideal male or female physique is something that doesn’t occur in nature, the burden of proof is kind of on you. Like, sure, maybe every woman wants a man with a dick made of solid gold, but these are unfortunately a rare finding. This survey focused on male body types that actually exist naturally in great numbers when you log off and leave your house.

In the case of the above body, 97% of women said they would date a man with such a physique, with 21% saying this body was their type—clearly the most popular body in the dataset.

Keep in mind that people rating a body as “my type” was never a unanimous or even a majority finding, even for the most popular bodies. The most popular female body, for example, was only the ideal “type” for 16% of men, while both men’s and women’s ideal types were distributed in smaller numbers throughout the whole dataset. The least popular bodies—both male and female—still existed as someone’s “type,” albeit among a smaller group of people. This tells us something pretty reassuring—there is a wide range of bodies that men and women find attractive, and while you can stack-rank which types are most versus least ideal (and I’m about to basically do that) there is a lot of variability to individual taste.

That said, if you are looking to maximize your dating options, you want to take advantage of supply and demand, and it makes sense to have a body where more people prefer it than have it.

Even among the bodies that were not frequently listed as someone’s type, there was typically a much wider range of women who found them acceptable for dating. For example, this body which had the measurements of a man who was mildly overweight—perhaps a bit of a “dad bod”—was still the ideal “type” for 3% of women, while considered acceptable by 70% of women.

However, variations on “slim but fit” seemed to be the most popular body type overall, which mirrored the results I saw with women, where “slim with curves” bodies were the most popular. This slim/fit male body also performed quite well. Overall, it had a 92% acceptance rate among women in general, with 12% rating it “their type.”

Funny enough, the most popular body (left) was actually designed to be shorter than average, while the second most popular body (right) was designed to be taller than average. However, without having these bodies next to each other with accurate height scaling, nobody would be able to tell. I’m guessing the “shorter” body performed better because of the broader shoulders and more drastic shoulder-to-waist ratio, although the body on the right still performed very well. Perhaps next time I will need to stage every body type next to a standard size chair so nobody is misled about height.

So- slim but fit performed the best, but what about slim but not muscular? Muscular but not slim? Bodies that were not slim or muscular? Don’t worry—I’ve got you covered.

First: the slim/average body without much muscle performed pretty well with women—an overall acceptance rate of 94%, with 6% saying that this was their type. So, this body type was just about as widely accepted as the slim/muscular bodies, but not as strongly preferred.

I created another body which was meant to be tall and skinny but not muscular. Overall, this body type performed worse than fit/slim, husky/muscular, or even slightly overweight bodies, but performed much better than obese bodies.

Most women still found the skinny, non-muscular body acceptable, although this differed by political alignment (left-leaning women had wider standards across the board—69% of them found this body acceptable, while the same was true for only 50% of right-leaning women.) And 6% of women overall said this body was their ideal type, with 67% of women overall finding it acceptable. This is especially interesting because the female equivalent (skinny but not curvy) performed much better with right-wing men than left-wing men.

Even controlling for their own body type, left-leaning women were much more accepting of a wider range of bodies than right-leaning women. However, there was one exception. The only body that was significantly more popular with right-leaning women than left-leaning women was this “husky” muscular build, which was considered the ideal type for 11% of right-leaning women, compared with only 7% of left-leaning women. Its overall acceptance rate among women was 76%.

One finding that wasn’t too surprising—older women were also more accepting of a wider range of bodies than younger women. Older women were also more likely to be overweight themselves, and overweight women were more accepting of overweight male bodies. Comparing women in their twenties to women in their forties, there were clear differences. For example, this body was accepted by 88% of women in their forties, compared with 72% of women in their twenties:

This difference was even more stark with bodies that met the BMI criteria for obesity. I have a separate article coming about findings related to obesity for both genders, but just as an example, this male body would be classified as obese. Only 15% of women in their twenties found it acceptable, but this number rose to 31% among women in their forties.

I saw a similar pattern with this body, which also met the criteria for obesity. 20% of women in their twenties found it acceptable, compared with 44% of women in their forties. Generally, very few women found obese bodies to be their ideal, but a wider swath of women found them acceptable for dating.

One of the most interesting findings was how women’s own body type determined what they found attractive. The top male body (fit/slim) performed the best with women regardless of body type (remember, we’re talking about the ideal) but after that point, things began to diverge. Women who considered themselves slightly to significantly overweight (think anywhere from chubby to obese) were far more accepting of overweight male bodies than slim and skinny women. If you look at the two obese bodies above, they were accepted by under 20% of slim/skinny women, but accepted by over 50% of overweight women. Also, while the “husky/muscular” body shown below was not a top-performing body with skinny/slim women (it received middling ratings from them), it was the number two top-performing body with overweight women, many of whom said it was their ideal:

It’s especially interesting that despite the “girlboss feminist with unreasonable standards” stereotype, it was right-leaning women who were pickier about men’s bodies than left-leaning women. This was not due to left-leaning women being more likely to be overweight themselves—there was no major difference between the political factions when it came to women’s self-described weight, except for the fact that far-left (not liberal) women were a bit more bimodal—more likely to be very skinny or significantly overweight. But even then, among all political alignments, female participants were most likely to classify themselves as slim/fit or average, so this is probably not something that skewed the data very much. Something about right-wing politics genuinely correlates with higher physical standards.

I also wanted to understand how the skinniest male body performed with women based on their own body types. Being a skinny man (without clear musculature) is not as conventionally attractive as being a skinny woman, and this was proven by the fact that the skinny, narrow-framed male body performed worse than the mildly overweight bodies. For reference, female version of this body performed comparably with average-weight female bodies and outperformed mildly overweight ones. Funny enough, women across the board, regardless of their own weight, were about 60-70% likely to consider this body acceptable. The one thing that stuck out to me was that women who were significantly overweight were the most accepting of the very skinny male body, with 71% considering it acceptable for dating. (Image below to jog your memory—it was also shown above.) Perhaps opposites do attract.

So, what are the main takeaways here?

  • Women pretty predictably follow a pattern of finding slim, fit male bodies the most attractive.

  • Left-leaning women, older women, and overweight women are more likely to be accepting of a wider variety of men’s bodies than slim women, young women or right-leaning women. (Women’s ages and weights correlated predictably, but politics did not significantly correlate with a woman’s weight.)

  • Most male bodies, with the exception of obese ones, are acceptable to more than 50% of women. Obese bodies are acceptable to more than 50% of women who consider themselves slightly to significantly overweight.

  • Unlike skinny female bodies (which, even without curves, perform moderately well) skinny male bodies without muscle perform slightly worse than mildly overweight ones, but still significantly better than obese ones.

  • “Husky” bodies with broad frames, fat and muscle are popular overall, but even more popular among right-leaning women and overweight women.

But perhaps the biggest takeaway is that what we understand to be conventionally attractive is true (the slim/muscular ideal for men) but there’s still plenty of variation in taste, to the point that you can’t reasonably declare that all women prefer slim/muscular men, the same way you can’t declare that all men prefer slim women with curves. Men with that conventionally ideal body type may have the most options, which is why the takeaway isn’t necessarily “accept your body however it looks right now and never do anything to improve,” but given that some things are out of our control, it’s good to know that no matter what you look like, especially if you are at a healthy BMI, your body is definitely someone’s “type.”
