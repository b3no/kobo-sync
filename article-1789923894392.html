First of all, Aria and I likely agree on the facts of average male preferences, especially if you are using AI porn, anime and sex dolls as an example of a revealed preference. I suppose I disagree that these things are necessarily a revealed preference for the vast majority of men, given that guys who prefer really outlandish proportions will actually be the most likely to seek out this type of content by default (if believable-looking women are your thing, you probably won’t bother watching AI porn or buying a sex doll in the first place.) But, yeah, guys like pretty, young women with hourglass figures, often improbably proportioned?

That said, I’m not offended by any of this on feminist or self-preservation grounds (the revelation that I am not the most attractive woman on Earth came quite a long time ago). I find this area of study very interesting, whether done by Aria or Aella or anyone else. And I’m fully aware that an “ideal” will likely not be realistic—neither is the ideal of the 6’5” blue eyed billionaire with a sex dungeon who also votes Democrat and carries the mental load. That’s why it’s an ideal.

But anyway, since Aria’s article presents us as disagreeing, I feel like I should clarify that we don’t, really, at least not on the basics. I will cover more of this in specialized articles later because this is an area of study with a lot of nuance, but anyway.

Most of our “disagreement” has been us misunderstanding each other’s points, albeit not with any kind of personal beef or malice. For example, I strongly disagreed with what I believed to be a prescriptive decree for all white women to dye their hair blonde (I would look sick, personally) and for all women without enormous boobs to get breast implants, preferably the biggest size they can imagine (Yes, a lot of men love big natural boobs, but many others prefer smaller or average ones to implants. Sydney Sweeney is so popular in part because her boobs are real!) Anyway, Aria says she never actually made these decrees, only offered these things up as options. If that’s true, then I guess I misunderstood.

But my major disagreements are more like minor quibbles about framing and nuance, as opposed to stark disagreements about the core principles:

  • I do not think this information is hidden or forbidden, especially not in a world where the Internet is wall-to-wall porn. I do not think men (especially men on the Internet) have been silent about their preferences.

  • I do not think porn (especially artificial porn or sex dolls) represents a clandestine, mysterious revealed preference which most men are lying about, either to themselves or to seem politically correct. (MILF is a very popular porn search phrase, and most men are not primarily interested in older women.) A good example of “revealed preferences” is the type of woman favored by men who have all the options in the world—men who are good looking, famous and rich. As you can see here, these men are all with conventionally hot women, but those women are conventionally hot in very different ways.

  • I think culturally and socioeconomically influenced preferences for certain aesthetics, hair colors, ethnicities, body types, etc, aren’t any less “real” than hardwired biological preferences, nor do I think they represent polite lies that men are telling to appease the feminists or the other men at the country club. Some preferences are hardwired, but others probably have a lot more variety which could be influenced by external factors. Doesn’t make the boners any less real.

  • And, most importantly, aside from just being interesting (which it is!) there is a limited amount of actionability to this, as far as regular women seeking husbands. If you’re a sex worker who is open to plastic surgery, or a creator generating AI porn, you can follow these instructions meticulously to maximize your ROI with a broad swath of men. If you are just an ordinary woman looking to marry a guy, you could certainly take some advice from these articles about fitness, hairstyle and grooming (nothing that would be too surprising, might I add) but most regular women do not need to get plastic surgery to resemble Wasian Kim Kardashian to find a husband. And aside from plastic surgery, there is basically nothing that women can do to maximize their bust/waist/hip ratios—those things are genetic and likely won’t fluctuate that much with weight or fitness. Trust me as someone who went down an extreme looksmaxxing rabbit hole about this. I did lots of crazy stuff to alter my body shape (outside of plastic surgery) and the impact was minimal, nothing any man would even notice. (And for reasons quite obvious, a lot of women are not interested—nor must they be—in getting breast implants, BBLs, and getting their ribs removed.)

The lack of actionability is fine—after all, when I covered ideal men’s bodies (which I will likely do again with a better generator that accounts for height) there was no actionable takeaway other than fitness, which, duh. I guess I just disagree that the purpose of these guides, for regular women, is anything other than curiosity and special interest. Most of us are not going to go into the Fashion Nova Model factory to get a new body.

All in all, more is coming on this topic, but please put it in the papers that I do not think Aria’s conclusions are wrong, we just quibble slightly about nuance and whatever action items might come out of this.
