The Queen of Versailles

How Nothing REALLY Bad Ever Happens to Rich People

Lance Corporal Scott Bukkake
Oct 20

My first favorite documentary is, of course, Guys and Dolls. My second favorite documentary is The Queen of Versailles, the 2012 Lauren Greenfield movie which captures David and Jackie Siegel, owners of Westgate Resorts in the wake of the 2008 financial collapse.

It’s a treat. Jackie Siegel rolls in with her great big tits and very early-aughts-coded fashion to show off her riches, namely the recreation of the Palace of Versailles that she and David are having built. This is, objectively, one of the most insane Rich Person flexes one could probably do. And it almost feels classless, building a recreation of Versailles in the great state of Florida because you wanted one. Jackie tours the unfinished Palace of Versailles (Florida) and shows off the furniture waiting in storage clad in period appropriate low-rise jeans and tiny tank tops. It’s just a delightful blend of low-class aesthetics and high-class aspirations.

Do not mistake my feelings for anything but sheer schadenfreude. When the crash comes and the Siegel’s have to make lifestyle adjustments, it is absolutely delicious. Their lifestyle slowly degrades over the runtime, while David loses his mind over Jackie’s inability to tamp down her spending. We get to follow Jackie through her current home (at the time) picking up dog shit grudgingly, because they had to cut staff and that’s who was taking care of the dogs. The children’s fish and reptiles die, and when she asks why no one fed or watered them, it’s apparent that their care was also handled by the help.

The primary means which David Siegel used to grow his business was by selling timeshares. The timeshares were usually financed which meant Westgate was handing out loans, then bundling those loans and selling them to investors to further grow the business. When the 2008 Recession hit, and the credit market dried the fuck up, their whole business model was essentially kaput. The means of financial engineering that crashed the housing market also crashed the timeshare grift. If you expect me to feel bad about that, then have I got news for you…

As a seasoned hater of the affluent, the documentary was like candy. While it opens with an air of Lifestyles of the Rich and Famous, eventually you’re following Jackie through Walmart and taking economy flights, while her unfinished palace sits stagnant. As a poor person who was alive and trying to survive after 2008, it just felt good to see a little suffering from someone n that demographic. If you believe that to make as much money as David Siegel had before 2008 one must be evil, then the downfall is simply just desserts.

In my fantasy, the Florida Palace of Versailles sat unfinished while Jackie’s fake tits slowly deflated. Unfortunately, if there is a god, he is not a just one because in 2022 Jackie Siegel got a reality show looped into documenting her attempt at finishing Florida Versailles. Because I am so unplugged, I wasn’t aware of this development until I was discussing the documentary with my ex-boss (Sicilian). He actually looked into it, watched the documentary and discovered the reality show.

Oh, no… My fantasy! Not my beautiful fantasy!

Even Jackie’s tits are bigger.

So, 2022. The Siegels have enough money to start completing construction on their gratuitous mansion. Conveniently, David Siegal was MIA for the entirety of this endeavor, by way of a “back injury.” Considering how, for the documentary, he didn’t seem to have a very good experience, it just seemed… You know, convenient.

Jackie, however, is just as ostentatious as she was in 2008, and is having a fantastic time hamming it up for the camera. Her children have grown and her oldest son takes on assisting her with the renovations and construction. I do not intend to break down each individual episode, but will outline some of (what I refer to as Jackie’s Follies).

The biggest folly is not subscribing.

Jackie’s Folly #1: Living Birds as Decor

One of the rooms the show focuses on is “The Morning Kitchen.” A place for the family to gather in the morning and eat breakfast. Jackie mentions that she’s super into birds. Like, as pets. Exotic birds. And within the morning kitchen she has an antique birdcage in which she intends to keep colorful, tropical birds.

But, she wants tropical birds that match the intended color of the upholstery on her furniture. Also, she wants flamingos in her yard. What we’re met with is a couple conundrums. Tropical birds in colors that match her upholstery would essentially be able to rip the antique birdcage apart, because it is made out of wood. Jackie cannot have the birds without this ornate-ass birdcage because aesthetics or whatever. Flamingos are incredibly hard to get permits for, and Jackie is told they’re one of the most difficult animals to own. So, no Flamingos. No Parrots.

Instead, Jackie gets toucans. She buys them right away, and then is told by the Toucan Purveyor that they actually need a lot of space to fly around and stuff. So she cannot have the toucans she paid thousands of dollars for right away in the fucking birdcage either. The entire time we were watching this (I watched all six episodes with my sister), we were in a state of rage. I might have mimed shooting the television with a machine gun while this happened… Maybe.

In the end they settled on parakeets, which do come in a plethora of colors and match the decor.

Jackie’s Folly #2: My Own Private Benihana

There is a strange thing with the wealthy. Having worked directly with someone who had a few million in equity, I’ve seen this in him and I see it in Jackie. They can’t seem to just enjoy something. If they like it, they want to own it. Maybe this is everybody, but I don’t know…? A theme I noticed with Jackie throughout the series and the documentary is, “Ever since I saw this cool thing I wanted one,” and that could range from a vintage slot machine… To a Teppanyaki grill.

Jackie tells the audience that one of her family’s favorite things is going to Benihana, that restaurant where they make an onion volcano. Flames come out of the onion! It’s cool as hell! Of course, she and her husband want their own Benihana in the palace. You’d think this would be easy for people as affluent as the Siegels, but there’s some technical problems with having a commercial style kitchen in a residency.

Again, they cannot find a manufacturer willing to put a teppanyaki grill into a residential home (though, I’m sure they’d be willing to grease the wheel). Like all whims, Jackie and David didn’t really think about this. A large commercial unit puts out substantial heat and fumes. Some units might require their own gas supply, fire-suppression systems, and specialized ventilation units. This is explained to Jackie by the builders. They lean into the fact that using the grill without very loud ventilation would make the house steenky.

In what Jackie claims is one of the hardest decisions of her life, she scraps the kitchen. Even though it was one of her biggest dreams.

Jackie’s Folly #3: Building Maintenance

So, say you have a house and it just sat for fifteen years. Would you be surprised when you walked inside that the roof was leaking? Well, the same thing goes for a million dollar mansion, too. Only repairing those problems also costs, like, millions of dollars.

What we discover, because Jackie flat-out says so, is that multiple teams have worked on the mansion at different stages of construction. If you’ve ever spoken with someone who also does construction, this is always a bit of a problem. While there is a correct way to do a job, you can’t always guarantee you’re working on something that was done correctly by the guy before you (anyone who owns a house can attest to this as well). This means there are weird leaks happening throughout the mansion and the marble is crumbling off the edifice in chunks.

These struggles also account for some of the hardest decisions of Jackie’s life. Because, in the mid-2000’s, she and her husband bought out an entire quarry to make sure the marble on the outside of their mansion all matched! And if they have to replace ANY of that marble how can they guarantee it will look right?!

Then, there’s the kids’ rooms. They designed the house when a lot of those kids were still in diapers, and now about half of them are adults. The rooms are smaller than they’d like and have features that no longer make sense. One son even has a small stage in his room that was meant for puppet shows or whatever. Needless to say, there is work to be done. Jackie doesn’t mind this, of course. She doesn’t want to be an empty-nester and would be perfectly happy to have her adult children living with her. I can’t really blame her, because it’s not like having a basement dwelling son or daughter eating food you’re tired of affording. If you can afford to build a mansion, there are no contentions to be had.

Folly #4: A Timeline

Jackie wants this mansion done by New Year’s, so she can have a big party. Again, this causes her to have some of the most difficult experiences of her life while also making the hardest decisions. Because this is framed for reality television, a more forgiving part of me wants to assume this is a matter of manufacturing stakes. Jackie seems stupid, but it’s not as if she doesn’t have the experience of dealing with the logistical nightmare of building a home… She’s been building Versailles for nearly twenty goddamned years.

But this New Year’s party hangs overhead like a noose. They’ve got to get everything done for the party… Jackie has told everyone about the party… She has to have everything ready for David, so he can enjoy the paaaaaartttyyyyyyyy.

The other timeline is that of David Siegel himself. In 2022, he was eighty-seven years old. Jackie acknowledges their thirty-year age gap and the fact that he’s reaching the end of the road. At the very least, she wants him to enjoy the damned house finished. Allegedly¹ he has spent months in the hospital during construction, suffering from a back injury and then pneumonia. His age and mortality are looming things.

So Jackie needs to make sure the stones for the ballroom floor come in from Indonesia in time for the party. Because this took place at the height of the pandemic, this is a logistical impossibility given the nature of ocean shipping at the time. The party is canceled, and instead they settle for a family dinner within the rooms of the house that are finished. The dining room, morning kitchen, and a replica of a British Pub. The entire family arrives to the unfinished Florida Versailles, draped in sequins and clad in suits.

On the absence of the Benihana Kitchen, David insists they move forward with it after it’s been completely scrapped. They need their own teppanyaki Grill.

My Beautiful Fantasy???

So, Florida Versailles is still unfinished. Jackie is in her sixties and David passed away in 2025. There is the claim that it’s almost finished. But “almost” is a word that does a lot of heavy lifting. “Almost” gets used a lot during the reality show as well, but only accounts for about four rooms in a 90,000-square-foot mansion.

Westgate’s leadership was restructured before David’s death, and the percentage of stake Jackie and her family have in the company is unclear. What I do know is that The Queen of Versailles Reigns Again only got one season (though they were certainly gunning for more), and Jackie is relatively active on social media, though her updates about Florida Versailles are far more scant than general lifestyle content and a few sponsored posts.

This is the part where I admit that I don’t find Jackie completely reprehensible. She never shies away from what she is and how she got to where she was: Jackie was a middle class woman who managed to marry a rich man after competing in beauty pageants. A lot of her Just Regular Folks affectations stand, and one can surmise that part of her aesthetics are simply a matter of appealing to her old, horn-dog husband. Like, you go girl.

Jackie now, at the very least, has enough money to travel, buy clothing and keep herself fresh with cosmetic procedures. If you get breast implants in your twenties, it’s not an impossibility that you may need a replacement ten to twenty years later. Similarly to the roof of a house or hot-water heater. It is an ongoing financial commitment. Hopefully, David Siegel made sure that whatever accommodations he made before death ensure Jackie has the ability to maintain her chosen lifestyle and the roof on Florida Versailles.

Jackie is in her sixties now, not so far behind David as she once was. As much as it hurts to admit it, the storybook about the Queen of Florida Versailles doesn’t have too many pages left, and I can only assume that Florida Versailles will eventually need to be sold to another person with too much money, until the roof tiles slide off and the marble crumbles. Because everything does.
