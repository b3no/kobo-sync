The National Council on Severe Autism, a righteous organization under constant criticism from the disability activist set, has chosen my book for its next book club. Check it out, and consider supporting the organization, which is culturally, politically, and medically distinct from almost every major autism organization, in a way that I think is beneficial and necessary.

Let me start by saying: everyone involved in this piece knows what it’s for. Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez knew what she was doing when her team arranged this interview, her team knew what she was doing, the Times reporter who played dutiful stenographer knew what the piece was for, and certainly the NYT leadership did too. The headline might as well be “Ahead of Midterms, Democratic Candidate Uses Newspaper to Announce Rightward Tilt.” There is, after all, a time-honored tradition in our media where a publication trades a conciliatory piece upfront in return for access during the campaign. Another time-honored tradition is a left-leaning politician tacking right to win a contested primary. And since the New York Times loves punching left and coronating future presidents, I’m sure the opportunity was irresistible to them.

I say all of this because I can predict some of the commentary on this post, which will have useful and useless varieties. The useful kind will be arguments about whether it’s substantively good for AOC to move to the right, or whether it’s politically necessary for her to move to the right, or whether it will be strategically effective for her to move to the right. Those are all questions worthy of debating. The useless kind will be people arguing about whether what I’m saying is actually happening. I’m not interested in debates about the nature of the piece, which has no reason to exist at all other than to let everyone (Democratic leadership, voters, the media) know that she’s doing what some of us always predicted she would do: move to the right as a bid to satisfy her galactic ambitions. That this big splashy NYT piece emerged the weekend before the midterms that will set the course of the 2028 presidential campaign is not a coincidence, and the language used in it is no coincidence either. “As she weighs runs for the presidency or the Senate in two years, Ms. Ocasio-Cortez hopes to establish herself as broadly palatable to Democrats” is the kind of thing you get a malleable reporter to write when you’re letting your party know that you’re going for it and that moderating away from your past values is on the menu.

The NYT’s Matt Flegenheimer writes, of recent campaign rhetoric from Ocasio-Cortez, “The flourish registered, almost, as the founding sensibility of a campaign in waiting.” Oh, honey, you don’t have to tell me. I got that part...
