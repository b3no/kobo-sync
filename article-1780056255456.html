Plus, details for how I made The Jumpsuit, and shopping recommendations for similar jumpsuits for the non-sewing inclined.

Each month, I cover all my favorite fashion from the previous month—not only my favorite outfits, but my favorite sewing projects, because a good portion of my clothes (not to mention my husband’s and kids’) are homemade. Perhaps you didn’t subscribe for fashion, but the good news is that fashion is only about 10% of my content, and with 4-5 posts every week, there’s bound to be something else that you like. (Please don’t be mad at me for the fashion. Please.)

Best Fits

I sincerely apologize to anyone who eagerly opened this article because they saw that I posted a “thirst trap” (your words, not mine) in the form of this jumpsuit, which was my big sewing project of the month, and they expected more of this:

Unfortunately for those people, most of my clothes look more like this, although for anyone interested in the craftsmanship of the jumpsuit I will be providing fabric recommendations and such.

Anyway, if you are still interested despite the fact that what is to follow is entirely fashion-oriented with nary a boob to be found, read on.

One of my favorite outfits this month was this orange getup, comprised entirely of second-hand items (the top must have been $20 thrifted, the skirt was $40 at TheRealReal—and look at those fun flower appliques!) I wore this for a child’s party with an orange theme. And a pop of blue courtesy of Vivaia sandals.

I also wore this to checks notes take my kids to the park? IDK man, I need excuses to wear fun clothes I guess. Fun fact: with the exception of the top (Aritzia) all of this is also second hand! The bag is a vintage Coach Soho bag for which I’ve found myself reaching constantly, and the belt was this cool piece I found at a random vintage pop-up. I had been searching for a metallic Western-inspired belt for ages, so I was thrilled to stumble upon it.

The bag makes its appearance again with the vintage leather trench coat I bought in Paris last month. Jeans are Abercrombie. When I bought them I wasn’t sure I’d ever find a use for them, but they’ve been surprisingly versatile.

This was an outift I intended to wear outside the house, but then I never left home that day. #based #tradwife lol. Anyway, the top was from some random boutique years ago and the skirt is Mango, plus thrifted, which means it was cheap as hell!

For a slightly cooler day, I wore my go-to Sezane Bobby cropped trench with a J.Crew Emilie cotton cardigan (a favorite of my spring fashion guide) and some high-waisted red pants from Spanx, currently on sale for $99. I was a little hesitant to buy actual pants from a shapewear company, but they’re pretty great! As for the shoes, those are the Asics that I’ve covered so many times before. Maybe a bit too sporty and casual for this look? Unexpected? You guys can be the judge.

This one is a bit less exciting than the others and decidedly very basic, but the Alo Yoga knit tennis set is 100% cotton, super comfortable, and ideal for casual weekend activities. You can find the cardigan here.

My favorite outfit of the month was a vintage ‘90s Betsey Johnson two-piece suit (dress and jacket) which I wore for a Mother’s Day dinner. I paired it with my STAUD beaded Tommy bag and a pair of Tamaris Mary Janes I bought in Paris.

Sewing Projects

Because we are planning to spend most of our summer abroad starting in June, I had to really hustle on the sewing this month to get our entire summer wardrobe in order. My husband didn’t want any new clothes, and my son is already covered because I sewed like mad for him last month. This month, it was all about me and my daughter.

Starting with some clothes for my daughter, I sewed this cute empire waist dress using some woven beige polka-dot fabric from Lyrical Fabrics. Unfortunately, they’re now out of stock, but this one is a good jersey alternative.

I also made this cute matching set, entirely from scrap fabric! I used the pink and white striped fabric to make her birthday dress last year but had an ample amount left, and I had no idea what I was going to do with it. Glad I found a use for it! I used this pattern for the top, which has been a go-to for me all spring.

Another matching set! This one is super soft cotton and modal jersey from Mood Fabrics. My daughter loves it!

As you can tell, she really loves pink (no, this is not me pushing my gender-normative agenda on her, it’s just her favorite color.) Here she is in a T-shirt and pants set that I made her. The T-shirt fabric is from Spoonflower and the pants fabric is deadstock from Mood.

I also made this dress using some pink polka dot poplin fabric from Lyrical Fabrics. I wish I could take credit for the pattern, but it’s one I found online ages ago which since seems to have been taken down. Thankfully I had it printed already!

I also made her these cute wide-leg capri pants with pastel rainbow stripe cotton fabric from Mood Fabrics:

Now, my own clothes!

This vintage leather jacket was already covered last month, but this month I paired it with a comic print skirt that I sewed out of stretch sateen from Mood Fabrics. I used my go-to skirt pattern which is insanely easy. In case you’re wondering, the bag is from By Far (second hand, of course.)

As you can tell, I really love polka dots. I made this coord set with dark brown with light pink polka dot fabric from Lyrical Fabrics. (Honestly, I’m starting to think the dots are white, not light pink, I can’t really figure it out. But either way, I like pairing this outfit with light pink.)

I also finally got to wear this cool co-ord set I made a couple months ago (it also has pants, but the pants make it look more like loungewear.) For this, I also used fabric from Spoonflower and the same skirt pattern listed above.

Pictured with my Osoi brot bag:

For a warmer day, I wore the black gingham pedal pushers that I made last month (fabric from Lyrical, but no longer in stock) and paired them with a black eyelet cotton top that I made last year (fabric here). Then of course, there’s my beloved Baggu shoulder bag that looks like a swan.

Close-up for fabric detail:

And now, of course, the jumpsuit. For anyone interested in recreating my metallic jumpsuit from the beginning of the article, you have two options. First of all, you can sew one yourself. Unfortunately I didn’t use a pattern but rather amended a basic high-waisted flare leg pattern to be wider, and then used an existing jumpsuit to trace a waist piece and bust pieces with the long straps. The style is called an “infinity jumpsuit” which should give you an idea of how they function. Basically, the top part is just a halter top with extremely long ties that can be wrapped around your waist.

I used this fabric, which comes in lots of colors. I also bought the aqua color but haven’t yet tackled a second metallic jumpsuit. I’m too busy working on a yellow/blue toile one first! Hey, I need one for daytime.

If you’re interested in that jumpsuit style but don’t sew, this Etsy seller makes similar ones. This retail one is also pretty similar. And the original jumpsuit that I used to make my pattern is one from Soi, which is a French brand that doesn’t ship to the US, but if you’re in Europe you may be able to get one.

Also, not a sewing project per se, but check out this insane haul of fabrics that I randomly found at a thrift store for $4 a yard!
