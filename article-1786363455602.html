You Should Dress Up For Your Spouse

In defense of looking better for your spouse than for your coworkers.

I know I don’t have a lot of experience dating, so I never give Hinge advice. I will not give sexting advice either. I have never really “sexted” unless you count the one time I texted my husband something uncharacteristically aggressive and, perhaps wondering if his wife’s phone was hacked, he responded with the heart emoji.

I have, however, been married for twelve years (together for eighteen) so when it comes to marriage, I think I have at least some degree of authority. Certainly, more authority than AureliusRawLiver1488 who thinks you should marry at twelve, live in a tree, and obliterate entire nations to prove your love for your family. So I will use my credentials as a Normal Happily Married Person to say: you should consider dressing up for your spouse and expending real effort into attracting them the way you might on a first date—at least occasionally.

Yes, even if it’s been twenty years. Yes, even if you don’t like your body. Yes, even if you don’t think your spouse cares. Yes, even if you’re literally neurodivergent.

Let me explain. Sometimes, when my husband and I are stuck in the daily routine of childcare, chores, and him asking me approximately fifteen times an hour where his fingernail scissors are, I play a game with myself that I encourage you all to play too: I pretend he isn’t my husband at all, that I’m single, and I just met him on a dating app.

Aside from the fact that I’d be pretty confused to see a guy from Tinder in my kitchen asking me where the oven mitts are, my first thought would be, Holy shit, this guy is perfect, God I hope we can go out. I mean, a funny, handsome, athletic thirty-nine-year-old Mediterranean guy with a good job who wants kids? And he has chest hair? (Okay, I already said “Mediterranean,” that’s redundant.) I wouldn’t just be into him, I’d think it was too good to be true and perhaps reverse Google Image him to see if he was a love scammer.

But married people (myself included) don’t often see each other that way. Even if you’re nuts about your spouse, you get used to them, and you can’t operate on that level of high excitement 24/7, especially between blowout diapers and administering the unspeakable torture known as “brushing a toddler’s teeth.” I don’t believe my husband sees me this way every day either. Sometimes, I’m sure I’m just this annoying woman constantly telling him she’s hungry or thirsty or has a headache. After enduring a decade plus of unpleasant experiences including but not limited to assembling furniture together, or being extorted by your insane neighbors, you have to try pretty hard to view your spouse in the idealistic, hopeful, and giddy way you’d view them if you just met them. But when you’re able to successfully complete this thought experiment, you begin to reevaluate the way you behave around your spouse—and the amount of effort you expend on attracting them.

Now, you might be saying, “My spouse? My spouse would love me no matter what! I shouldn’t have to try to ‘attract them.’” If you’re a woman, more specifically, you might be thinking, “What bullshit, my husband would jump my bones if I was wearing giant sweatpants and a oil-stained T-shirt.” But that’s not the point. Yes, the daily routine of marriage means that if things are going well, you’re attracted to each other even when very little effort is expended. But by putting forth the type of effort you might expend on a first or second date (not every day necessarily, but occasionally!) you can increase the degree of excitement both of you feel in the marriage and reduce that feeling of complacency (which can lead to a real “roommates” vibe over time.)

Of course, there are some exceptions. I’m sure some of you are saying, “But what if your spouse is awful to you all the time/cheating on you with your mother/just got arrested for being the notorious Hudson River Flasher?!” Okay, well that’s a bit above this article’s paygrade. “What if my spouse is blind and has no idea what I’m wearing? What if my spouse has a fetish for sweatpants? What if I’m allergic to clothes?” Look, I don’t know. If you believe you are part of some massive exception, you probably are. This article is for regular people in regular marriages (or long-term partnerships that have not been legally solidified. Is everyone happy now?)

Anyway, back to my favorite topic: me. I noticed earlier in our marriage (before kids) that I put lots of effort into my outfits when I went to work, but then as soon as I got home, my nice clothes came off and were replaced with baggy, uncoordinated and often stained lounge clothes. I dressed up really nicely around people with whom I did not want to have sex, including my manager who I actually loathed, but then I returned home and looked like crap for the one person with whom I did want to have sex.

And yes, I’m sure my husband still found me attractive. But the discordant nature of my appearance-related effort felt weird to me. Why was my horrible boss—a guy weird enough to bring a tiny scale with him to a lunch meeting to weigh his food—privy to a more dolled-up version of me than the one man I actually wanted to sleep with? Still, I didn’t really change my behavior. It helped that my husband and I had lots of date nights at the time, so he still got to see me at my best every weekend.

But once we both started working remotely, my loungewear and lack of any styling at all became the default all the time. Yes, I would dress up if we were specifically going out on a date, but as we got older and had kids (and uh, libcuck voice GLOBAL FREAKING PANDEMIC) date nights became fewer and far between. You guys might be like, “Wait, you? You’re always posting your fancy outfits!” but those are the outfits I have posted just for times I’ve left the house, plus I’ve made a conscious effort to expend more effort for everyday occasions in the past few years. Either way, for a while, especially right after becoming a mom, I was living in lounge pants and sweatshirts basically 24/7, thinking about the types of clothes I might enjoy wearing but never wearing them because I had “no occasion.”

The clothes were just one aspect of the lack of effort I was expending. I was not going out of my way to do anything nice for my husband unless it was his birthday (and even he will tell you I absolutely botched his 2021 birthday by surprising him with, drum roll, a Zoom call with some of his best friends!) I loved him, but I was absolutely not treating him like someone I had just started dating. And, as I said before, that’s not a realistic thing to do 24/7. But…I wasn’t doing it at all.

There are times when this unromantic approach makes sense. When you have a new baby, you and your spouse are basically trying to survive while dodging projectile sharts, and wearing a nice pair of jeans is probably the last thing on anyone’s mind. But the idea that being a parent at all means that you won’t ever be able to expend effort on your fitness and appearance is simply not true. I’ve spent a lot of time in Europe and I have not noticed this phenomenon. Are French moms walking around in stiletto heels and bodycon dresses with perfectly flat tummies and self-supporting boobs, while their husbands have washboard abs underneath three-piece suits? Of course not. But there is some effort expended that you just don’t see as often with American parents—an absence of loungewear outside the house, attainable degrees of fitness and grooming, a decent shirt instead of a ratty hoodie. Perhaps they simply have more free time and less pressing work, as anyone who has attempted to get a French coworker to do something on a Thursday or Friday, or frankly, Monday or Tuesday, can attest. But Americans do still have some free time, and we have mostly decided that “attracting your spouse” is a ridiculous, unnecessary endeavor which adds nothing but extra stress to your life.

I’m arguing it adds something very beneficial—both to your spouse’s life and to yours.

First of all, putting effort into your looks—even if you are not a Fashion Person and are just putting on a nicer pair of pants instead of sweatpants—makes you feel better and just puts you in a better mood. During my first remote job, I worked very closely with a sixty-something man who worked remotely in a suburb several states away. I always assumed he was working from an office because he was, without fail, always wearing a crisp button-down shirt. When he told me he worked from home, I asked him why he was always dressed for the office. He told me that wearing “office clothes,” even if he was completely alone and had nowhere to go, put him in a confident, productive mindset for work, boosted his energy, and helped him feel that the “workday” had officially began. A few times, I took his advice (in my case, wearing a nice sweater and jeans instead of lounge clothes) and I also found that my energy throughout the workday improved, even if I was in my bedroom.

It’s very easy to assume that wearing pajamas (or some pajama-adjacent thing) is the right thing to do when you’re not in a great headspace but often the opposite is true. As I’ve written about before, I have OCD, and sometimes my OCD puts me in what I will call “cozy paralysis.” In these moments of cozy paralysis, I’m so worried about whatever the fear du jour is that I really don’t feel like going anywhere or getting ready. I want to stay in my pajamas, snuggle my kids, have a cup of tea, read a book, and generally retreat from the outside world as much as possible. In my experience, one of the only ways to shock myself out of this mode is to get dressed in something meant for going out and Doing Things. I’m not going to blanket-condemn anyone struggling with mental illness who can’t bring themselves to get dressed, but I will say that at least in my case (husband’s preferences aside) forcing myself to wear real clothes has a measurable effect on my mood and energy.

Look no further than the Italian concept of bella figura, or the philosophy that you should prioritize putting your best self forward, not only with how you dress and groom but with a degree of confidence and grace (presumably aided by feeling good about how you look!) Bella figura is not unique to young, naturally svelte, beautiful singles. It’s a philosophy intended for all of society, from the elderly to parents to college students, and it’s why you generally don’t see Italians (or any Europeans, really) walking around in their pajamas, even just to go shopping.

But back to the spouses! Let’s say I’ve convinced you to dress up a bit for your spouse, but what does that really look like? If you were a single person looking to attract a wide swath of people, I could compile survey responses and studies to give you some ideas of what different genders and orientations find sexy (and I plan on doing a new survey on that soon since the previous one wasn’t ideal) But most likely, your spouse has particular preferences which may or may not align with the average, conventional preferences for their gender. For example, my husband’s are:

  • At home: polished athleisure, typically leggings + sports bra or crop top (more specifically, these Skims leggings with a crop top or the Alo Yoga heart throb set.) Jeans or other high waisted pants with a fitted yet casual top (think ribbed turtleneck or something similar) is also appreciated.

  • Out of the home: pants with coordinating tops or jumpsuits, can be loose or tight as long as there’s some kind of waist sash or belt. (He’s a big belt guy.) Honorary mention: jeans that are tight through the waist and hips—anything that happens below the ass cheeks is immaterial. He also likes dramatic belted coats and trenches depending on weather.

You might be surprised to know that dresses don’t do much for him, even if they’re short and tight. He isn’t a fan of short-shorts either, although I’m sure he’d be fine with them inside the house during the summer. He also says he doesn’t give a shit about shoes. Some guys practically have a fetish for knee-high boots or high heels, but my husband has told me many times that he simply doesn’t care about shoes as long as I can walk in them so we don’t miss our reservation. So, in this instance, your mileage may vary a lot. And it seems instead of telling you how to attract your own spouse, I’ve given you a step-by-step guide to attract my husband. Please don’t seduce my husband. Stay away from the jumpsuits.

As for me, I really like it when my husband wears crochet polos or textured-knit button downs, and for special occasions, I like when he throws on a sport coat. In cooler weather, I like him in a professorly cardigan with straight-leg jeans, especially paired with his glasses. I also like his matching sets for summer—you can see more of them in his “dad fashion guide” he wrote here.

You might look at the clothes we wear or prefer and think “Hell no, not my thing” and that’s completely fine because you aren’t married to us. You probably know what your spouse is into, but if you don’t, they probably won’t mind being asked as long as they can be assured you won’t be annoyed by their answer.

Also, when I say “dress up” for your partner, I don’t mean wearing something ridiculously dressy for the location or occasion. Instead, use the “first date” rule. If you were having a first (or second, maybe third) date with someone you really liked at this location, how would you dress? For a date at a nice restaurant, I would wear something that fell into the realm of cocktail attire. If I was meeting a date at a nearby park, I’d probably wear comfortable boots with flattering jeans and a fitted top, maybe a cool jacket if it was cold. If I was having a date over at my house for dinner, I probably wouldn’t wear shoes at all, but I wouldn’t be in my pajamas either—perhaps some pull-on high waisted flare pants and a sweater, depending on the season. When I’m not sure how to “dress up” for my husband, I just use this rule. I implore you to use the same rule.

To some extent, marriage is about being comfortable with someone—relaxing on the couch with no makeup, digging into a bag of sour candy and arguing over who gets the cherry flavored ones while missing the incestuous details of the latest House of the Dragon episode. But there’s a fine line between comfort and complacency—between your spouse being your best friend and your spouse being just a friend. Sometimes, putting in just a little more effort than you might otherwise—even without a particular occasion or event in mind—can signal that you aren’t just in love with them, you still have a crush on them, and they’re still as exciting to you as they were on a first date. And that, my friends, is why you should consider dressing up for your spouse.
