The Angel in Bentonville

Lance Corporal Scott Bukkake
Nov 24
 
READ IN APP
 

There is an angel in Bentonville, Arkansas. It arrived on Earth in 1963 and was neutralized by a small group of CIA operatives, then transported to this very holding facility here in the Great US of A. What those CIA operatives came to discover is that, no matter how thoroughly you neutralize an alleged biblical angel, it only stays neutralized for about twenty-four to thirty-six hours.

Within the containment room, regardless of how much the body is destroyed, it just comes back. Just about everything has been tried, short of nuclear intervention, according to the scuttlebutt I picked up in training. I press a button, the angel goes up in flames, and I watch its body curl over and flake off into ash. But come dinner time, I’ll have a half-formed, skinless homunculus crawling across the ceramic tiles. If that sucker comes whole completely without being neutralized again… Well, I’m not sure what’s gonna happen. That part wasn’t in the training.

I guess you can call me Number Three. I’m the third one who’s been in charge of neutralizing the angel. Before he retired, Number Two told me that Number One had told him (before he had also retired) that the whole “Angel” thing was a pretty hefty point of contention among the government scientists. They still don’t call it an “Angel,” if they’re even doing anything with it anymore. I don’t see anyone else. It’s just me and him. It. I don’t know.

To me, it looks like an angel, so I’ll call it an angel.

He resides in a square room. Tiled floor. Tungsten, titanium, glass fortified with polycarbonate. Smudges of ash. And he sits there on the floor, half-formed until I press the button. A reedy little fella. Dark skin, dark hair. Big, wet eyes with the kind of lashes you see on cows. Long and curly. Staring and waiting for me to push the button, every time I clock in. We’ve never spoken. He has wings. I’ve seen the interior architecture of bone and sinew. I’ve never seen him fully formed.

I don’t have a wife. It’s easier that way. The angel is too big of a secret to keep. I don’t think I could love someone, pretend to go to some office, press the button, come back and lie about what a shit day at work I had. You’d have to look at some nice woman’s face and lie, lie, lie, until Number Four gets recruited to take the reins. By then, my pension will be better spent on a home care nurse than a wife, anyway.

No children, either. Not even mistakes, thank God. A man with secrets like mine ought to just be a secret himself.

But it’s that question. That big, burning question that hangs over every day of the routine… I get out of bed. Why is it here? I brush my teeth. Why is it here? I eat my breakfast. Why is it here? I drive to the compound and take that elevator down, down, down. Why is it here? I step behind the glass and the angel and I look at each other. Why is it here? I press the button and flames erupt from the ceiling of its cell and render it into chunks of bone and ash. It doesn’t scream. He doesn’t scream.

I go home and do it all again. And that question. Always, that question. Turned into a decision. I was going to talk to it.

For the first time in seven years, I’m buying a pack of cigarettes. I don’t think it’ll keep my hands from shaking, but it seems less pathetic than day drinking. And Lord knows, getting a DUI would really put the kibosh on my very important job.

The control room has one of those green plastic ashtrays with a line of grooves to nestle many cigarettes in. It’s been sat on top of a file cabinet since before I came here to do this job. For the first time in probably twenty years that ashtray has a friend. As I watch the Angel slowly pull itself back together and come whole again, I leave a row of little fallen soldiers in those grooves.

There’s also a speaker on the command console in front of the glass, with what had once been a sleek-looking metal intercom, now peppered with rust. It’s just a few unplugged wires under the command center before a little red light turns on.

The Angel looks at me. He’s missing half his scalp and an arm yet, sporting patches of raw red where skin should be. Muscle and tendon hanging down like Red Vines against exposed bone… So I’ve got time.

I hear the echo of my own voice from behind the glass. A single, solitary, “Hey.”

The Angel’s lips move.

“Shit. Hold on,” I say into the intercom, and futz with the buttons until I figure out the two-way channel. “Say something,” I ask.

With a crackle of static, I hear the angel. His voice comes as a steady baritone, devoid of an accent and too smooth for someone who’s sat in silence for Lord knows how long.

“Good morning.” He doesn’t smile. He stares back at me with those big, brown eyes. If the angel was surprised that I was talking to it, I couldn’t really say.

I watch a bead of sweat drop onto the tarnished metal of the intercom controls and I clear my throat. “What—” I have to clear my throat again. The cigarettes, I tell myself. “Why are you here?”

The glossy, exposed muscle on his forehead twitches where his eyebrow would be. He doesn’t blink and his expression doesn’t change outside of that little twitch. “To call you all home, Douglas.”

“—What?”

“I was to deliver the message and sound the horn to call you all home.”

The tendons and muscle slowly wind around the bone of his arm. It’s gotten longer. The skin is growing on his scalp too.

“I, uh… I’m a Lutheran,” I say into the speaker, lamely. The Angel tilts his head in an apparent gesture of confusion, and I assume that the flavor of my upbringing means fuck all to him. Small hairs begin to grow on the freshly formed skin of his scalp. Veins curl around the newly formed bones of fingers. I wipe the sweat from my forehead. “Why do… Why do they keep you here?”

The Angel does not break his gaze, but considers the question. “So that they can stay. I have heard many reasons.” I feel an immediate wave of embarrassment about my own curiosity. Of course. Why else would an angel show up on earth? The Angel just keeps staring at me, and for the first time his expression shifts into something akin to curiosity. “Douglas? What is ‘Shareholder Value?’”

I cut the two-way channel with an abrupt, stuttered, “Sorry,” and reach for the button that immolates him. After the fires stop and the angel is a pile of ash and bone, I grab the wastebasket from under the console and vomit up my breakfast. Too many cigarettes, I tell myself.

There is an angel in Bentonville, Arkansas. I’m watching him where he sits on an ash-scarred tile floor and he’s watching me. The paper-thin flesh of a lid grows across the slick surface of his eyeballs. The angel blinks hard and I wonder if he’s immune to pain and discomfort, or if he’s just gotten so used to being burned up that he stopped screaming by the time I took this job.

Hairs grow on new skin, and feathers begin to push through the crepey surface of his wings. He’s never made it this far before and I can tell. He actually looks anxious that the flames aren’t coming. He curls one of his wings towards the front of himself and brushes his fingers over the surface of the freshly sprouted feathers. He’s died every day for sixty years, as far as I know, so it’s been a while since he’s felt them.

The console doesn’t have a lot of buttons. The thing is so damned old that most of the writing has worn off. I wonder, sometimes, what the label on the Kill button said. But there is one that still has words beneath it. Lock/Unlock. I was never given a reason to press that one in my training. It has a pleasant click when I press it down.

The Angel and I both pause when the hydraulic locks clunk open on the door to his enclosure. I close my eyes. I hear the door open and the soft padding of bare feet that stop behind my chair. The Angel lays his hand on my shoulder and gives me a single warm squeeze before his feet carry him out of the room.

I light another cigarette and stare down at the overburdened ashtray. When I reach the filter, I hear it. A booming siren that shakes the glass, even down in the subterranean pits I work in. And it was a beautiful sound.
