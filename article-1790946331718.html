Hello folks! Here’s the latest bimonthly roundup of writing written by subscribers, for the month of July 2026. Readers, please take a little time and see if any of these descriptions appeals to you. This post will exceed the allowed size of most email clients so please click through! I’ve discovered so much great writing through these roundups, and many who submit things report that they’ve meaningfully grown their audience this way. If you aren’t a subscriber and you want to take part in this opportunity in July, you know what to do. Be kind in the comments, far kinder than you feel you have to be with me.

Old Mole, Nothing “Left” about MAHA

MAHA’s defining attitude is one of selective skepticism. It casts doubt on approved vaccines and, at the same time, it touts speculative remedies. We might think that this attitude derives from a MAHA critique of capitalism: Big Pharma captures regulatory agencies to suppress the folk and the nontraditional. We would be making a mistake. MAHA’s selective skepticism is a cocktail of libertarianism and Social Darwinism. Libertarian in its objection to mass inoculation, and Social Darwinian in the way it appeals to herd immunity.

MV Perry, Letter To Ismail

A letter written to deceased photographer from Gaza, Ismail Abu Hatab, including three photographs of his work. Bio: Ismail Abu Hatab was a decorated photographer and filmmaker from Gaza City. He curated his photography into an exhibition called Between Sky and Sea, an immersive experience that also included a film, a soundscape based on the ambience of Gaza, and a recreation of a tent like those in Gaza city. The exhibition was based on resilience and ordinary life in Gaza through siege and displacement. His photography can be characterized as artistic photojournalism – equally valuable for their aesthetics as for their documentation of Gaza at the time. Ismail was martyred by Israeli forces on June 30, 2025, just one week before Between Sky and Sea was originally set to launch in Chicago. This was also not long after he had appeared on Al Jazeera + to publicize his work. Earlier this year, his film for Between Sky and Sea won “Best Documentary Short” at the Chicago Palestinian Film Festival. This coming fall, his photography will be included in an exhibition at the Chicago Cultural Center called Resisting the Long War, as part of a veteran arts triennial that confronts US militarism through arts.

Robert Francis Lynch, I Was Wearing a Tutu When I Started Bragging About My PhD

This essay traces AA’s unlikely rise and extracts practical lessons for modern founders about building communities around enormous problems, compelling storytelling, durable traditions, and decentralization only when a movement is ready for it.

Dhruv, Off the Sidelines: Why South Asians Rarely Go Pro

However, there is a vast difference between participation and professional success—more than just a gap, it’s a chasm. Why don’t South Asians go pro? Here’s my answer.
