Hey… Why don’t you come closer so I can whisper something in your ear?

You’re not special. You should just be normal. If you’re not good at being normal, you should do better. You are not the exception.

Just kidding. I would never tell you that.

If you haven’t figured it out yet, I generally hate when people dish out life advice. In fact, when I do it myself I find myself struggling not to add a list of addendums and exceptions because at some point it becomes impossible for “One-Size-Fits-All” solutions to solve life’s problems.

I don’t trust it and I don’t respect it. Because I am almost forty, I’ve codified my own experiences into lessons and truths that apply to someone like me. If nature and nurture came together perfectly enough to create someone under the exact same conditions as myself, then I might be able to offer them something. However, that’s pretty unlikely.

For now any advice I might offer comes with addendums and exceptions. I believe it takes all kinds and that there is no one definition of success that works for everybody. As mentioned in some of my patented dating slop, people are all working with different buffs and debuffs that affect how they’re able to navigate the world they live in.

Business Is Picking Up

King of the Hill, season 10, Episode 8.
Business is Picking Up centers around Bobby Hill shadowing a man called Peter Sterling for his school’s career week. Sterling runs a business called Earth Cleaners, which specializes in, to put it succinctly, cleaning up shit. It’s not a glamorous job, but Sterling somehow manages to make it look good.

Peter is an attractive, charismatic man. His business is successful. And Bobby is swept up in it so much he starts his own business, Beverly Hills Vomit, where he cleans up puke at frat houses. Of course, Hank is Bwaahhhhh~ horrified by this. What Hank understands that Sterling couldn’t see outright is doughy, effete Bobby Hill isn’t going to make cleaning up human waste look very good. It’s going to be another thing that opens his son up to ridicule, when Hank knows there’s already so much stacked against him. He has to break it down to Sterling until it actually clicks, and together they work to successfully dissuade Bobby from a career in vomit.

There are a lot of Peter Sterlings out there, who can’t see their own operating advantages and fail to understand why their own life is not a good fit for others. While Mike Judge offers a very sympathetic interpretation of such a scenario, I am less inclined to do so.

The Damage is Done

Not unlike Popeye, I ams what I ams. What happened, happened. I was a weird kid and now I’m kind of a weird adult with strange proclivities. I’m almost forty, and even though I am working on my weight, I’ll probably never be attractive without intense surgical overhaul, so I’ll never reap those social advantages to their full effect. I was made fun of enough as a child to have a base lack of trust for people who fit in. I have no edge with these people, socially.

What might have been sour grapes twenty years ago is simply how things are. The act of deciding you didn’t want something to protect your ego is certainly a real phenomenon, but that only really lasts for so long before you actually just learn to live without. The forging of a different path isn’t intentional. It just happens.

So now, even if I get the job, develop the habit, and greet the world with fake-it-till-you-make-it confidence, there is (what feels like) an internal structure that has crystalized. Normal, I am never going to be. Trying to bend my edges into a socially acceptable configuration, which I have often had to do for jobs, feels like performing a spiritual minstrel show. I believe there are a lot of people who feel this way, when trying to navigate normieworld (haha). Having not been given a chance to be included when that process of crystallization was happening, I missed the window.

I’m not sad about this. I have been sad about it before I forged my path, but now its just a thing that happened. I do not suffer from the curse of Greener Grass. Even people with every advantage can suffer the curse of Greener Grass. It’s not about standing on a lawn of disadvantage. I’m ultimately thankful for all the relationships I’ve made and where my life has ended up, and to consider that some dramatic change twenty years ago could have made things different just isn’t a thought I carry with me.

The World Where We’re All The Same

The Peter Sterlings of the world are different from Mike Judge’s far more generous presentation. While there are plenty of people willing to tell you how to get into the club, I am inclined to believe that they wouldn’t be happy if a weirdo actually managed that.

They like being able to dole out advice that people in general society can nod their head at. They like the status quo. They’ll tell you to suck it up and take the job, even if it’s a job that kills you inside. They’ll tell you to just have kids, even if you insist you do not want that. Just do it. Just be normal. Any resistance or critique is met with declarations of cope and jealousy. Sour Grapes. They won’t, or will refuse to, understand that some things actually just don’t work for people. They don’t understand that we’re not high school sophomores dreaming of being invited to the cool party. Most of us have thrown our own parties with like-minded individuals.

If they don’t understand in earnest, that’s one thing and that’s forgivable. If they refuse to? Then I can only assume that the advice they’re shelling out is for their own ego. The problem with these matters is that everything can hide behind a cloak of good intentions. Good ol’ Just Telling It Like It Is wisdom. But middle age has given me the gift of experience, and I also know the difference between being frank and being a dick.

What the Advice-Givers do when they refuse to acknowledge this divide, is to turn it into a moralistic failure of spite or laziness. If I did it, so can you. And if you can’t there’s something wrong with you! In their eyes, everyone is equally capable, except those who are conveniently unfortunate enough to be part of a latte-serving underclass… And if that underclass wants a higher minimum wage, they should just learn Excel and be normal. As much as I hate to chalk everything up to status games (shout out to Walt Bismark lmao), this shit is a status game.

They don’t want you to be better.
They want you to fail trying to succeed at what they do, instead of finding your actual place in the world. If we were all the same, there would be nothing that made them feel special (except the Robitussin they keep in their desk drawer at the office). They’d need to invent some other arbitrary status game to feel a bit of power.
