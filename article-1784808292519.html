Why Even Have Kids If You Let Even One Person Help You With Them?

The scope creep of "outsourcing" parenthood

In case you subscribed recently for sex/relationships content and do not want to read anything remotely adjacent to parenting, never fear—more of that is coming on Monday, and given that I publish almost daily, I have quite literally hundreds of archived articles on those topics, including but not limited to: is sex really like cold pizza for men? Is the Internet giving everyone a cuck fetish? Which male bodies do women rate the highest, and do those preferences change with the woman’s body type? And…much, much more. Today is about parenting, tomorrow will be a personal essay, and I promise to resume the sex stuff on Monday. Until then!

One particular Mom Twitter drama has been seared in my mind for over a year. A remote working mom posted that she wanted to spend time with her young children throughout the day during her lunch hour or other moments of downtime, so instead of putting her children in daycare or hiring a full time nanny, she did something a little different—she hired a nanny to deal with the less meaningful moments of parenthood (such as vacuuming crumbs or preparing snacks) so she could save time on chores and spend more time reading, snuggling and playing with her kids.

She was told, by multiple people, that she “shouldn’t even have had kids.” What was the point, they asked, if she was just going to outsource it all?

And, well, drama like this is circulating now, in part thanks to a classic “rich people rage bait” Wall Street Journal article describing six-figures-worth teams of childcare professionals hired by “CEO parents.” And perhaps people are right to grimace at this extreme approach—it’s certainly not one I would take, even ifI had the money, which I don’t—but the discourse has spun into a variety of innocuous ways of getting help that apparently make having children completely pointless. Invariably, these people will say the same thing they said to the remote working mom: Why even bother having children if you’re going to have someone else do X for you?

The problem is, X can be just about anything. It’s gone so far as to encompass sending your children to school, something done by upwards of 95% of moms.

More importantly, the remote working mom in my initial story spent more time with her kids than basically all full-time working moms (or, ahem, any dads.) Most kids of two working parents, remote or not, are in some form of childcare and do not have lunch with their moms every day. It became obvious, almost immediately, that people weren’t angry at her for not spending enough time with her kids, even though they pretended that was the issue. They were angry at her for being in a privileged position (something she didn’t deny!) to be able to hire people for the less fun parts of parenthood. And despite being a SAHM who spends basically 24/7 with my kids outside the school year (in the summer, I have zero childcare) I couldn’t imagine any good reason to judge this woman.

From the trad crunchies to certain lefties who just hate parents, it’s fun to rag on lazy parents for outsourcing, offloading, or essentially refusing to parent their own children. And while people who have $250,000 teams dedicated to every aspect of childcare are going too far, the amount of people who truly outsource parenthood to that degree is a rounding error and not worth the moral panic. Instead, the label of “outsourcer” has creeped onto basically any parent (always a mom) who hires even one person for occasional help with something.

In particular, the “why even bother having children” mindset is especially striking on Substack, where it’s also popular to say that parents need a “village,” and that in order to increase the number of children one has (essential, by the way), you must chill out a little about your “neurotic millennial intensive parenting.” I know, I know—you’re going to tell me these two groups of people are different and I’m combining them in my mind to feel superior. Well, I don’t want to put anyone on blast, but they’re not mutually exclusive. Or at the very least, the Venn diagram overlaps a bit.

When these people say that parents should “be less neurotic,” they are referring to imaginary rich-coded neuroticisms which are easy to dismiss, like needing your children to have a bulgogi food truck at their birthday party, or risk-averse neuroticism, like worrying about your child breaking a leg when climbing a tree. They are not referring to things like “believing oneself to be a failure of a parent if day camp is leveraged in the summer.” No, this apparently is totally worth being neurotic about, because a parent who allows someone else to teach their children to learn how to swim, ride a bike, or anything else (even if the parent is just not a very good swimming or bike instructor, as many of us aren’t) is “outsourcing” parenthood just like those evil CEO parents who can’t be bothered to wipe nary a single butt.

Other acceptable worries include: your child spending even one night in their own bed before the age of seven (guaranteed attachment disorder), your child knowing what a TV or God forbid a tablet is before the age of thirteen, your child ordering off the wretched kids’ menu instead of eating home-slaughtered beef heart for lunch, or your child entering any kind of “institution” whether public school, summer camp or even a singular piano lesson, because at that point, THE STATE has basically ADOPTED your child.

People largely agree that modern parents are too stressed out and don’t have enough support. They agree that parents are having fewer children than they’d like to have. Presumably, the answer to this quandary is for parents who can handily afford it to hire help now and again, especially for things like cooking and cleaning which might reduce time spent with children. But…no! Not like that! That’s outsourcing!

Since when has “raising your kids” become mutually exclusive with ever receiving help with particular things? Hired or not, basically every parent in history has received help with their children, but within this particularly class-resentful “outsourcing” discourse, the only help that counts as the bad type of help is hired or unrelated help. (It’s fine if your kids spend a week at the grandparents’ house, it’s a stain on your parenting if they even attend a part-time day camp.) And the tent for unacceptable “hired help” has become so inordinately capacious that I have seen homeschool mothers insist that any SAHM who sends her children to school is “outsourcing motherhood” and therefore shouldn’t have had kids at all or call herself a SAHM. I’ve seen some of them say, “If you aren’t homeschooling your children, what are you doing all day?” (It feels worth pointing out that in the “olden days” of the 1970s, a tiny fraction of American children were homeschooled.)

While I know this doesn’t help my case that this belief goes beyond “wackos on the Internet,” famous pronatalists Simone and Malcolm Collins believe that a “nightmare” of outsourcing includes basically anything or anyone which might interact with a child other than his or her parents. Does your child enjoy playing on the school soccer team? Well, darling, I’m afraid you’ve just outsourced to two ghoulish parent-substitutes!

Imagine your raison d'être is getting people to have more kids, but then you don’t want working parents to use anything that would make having multiple kids more attainable. Sounds legit!

Most of these people would probably make exceptions for childcare provided by a grandparent or relative. But for reasons that cannot entirely be pinned on parents, most parents today do not have easy access to familial or community childcare. Even if parents don’t move far away from their parents, many millennials report that their parents just aren’t that into the grandparent thing, and would rather spend every other weekend on a cruise. Many of us were born into smaller families, and do not have a plethora of available aunts, sisters, and other always-female relatives available to be free nannies and educators. And let’s not forget that the trad mothers of yore often left their kids completely unsupervised all day to play around the neighborhood, something that now warrants a CPS call from your nosey neighbor or your arrest.

You might say this belief system only exists among a fragment of crazy trad moms and men in Malaysia pretending to be women for monetization dollars, but there’s more! During a surprisingly left-wing argument a year ago about modern children’s literacy, a bunch of childless leftists decided that parents were “outsourcing parenthood” by…expecting teachers to teach kindergarteners how to read. Multiple people said that teaching your own child to be fully literate by the age of four was the bare minimum and that if a parent wasn’t willing or able to teach their four-year-old (or even three-year-old!) to read, they were neglectful and setting their kids up for long-term failure and a lifetime of “feeling like a burden.” Teaching was apparently NOT the job of checks notes teachers:

Teachers piled on, saying that “lazy parents” were dropping their painfully illiterate four-year-olds off at preschool and saddling them with the outrageous job of teaching them how to read. This was especially ludicrous for me, given that my son was four at the time, his preschool teacher was still teaching the ABCs, and there was zero expectation that the children be fully literate even by the end of the year. It’s fashionable to accuse modern parents of being lazy and neglectful, but it struck me as particularly odd to assume that “teaching” was NOT the job of a teacher.

A similar argument happened toward the end of the covid pandemic, when parents who wanted schools to open were accused of “offloading their kids” (aka BIOWEAPONS) onto teachers, as if there was no reason a nine-year-old would go to school other than to be supervised by a glorified babysitter, and as if there were no reason other than hating their kids that a parent with no credentials wouldn’t want to homeschool while also working a full-time job. One person, who described themselves as a communist, said that if parents really cared about their kids, one of them would stay home full-time to homeschool, but alas, mothers who continued to work were just “selfish.” Your communists, sir.

In this case, the issue was not class resentment, but general parent resentment, which is another issue entirely from the type of ire the working mother with the nanny faced in my initial anecdote. That said, my point remains: we need to either accept that some outsourcing is fine, or change the increasingly-broad definition of what “outsourcing” is. If school and sports teams fall under the umbrella of “what was the point of having kids?” then there really is no point of having kids for upwards of 95% of parents, which is quite a statement to make if you’re a famous pronatalist.

None of this accounts for the fact that different moms have different limitations (it’s never dads who are “outsourcing,” curiously, despite the fact that almost every dad has someone other than himself performing the vast majority of childcare.) Some moms are built to spend much more time with their kids than other moms, who burn out faster. Some moms are genuinely just much better teachers than other moms (my attempts to teach my son to read failed miserably—and then it turned out he had taught himself by following along as I read to him.) Some moms are better at playtime, while other moms cannot bring themselves to comply with a nonsensical play-pretend world. Some moms are better at cooking, some moms clean up faster than other moms…you get the point. Given that not all moms are equally talented at everything that encompasses parenting, would it not make sense to have someone who is skilled in a particular area help you? By this, I do not mean hiring a “storytime doula” to read stories to your children every night because you can’t be bothered to crack open Grumpy Monkey. I mean that perhaps, if your child wants to learn to ride a bike, and you’re not very good at riding a bike yourself, nor are you a very good teacher, it’s fairly reasonable to send them to bike camp, especially if they need something to do in the summers while you’re at work.

This also does not mean that the mothers who enjoy spending 24/7 with their kids are inherently superior mothers. Quite the contrary—I’m sure some of these moms are fantastic, and then others are clingy, overly anxious, and their kids might be happier with a higher degree of outside socialization without Mom hovering. (I am fully aware that I veer a bit close to the latter—and I’ll be honest that because I need to write and watch my kids, I do—gasp—let them watch Bluey sometimes.) But unless someone can prove that, say, hiring a swim instructor for your children has long-term negative effects (and good luck with that one!) then perhaps we need to think more about what “outsourcing” panic is really about.

If it’s truly about time spent with children, then outsourcing minor tasks to optimize time spent with children is fine. If it’s about paying someone, then sending your child to public school is fine. Realistically speaking, all of this is fine and it’s only the extreme cases—too extreme for any of us plebs to bother discussing—which are the problem.

But most importantly, if you believe more women should have children, or that mothers should have more children, something’s gotta give. The segment of mothers who would stay sane while homeschooling, performing 24/7 attachment parenting, and never once receiving outside help is slim to none. If “raising the birth rate” is something you genuinely care about (and I have my doubts that this is a real concern and not just a cudgel for personal pet topics) then that would mean making having more children palatable and accessible for women who might only be naturally inclined to have one. Unless your goal is simply to punish mothers in a bottomless pit of shame, why not make motherhood more doable for the women who are not in the 99th percentile of natural aptitude?
