How AI is breaking the internet (and everything else) ft. Cory Doctorow

AI CEOs are promising “an age of miracles” as the web goes dark, the banks go offline, and planes get grounded nationwide. I sit down with the ‘Enshittification’ author to discuss.

Brian Merchant
Nov 18
 
READ IN APP
 

The internet as we know it seems to be falling apart.

On Thursday, November 12th, Microsoft suffered a massive global outage, shutting down access to Outlook, Teams, and websites running on Azure, for not one but two days in a row. Next, the Federal Aviation Administration’s system crashed, forcing the agency to ground flights nationwide for just the second time since 9/11. The hits keep coming; this week, American banks like JPMorgan Chase saw their websites crash, taking payment processors like Zelle down with it. The same day, United Airlines’ system went down, then Apple Pay crashed, too, for good measure.

What did everyone immediately turn to blame? AI, of course.

Why wouldn’t we? Headline after headline has blamed “rogue AI” for corporate hacks and disruptions. Many Americans experience AI primarily as a annoyance or cause of anxiety; a layer of slop coating the internet, a scam enabler, a bot they’ve had to incorporate into their workflow whether they want to or not, a feature that keeps returning dubious results where reliable web search used to be.

AI promises miracle cures. We expect grounded planes

Brian Merchant

Nov 14

The vibes in AI world are bad.

Now, it’s looking less likely that AI systems had much to do with most of the outages. Well, at least not directly. There were no “rogue agents,” but AI still very much played a role. Take Microsoft. The tech giant laid off tens of thousands of employees in recent months, pointing to increased efficiencies from AI. Those employees might have well been the ones able to prevent or correct the outages, or freed up the time an energy for others to do so. And don’t forget, the FAA was an early target of the Trump administration and its AI-obsessed, Elon Musk-led DOGE effort to gut government agencies and replace them with automation; 400 employees were laid off last year.

But this is not the AI story that Silicon Valley wants anyone to hear. That AI’s impact on productivity has been a deeply mixed bag so far, that workers are often in open revolt against AI systems imposed on them, that firms are reducing token spend and hiring back employees they laid off for AI, that a majority of Americans are not excited about AI but concerned.

Silicon Valley especially does not want anyone to hear this as Anthropic and OpenAI make the final push for their much-anticipated IPOs, and pitching themselves to investors. They’re going red in the face repeating the same utopian pitches for AI they have for years now: It will cure cancer, Alzheimer’s, every disease. In ten years! It will be an age of miracles! Sam Altman actually said that AI is about to deliver us a genie that can grant any wish! These are their actual, exaggeration-free pitches. And at a time when planes are grounded and servers are down, and everyone’s blaming AI, no less. The Trump administration, for its part, rather than addressing AI’s role in the mass airplane grounding, directed reporters to refer to it as “Super Intelligence, or SI.”

It’s an act of aspirational branding. But this branding exercise is wearing thin. People can see for themselves that the systems are not functioning as promised. That, unless they’re a software engineer, a startup founder, or a content generator, most of our lives have not improved as a result of AI. Oftentimes, they’ve gotten worse.

So what gives? What’s really happening with all of our crucial infrastructure? Did AI destroy the internet as we know it? How did we get here, and where do we go next?

To answer those questions, I spoke with the man perhaps best suited to serve as a guide through this crumbling digital landscape of ours: the science fiction writer, online activist, and Enshittification author Cory Doctorow. We had a wide-ranging conversation about why the enshittified web is falling apart right now, the perils of over-automating with AI, the general state of the bubble, and where we go from here. I hope you enjoy it as much as I did.
