The Line Podcast: Canada wins, separatists lose

A referendum. A military tragedy, and a pathetic government response. Plus, a personal update from Matt, and a comment on the health-care system.

Line Editor
Oct 23
 
READ IN APP
 

In the latest episode of The Line Podcast, Matt Gurney returns from a leave to care for his wife and wonders if he’s missed anything over the last two weeks. Turns out he has. It’s his first chance to sit down with Jen Gerson and talk about the Alberta referendum, and Jen — finally having gotten some sleep — offers her concluding thoughts on … all that. Matt gives his reactions and asks Jen a fairly basic question: what, exactly, was the point of the whole exercise? They agree that Danielle Smith doesn’t come out of it looking particularly good. They also agree she might just skate anyway, especially since the separatists lost so badly that a lot of them might just move on to the next grift.

This episode is brought to you by BioCanRx. We’ve been partnering with federally-funded Canadian not-for-profit BioCanRx to learn how made-in-Canada immunotherapies offer new ways to beat cancer. This month we’re addressing common questions, like: is Canada behind on immunotherapy?

Canadian researchers continue to be global leaders at discovery research. Where Canada lags behind is in bringing these discoveries from the lab to the patient. While many immunotherapies come to Canada for clinical trial or market authorization, few are based on Canadian research or developed in Canada, meaning we access them when their international sponsors choose, at whatever prices they want to charge –– provided Health Canada approves and the provinces decide to pay.

Developing more Canadian discoveries into approved therapies in Canada would help reduce Canada’s dependence on other countries, enable our health systems to learn what it takes to deliver these novel therapies while collecting data to assess their value via early stage clinical trials, and provide patients with much-needed access to advanced therapies. A win-win-win scenario.

BioCanRx continues to work with researchers and policymakers to deliver on this mission. Thanks to them for continuing to partner with The Line to share this information and visit BioCanRx.com to learn more.

Next, your hosts turn to the catastrophe at CFB Wainwright, where some of the worst instincts of Canadian governance were on full display. The safety of the troops and care for the families of the fallen obviously come first. But the disaster also became a textbook example of how Canadian institutions respond to emergencies by smothering transparency. Matt and Jen go through what was known, what was revealed and, yes, read aloud what may be the worst official communication ever produced by a Canadian government comms person. Matt also responds to criticism that the military probably didn’t yet know the answers to the questions The Line wanted answered. There’s just one problem with that defence: someone leaked The Line the memo. They knew. So ....

This episode is also brought to you by Cameco. Building Canada’s energy future means creating jobs for Canadian workers. That’s why the AP1000 reactor is the right choice for Canada: it is already operating and ready to deliver the power we need. Choosing the AP1000 means a reactor built by Canadians, creating skilled jobs in construction, fabrication and the trades while keeping more investment and economic benefits here at home. If we are serious about creating Canadian jobs and strengthening our economy, the choice is clear. To learn more, visit ap1000.cameco.com.

Finally, Matt offers a personal update on the family medical emergency that took him away from the podcast. He’s still figuring out how much he can and should say publicly, but he has some broader thoughts about Canada’s health-care system. It turns out all the clichés are true: it can be astonishingly good and absolutely awful at the same time. Matt also has some very sharp and specific criticisms of one group of hospital professionals. He thinks the problems there are serious enough that they could end up collapsing the system, literally. Canadians will die because of this; Canadians are already dying of it, and Matt got to watch one of those deaths with his own eyes.

The harder question is what anyone can do about it.

All that and more in the latest episode of The Line Podcast.
