Why rich people sometimes look like shit

This tweet is not an isolated incident. There was similar drama a while back about Lauren Sanchez’s Met Gala outfit and wedding dress. And it’s not just Lauren Sanchez. Many tacky ultra-rich people have earned ire online from Kim Kardashian to Paris Hilton (at the height of her fame) to Elon Musk’s ill-fitting T-shirts and skinny jeans to Mark Zuckerberg’s new “only white kid in the hip hop dance club” look.

The refrain is always the same: money can’t buy taste. The assumption here is that a super-rich person should dress a certain way—a certain “tasteful” way, which just so happens to read “rich.” And if they don’t, they’re not really worthy of their money or their fame, because they have failed to be born with taste.

And of course, while money can’t buy taste, they are somewhat synonymous. While we’re supposed to pretend they’re unrelated, it would not be so remarkable to notice that money can’t buy taste if taste was not in some way adjacent to money. Because of course the fuck it is. As I’ve written about before, even the “anti-capitalist” fashion snobs can use “taste” as a surrogate for “looking rich” so they can dodge any obsession with money and class, and claim that, no, they don’t care about looking expensive, they care about having taste (which looks expensive.)

TikTok influencers are also obsessed with clothes that signal wealth, via “old money” looks. The old money fashion fixation implies that there is a somewhat secret, ultra-wealthy echelon of society who doesn’t dress the way the plebians think they dress. You see, regular morons think that old money people are wearing flashy Gucci belts but actually they’re wearing unbranded crisp Oxford button-downs and trench coats! Groundbreaking! (And now, dressing that way reads “TikTok influencer,” not “old money.”) This distinction also allows regular people who are neither old money or new money to safely look down on people much richer than they are for “having bad taste,” because in their minds, every new money person is wearing a giant gold chain like Flavor Flav, and they’ve secretly unlocked the old money look by wearing a white T-shirt and jeans.

But as I’ve said before, there is no such thing as “old money” dressing. There is no such thing as “billionaire” dressing. As you can see, these people all dress differently from each other. Lauren Sanchez doesn’t dress like Kate Middleton. Leah Benh is literal Norwegian royalty, and dresses in a way that would never make it onto an Old Money TikTok inspo video:

There is no reason we should expect the ultra-wealthy, whether new money or old, to dress in a way that reads as especially classy, tasteful or understated. Why? Because they don’t need to! If you already have billions of dollars and you’ve run out of fun things to do, having already hunted a few desperate college students through the woods on your family compound out of boredom, why on Earth would you need to signal to the world that you have money? Who cares?

Now, the old money “experts” kind of understand this, which is why they’re quick to remind us that the aristocracy of England is puttering around in dirty Barbour wellies from 1978 and wool fisherman sweaters laden with moth holes, as if Billy Crystal suddenly became an unhoused body during When Harry Met Sally, because they don’t need to try. Fine. But why must we assume that looking like an unassuming farmer is the only way that an already-too-rich person might choose to express themselves if freed from the burden of striving? Surely, this might be the way some old money (or ultra-wealthy) people dress, especially if they were the types who didn’t care about fashion very much in the first place, who were merely purchasing designer duds the way a person on GLP-1s begrudgingly mows through a Starbuck’s protein bowl. Without the pressures of needing to appear rich, they are free to wear just about whatever and in their case, the “whatever” basically amounts to functional rags lying about. But what if someone really loves looking like a Miami club rat? They may not dress this way if they’re trying to get taken seriously and attempting to look wealthy (oh sorry, I mean classy.) But once they become a billionaire and have nothing to prove, they can drop whatever “understated” fashion items they felt obligated to wear and dress like they’re selling coke at a Pitbull concert.

Sure, you’d wear a beige sweater if you actually wanted to, and I’m sure many people genuinely find beige sweaters appealing, but I firmly believe that a lot of people just dress this way to look richer. If you don’t need to look richer, you can ditch the Eileen Fisher.

I’m sure some bad-faith readers are about to inform me that they like dressing in an understated manner and have no aspirations to look rich. It’s fine. I believe you. I am not denying that some people might happen to enjoy looking understated and classy without necessarily caring if people think they look expensive. But that also means that not everyone considers such a look aesthetically appealing. Some people want to look like a fucking pimp, and if they’re already very rich, they’re much more free to do it without worrying about what people will think.

Yes, you may notice that Mark Zuckeberg or Elon Musk or Jeff Bezos has no aesthetic sense. They all look so trashy! Yeah, they’re allowed to have no aesthetic sense. It’s kind of like how your average middle class guy needs to work out and get a good haircut to find an attractive girlfriend, but a Russian oligarch can have a harem of hoes while looking like Baron Vladimir Harkonnen’s uglier cousin.

I’m also sure that someone will be quick to remind me that I am not a billionaire, I am not old money, and I don’t know any billionaires. All of that is true! But the same, by the way, is true of all the TikTok influencers who claim to know all the secrets of understated ultra-wealthy fashion. The reality is that billionaires and royalty are regularly in the news, you can literally just look at them, and you can plainly see that they all dress differently. There is no singular sartorial theme between them that reads “billionaire.” Many of them would not necessarily look rich at all if we didn’t know who they were. That’s the point.

But this is frustrating, because for many people, fashion is about emulating the ultra-wealthy, even if they don’t admit that’s what they’re trying to do. It’s tempting, of course, to believe that you can bypass the designer labels and high prices and actually emulate them by just wearing Quince unlike all those gullible rubes shelling out tends of thousands on Birkins. But of course, it’s not the minimalism (or maximalism, for that matter) that makes someone look like a billionaire. A lot of billionaires just don’t look very good, and don’t look like billionaires at all. Besides, given the lifestyle of the average person, nobody will confuse you for “old money” because you’re wearing a well-tailored trench. They might think you look nice, but that’s not at all the same thing.

And yet, we are told time and time again that there’s some secret “insider” trick to appearing high-end which is actually lower-effort than whatever the idiots are doing. Apparently you shouldn’t paint your nails or wear mascara because the real fashion people are eschewing those things, even though a regular woman discarding their Great Lash will probably just look slightly more tired, not like a secret fashion insider.

The only trick to looking like a billionaire is, unfortunately, actually being one. And while you might be able to elevate your look with higher-quality clothing, I regret to inform you that I saw a friend the other day wearing the most expensive thing I’ve ever bought (a GANNI jacket) and she complimented it, while asking me if I got it from Zara or Mango. Other people are simply not calculating how “expensive” we look on a day-to-day basis, and most likely, any attempt that a regular person makes at looking higher-class will not register to anyone at all. Yes, even if their shortcut is a ratty sweater because it’s apparently what King Charles wears when he’s at Balmoral Castle. You’ll just look like a guy in a ratty sweater.

And, if you attempt to look like a billionaire by copying Jeff Bezos and Lauren Sanchez, you will just look like you’re waiting in line for a South Beach club called X-cite. And of course, if that’s what you love, that’s just fine.
