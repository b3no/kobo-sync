The Base of the Democratic Party is White Women, Obviously

I don’t know why we would feel the need to pretend that that’s not the case

Freddie deBoer
Nov 9
∙
Preview
 
READ IN APP
 

They’re saying woke is back, but don’t miss the irony and resignation involved. Many, many jokes crowing about the return of the social justice era have been told since Tuesday’s unprecedented blue wave, but most of them are indeed jokes - the comedy is front-loaded, the perspective baked in. The humor depends on the understanding that, whatever has arrived, it’s not a redo of the “be afraid” era of social media tribunals and hyper-sensitivity to language that we saw from 2016ish to 2021ish. This is true in part because even many people who are happy to see American liberalism ascendant again don’t want to go back to that world. Personally, I was never one to suggest that woke was forever. One of the biggest weaknesses of that approach to politics is that it’s so deeply unpleasant.

Of course, social justice politics never went away entirely; no tendency in American politics ever does. And there are some who benefitted in the last regime who would naturally like to bring it back. I thought this New York magazine piece was quite a throwback to Trump I-era progressive politics. It’s a celebration of Black voters, particularly Black women voters, thanking them for bringing us the Democratic midterm victory. It demonstrates many of the tics of bad social justice writing and reasoning - celebrating a minority group from a stance of pure abstraction, reducing politics to a vague set of symbols, treating association and tribe as the only meaningful factors, and playing very fast and loose with the facts in doing so. And of course the commenters (who we can safely guess are dominantly white) ate it up, beating their breasts as they celebrate Black women in vague, general, and deracinated terms. Giving people the opportunity to feel righteous for celebrating Black people (whatever that means) is the core appeal of the piece. None of this is how recent Democratic victories were achieved, for the record. It’s not just that the James Talarico and Troy Jackson and Scott Colom campaigns avoided the rhetorical excesses of the woke moment, but that Zohran Mamdani and Nithya Raman did too, explicitly socialist politicians who embraced pocket book issues and plain language. That’s the future, if the American left-of-center is smart enough to choose it.

Now, I think that this kind of crude demographic political analysis is of little value in general. But since we’re doing this again, apparently, it’s worth telling the truth: the base of the Democratic party is, in fact, white women, if the concept of a base has any meaning. It simply isn’t true that Black voters powered the 2026 midterms for the Democrats; in every meaningful sense, white women were far more responsible for that victory. And the only reason to pretend otherwise is to satisfy the bizarre rituals of contemporary progressivism...
