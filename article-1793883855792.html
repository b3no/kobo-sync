Men Are Hornier Than Women (Terms and Conditions Apply)

Yes, men’s and women’s sex drives are different--but they overlap more than you’d think.

Genderslop thrives on the cold truths about how men and women are polar opposites in every conceivable way. Bonus points if you can imply that the Bad Gender could never possibly understand the Good Gender’s feelings and experiences. But even if you’re speaking without a clear agenda, the best takes are the ones that simplify straight men and women’s behaviors, desires and abilities into clear, oppositional buckets. Men are visual, women are emotional. Men are logical, women are emotional. Men are horny, women are emotional.

This type of content does so well in part because for a little while, it was in vogue to insist that gender differences didn’t exist, and that anything we noticed that seemed different about the genders was either a figment of our imaginations or purely socialization-based. As a result, blunt declarations that men and women are polar opposites make people feel really good, because they can feel like their “controversial” opinions are being validated by a brave truth-teller.

But the reality lies somewhere in the middle. Men and women have on-average differences, but these differences can be starker (or less stark) depending on the topic. Moreover, there is a great degree of variability within each gender, where a man can easily align more with a random woman than a random man on any given topic.

Let’s take the discourse of the week (aside from all this “election” stuff): men and women’s relative sex drives. This woman tried to show men what being nagged for sex feels like by relaying them a hypothetical scenario in which their wife or girlfriend is begging them for sex while they aren’t horny:

As you can imagine, her edit didn’t help. Men responded that this was a very appealing situation, and that there would never be a scenario in which they were “not horny at all,” especially for a woman already attractive enough for them to date or marry. One guy turned the tweet into a copypasta and asked women to imagine feeling like they didn’t want to shop while their husband begged to take them shopping (honestly, I chuckled.) The takeaway: women just don’t understand the degree to which men are absolutely ravenous for sex, anywhere, anytime. Men are so sex-obsessed that they are never “not in the mood,” and if a man claims not to be in the mood, he’s either gay, his wife is fat, or he’s cheating on her.

Look, I agree men are, on average, hornier than women. The fact that women can go on testosterone to increase their own sex drive should put the “Are men actually hornier than women?” question to rest. Of course they are! I agree this hypothetical “woman begs for sex” setup was a bad example, because if you polled most men (many of whom are getting less sex than they’d like) “How would you feel if your wife begged you for sex while you were, checks notes, fixing shelves?” they would be up for it, especially in a hypothetical scenario.

But “men are always horny, women aren’t” isn’t exactly true either. It’s not just that there are exceptions and outliers, but that men and women experience sex and sexual desire differently, and then within the buckets labeled “man” and “woman,” there’s quite a bit of variability in sex drive and attitudes about sex.

As anyone who has perused r/deadbedrooms before would know, there are still plenty of women who want sex more than their male partners. In a less extreme example, there are plenty of men who aren’t low drive, but would be perfectly happy to have sex “only” two or three times a week. There are other factors that can affect sex drive for both men and women. Most men do not live out their whole lives quite as horny as they were in high school and college, which makes sense, given that testosterone drops over time. Women’s sex drives can increase or decrease dramatically within the same menstrual cycle. “Women and men have exactly the same sex drives” is not true, but neither is “Every man is down to fuck 24/7” or “Women don’t actually like sex.”

There is also quite a bit of variability between the sex drives of different men, but it’s hard to get honest, self-reported answers on this. When a man is having no sex, or less sex than he’d like to have, he begins to develop what I’ll call Buffet Syndrome, wherein he imagines a bottomless appetite for sex based on the fact that he’s especially hungry for it, but if actually presented with an opportunity for 24/7 sex, he’d likely tap out at some point, probably earlier than he expects.

I noticed (and satirized) this phenomenon after witnessing it many times on Reddit threads. Single men on Reddit often pontificated about how much sex they would have (including, frequently, non-reciprocal oral sex) if they were ever blessed with a female partner. And yet, Reddit is also full of women who want more sex than their male partners and whose male partners can’t bother with foreplay.

I don’t think men are necessarily lying when they overstate their sex drives (although I’m sure some are exaggerating for bravado.) But who amongst us has not laid eyes on an Indian buffet, loaded up their plate with scoops upon scoops of butter chicken and saag paneer, maybe a couple dollops of chutney and an entire stack of garlic naan, only to discover that while you were hungry enough to eat a full meal, you were not in fact hungry enough to eat twenty pounds of Indian food? A man whose wife constantly has a headache may truly believe that if her sex drive was not an issue, he’d be laying pipe eight times a day.

Yes, men are hornier than women on average, but they converge more than you might think, at least in the abstract. When I ran a survey on desired sex frequency, men and women mostly aligned on “a few times a week.” You could argue this is a stated preference and not a revealed preference, but I have my doubts that any man would round down his interest in sex, given that performatively claiming to be a Mythos FuckBot is the more socially desirable thing to do.

Of course, you can see the areas in this chart where men and women diverge. More men want daily sex than women, and more women are completely uninterested in sex than men. The gap is slightly wider when limiting to married people with kids, which is not terribly surprising, given that postpartum hormonal changes, breastfeeding and childcare are things that would dampen a woman’s sex drive more than a man’s. If these numbers hold true, you can easily imagine that a great deal of couples will involve a man who wants sex more than his wife—but there will be many others who are aligned, and perhaps some where the wife wants sex more. Also, both partners’ sex drives could change multiple times over the course of the relationship, meaning that they might experience both dynamics at different points.

Plus, a man’s theoretical degree of horny-ness, imagining all the women in the world, might be different from the reality once he’s just married to one woman. Via the Coolidge effect, male sexual drive and recovery time might improve in a scenario where he has multiple female partners because a great deal of male sexuality is tied to novelty. Of course, most men will never find themselves in this situation, and you average man may prefer a “boring” monogamous marriage for other reasons, and have ethical objections to infidelity. But this is another reason why men might overestimate their sex drive, especially when single. It’s not that they’re imagining better-looking women than who they could pull (although it’s possible) but that they’re imagining all women, not one specific woman every day.

While most people today acknowledge that men are hornier than women on average, the stereotype actually used to go the other way. The ancient Greek myth of Tiresias follows a blind prophet who reveals that women enjoy sex more than men by a ratio of nine to one. According to legend, he was blinded by the goddess Hera for revealing this information. Early religious and medical texts cast women as insatiable and “illogical” in their sexual appetites, seeking to drain men of their sexual life force, while men were seen as more restrained and logical.

While these beliefs were pretty off-base, the reality is not that women are “meh” about sex and men are always in the mood. This perception is being driven online, and I am confident that the Internet—especially this corner of the Internet—is full of men who aren’t having as much sex as they’d like to have!

My own (much more rudimentary) findings about the overlapping sex drives of men and women are backed up by a 2022 meta-analysis, covering over 600,000 people. This study also found that men have stronger sex drives than women, but likened the difference to average body weight between men and women. It is not exactly hard to find a woman who weighs more than a man, even if your average man weighs more than your average woman. According to this meta-analysis, about 25% of women have higher sex drives than the average man—just about what I would expect.

Enthusiasm for sex is also not just about how often you want to have it, and given that men and women experience sex differently, this will be harder to measure on any survey. Earlier, I wrote about how women have been maligned as “not really wanting sex” because they are more discerning about their sexual partners and environment. The implication is that men view sex like pizza—it’s always good, even if it’s bad—while women only accept “good” sex, and only under the right conditions. But my argument was that a pizza snob can still really love pizza! A woman can only want sex under very specific circumstances and still be really interested in sex—it’s just unfortunate for her that the type of sex she wants isn’t so easy to procure. One would not malign a man with a very specific fetish as “not really into sex.” He is into sex, in fact he’s a certified sexual pervert, but unfortunately for him the only way he can enjoy it is when he’s being suspended from the ceiling by someone wearing a Hamburglar costume.

The same thing happens with the declaration that “men are visual” while women apparently aren’t—and this generalization is also served up as evidence that men and women have wildly different sex drives. Yes, men are visual. Yes, if you wanted to generalize the average man and the average woman, the man might be more visually-driven than the woman. But this generalization is often used to assert that women don’t care about looks at all, or that they deprioritize men’s looks far below things like “resources.” As I’ve said before, leveraging beauty for resources is more of a survival tactic than a desirable sexual state for most women (if it were, strippers would genuinely be attracted to their clients.) As it turns out, women have plenty of “hardwired” physical preferences for men. and if anything, they’re likely to undercount these preferences in self-reporting, because women are told they aren’t visual, and they feel weird and guilty about liking conventionally hot men. Women also tend to feel weird about expressing desire at all, and feel more comfortable being the desired party. Again, I’m making generalizations—not all women are like this. But these are more meaningful and true generalizations than, “Men only care about looks, and women don’t care about looks” or “Men care about beauty, women care about resources.”

In fact, a speed dating study in 2008 showed that looks were the primary driving factor for both men and women. When asked to describe their ideal partner, men and women exhibited the answers you might expect of them, per gender stereotypes, with men weighting looks more heavily than women. But this was not replicated in a real-life speed dating scenario, nor were the people most favored by men and women on dates necessarily similar to what they had described as their ideal before the dates. The takeaway here: both men and women aren’t great at describing what they actually want, and how they prioritize partners in real life is surprisingly similar to each other, while diverging from how they claimed they would prioritize partners.

So: are men and women more alike, or more different? As annoying as it is to say, it probably depends on the man and the woman involved. Generalizations can be made, only insofar as they related to the average man or woman, which still leaves a lot of room outside those averages—areas which may overlap with each other quite a bit.
