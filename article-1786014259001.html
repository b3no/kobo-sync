How I Almost Got Expelled From Theater Camp

My attempt at starting a "secret club" immediately went wrong, in a worse way than I expected.

When I was in high school, I took it upon myself to write a two-hundred page dramatic memoir about my summers in theater camp. My parents, bless their hearts, read it (or pretended to read it) and tried their hardest not to laugh (I understand my work is now intended to provoke laughter, but back then, I was dead serious. If anything, I wanted them to cry as if they had just finished reading Angela’s Ashes.)

Although I had not used these words (it was 2004 and therefore, decidedly, “too soon”), I had framed theater camp as my metaphorical tour in Afghanistan, a gritty experience rife with trauma that molded me into the seasoned, serious fifteen-year-old I had become. Funny enough, I actually did have material if I wanted to write about trauma and scandal. My theater camp employed a few notorious perverts, including a high-school-aged counselor who was known for hitting on eleven-year-olds (to the point that he was referenced in a campwide improv skit when someone was, for some reason, allowed to use “pedophile” as a prompt).

For some reason, I did not use that material in my memoir. Instead, I focused on the truly shocking stuff, like the fact that I was constipated for two weeks, the fact that I had a scab slough off in the waterfront because I was on an inner tube that went too fast, and the fact that I got pantsed one time (I maintain the pantsing was slightly traumatic, as I was exposed as having a particularly voluminous bush and my crush ran from me in response, screaming, “My eyes, they burn.”)

But there was only one experience from the book that I truly believe still warrants coverage—not because it was traumatic or horrible but because it was very, very ridiculous: the fact that I almost got expelled.

When this happened, it was actually my second year of theater camp and, thankfully for me, I already had a friend group baked in. Even in theater camp, I was not particularly popular, in part because I’m not actually that good at theater, I’m just a bit of an attention-seeker. So I made friends with a few other weird kids who were doomed to eternally fill ensemble roles on HMS Pinafore and putter around camp being freaky and annoying. My best friend, June, stuck out because she was one of the only girls at camp without Jewish heritage (she looked as if an 1800s racist had tried to draw an Irish person from memory) and prided herself on being both funny and a bit mean (I thought she hated me for the first week of camp.) June seemed to be in some contest with herself to include the F-word as much as possible in sentences which had no need for profanity (ie: “Let’s all get some fucking breakfast.”) June and I were a trio with a boy named Carl, who was gay and in a closet made of saran wrap.

June, Carl and I did basically everything together. We ate every meal together and when we had our choice of activities, we would colonize a table at the needlecraft station and hand sew little pillows, toys, and in my case, increasingly safety-pin-adorned halter tops.

But as much as June and I loved hanging out, just us and Carl, we were both pretty boy-crazy, and we had surmised that Carl was not that type of boy, despite his barely-in-character flowery declarations that he “really wanted to eat out” various girls on campus. June and I were not popular with boys. Boys made fun of June for being chubby, and they made fun of me for being flat-chested. One creatively made fun of me for having three small moles on my forehead, telling me I looked like a Dalmatian. It’s honestly very impressive to give a twelve-year-old girl a new insecurity, especially when that twelve-year-old girl was me of all people, who was already preoccupied with a fear of premature jowling.

Our only hope for getting male attention—from anyone other than the pedophile counselor who had already propositioned me a few times—was a fun little game called Truth or Dare. I had played Truth or Dare the previous year with a group of slightly older kids. One of them had dared me to “French” an older boy that I found particularly gross, and in response I scampered off, clutching a long-legged monkey plush whose hands Velcro’d together, which I had for some reason brought along with me to the game. Not my finest moment if I wanted boys to take me seriously, but at the time, I was hoping the dare would be something along the lines of cheek-kissing.

But this year was different. Within the first few days of camp, I had my first camp boyfriend: a skinny blonde boy my age who listened to Blink-182 and who had not only Frenched me but had also grabbed my “boob” (not sure it counted, really) over the shirt. We broke up soon after when I had Carl go on a spy mission to interview him in his bunk for a “documentary about dating” so I could get footage of him talking about me. I probably would have gotten away with it if I hadn’t been quite literally hiding in the bushes cackling as Carl emerged from the bunk.

But I think that boyfriend was a bit of an anomaly for me. Once we broke up, I had a hard time conjuring up any more age-appropriate male attention. And I saw sexual energy everywhere I went. The older kids at camp—and in hindsight I realize that sixteen-year-olds who attended theater camp were probably not actually “cool” in any real way—would hang out late at night in empty playhouses and pavilions, doing what I could only imagine was the coolest, most depraved stuff ever. Maybe they were even Frenching. In particular, I was obsessed with a short Jewish girl named Carol whose thick black curls went all the way down her ten-inch-long back, whose bubble butt was rivaled only by her enormous boobs barely contained by a collection of identical white ribbed tank tops, and who always seemed to have a hot bad-boy boyfriend in skater clothes.

I knew that June and I could never be Carol, but we could at least try to create our own Carol-adjacent world. So we created “The Pretty Pony Club,” where we would participate in our own fucked up antics from the outdoor porch of a theater building at night.

The Pretty Pony Club was a satirical name, because we saw ourselves as so badass and edgy that everyone was supposed to know such a name was sarcastic. After all, the main purpose of the Pretty Pony Club would be rousing and scandalous games of Truth or Dare! Unfortunately, we looked exactly like the kind of pre-teen girls who would be in a literal pretty pony club, so this “joke” didn’t really work. Carl managed to get a few (presumably) straight guys from his bunk to join us, under the guise of finally getting to play Truth or Dare with girls. June and I considered inviting more girls to play, and we eventually invited a stinky Wiccan girl named Julia, but decided against anyone else, lest they mog us.

Carl brought more participants, but none were particularly exciting for us (and I’m sure the feeling was mutual.) All of us were somewhere between twelve and thirteen. Three girls, four boys. Of the boys, there was Carl (of course), a dynamic duo comprised of Jake (a tall, painfully shy lad who appeared at least thirty but was in middle school) and his sidekick, Matt (a short, underdeveloped motormouth who appeared about nine but was also in middle school.) Then, there was “Shaggy,” another middle school aged boy who used such a name (not his real name) because he resembled Shaggy from the Scooby Doo series.

Our first night playing Truth or Dare, everyone kept picking truth (including me) and Julia kept killing our vibe by repeatedly asking people questions like, “Have you ever eaten a spork?” I realized that while I found Frenching these boys to be disgusting, I did not find it embarrassing for them to see me in my underwear, given that it provided about as much coverage as the bikinis they had seen many times at the waterfront and I wouldn’t have to touch them. So in an effort to be the edgiest person in the Pretty Pony Club, I somehow incepted the idea for June to dare me to “moon everyone” and by “everyone” we meant all the people walking past the porch, which was nobody because it was nighttime. Dutifully, I pulled down my shorts (keeping my full-butted smiley face underwear on) and mooned an abyss of nobody, then giggled and sat back in my seat.

From there, June would dutifully dare me to do things like that—such as “pole dancing” against the column of the building without actually removing my clothes, and at one point, “flashing” Carl by lifting up my shirt and displaying my Limited Too sports bra with a panda bear on it.

Word got around that June and I were partaking in something exclusive that involved boys. Most girls in our bunk probably didn’t care or thought it was cringe, but one particular girl was hellbent on joining—a particularly annoying girl named Katie. We had specifically avoided inviting Katie because we couldn’t stand her. She was constantly getting care boxes from her overindulgent parents, full of those thirty-two-packs of mini Doritos along with a treasure trove of full-size Snickers bars, and then refused to share with anyone. She was obsessed with telling obviously-untrue sexual stories, like the fact that she supposedly had sex with a hotdog when her dad briefly turned away from the grill. She often talked about masturbation and informed us all that we too could masturbate if we only lied underneath a running faucet, to which I said, “Ew, disgusting!” but mental notes were absolutely made.

In hindsight, there wasn’t all that much separating June and me from girls like Katie. Like us, she was insecure and attention-seeking and had a hard time reading social cues. Perhaps we didn’t like seeing this tendency reflected back to us. We tolerated Julia because she was so happy to play second fiddle, but Katie was simply too big of a personality to fit into the Pretty Pony Club. Also, given Katie’s tales of sexual degeneracy, we were afraid she might raise the bar for us and the “mooning nobody in particular” dare would no longer seem so daring.

We refused to let Katie join the Pretty Pony Club. In hindsight, we were kind of bullies to her. It felt justified because she wasn’t a very nice person either, but I think we were a classic case of the downtrodden person who gets a tiny bit of power and lets it get to their heads, with zero self-awareness about how much it sucked to be bullied. Also, because we couldn’t keep our traps shut, Katie still found out everything we did at the Pretty Pony Club. And she discovered the most scandalous action of the Pretty Pony Club yet—I had been dared to take my bra off underneath my shirt and let Shaggy tweak my clothed nipple while making some mechanical-dial-adjacent sound.

A few days later at breakfast, Katie came up to me and June and, in a faux-ashamed voice, said, “I’m really sorry, guys. I kind of ratted on your Pretty Pony Club. Fred wants to talk to you.”

Fred was the head of our camp (perhaps the proprietor?) and he was such a terrifying person that I’m scared of him even to this day. Fred was older—I want to say about sixty—but a huge, macho, imposing force. He almost never walked, preferring a small John Deere truck about the size of a golf cart. Rumor had it, he hit and killed a squirrel with his John Deere and evilly declared, “Another one bites the dust.” In hindsight, this story was probably not true, but the kind of dramatic tale a bunch of theater kids might cook up. Still, it was the kind of thing he would do.

I had one run-in with him before, when my bunk had been instructed to leave an orchestra performance early for a nighttime bonfire field trip. Our counselors told us to sit in the back bleacher row and then climb down to get on the field trip bus when it arrived. Somehow, Fred was unaware of this plan, and when he saw me (the last kid in the row) sneaking out to escape he demanded that the orchestra pause so he could get onstage and scream at me—and only me—for thinking the orchestra was so boring that I couldn’t stick around and be polite. I don’t think Fred actually remembered my face from that moment, so I wasn’t worried that I was still on his shit list, but I was worried that he might literally snap me in two pieces and dissolve me in a vat of acid once he heard the details of the Pretty Pony Club.

June and I, absolutely terrified beyond belief, held hands as we walked toward Fred’s office, a modest cabin with ivory siding at the front of the campus near the gates where our parents had come to drop us off. We entered and sat in some scratchy upholstered chairs for a while, staring at each other and saying nothing in particular, until his secretary told us he was ready to see us.

The two of us entered and sat in similar chairs, next to each other and across from Fred, who crossed his burly arms over his chest. He wore a polo shirt, slightly unbuttoned, displaying copious white chest hair. He didn’t yell. In a way, that was more terrifying. He wasn’t mad, just disappointed, and he was so disappointed that he was going to eat us. He just asked us one simple question: “Can you tell me what happened with this…uhhh..Pretty Pony Club thing? Do I have that name right?”

“Yes sir,” June said.

“I can tell you what happened,” I said, momentarily jazzed to be asked to divulge stories of my life, even if the circumstances were less than ideal. “See, June and I really wanted to find boyfriends so we started a club for Truth or Dare. And we got some boys to join and we did some funny stuff together, obviously nothing that bad, nobody had sex or even Frenched honestly but there was some light flashing.”

“What is light flashing?” Fred asked.

“I took off my shirt or pants but I had underpants on,” I said. “The only time I didn’t have a bra on was when Shaggy felt me up but I was wearing a shirt.”

“Okay, that’s what I wanted to talk about,” Fred said. “I need you to tell me exactly where Shaggy touched you.”

“Uhh…like in the second base way?”

“Can you point on your body where he touched you?”

“Oh uhhh…like here?” I pointed to the bony area in the middle of my chest, because despite my adventures with the Pretty Pony Club, it still felt awfully vulgar to point Fred towards my nipple.

“Can you describe this person physically?” Fred asked. Now this was a bit confusing. Was he trying to figure out if I was punching above my weight?

“People call him Shaggy because he looks like Shaggy from Scooby Doo,” June said. “So basically like that.”

“And he never shared his real name with you?”

“No? I mean, the guys in his bunk probably know it.”

“Okay, so which bunk does he work in?”

“Work?! No, he’s a camper.”

At that point, June and I realized that Fred was under the impression that a counselor, going by the pseudonym of “Shaggy,” was taking young girls into an isolated location in the middle of the night to molest them. Perhaps this was why he cared so much about the Pretty Pony Club, on a campus where teenagers frequently gave each other oral sex in the woods. But Fred couldn’t very well tell us it was no big deal, so he informed us he would be calling our parents—who were scheduled to pick us up at the end of camp in a few days anyway.

Desperate to get ahead of the narrative, I enrolled myself in the “computer station” later and logged online, bypassing my usual desire to watch Homestar Runner Videos, to email my parents everything—every detail from the mooning to the flashing to Shaggy tweaking my nipple. This was, without a doubt, the most damning and most embarrassing email I’ve ever sent in my life, and I don’t think my parents were very happy about it. This made for one of the most awkward reunions we’d ever had when they came to pick me up. And to make matters worse, Fred never called them, so as a character on Shaggy’s favorite show might say, “I would have gotten away with it too.”
