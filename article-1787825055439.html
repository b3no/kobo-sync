The “anti-social century” with Derek Thompson

What happens as people spend more and more time alone?

Matthew Yglesias
Aug 27
∙
Paid
 
READ IN APP
 

Derek Thompson coined the phrase “the anti-social century” to describe an incredible rise over the course of the 21st century in the amount of time that people spend alone. He recently joined Jerusalem and me to talk about it.

This is an issue I’m concerned about. Recent technological progress has been very concentrated in the realm of home entertainment pursuits that have left people spending more time alone and lacking human connection.

Jerusalem is more optimistic — insisting that we distinguish between loneliness and aloneness and that we see much clearer evidence of a rise in the latter than in the former. There’s nothing wrong, she argues, with people being alone as long as it’s not making them lonely and sad. It seems to me, though, that people increasingly are sad and angry even if they don’t say that they are lonely. And people who espouse moderate or liberal politics need some explanation for bad vibes that doesn’t cede the substantive ground to illiberal forces.

Derek brings some data to help draw these distinctions, and we discuss it all.
