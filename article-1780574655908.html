My Brother Wrote a Short Story About Me

Is this a short story or a long-form roast? We shall see. Presenting: Mommyland

Lots of writers spend countless hours trying to get even just ten people to read their work. My brother, who has a day job he enjoys and zero dreams of becoming a professional writer, got pretty lucky: his writing debut will be going out to thousands of people and he didn’t have to kiss any asses or even “work his network.” All he had to do was occasionally text me back. Nepo baby alert!

Anyway, what follows is a short story my brother wrote, inspired by, and I quote “the fact that you vacation on Looney Island every few weeks.” He’s correct that I have pretty bad OCD, and as one of my top confidantes, my OCD is exhausting for him. So this short story is basically a way for him to vent about how annoying I am. When I first read it, I did not infer that it was about me and I said, “That poor woman.”

Although this story is clearly inspired by me, it’s not a 100% accurate, biographical story, and most of the things in this story are inspired by things I’ve done or said, but not things I’ve actually done. So please, no, “Did you really do that?” No, I didn’t.

Now, the last question you’re going to ask: “Why would you paywall your brother’s short story? I don’t give a shit who your brother is! Why would I pay for that?” The answer is simply because I’m not sure I want everyone on Twitter having this much of a peek into my mental illness. Self-preservation paywall only, folks.

Without further ado, this is my brother’s short story, Mommyland:

I wake up in a sweat. It’s 3AM. A recurring nightmare: the boiler in the basement breaks and the smell of oil fills the house. I try to wake up Howie, but he’s hopelessly asleep. I see a drip-drip-drip of his saliva fall from his lip onto a small wet mark on his pillow. I run downstairs to find Jacob and Annie, to scoop them up and hold them to my bosom like I did when they were my miracle infants. Jacob is asleep in his room, still wearing a child’s onesie he is every day growing too large for. For a moment the baby I nurtured, the baby who would look at me with big brown eyes is gone and a nascent young man takes his place.

I try to wake my Jacob, but he won’t stir either. He’s just as trapped in sleep as his father. I sprint out the room to find Annie. She is still small enough for me to hold. I grab her in my arms. Wake up baby. Please just this once wake up. The smell is stronger. We’ll all pass out and perish if I don’t do anything. Someone has to do something.

I run down the final flight of stairs to the furnace. Please baby, just wake up this time for me. Please. I make it down to the furnace, baby Annie still in my arms. Fast asleep. A cute little smile on her face. The brush of her breath against my collarbone. Baby. I come down to the furnace and inspect it with my free hand.

Then I see the bad news: the furnace is leaking black viscous oil from its feeder pipe. The ground floor is slick with this stuff. Please baby, wake up. Wake up one time. If you wake up this one time, I won’t ask anything of you ever again.

The liquid pools and collects at my ankles. Then my knees. Then my hips. I wade across the basement and stumble up the stairs. Baby Annie looks so placid in my arms, totally unaware. I use all the strength in my body to carry Annie in one arm and Jacob in the other. I’ve become a superwoman, using every last ounce of strength in my body to carry these two hulking kid-babies up to our bedroom loft. Howie, that sweet, sweet man is still asleep. Snoring. I love him.

I get to our top floor and close the door. I open the windows. As I do, the rush of the oil smashes open the door. It’s everywhere, up to my neck. Howie, Annie, and Jacob are floating still asleep on the slick. Then all at once they finally wake up. Finally. They stare at me, but their brown-hazel eyes are gone. Their cute husband-baby-kid eyes have gone fully jet black. Oil black. The oil spill has drenched and covered their eyes. My petrol heart is black too. My head goes light.

I wake up. Howie is drooling and snoring like he always does. Next to me, faithfully and foolishly like he always is. But this time, there’s no scent, no slick. I run down to see the kids. Annie is fast asleep, but Jacob wakes up.

Hi Mommy. Is it morning yet?

No it’s not honey. Go back to sleep.

Why are you in my room if it’s not morning?

I smile and hug him. I tell him I love him with all my heart. The maximum amount. I ask if I can sleep on his floor and he nods. I sleep peacefully.

5AM. I see the small glimmers of pre-light. It’s peaceful: Jacob and Annie are still in the cloudy dreamland that only children can access, which frees me up to begin my morning routine.

I check my email. “Hosting for Two: Weekly Stats.” Click. 267 new paid subscribers. People who are willing to pay me $4.99 per month. I check my stats for the Unlimited Reassurance livestream I ran last night. 6000 viewers, 87 new paid subscribers.

The trouble with my online presence is that I hate the exposure. I never show my face or use my real name, and unlike the “mommy influencers” who try to profit off of showing their kids to the internet, I keep my life totally private. I’m terrified that one day, some creep/pedophile/murderer will come across my channel. I’d never be able to forgive myself for being successful if it put my kids in danger.

Virtually every woman I know who makes content on the internet is subject to abuse. Sometimes I want to quit — how could this be worth it if it put my family in danger? I feel so selfish sometimes, no matter how many times Howie tells me that Hosting for Two is the best thing that’s ever happened to me.

So that’s why I keep a running list of potential threatening actors, because you can never be too prepared.

Limited updates for the week: Eddie (the guy who called me a cunt at CVS once, I found his profiles by reverse image searching a surreptitious photo I took while he was checking out) is getting engaged! Really sweet — that probably makes him a viable candidate to drop off this list entirely, but I’ll have to wait at least a year to see if his relationship sticks. Some of the most prominent serial killers in American history have been married.

James, the elderly registered sex offender who lives 23 minutes away is probably not a threat (anymore!) either. He recently posted a photo of himself — he looks frail, has to use a walker, etc.

The final, and by far the most triggering stalker check I do is for AureliusThoughts. It’s one of the weird roman statue avatar accounts online. He actually did repeatedly harass and threaten another woman writer I’m friendly with. He’d send her the creepiest obsessive messages. One day he claimed he saw her walking around her neighborhood.

AureliusThoughts has followed me. He’s never commented or said anything untoward to me, but he does open almost every single one of my daily subscriber emails. I’ve tried to contact the police about him, but they won’t do anything — it’s hard to file a police report against an online pseudonym who has never talked to you. Every morning my heart races when I check my DMs — waiting for the day he decides to turn his parasocial obsessions onto me.

I breathe easy, for now.

The number 9 (3x3!) has always been a really lucky number for me, so I like doing important activities on days that remind me of 9.

Today is August 1st (08/01 → 9!), so I’m really optimistic about how things can go. We go to Target to get school supplies for Jacob for his first day of kindergarten. Jacob is wearing the same onesie he’s worn for years. It’s a NASCAR onesie with car and checkered flag motifs all over, even though Jacob has never been in a car before and has no idea what NASCAR is. Jacob loves how the onesie feels on his skin, even as he’s nearly bursting from the seams in this outfit that’s clearly too small for him, screaming and crying when I try to put him in a big-boy-outfit. This gives me the first challenging dilemma of the day: do I make him wear a big-boy-outfit by force, running the risk of inflicting pain and trauma upon him? This has the benefit of getting him used to big-boy-outfits, which decreases his probability of being bullied or suffering from arrested development. On the other hand, the struggle and conflict creates the risk of extreme suffering, which over time can cumulatively increase the odds that he goes no-contact with me when he is older (due to repeated trauma). Additionally, if he gets a bruise while flailing around, it could also attract the attention of Child Protective Services.

There are some parents whose kids should be taken away from them, but 1/3 children have CPS called on them at some point, often for trivial non-infractions that are simply the cost of doing business as a parent. And for the unlucky ones, innocent and well-meaning parents are tarred and feathered, their kids ripped from their arms and sent to foster care, where unknowable horrors are inflicted. I heard a story about a woman whose children were taken away because a malevolent doctor reported diaper rashes as evidence of repeated abuse. People simply do not believe that this story is true, but of course it is: watch the Netflix documentary. It’s streaming now.

This brings me to my other option: allow Jacob to continue wearing the NASCAR outfit until he physically grows out of it. Once again, a choice that is made for me. I’m comforted some by the fact that Jacob’s love for NASCAR Outfit is abstracted from the concept of a car or a racetrack. The collective obsession in our country with cars, and the way cars are marketed to children and families is one of the most grotesque parts of life today. All sorts of well-meaning people talk about gun control, but nobody has the courage to talk about Car Control. Cars are loaded guns, barreling down residential streets, in the hands of psychopaths and alcoholics who care more about getting to their destination five minutes faster than they do about saving the life of an innocent child. Our streets are teaming with wannabe murderers who drive dangerously with no regard for others. If they actually do hit someone? They never face real consequences. I joke with Howie that if Luigi had just run over the Health Insurance Guy with a car, he’d have faced 6 months probation.

Jacob plops into the stroller. He has a vacant look on his face. He’s listing countries again.

Afghanistan, Albania, Algeria, Andorra

I cherish the moment before becomes a grown up boy and doesn’t want to even see his mom anymore. His little baby accent only has a year left before it’s softened by our school system.

Afghan-tan, Awbania, Awgewia, Andowa

I think about how on his first day in school, I will be surrendering my child to the cold arms of the state for the first time. To be clear, I’m a liberal and I think that the government is usually good (food stamps, good! Pensions for old people, good! Unions, good!) but there’s something about dropping my son off at a state institution that makes my heart sink. Every few weeks I ask Howie if we can homeschool Jacob. I can’t guarantee that I’ll be a good teacher, but at least Jacob will know he’s loved, (with the added benefit of protection from bullying, cyber-bullying, school shooters, etc).

Howie is a sweet man because he says no to me each time in the kindest, most gentle way. He barely even registers that I’m asking once again. He just shakes his head and moves on. Howie is the sweetest, most handsome, most wonderful man in the world and I don’t know how I won him and I have no idea how to keep him.

He brings Annie out to the stroller. Annie’s long curly auburn hair frames her little baby face. I wonder what’s going on in her head. She barely talks, but knows how to ask for two things: food (whatever Howie is eating) and her velvet red dress (vewvet dwess wight now!). I’ve had to buy her multiple alternates on the “red velvet dress for a two year old” theme because she seldom leaves the house without it.

This morning I made pancakes for them — the nice Swedish pancakes that you cook at high heat on the griddle. Jacob barely touched his, preferring to lick the honey and lemon I poured onto his plate directly. Howie and Annie silently scarfed up a massive haul of tiny circular pancakes. By the time I was done, there were almost none left, but I really didn’t mind. I’m around my own cooking all day.

I push the stroller down the block. Annie is staring at Howie, who is looking up directions on his phone. Then I remember.

Howie? Did I remember to turn off the stove?

I’m sure you did Jess.

When you say that you’re sure I did, do you mean that you saw me do it, or that you just think it’s unlikely that I didn’t and you want to reassure me?

I’m sure you turned off the stove. How many times have we had this exact conversation? You’ve literally never left the stove on.

Howie, it will give me peace of mind if you give me an absolutely certain guarantee that the stove is off, otherwise I’ll have to check.

Howie sighs and gives me a loving, glassy-eyed look. I’ll apologize to him later, after I’ve confirmed that my family is safe from potential fumes or explosion risk.

Just give me one second Howie, stay with the kids right here, I’ll be back.

I run to the door in my heeled espadrilles (I try to go quickly when I’m checking these things, so as not to disappoint Howie any more than is needed). I remember how when we lived in San Francisco I could run up and down the huge hills in Pacific Heights in heels much taller than these. I’ve still got it. I open the door, turn the alarm off. I check the stove. It’s off. Phew. I twist the nobs a bit to make sure they’re not in the weird no-man’s land where the gas is still technically on, even if it looks off. I’m satisfied. I turn the alarm back on and run back to Howie,

Fee-zee, Finwand, Fwance

I smile at Howie. It’s all good. I look at my sweet babies, I soak in the glow of the perfection for just a moment. Ah, shit. What if in checking the stove nobs, I’d accidentally turned one that was previously off back on? Just by wiggling it too much.

Howie, don’t you think that’s a valid concern?

Jess, actually if you go back in a second time, we’ll be here all day, you know that.

I get it but this time it could be a serious issue. What if someone walks by our house and smells gas? That could be an ecological problem, a safety problem and a potential CPS-bystander problem.

I run back to the house, this time a little faster. I check (I don’t touch the nobs). We’re ok. Phew. I turn the alarm back on. I leave. Close the door behind me. Come to think of it, I’d feel a bit more comfortable if I did this a third time, doing it twice feels wrong. I won’t be able to enjoy my day out if I’m thinking about wanting to do this a third time.

I run down to Howie. Please babe don’t be mad. Just one more time, I promise. I run even faster (I suspect Howie at least appreciates how in-shape I am). Open the door. Look at the stove. Smile, Phew, that’s a relief. I close the door. Walk down the stairs. Fuck.

I forgot to turn the alarm on. Burglars, intruders, home-invaders, creeps, etc. I have to run down to Howie to complete the set of three.

I’ve messed up everything for the day. Howie! I forgot to turn on the alarm again. He looks blank. I run back towards the house before he has the chance to get mad at me. I know that by the time I get back he’ll either give me angry eyes or sunken eyes.

I resolve to do it all properly once and for all: run up (full sprint), open door, look at stove (smile), turn on alarm. Close door. I run back to Howie, I look at him briefly. Ah it’s sunken. Shit. I’d actually much prefer he just yell at me and tell me how bad of a wife or mother I am for exposing them to my shit.

6 more trips, 9 total. The house is finally in good order.

Twinidad and Tobago, Tune-eeza, Tow-key, Town-men-Tan

He’d never admit this, but I know Howie would prefer a cooler younger woman who wouldn’t cause him any trouble. If I vanished tomorrow I’d be a footnote in the lives of my whole family (“oh that crazy mother of yours!”) but nobody would really care. And if he’s not mad at me at all, that’s even worse!

Yemen, Zambia…

I’m glad I’ll be there when he wraps up. Whenever he finishes Zimbabwe I pick him up and hold him in the sky. Buddy welcome to Air Jacob, we’re here to fly with mommy all across the world. What country do you to go to this evening?

And the Lovely says, “I want to go to huggyland today!”

And I shower him with hugs and kisses. I have roughly 120 million seconds left until he starts to go through puberty and never wants to visit huggyland, kissyland, or mommyland ever again.
