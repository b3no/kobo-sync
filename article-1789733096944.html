All Men Are Firefighters, All Women Are Marketing Executives

The email job bubble is wrong about women's work.

In case you missed it, 98% of new non-farm jobs added to the US economy in August went to women.

Because the Internet is the Internet, we saw two very incorrect conclusions drawn: either that this news represented a major #girlpower success for women and was a referendum on men being unemployable losers, or that the nefarious feminist forces behind the dastardly Gen Z Boss and a Mini video were, right under the Trump administration’s noses, continuing to orchestrate the hiring of useless women for cushy six-figure (yet fake) marketing executive roles, robbing men of jobs and tanking the birth rate.

One explanation came a bit closer to the truth: the theory that most of these jobs were low-level roles in healthcare, which men typically don’t seek (and yes, healthcare is an especially fast-growing sector.) But the full story is even less clickbaity: yes, there was some job growth in healthcare (with more than half of those related to unsexy home health care services) but the bulk of these new ladyjobs were in hospitality, food service and education—also roles that tend female, and are not particularly cushy.

Ready to get even less gendersloppy? It might all be statistical noise anyway. People with more than one job would be counted twice, and women are more likely than men to cobble together multiple part-time jobs. Womp womp.

So no, there is no huge leftist conspiracy to push men out of the workplace, nor are women taking over all the Fortune 500 companies in what Fox News might call a “girlboss caravan.” Instead, this news just tells us that most working women are not who we imagine when we are in the white collar trenches of the Internet gender wars. Most working women are nurses, food service workers, home care aides, and teachers, not marketing executives or even “HR ladies.” Most women are not working particularly highly-paying, comfortable or cushy jobs at all, nor are they overwhelmingly taking over the jobs that men want. Most women’s salaries pay for rent and food, not handbags and manicures. And perhaps most importantly, if someone really wanted to push women out of the workplace to make room for men, they would have to figure out what to do with all the understaffed hospitals and nursing homes.

Over a year ago, I wrote Doge Is About Sex, where I postulated that the cheering on of federal layoffs eliminating “wasteful” employees was actually a fantasy of limiting women’s options for independence, which would hopefully force them to lower their standards for sexual partners. The plan (Nathan Fielder voice) was based on a few big assumptions, chief among them that federal layoffs would primarily impact women because women have the most useless jobs, and that the government had been purposefully hiring women for imaginary roles thanks to Biden-era DEI initiatives. Also, “job that would be eliminated under DOGE” was conflated with “job which AI would likely replace.” Layoffs and AI became synonymous (both would push women out of work, but not men) and along with cheering on DOGE layoffs, they started cheering on AI taking human jobs, because the assumption was that only girly jobs would fall under either of these categories.

Everywhere, I was called an insane, paranoid misandrist for believing anyone ever thought this, even though I saw people say it directly. (At this point, I was told that “nobody is saying that,” followed by “Only crazy people are saying that” by the exact people who were, in fact, saying that.)

But I knew I sounded crazy. As I said in my followup piece on the topic:

I know that A.) this thesis sounds almost as unhinged and farfetched as the left-leaning version of whatever Candace Owens says after two martinis and B.) left-leaning people (especially women) sour at the idea of a “sexual marketplace” existing at all. But whether you believe that sex and dating is inherently a game of power or not, it doesn’t matter—a lot of other people believe it is, and it colors their beliefs. Ergo: a lot of young right-wing men believed that DOGE layoffs (among other large-scale variables) would ring in, more or less, a male sexual revolution. By removing undeserved privileges (ie: jobs) from women, men would have more relative power and better options, while women would have fewer options, less relative power, and all would be right in the world.

And, as it turned out, federal layoffs affected slightly more men than women, because despite what SpartanLiver1488 is telling you, even if you were able to laser focus on “useless” jobs, many of those jobs would also belong to men. (Of course, it’s also possible that many worthwhile jobs were eliminated due to DOGE being a dumpster fire, but the point is, we never saw any woman-centric layoffs.)

But of course, they’re still opining on the fantasy of pushing women out of work—not re: DOGE anymore, but just…in general. I became the main character when I made the radical misandrist statement that many women like having jobs because they like making money, just like many men do. My critics couldn’t quite decide which point of mine to combat, so they settled on a variety of newfangled spins, such as the belief that all men secretly want to be stay-at-home dads but women won’t let them do it, to again regurgitating the idea that if women just stopped working, men would have their rightful jobs back, and everyone would be happier for it (except for the men, I guess, who would be yearning to stay home and raise babies.)

But anyway, all of these arguments again center on the belief that women have “fake” jobs. And yes, some women have fake jobs. I have famously been employed in fake jobs and I’ve been open about it. Some men also have fake jobs too—in fact, during my tenure at Fakey Fake Inc, plenty of my equally useless coworkers were men. If you define a fake job as a job that does not need to exist at all, yes, I agree those are real, but I disagree that only women have them, or that most women have them. If you define a fake job as a job that AI could do better, well, there’s no guarantee that this will be specific to women either. Peter Thiel himself even said that “math people” would be more impacted by AI than “word people.”

Meanwhile, the jobs sectors that are growing fastest and least likely to be replaced by AI include roles typically held by women. In fact, nurse practitioners topped the Forbes list of jobs least likely to be impacted by AI, and nursing is the occupation predicted to grow the most over the next ten years.

Of course, men can be nurses! Somewhere between 10-13% of nurses are male, and given the strong need for nurses, I’m sure hospitals aren’t turning men away on the basis of simply being too penisy. But most men do not want to work as nurse practitioners, and fantasies about pushing parasitic women out of the workplace are far more fun when you imagine these women as your incompetent team lead forcing you to share a “fun fact,” and not the middle-aged mother of three grown children who pays the bills by helping your grandfather use the bathroom.

There are, of course, other fast-growing occupations: solar and wind turbine technicians, physical therapists, occupational therapists, and other medical technicians and assistants. But I can’t help but see lists like these and conclude that everyone discussing these matters—yes, including those going to bat for hardworking blue collar men—are just really out of touch with what most working people are actually doing.

I suppose I shouldn’t be surprised. Who would be available to argue on Twitter for eight hours a day, other than white collar workers whose fingers are only calloused by furious typing? Perhaps the “useless job” call is coming from inside the house.

The US Department of Labor actually has the data on which jobs most women hold. Unsurprisingly, a huge chunk of American women are nurses (even more if you include nursing assistants and personal care aides) and teachers (more if you include teaching assistants). Yes, “manager, all other” is the third most common occupation for women but before you gloat about all the phony girlbosses, be aware that “miscellaneous manager” is actually the most common occupation for men, according to the American Institute for Boys and Men. (That link also contains a map of the most common jobs for women across all US states, and yes, almost all are in the nursing category, some in education.)

If one were to push women out of the workplace somehow (big question about the “somehow”) it would not be as simple as abolishing HR and refusing to hire women for social media marketing roles. You could remove any corporate role that seems even remotely fake, and the vast majority of working women would still be employed. As shown by DOGE, you could try your darnest to get rid of every “fake” federal job and still lay off more men than women. It’s impossible to remove women from the workplace en masse without engineering some robots who are particularly good nursing and teaching (at which point all of men’s jobs will likely be made obsolete too.) Sure, you could simply hire men for all jobs, but then you’d have to deal with how few men actually want to be nurses and elementary school teachers.

Of course, people will still argue about women taking over the workplace with their pronouns and their TikTok dances, no matter what I say. They don’t want to “fix” the birth rate, but are more interested in using the birth rate as a cudgel to oppress women (or I should say, fantasize about oppressing women). It’s going to be very hard to convince these people that most women are working in jobs that aren’t fake, nor particularly desirable for most men.

But if we wanted to identify who is working a fake job, I might start with the people who have the time to tweet all day at work.
