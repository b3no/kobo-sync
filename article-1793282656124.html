Milk, Spoons and Cups

An Anti-Natalist Anecdote

Lance Corporal Scott Bukkake
Oct 29
 
READ IN APP
 

About a month after my sister and three of her children moved in with me, I boxed up a large quantity of silverware and dishes. When you buy a new house, people will give you their old shit, and for my husband and I, this had meant more dinnerware than we actually needed. While a surplus of dishes seemed like a good thing with extra people moving into my house, counterintuitively it created a logistical nightmare for the kitchen upkeep.

When my husband and I bought our dishwasher, it was a wonderful occasion. Two people and a dishwasher is easy. Between cooking and eating while two people work full time, the load of dishes fits into the rhythm of a day. You get home, pull out the clean dishes, put in the dirty dishes and run it. Badda Bing, Bada Boom. Now, no one has to sit at the sink and hand wash dishes during their precious time away from the job. The American Dream for someone like me, a dyed in the wool chore hater.

But when we welcomed my sister and kids into our home, during summer break, there was a very apparent problem. Three people were making meals and using utensils, different ones, multiple times a day. I would come home and the sink and counter would be stacked with plates, bowls and silverware. It was no longer feasible to just load everything into the dishwasher. Now there was a dishwasher running and handwashing would need to be done if the kitchen was to remain clean and functional.

So, I boxed everything up, ensuring there was no more than a single load worth of dinnerware available. If you ran out of clean silverware? Tough Titty, do a load of dishes or clean a fork. I was applying adult logic to Teenager Problems, however. So new challenges presented themselves.

No clean cups? You can use a sauce dish or measuring cup in a pinch. No spoon for your cereal? A larger measuring spoon will suffice. No bowls? Just use a food storage container instead! If I needed a measuring cup for something I was cooking or baking, that meant it was in someone’s bedroom being sipped out of. So you issue edicts. Once a day someone has to do a load of dishes. But the teenager is a lazy creature. And they will expend as much effort to not do something than it would to just do it, just out of pride. The teenager will hold their finger up and say, “But it was not I who made all the dishes!”

So you take away their internet. They locate their Nintendo DS and use that instead. So you confiscate their devices. They sleep more and take an interest in what you happen to be watching on TV. You cook dinner, they grab a plate and a fork and eat what you cooked, but they will not take five minutes to load the machine that washes dishes for you.

Screaming matches between my sister and her children were frequent. My sister was working two jobs. A regular part time gig and her own business. If she was at my house, it was to sleep and yell at her kids to stop sucking. My sister had been living in a house with all four of them, and they had all had to handwash dishes, so you’d think the ease of finally having a dishwasher would seem like a boon. But instead it created a loophole. If it’s so easy, why don’t you just do it?

Touché.

School starting up created another loophole (But did eliminate two meals worth of dishes for two children). I tried to appeal to their empathy (which was unfortunately nonexistant because they were teenagers): Your auntie and uncle wake up every day before you, and get home from their jobs after you. Your auntie would like to get home and make dinner right away, and then everyone gets to eat at a reasonable hour and have enough time to enjoy the evening too. If you did a load of dishes, just enough to clear the counter, that makes it all possible!

Fuck you.

It’s been three years since the move.

My oldest nephew, the one child who did not move in with me has instead been living with my mom (HIS GRAMMAWWWW). He helped take care of my dad after a stroke affected his mobility until he passed away. Now he helps my mom. He cleans up after himself and does dishes, even though he’s essentially in a state of arrested development and nearing his mid-twenties with no job. In spite of that, I am jealous of this arrangement. Her kitchen is always clean.

My youngest nephew, the moment he was eighteen, left to join the marines and currently resides in California. He’s doing well, and importantly, he’s not at my house making dishes that need to be cleaned.

The second-oldest nephew works part time as a package loader and lives in my basement. He chips in for groceries and will do any task that his asked of him, but never initiates on his own. However, if he hears a loud noise he’ll usually run upstairs to make sure the dog isn’t having a seizure and knows how to handle the dog’s medications if we are not around. Good (enough) kid.

My niece, the youngest child, is a freshman in high school. She is at the most adversarial age, hormonal and angry all the time. She will only do things after her internet and devices have been confiscated. Because of this, she has gotten very good at hiding her phone. We have given up on asking her to do anything for the most part. Chores and the confiscation of her devices are her punishments for skipping school— which she doesn’t even do in a cool way! She just waits me out until I risk being late for work and sleeps! LIKE A LOSER WITH NO FRIENDS.

We have gotten control of the dish problem. For the most part. You know how I mentioned that my nephew chips in for groceries? This is not a monetary exchange. He just buys groceries. Before buying groceries, he will come upstairs from the basement for a brief consultation. “Do we need bread? Do we need eggs? Is there anything specific you want?” But most of what he buys is basically food for him. Snacks. Microwave meals.

When I get groceries, its usually a weeks worth of meals, and snacks that have to be prepared. We’d be an ingredient household. My niece and nephew also have an aversion to cooking. My nephew is, at least, honest. He says he doesn’t like the time it takes to cook something good. My niece has baked cookies for her girlfriend (then not cleaned up after herself), but that’s about it.

Recently, my nephew has been buying chocolate syrup. For about a month, there has been the constant imbibing of chocolate milk between him and his sister. My glasses and spoons have been disappearing, now. You know how milk starts to like… dry out? They’ll make the milk, drink it, set their cup down by their Minecraft Conveyance Machines… Maybe an hour passes and they both think, “Man, I could go for another glass of chocolate milk!” But their cup and the spoon to mix it are all coated in old milk… So they get a new cup and spoon. So on and so forth.

In general, dish hoarding has always been a bit of a problem. We don’t have a family dinner. A meal is cooked. It’s open to the household for about two hours, and then it gets packed away in the fridge. Everyone works odd shifts. My sister shows up and leaves at all hours. Dinner is available hot, or you can grab it from the fridge if you miss it fresh. So I have to constantly be on the two kids to bring their dishes to the kitchen. And it’s always, always, always gross, scummy milkware. Old, gross milk that has solidified by the heat of a laptop fan overnight. Perhaps the cup was tucked into a seldom-seen corner, too? Then it has turned into cement that used to be chocolate milk by the time it’s discovered. I’m tired, boss.

In my fantasy, when they eventually move out on their own… When the nephew gets his own place and when my niece goes to college… They learn. If familial shame and appeals cannot breech through their inability to clean a dish. Perhaps someone they care about discovering they live like goblins will do the job. Until then, I must fight the good fight and yell at an adult and almost-adult to bring me the pile of dishes from their bedrooms. I will gently suggest that they can run the dishwasher themselves. My nephew will probably do it. My niece will mutter something and retreat upstairs to the Minecraft Machine.
