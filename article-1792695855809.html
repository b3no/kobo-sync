“Appeasement, Ohio”

By George Saunders

Yeow, the old Ohio place. My cousin Maribeth looks about the same. Her son Kip’s a man now: big boy, around two-fifty, thick pink arms like tattooed hams.

I meet Carl, Maribeth’s third, possibly fourth, husband: nice guy, sprig of hair on the front of his head like a Kewpie, but white hair, not blond.

Also Mick, a pal of Kip’s.

“Eggs?” Carl says.

“No, thanks,” I say.

“I will,” Kip says.

“Scrambled,” Mick says.

“Unless you boys no longer believe in eggs,” Carl says. “Or chickens. Mick, still believe in chickens?”

“Yes, sir, still believe in chickens,” Mick says, maintaining discipline.

“Upstairs, in the attic?” Carl says. “Kip? A box of Civil War coat buttons, from your great-great-whoever. Plus his bayonet.”

“Anyone can make a button,” Mick says.

“Anyone can put a bayonet in an attic,” Kip says.

“Look how I trained him up,” Mick says.

I’m trying to connect the beaming giant that is Kip with the shy little guy I used to carry up to bed.

Carl sets down a plate of toast.

“Food for the brain trust,” he says.

“You’re being sarcastic,” says Mick.

“Ma’s a believer,” says Kip.

“Well, you kids make a strong case,” Maribeth says.

“For example,” says Kip, spinning his Dell around.

Headline: “Gettysburg Archaeology Proves No Battle.” Author: “Ned.” Photo: metal detector leaning against a palm tree. Nailed to the tree, a laminated sign: “Locale: Gettysburg, PA.”

“Zero palm trees in Pennsylvania,” Carl says.

“That you know of,” Kip says.

“That anyone knows of,” Carl says.

“What’s your source?” Mick says in a faux-military voice.

“Having been there many times,” Carl says.

“Have you walked literally every square foot of the state?” Mick says.

“Shit’s sake,” Carl says.

“Language,” Maribeth says. “We don’t swear in this house.”
