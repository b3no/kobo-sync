The Age Gap Take That Everyone Will Hate

A lot of age gap shaming seeks to enforce a social contract--and it's not only women who do it.

Somehow, somewhere, I earned the reputation of a “bitter old feminist” who “hates age gaps.”

This isn’t true, as you’re about to find out, but I think I know why people assume it’s true. I don’t think adults should sleep with people under the age of consent, nor should the age of consent be lowered, nor we need to “destigmatize” attraction to those under the age of consent (hugely controversial in 2026, I learned). I’ve repeatedly said that large age gap relationships in any direction weren’t the historical norm among monogamous societies for most of Western history, nor are they the norm now, nor are they the desired outcome for most young people. But that’s just factually true. Even going back to England in the 1500s, most marriages were between a woman in her mid-twenties and a man a couple years older. This pattern of a 2-4 year age gap at first marriage has remained remarkably consistent in the Western world for centuries, despite all the weird historical fiction about old-timey marriages being between a forty-five-year-old man and a sixteen-year-old girl.

The truth is, I’m actually fine with legal age gap relationships. Say, between a twenty-five-year-old and thirty-seven-year-old. Even a thirty-seven-year-old and a sixty-five-year-old, as weird and unappealing as that sounds to me. I do not believe that people with big age gaps “have nothing to talk about” given that my mom and I can talk for three hours straight despite having no new information to share.

I just don’t think these pairings that common, especially when it comes to anything serious—and if marriage comes into the picture, they are associated with an increased risk of divorce. But morally speaking, are two consenting adults doing anything wrong simply by entering into an age gap relationship? No.

And yet, some people are mad about them. Most of us are used to the women who get mad about age gap relationships between a young woman and older man. Haven’t we all heard people say, time and time again, that Leonardo DiCaprio only dates young women because “older women don’t want him?” Yeah, I’m sure older women have been fighting off Leonardo DiCaprio for decades. Anyway.

But despite the stereotype, this kneejerk reaction (and the underlying motivation) is not unique to older women, nor is it unique to women at all. In fact, almost every demographic, except for people actively seeking out age gap relationships, has a remarkably similar reason for shaming them.

The answer to why older women get upset about age gap relationships in this direction is pretty simple, and men accuse them of it all the time already. Women generally prefer men around their own age, and the pool of eligible people of any gender dwindles with age. Many men enjoy dating younger, but women don’t, so if you’re an older woman and the available men your age mostly suck, you don’t usually have the option to date younger to get around this (I’ll get to this in a bit, but it’s usually the older women who aren’t interested in younger men, not the reverse. So they have the option, but they don’t want to take it for a variety of reasons.)

So naturally, when men in your very small desired pool (say, age 40-50) are able to snag partners who are considered more attractive than you are (read: younger), it feels bad. This is also why some short men and tall women feel annoyed when short women date tall men. We want to protect our desired pool.

I will give older women the benefit of the doubt because many of these women have had unpleasant age gap relationships in their twenties with men who they felt leveraged their life experience to take advantage of them. But predatory relationships exist across all ages (one could argue that in certain age gap relationships, the younger person could be “taking advantage” of a vulnerable elder) and ultimately, this is why we have laws around these things. Perhaps very large age gaps can get a little creepy and weird (I certainly don’t find them appealing personally), but if someone is an adult, they can date who they want, and that includes people who are not ideal partners, of any age. Are there, potentially, certain traits that we see more frequently among older men who are exclusively interested in younger women? Are these men more likely to be the kind of guys who might trade these women up as soon as they get older until they are too old themselves to pull it off, a la Leonardo DiCaprio? Possibly, but younger women can (and often do!) say no to these relationships, and any sexual behavior that needs to be policed is already being policed by, well, the actual police.

All that said, we don’t hear much about the people who get angry about older woman-younger man relationships, mostly because we don’t see as many of those relationships at all, but also because you would think (at least on the face of it) that this pairing would be less threatening. Older men generally prefer younger women, so seeing women their own age pairing up with younger men shouldn’t invoke any particular emotion. And younger men shouldn’t have much to say about it either. Unlike younger women, they likely have no experience being lecherously ogled by their mothers’ friends, nor are they constantly avoiding aggressive older women at bars. You would think, at least in theory, that this pairing would earn mostly responses of “weird, but fine” or “live and let live.”

But as I’ve learned from recent coverage about a supposed increase in older woman-younger man relationships, some people are mad about this type of pairing too—not just older men, but younger men! And it all hinges on a very specific consequence narrative that exists to keep the opposite sex in check.

We like to imagine that social norms have all completely deteriorated because everyone is polyamorous and pansexual now, but that’s actually not true. We still live in a small-c conservative culture, where most people’s sexual preferences and ideal outcomes are pretty typical. The unconventional identities and experiences make it to The Cut, but most people still want normie lives.

This is not entirely bad. An obvious example is the fact that there are social penalties for infidelity. If you are friends with a couple and they divorce because one of them cheated with every person in town, you will likely have more allegiance to the person who was cheated on. Sure, sometimes it goes too far when it veers into the territory of unsubstantiated gossip apps, but in general, it’s good that as a culture we have some sense of shame and social taboo. And, as much as I know it makes me sound like a Republican, I think monogamous marriage with children is desired for most people, and while we should accept that some people aren’t into that and not pressure them (polyamorous, childfree and whatnot) it’s good that we have a taboo against infidelity or being a deadbeat parent.

And as crazy as it sounds, given the ever-increasing age of first marriage, heterosexual men and women have vested interest in making sure the other gender settles down on the younger side. Not eighteen, but likely not forty either. The more people who are available for marriage at twenty-five or thirty, the better our chances of finding a high-quality, attractive, long-term partner, which is what most people want. (The number of people who genuinely just want to play the field forever is actually pretty small.) If your objective is marriage or life partnership with the best catch you can find (as it is for most people) your motivation is to make sure that the highest number of attractive people in your desired age bracket are as open to that as you are, and available to you.

Given that most young men prefer a woman a couple years younger, and most young women prefer men a couple years older, and people generally date similarly to themselves, things usually work out without much drama! We hear a lot about the strife between the genders, but we don’t hear much about the nice twenty-four-year-old girl who meets a nice twenty-six-year-old guy on a dating app and gets married three years later.

But to keep this social norm going, there has to be a consequence for failing to adhere to it. That consequence is missing the boat. The best way to convince someone to get married at a “normal” age, to an age-appropriate person in their “league,” (which, I maintain, is usually a pretty desirable path without any social pressure) is to insist that if they don’t do this, they will regret it. Monogamous heterosexuals have to instill some sense of scarcity in their targets—if you don’t marry me, you won’t get anyone better. We may not be insane enough to ever say it to anyone in real life (protip: I do not recommend saying this to your bus driver) but if we are dating someone who believes they can get anyone, and believes they have forever to do it, we are at a direct disadvantage.

Despite the fact that we see the most age-gap shaming coming from women, that’s probably not because women enjoying shaming men more than men enjoy shaming women. It’s because most age-gap relationships involve an older man. If you zoom out beyond age gaps and into general shaming of all time-oriented romantic transgressions, men probably attempt to terrorize women just as much when it comes to “missing the boat.” See: bombarding single woman over the age of thirty (well, it used to be thirty but now it’s more like twenty-three) with memes of empty egg cartons, implying that if they are single, their standards are too high and they’re running out of time to have children. They’re not just doing this to be cruel, although that’s obviously part of it. They perceive their own sexual interests to be sabotaged by a woman who is inadequately desperate, and the older the woman is, the more desperate they believe she should be.

Plus, there is some truth to the idea that someone might actually miss the boat. Given that most young people prefer partners close in age to them, it takes more work (and more dumb luck) to find a willing, attractive, much younger partner who takes you seriously for something other than a meal ticket or novelty sex as you get older. Celebrities can find younger partners all the time, even for marriage, but celebrities are uniquely attractive, rich and powerful. Your average fifty-year-old person is not appealing to your average twenty-year-old person. And if you are a woman who wants children, there are even more legitimate reasons not to want to wait too long.

Most people who believe in some degree of socially enforced monogamy—maybe not Sharia law, but a societal expectation that most people marry their peers and then have children—do not want age gap relationships to happen where their desired gender is the older party. That means women (even some young women!) will be annoyed by age gap relationships where the man is older, and men (even some young men!) will be annoyed by age gap relationships where the woman is older. (I’m aware many of you reading this now are not bothered by either one, but I’m speaking in generalizations.)

As I’ve said, we are all very familiar with the accusations lobbed against older men dating perfectly legal, albeit head-turningly young women. No, I do not believe Al Pacino is a pedophile. No, I do not believe RFK Jr. took advantage of Olivia Nuzzi, although it’s possible he spiked her cocktail with a grizzly bear testicle extract which he sourced on his family camping trip. And honestly? I don’t believe the women saying these men are pedophiles or “taking advantage” of vulnerable women even believe it themselves.

The “problem” with older men dating younger women is that it sends the message that settling down young is not required, that a man can just play the field endlessly until he’s fifty and then find a gorgeous twenty-year-old wife—or worse yet, trade up their spouse for a newer model whenever they feel bored. Of course, very few people actually can pull this off (read: most men are not Al Pacino) but women are often particularly incensed when men do this because the narrative of “You will regret this when you’re old and nobody wants you” is required to keep this social contract going. If a man believes he can get a twenty-year-old (whether he actually can or not) he will have less motivation to be “realistic” and date a woman his own age.

For a while, I did not think men had particularly strong opinions about older women and younger men, other than occasionally saying, “Just imagine if the genders were reversed!” But as it turns out, they do, and I suspect it’s based on the same exact narrative in which older women are so invested.

The New York Times recently released a piece about how older women are apparently “in demand” by younger men. How true or new is this? I have no idea. Obviously, age gap relationships with an older woman are less common than with an older man, although I think that’s partly due to older women being very uninterested in this setup. I also do not think your average young man is specifically looking for an age gap, but for that matter, neither is your average young woman. I have seen a couple remarkable age gaps in this direction, though. I once attended a bougie kid’s birthday party with no fewer than three food trucks, where I thought a handsome male caterer was being too chummy with all the guests until I realized he was the twenty-two-year-old boyfriend of the child’s hot and rich forty-something mother. But stories aside, obviously these outcomes are pretty rare.

The aforementioned NYT interview covers quantitative data (an apparent increase in young men on dating apps exclusively seeking older women) and some interviews with young men about the reasons they prefer older women, plus older women’s testimonials about getting hit on by lots of young men. This article did not make me feel any particular way, as a married woman who would not be interested in much younger men even if I were single, but it was interesting to read.

However, a myriad of popular comments about the article on Twitter, Substack, and other places showed the same degree of bruised vitriol that older women express against Leonardo DiCaprio, usually paired alongside accusations that any single older woman is only in that predicament because she wasted her youth “competing with men in the job market,” as if husbands never die or divorce you, or as if some people (men and women) don’t have their twenties and thirties eaten up by multiple long-term relationships that…just don’t pan out. Girlboss accusations abounded, per usual, because a hypothetical woman who probably has a job was in the picture. It was extremely important that women who had, you guessed it, missed the boat, be punished for this, and not rewarded with young partners. These messages came from both older men (who claimed not to be interested in these disgusting hags anyway, so why the fury?) but even some younger men who were suspicious that the NYT was pushing an “agenda” meant to manufacture consent for procuring dry-aged pussy. Multiple pundits and commenters brought up recent films like Babygirl which star older women alongside younger men, and accused filmmakers of trying (and failing) to “indoctrinate” young men into finding these pairing hot.

First they came for Babygirl and I didn’t say anything because I hated Nicole Kidman. Then, they came for the Big Ass MILF category on PornHub.

The New York Times was also accused of being run by a cabal of middle-aged women with a particular interest in boosting their readership’s egos, or attempting to deliberately lower the birth rate by pushing relationships that are unlikely to involve procreation. (The publication is, however, only about 50% female.) And a lot of this anger wasn’t even directed specifically at older women, but at younger women, warning them not to believe this nonsense, and not to believe they the option to wait too long. The CHH pill vindicated oncemore!

The message was clear: we cannot let these women get away with ruining the revenge fantasy. Older, single women must be chastened and humbled, not flattered! We cannot let women—even currently-young women—to believe this option is even on the table! This is exactly the same narrative that attempts to punish men who date younger women! (Although to be fair, nobody accused these women of pedophilia for dating twenty-nine-year-olds.)

I concede that older woman-younger man relationships might be treated in a gentler way than the reverse in certain brands of liberal media, with an air of “you go girl” as opposed to “You sick pervert, he’s only twenty-seven years old” but this double standard framing is partially because older woman-younger man relationships are markedly less common and therefore less conventional, and therefore more compelling, or at least someone at NYT probably thinks so. There is a reason there is a TLC show called My 600 Pound Life and yet, no TLC show called My 150 Pound Life. This is not because TLC is run by a shadowy cabal of morbidly obese people seeking to normalize a fetish for their kind.

There is also the sense that, because youth and beauty are so connected for women in our society, older woman-younger man pairings feel a bit countercultural, the same way we might want to read an article about a 5’3” man accepting his height and finding happiness, but would do major eye-rolls reading the same article from a man who was 6’2”.

But let’s be real. If The New York Times and a couple romantic movies starring older women and younger men were trying to “indoctrinate” young men into being with older women, should we lob the same criticism at all the movies—countless ones, in fact—which pair men thirty years older than their female love interests, without any narrative explanation for why this would happen (such as: he’s exceptionally rich/famous.) No. We tolerate these movies and accept them as part of life, unrealistic as they may be. But the minute even two films employ this casting decision with an older woman, there must be a nefarious “agenda.”

Either way, despite how differently the media treats older women and older men, the motivation to keep our social norm going is pretty strong among the people watching and reading, and remarkably consistent regardless of gender: You should have missed the boat.

The reality is that none of us really need to employ shame to keep that social norm going, because no matter how much we hear about age gap relationships, they just aren’t the norm. I repeat this so often because I think it genuinely is dumb for people to purposefully bet on snagging a younger partner when they’re much older, but I also think it’s dumb to make a big deal of these relationships at all, in either direction. We hear about them because they are relatively unusual. They are unusual not because they’re taboo and because the feminist harridans of our society have shamed them into oblivion, but because they just aren’t the first choice for most people who have the option. But if you want a front-row view of all the challenges and perks of a scandalous age gap, don’t worry—I wrote one about being in a marriage with a (gasp) two year age gap. She was only thirty-six, you sick fuck!
