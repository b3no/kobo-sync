Are Straight Men Are the Gay Men of Straight People?

If straight men and women’s sexual appetites were the same, would their sex lives look like that of gay men?

Last week, I covered the (mostly true) stereotype that men are hornier than women. So today, I want to talk about the sex lives that straight men would have, if women had the same sexual drive and preferences as men.

Whenever the topic of male sexual behavior comes up, gay men are given as the example of what things would look like for straight men if women (and their lower drives and different preferences) weren’t there to complicate things. While the number has been overstated, 30-50% of partnered gay men are in open or monogam-ish relationships, compared with only about 2% of heterosexuals. And while I don’t want to rely on lazy stereotypes, we all know that a gay bar looks pretty different from a lesbian bar, or even a “straight” bar, for that matter.

So I sought to answer the question: are straight men the gay men of straight people? If women matched men in their sexual appetites and interests, would straight dating look exactly as it does for gay men?

The answer: mostly yes—but with one major “no.”

First off, casual sex. For a long time, I’ve asserted that heterosexual casual sex is more enjoyable for men than for women. And at least when it comes to self-reports, straight men and women both are more likely to rate married sex higher in quality and enjoyment than casual sex. From my survey asking just about the most recent sexual experience, you can see that a casual hookup with a stranger was by far the least enjoyable sex for both men and women, but women enjoyed it even less than men did, while men and women were pretty aligned on enjoying sex with a partner or spouse:

And of course, plenty of women enjoy casual sex or want to have it, but clearly men seek it out more. This could be driven partially by concerns about safety, but given the strong orgasm gap within casual sex (and the fact that testosterone probably just makes men hornier overall) I find it hard to believe the interest is anywhere near equal. Avenues for finding casual sex partners seem to be inundated with men, and any woman who takes part usually finds herself drowning in unwanted messages. Clearly, “men are more interested in casual sex than women” is not a graceless generalization! But to what degree is this difference specific to heterosexuals?

In a different study, I asked about hypothetical sexual experiences, alongside whether or not people have actually done these things. When I asked about casual sex, a clear pattern emerged: women (especially straight women) were less enthusiastic about casual sex than all men. However, gay, bisexual and straight men were all roughly equally enthusiastic about casual sex, but gay men were significantly more likely to have actually done it many times, especially compared with straight men. 61% of gay male respondents reported having casual sex many times, compared with only 19% of straight male respondents.

First, I want to address the buried lede here: lesbians had about as much casual sex as straight women but were significantly more enthusiastic about it. This is not the typical lesbian stereotype (as parodied hilariously by the SNL skit, “Cherry Grove.”) But perhaps this stereotype is just plain wrong! It’s unlikely that lesbians are having lots of casual sex—when I filtered for encounters involving two women in my “most recent sexual experiences” survey, only two were truly stranger hookups, and you can see in the chart above that lesbians aren’t having casual sex “many times,” the way gay men are. But their overall impression of the activity seems to be positive, indicating that on the occasion that lesbians have casual sex, it’s likely a better experience than it is for straight women.

Anyway, back to men. As you can see, there is virtually zero difference between straight and gay men’s interest in casual sex, but a huge delta in how often they actually get to have that kind of sex. Clearly, straight women and their relative lack of interest are the bottleneck.

But what about polyamory?

I was a bit surprised by these results. Lesbians, bi women and bi men were more interested in polyamory than gay or straight men, and straight women were the least interested. Lesbians were also the most likely to currently identify as polyamorous. But when it comes to overall interest in polyamory, gay and straight men are actually very aligned, with yet another (but smaller) delta for the likelihood of actually practicing polyamory.

Therefore, polyamory is unlikely to be preferred by gay or straight men. But the pattern continues: gay and straight men have roughly similar interest in polyamory, while straight men are half as likely to actually be polyamorous, probably driven by the fact that very few straight women find this appealing.

Now, threesomes. Guess what? MEN LIKE THREESOMES.

Again, gay and straight men are basically identical in how appealing they find threesomes, but thanks to straight women mostly finding threesomes unappealing, only 16% of straight men have had one, compared with 57% of gay men. If anything, straight women’s lack of enthusiasm is the outlier here, with most other demographics finding threesomes appealing.

But now, the one area where straight men’s desires seem to depart from gay men’s, which may explain another part of why straight men’s sex lives don’t look like gay men’s.

When it comes to the hypothetical appeal of open marriages, gay men find it more appealing for both people to have the freedom to sleep with other people, as opposed to a one-sided open marriage where only the respondent can fuck around. Meanwhile, straight men find “fair” open marriages significantly less appealing, while they find a one-sided open marriage (where they can sleep around but their wife can’t) more appealing. Straight women are unlikely to find either scenario appealing.

Clearly, straight men’s preferences align with gay men’s in basically every way (aside from, well, wanting to have sex with men.) But they depart when it comes to a partner’s fidelity. Casual sex and threesomes are just fine and dandy, but relaxing on the couch with a good movie while your wife goes to the club to get railed is just a step too far.

So, if straight women matched straight men on sexual interest, would their sex lives look like that of gay men?

Somewhat, but not exactly. Presumably, in this scenario, straight men would have more threesomes, group sex and casual sex. But that horny utopia falls apart when jealousy and territorial feelings enter the picture, and straight men are far less open to sharing partners than gay men.

What we have right now is a scenario where there is outsized demand for casual sex (from men) and then restricted supply (from women.) This isn’t because sex is something a woman gives a man, but because there are just way more men who want casual sex than women, so the women get to be the choosier sex. As a result, a woman who is open to casual sex can afford to be very picky, because this imbalance means she has lots of options. Therefore, as far as straight people are concerned, the most attractive men have the most casual sex. Arguably, “hot people have more suitors” is something that transcends sexual orientation and could apply to monogamy too, but is likely especially magnified for straight people having casual sex, given the tremendous imbalance in interest.

This imbalance isn’t quite so stark with gay men, because a higher proportion of them are open to casual sex. It’s almost as if all the straight men stayed exactly the same except for developing sexual attraction to men. Gay men have almost all the same desires as straight men with no straight women acting as a bottleneck, so as a result, they have a lot more casual sex.

As you can see here, among people under the age of forty, gay men are significantly more likely to have had more than twenty sexual partners than anyone else. Straight women and straight men are, as you would expect them to be, roughly equal.

So imagine a world where straight men and straight women are both equally DTF. Given what we already know about the true desires of straight men, both of their body counts would start looking a lot more like the “gay men” column. In this world, if you were a thirty-year-old straight man looking for a girlfriend or wife, approximately 60% of the women you met would have had twenty or more previous sexual partners, and most likely, so would you. Your future wife would likely have had several threesomes or group sex experiences, and might be entering the relationship with an interest in sleeping with other people.

At this point, I think the fantasy falls apart for straight men. They may have all the same desires and fantasies as gay men, but unlike gay men, most straight men (not all) probably find this degree of promiscuity a turnoff, and aren’t particularly eager to allow this behavior to continue once in a relationship. That’s not to say gay men are always happy in their relationships, but by virtue of all being the same gender and orientation, they at least have more alignment. Straight men want the casual sex (not just for the most attractive men, but for everyone) but don’t want to share a partner, and they don’t want a partner who gets around. The math ain’t mathin’!

(I don’t judge straight men for this. Plenty of straight women have equally implausible fantasies! My point is, even if women matched them on all sexual appetites, things likely wouldn’t go all that well.)

In this world, heterosexual men would have to adjust their feelings about having an experienced spouse if they eventually wanted to get married (as most do.) Perhaps straight men would consider this a worthwhile tradeoff for the ease of obtaining lots of casual sex and threesomes before marriage! But in order for any of this to work long-term, they would have to get over any heebie-jeebies about a partner’s dating history. And while I think that most of the time, promiscuous straight people just marry each other and it’s fine, most straight people aren’t promiscuous at all—typically because women don’t want to be. And while ranting about “body count” might be specific to incels on the Internet, I firmly believe most straight men are a bit squicked-out by a partner with an extensive sexual history that goes far beyond their own. The question is: if all straight men had a long sexual history, would they care as much about a woman’s past? I’m still not sure!

So, in order to have the sex lives of gay men, straight men would have to get over this particular hangup about body count, and perhaps start being open to sharing partners. Straight women, on the other hand, would have to change…basically everything.
