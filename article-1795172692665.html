Why Can’t Leftists Just Admit They’re Unpopular?

Sincerely: What’s wrong with being unpopular?

Jeff Maurer
Nov 20
∙
Preview

Despite an avalanche of findings that moderate Democrats performed better than progressive in the midterms, many leftists are determined to be the guy arguing that evolution is fake because there are still monkeys. Matt Bruenig and Mehdi Hasan produced statistically illiterate¹ “debunkings” that I found less convincing than the effects in a ‘50s B-movie. Leftists on social media faithfully recited the talking points spread by Bruenig and Hasan like a parrot with a coke problem. If any leftist anywhere has admitted that their views are unpopular, I haven’t seen it.

Why do they care so much? After all: This conversation is just about electability, not moral correctness. Admitting that your ideas are unpopular doesn’t mean that they’re bad. Personally, I have so many unpopular beliefs that if unpopular views were peanuts, I’d be George Washington Carver’s stool. It should be easy for someone on the far left to say “Leftists are less-popular than normie Democrats, but so what? We’re right on the merits and the public will discover that with time.” I think the fact that leftists seem constitutionally incapable of saying that tells us something...
