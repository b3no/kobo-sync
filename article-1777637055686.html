It’s officially May, which means I’m finally finished compiling the details of my annual April voyage to Paree (How insufferable can I be in one sentence? I know no limits!) Anyway, last year, I wore my usual attire and shamelessly gave Emily in Paris even though I was just wearing my usual clothes. But my usual clothes have changed a bit, and as a result so have my Paris outfits. It’s hard to pick just two photos to illustrate my “Paris makeover” (I’ll have you decide if it’s a glow-up or a downgrade) but here’s what I wore in Paris in 2025 versus 2026:

You’re about to see all my best outfits from this trip, but more importantly, all the cool French brands I learned about, plus some insane vintage finds, and even a few French brand recommendations for the men, courtesy of my husband who I’m pretty sure went into every pop-up store in the city.

The tricky thing about packing for a trip (especially when you are also traveling with multiple children) is you can’t pack that much variety, because you will probably have to repeat a bunch of items in different combination. This is especially hard for me, someone who likes to wear every color imaginable. So for this trip, I focused primarily on navy as a “base” color with pops of red (although you’ll be happy to know a red beret was not part of the equation). And of course, I did some shopping.

Instead, what I used to anchor most of my casual outfits together was the Sezane Bobby jacket in navy. This is a really different silhouette for me because it’s extremely wide-cut, but I wanted to try something new. Perhaps this is just my own neurosis, but I felt like the jacket (especially paired with relaxed-cut jeans) looked way better in movement than in static pictures. So I guess you will just have to take my word for it that it’s a good jacket! Here I paired it with the Dudley cardigan from Frankie’s Bikinis (yes, a swimsuit company makes surprisingly good 100% cotton sweaters), the Emmett jeans from Madewell, and some old high-vamp flats from Vagabond. (I know the bean-shaped crossbody isn’t necessarily the most on-trend style, but practically speaking I had my hands full too often to get away with a hobo or shoulder bag. In case you’re curious about it, you can buy it here.)

One of my preppier outfits for the trip: an old Sandro knit dress that I found on TheRealReal with my Maje trench coat and some cheap Franco Sarto burgundy flats.

By the way, a cool French brand I stumbled upon in the Marais—Maison LaBiche. Compared to most stores in Paris, this one was super open and airy, which really fit the overall vibe of “slightly upscale beach vacation clothes.” I found this really cool cotton co-ord there, which I’m definitely going to wear the hell out of this summer (It also came in white with red embroidery, which is basically begging for one of my kids to smear chocolate on it. A Word from Mr. CHH About Paris Food

Bonus! My husband wanted to write about the food in Paris but didn’t want a full article about it—so here’s a little bonus content from him:

I like food. While I wouldn’t call myself a gastronomy expert, I am weirdly fixated on everything food related, so much so that I have multiple Claude agents managing different food obsessions of mine.

I love that many of the best restaurants in Paris do a lunch prix fixe. While their dinner entrees typically run in the 30-40 euro range, for lunch you get an appetizer, main and dessert for 25-35 euro. For someone who is actively trying but is not yet on that CHH payroll, I appreciate these budget eating options. The lunch menus are limited, usually just two options per course, and maybe not as innovative or refined as their dinner menus, but 25 euro for a great three courses is still a steal. Plus, when you have kids and restaurants don’t open till 7pm for dinner, lunch is often the preferred dining out meal. That is unless you want your 5-year-old son having a meltdown at 9:30pm screaming that he is going to “grind the waiter’s soul,” for a lack of chocolate cake on the menu, to pay tribute to The Elder.

For my final Pah-ree observation; Over the last decade since I’ve been going to Paris, restaurants like Septime in the 11th arrondissement, have been at the forefront of the neo-bistro movement’s newer generation, where young, often Michelin-trained chefs serve seasonal, market-driven food that’s lighter and brighter than traditional French cuisine.

On this last two-week trip to Paris, over 50% of the restaurants we ate at were led by Asian chefs, most of the time Japanese, doing French cooking. This isn’t fusion. This is Japanese chefs doing French cooking, with a touch of Japanese influence in the dishes. This isn’t a new thing, but it is becoming more and more prominent with the cool restaurants in town.

Why does this work so well? The French are very passionate about their craft. If you go to Fermentation Generale in Paris to buy kimchi, you will taste every kimchi they have out of a barrel and talk to them for twenty minutes about fermented foods. I’m sure that sounds boring to most of you, but for someone who has agents managing agents to make sure they eat at the very best restaurants, I quite enjoy it. The Japanese are even more passionate about mastering their craft which is why I believe they gravitate toward French cooking because it’s arguably the cuisine with the most precise technique involved. Japanese cooking emphasizes precision and brightness mapping perfectly onto the newer, lighter French style. It is a marriage made in food heaven.

Until next time,

The Elder
