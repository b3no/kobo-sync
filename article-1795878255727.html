You are not allowed to enjoy GTA VI

Welcome to The Weekly Scroll!

The Weekly Scroll is a once-a-week roundup of the most interesting, strangest, and funniest stories from around the social internet.

This week, we’re examining why the online right keeps fighting losing cultural battles, why involving influencers in politics is a bad idea, and why Carly Rae Jepsen is having a moment thanks to GTA 6.

GTA 6 and the culture war

Grand Theft Auto 6 has dominated the cultural conversation for the past week, selling tens of millions of copies and receiving rave critical reviews. But not everyone is happy. Right-wing gamers have review-bombed the game on Metacritic, where professional critic scores average 99 out of 100 but user scores are hovering at 5.7 out of 10. Why are so many people upset with the game?

The Grand Theft Auto franchise has always embraced chaos and criminality in its games. You can rob liquor stores, steal cars, get into shootouts with police, terrorize civilians, and much, much more. Grand Theft Auto 6 has all the same guns, rocket launchers, and attack helicopters as previous installments and features the same style of violent missions to complete. But it also has one new feature that’s driving the online right crazy: a stable, monogamous relationship with your girlfriend.

Right-wing streamer and roach enthusiast Asmongold, after realizing the main character, Jason, was buying flowers and clothes for his girlfriend, Lucia, called the game a cuck simulator. He also said he was “simped out of my mind” while playing as Jason and that it was “actually over,” while another account suggested that the game could have made billions more if the girlfriend had been white rather than Latina. Trump-friendly Twitter aggregator Autism Capital complained that you have to do nice things for Lucia in the game, which is also why looksmaxxer Clavicular called it insanely cucked.¹

There are a lot of very, very easy jokes to make here about how incels can’t even keep a girlfriend in a video game, but beneath the surface, something interesting is happening. After all, Grand Theft Auto 6 features a white, jacked, ex-military male protagonist. He has an attractive girlfriend who is almost impossibly horny for him, and he gets to run around blowing stuff up and being a badass. How did anyone convince themselves this game is an example of progressive wokeness?

Like the right-wing freak-out about The Odyssey, this backlash exists in a strange liminal space of our culture. It’s concentrated among a set of heavily online, incel-adjacent New Right influencers and personalities. Those personalities had a moment in the sun for about a year in Trump’s second term, and it’s worth explaining why these freak-outs keep happening and why they’re losing influence.

This is not the first time something that should be conservative-coded has been trashed by the online right and ended up being celebrated primarily by liberals and independents. Whether it’s video games, The Odyssey, Bud Light, college football, or Cracker Barrel, conservatives seem addicted to getting mad at their own cultural artifacts and ceding them to the libs. Why?

I want to propose a sort of Grand Unified Theory of Conservative Culture Meltdowns:

In the conventional overreach-to-backlash cycle, a political faction pushes a cultural position too far, normal people disapprove, a backlash develops, and that faction loses influence. This describes a number of recent issues, like how the left went too far on DEI initiatives or how Trump’s immigration enforcement became unpopular.

But the online right version of this cycle has some distinctive features that mean they pick precisely the wrong fights from the start, and end up in a self-perpetuating cycle of alienation.

  1. Grievance and picking fights: These cultural battles always begin with some kind of grievance. The online right is mad about a variety of subjects — feminism, multiculturalism, woke politics, immigration, transgender people, etc. These grievances percolate through social media networks, and right-wing influencers search for examples in the broader culture.

    But the online right is, for the most part, culturally illiterate. They’re frequently unfamiliar with artistic conventions and unable to tell the difference between depiction and endorsement.² They mistake small creative decisions for evidence of an organized, ideological conspiracy against them.

    They might latch on to a transgender spokesperson for a brand of beer, a trans actor in a movie, or brown students existing at a football game. But because they’re culturally illiterate, they choose targets almost anti-strategically and get angry about things that are both conservative-coded and popular.

  2. Purity spirals: Because the online right is anti-pluralist, they can’t accept partial victories. It doesn’t matter if most Bud Light spokespeople are normie cis people; one trans spokesperson means the whole brand is woke. It doesn’t matter if college students are masculine and wearing cowboy hats and cheering on the football team; the fact that too many are brown makes it an invasion. They are cultural absolutists: Anything short of total victory is treated like a defeat.


    Social media also incentivizes outrage. The algorithm selects for performative anger and for escalation, and outrage is inherently more viral than moderation. This leads right-wing influencers to over-the-top denunciations of whichever thing is now “woke.” Real patriots won’t tolerate, um, you know, the woke redesign of Cracker Barrel. Boycotts are arranged, anger is vented online, and enjoying the offending brand or piece of media becomes a sign of ideological disloyalty.

  3. Self-isolation: Ordinary people, including normal conservatives, find the online right’s hysteria to be off-putting and ridiculous. They’d rather enjoy popular things, so they check out of these communities. The remaining online right retreats further into its bubble, distancing itself from the rest of mainstream American culture.


    And as the online right becomes alienated from the rest of America, that deepens the initial conditions that led to the outrage. A smaller, more isolated right wing feels more and more aggrieved, and as they lose touch with mainstream culture, they become more and more culturally illiterate. The cycle inevitably kicks off again when an unsuspecting brand sells Pride Month socks or casts a Black woman in an important role.

This pattern describes virtually every culture war cycle driven by the online right in the past two years. It explains why so many conservative-coded things have been denounced by the online right, and how they managed to lose so much cultural capital in such a short period of time. If you’ve been confused about why the online right keeps bashing massively successful media franchises while it retreats further into deranged nonsense like Malfoid or Anakin memes, this is why.

Let’s talk about the Carly Raenaissance

In two of GTA 6’s pivotal plot moments, the criminally underrated Carly Rae Jepsen banger “Run Away With Me” plays as the main characters bond. The song, which Paste Magazine rated as one of the 100 greatest songs of all time, did not achieve commercial success upon its release and never even charted in the United States. But thanks to its inclusion in the game, it’s being discovered by new fans, and its Spotify streaming numbers have increased to more than 20 times their pre-GTA 6 baseline. There’s a decent chance it may end up on Billboard’s Hot 100 for the first time ever, 11 years after release.

Now, something you may not realize about me is that I am, to put things lightly, fond of Carly Rae Jepsen. I’m personally thrilled that she’s getting her flowers and that new fans are discovering her music. But this kind of rediscovery isn’t new — in fact, it’s becoming more and more common thanks to music streaming platforms.

(chart from the Substack Stat Significant)

Sometimes a song re-charts because it’s a holiday classic. Sometimes it’s because a beloved singer died, like when five Dolly Parton songs charted after her death. And sometimes it’s because the song is featured in another form of hit media, like “Run Away With Me” in Grand Theft Auto 6 or Kate Bush’s “Running Up That Hill” in Stranger Things. But you’ll notice that re-charting really took off during the streaming era.

This is all possible because of how the charts are calculated now. The most important metric for the Billboard charts used to be one-time purchases of albums and songs, but today the charts mostly measure streaming popularity. That’s why a song like “Choosin’ Texas” can stay No. 1 for such a long time, and it’s why it has become much easier for older songs to re-chart. You don’t have to go out and repurchase them; you just need to switch up your Spotify playlist.

Rainbolt found an Epstein house

Trevor Rainbolt, a GeoGuessr savant who can look at a patch of dirt and tell you which part of rural Uzbekistan it came from, has located one of the houses in the Epstein files based only on a handful of details in a few grainy pictures.

“Finding the Epstein house” is actually underselling what Rainbolt has done here. He’s one of the most talented open-source researchers on the internet, and he seems to have uncovered information about how the Epstein files were put together that wasn’t yet public knowledge. The power of obsessive internet autists remains undefeated.

The Brits are mad about ‘Bomboclaat Pussywagon Chicken Nuggets’

For reasons known only to God and Andy Burnham, a few weeks ago the British government decided it would be a good idea to host a group of influencers at 10 Downing Street. Predictably, the event has blown up in its face after one of the influencers filmed a TikTok video of himself in front of the building lip-syncing the words to the nonsense song “Bomboclaat Pussywagon Chicken Nuggets.” Conservatives in the U.K. are in an uproar that the dignity of the government is under attack, and even Burnham had to backtrack and call the video “not acceptable.”

I wish I could tell you there’s some deeper meaning behind any of this, but there’s not. The “lyrics” (if they can even be called lyrics) are essentially a random collection of words strung together because they sound funny. There are some vulgarities included, but none of the phrases has any actual meaning. Audio is the dominant meme format on TikTok — usually the joke in short clips is more about whatever funny 10-second song is going viral than about what the creator is actually doing in the video. And this is just a brainrotted TikToker doing an incomprehensible meme in front of the most important building in Britain because he thought it would be funny.

In related self-destructive left-of-center news, the Democratic Party has announced that it will incorporate influencers and creators into the primary debate process during the 2028 presidential cycle. This is the type of idea so unfathomably stupid it could only have come from professional Democratic Party staffers.

I really shouldn’t have to spell out why, but just in case any of them are reading this: “Creators and influencers” are cancer. I say this as something of a “creator and influencer” myself. They are malignant narcissists. They’re unprofessional and unreliable. They are also, as a rule, erratic airheads and dopamine addicts chasing whatever idiotic trend is currently going viral. You’ll need to vet years of inane content to make sure they don’t have any past scandals about shouting slurs, committing crimes, promoting scams, or harassing people. If you make them angry for some inscrutable reason, they’ll happily stab you in the back to their legions of fans. Why would you ever deal with them a second longer than you were forced to?

Does anyone think it was a good idea for the Trump administration to turn its briefing room into Conspiracy Influencer Hour? No? I hate to be the one to break it to you, but your side’s influencers, even if they aren’t Nazis, also suck.
