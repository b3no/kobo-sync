Welcome to another Fashion Friday! Each Friday, I will cover something fashion-oriented. Sometimes fashion for men, sometimes sewing projects, sometimes just essays on how fashion intersects with social dynamics, and more! (For those of you who don’t like fashion, never fear, this is unique to Fridays, and I publish nearly every day.)

This time feels extra special to me, because I’m covering October, my favorite month for fashion. The colors are nearly universally flattering (who doesn’t look good in burgundy?) and the weather is just cold enough to give you an excuse to wear a cool jacket, but not cold enough to warrant bundling yourself up like Ali G in his 2026 movie (don’t let the image fool you, the movie is borderline-unwatchable. Sad!)

This month, I wore a lot of burgundy, a lot of vintage, and a non-zero amount of Victorian-inspired bustles. Onwards!

As indicated from the title, I got really into vintage Gunne Sax this month. Gunne Sax was a clothing brand that originated in 1967 in San Francisco, specializing in a really fun aesthetic where Summer of Love meets Edwardian meets cottagecore (you’ll know it when you see it) and you can still find it in abundance on second-hand clothing sites, although finding a good deal can be a bit harder. Also, watch out for sizing! Vintage clothes run small, but this brand seems to run really small, so always check measurements.

For a casual day out with my kids, I wore this denim Gunne Sax dress with floral trim, alongside some Miu Miu inspired moto boots from Gisele Moire, a vintage Coach shoulder/crossbody bag in teal suede, and a 1970s vintage suede jacket in chocolate brown. Arguably a little busy, but I was happy with it.

For a daytime date with my husband, I wore the same 1970s vintage suede jacket with some Abercrombie flare jeans and a second-hand Ralph Lauren bowler bag.

More Gunne Sax here for a family dinner with our kids and my mom. This Gunne Sax dress is actually all over second hand sites, so hardly a rare find, but it’s an instant favorite. I was actually debating over whether or not the bow motif Self Portrait satchel was too “precious” at my big age, but I realized it goes quite well with this dress, so I’m keeping it. Shoes are Cole Haan.

(In case any men are wondering about my husband’s outfit: Jacket is Marcel et Marcel, a brand he found in Paris, shirt is Kith, and shoes are Schmoove.)

If you told me last year that I would be wearing a giant fucking bustle I would not have believed you, but somehow I’ve gotten into them via some 1990s/2000s vintage Betsey Johnson jackets. There’s this one, which I initially thought was from the mid-2000s but was actually shown on the runway in 2010:

Runway shot. The bird on the hat! Fucking Portlandia vibes!!

I also have a similar one in black, which is worn as more of a standalone corset-style top. I wore this one with a fun all-black Goth ensemble for our family Halloween party:

The bustle!

I also wore it for another Halloween party, paired with some ridiculous wide-leg pants I sewed (details coming in my latest sewing roundup) and a velvet Simon Miller shoulder bag.

The fun jackets continue! This one is not vintage, and is in fact a Ganni jacket that I bought for myself as a birthday present a few months ago. This is way out of my usual price range, but it was all worth it to meet a friend out and have her ask me (with no shade intended whatsoever) if it was Zara or Mango.

As I’m sure you can tell, I’m really into slightly Victorian-inspired silhouettes right now, and this fine wale burgundy corduroy top I sewed last month is no exception. That’s another vintage Coach bag on my shoulder, alongside some lounge pants from the Spanish brand Oysho (found in Paris) which feel like pajama pants but from a distance, sort of resemble trousers.

This jacket is another 1970s vintage find, although I’m realizing those buttons are so difficult to fasten and un-fasten that I may need to replace them by hand. But the jacket did look cute with some linen blend trousers from Abercrombie (on sale for only $50!) and that same vintage Coach bag in teal.

My favorite bag made another appearance alongside a skirt I made out of some leftover fabric that I previously used to make my Thanksgiving dress (coming soon.) The boots are Sezane and the top is Skims.

The same boots appear here with a really fun coord set from Tyler McGillivary, which I got at their sample sale. I know I previously had a rule against clothing that looked like things, but I had to bend it for this set.

Hope you all enjoyed this monthly fashion roundup! I’ll make sure to post my monthly sewing roundup at some point in the next week or two. Share your thoughts in the comments!
