Losing Weight

How to Solve All Your Problems and Become Hot, While Committing Acts of Vengeance Against Your Enemies.

Lance Corporal Scott Bukkake
Aug 13

I have a proclivity for making friends with men who are very serious and secure in their masculinity. Two of my dearest friends are about the same age as me. They are both bald, tattooed men with big beards. They are both 6’4” and skinny. They were both, to varying degrees, in the local metal scene and are now working class dudes with families. This was not intentional. I just get along with Guys Bein’ Dudes.

In the Group Chat, a voice memo was sent: One of my longboys was upset because he was getting fat. Getting fat, for him, meant he was kind of getting a gut and drifting into That Yellow Bastard territory.

In the days that followed, after a lot of research, he discovered the culprit. Dr. Pepper. This particular gentleman had quit smoking and drinking, so the fact that a few cans of Dr. Pepper could be pushing him over his daily maintenance was particularly discouraging. He would have to quit the Dr. Pepper too, which was considered his one last hedonistic indulgence.

What was particularly notable about this to me was one of the voice notes that he left on the matter: “I didn’t even know soda made you fat!”

Then there’s my fat ass, staring at the phone with my jaw ajar with thumbs hovered over the keyboard. I must have typed some variation of “How the fuck do you not know soda makes you fat?” And I realized that some people don’t really have to think about what they put in their body beyond the likelihood of it giving them diarrheas.


Fat People Know How to Lose Weight, Usually

I’m not saying there isn’t a fair share of Tammy Slaytons out there who think a diet sodie cancels out a doughnut or five. But everyone mostly understands the concept of calories in/calories out. The less you weigh and the less you do, the less of those calories you generally need, and you can kind of tweak activity and food to get yourself to a deficit. And that deficit should make you lose fat.

While the lofty dream of maybe there is a secret third thing¹ might linger in the back of one’s mind, the rudimentary basics are well understood. When you are trying to work within a calorie budget you understand very quickly that a can of delicious Dr. Pepper, while tasty and refreshing, provides you with no benefits while essentially slotting into a space that could have easily been replaced by something more filling and nutritious.

When caloric debauchery has sent me past my usual zaftig baseline, I could usually rein things in and get back to that baseline. Not that my baseline has been particularly aspirational (One Girl’s Fat is another Girl’s Less Fat). But, I could get back to it whenever I strayed too far by making minor dietary tweaks.

I am a creature of appetite. I love meats on sticks. I love cotton candy. I love a three-spaghetti dinner and post sghetti lounge on my couch. I know how to make really good cream puffs, and that knowledge is a constant demon on my back, because I essentially have learned how to cook my own meth.

The defining obstacle in doin a weight loss is a person’s willpower to stay the course.

GLP-1, Part One

It had been about four months between visits with my in-laws. My husband’s mother and sister were suddenly very skinny. Spider-legged beneath their jeans and cheek-boned to the max. On the drive home, my husband and I both agreed they’d probably been using a GLP-1. Later, they would cop to using the drug with my husband, after he flat-out asked. He is many things, but subtle is not one of them.

My husband’s sister and mom make up two of the three people I know personally who have used GLP-1s openly. The other was my boss at the Restaurant Equipment Store. To my knowledge, he’d always been fairly big. But when I was working there, he was at his biggest. One of his friends stopped in one day and had lost a great deal of weight on tirzepatide, which he used to control his diabetes. For about two months after that, my boss pursued this miracle medicine with a dogged persistence, but his regular doctor kept denying him since his bloodwork was apparently great (in spite of his resemblance to the Mucinex monster)

My boss did eventually find a grey-market alternative, but he was also a restaurant owner who sold restaurant equipment (this was why he was fat to begin with. His whole life is literally food). And his lifestyle could not be tamed by the jab. He would eat until he was sick, then quietly suggest I take a smoke break in the warehouse so he could rip ass in the office (apparently a side-effect). He washed out of his GLP-1 journey and remains fat as fuck.

The Unstoppable Appetite and the Incredibly Stoppable Body.

Oh, Food and Me. Once, I went to a 50’s Hamburger joint for my 9th Birthday and ordered a chocolate shake. I was not aware of the sheer size that this shake would be when I ordered it, but hot damn! I love big food. I love a pie-sized cheeseburger. I love a trough of slop.

Anyway, I sucked that fuckin’ shake down so fast I made myself sick and had to go ralph about fifteen minutes afterwards, then went back to my birthday cheeseborger. Can’t Stop, Won’t Stop, baybeee.

And I have wondered what triggers this proclivity. Is there some ancient memory of famine in my DNA that compels me to shove endless shit into my mouth? Do I have some undiscovered, less retarded (more retarded?) variant of Prader-Willi syndrome? Do I need locks on my cupboards? Do I need to be handcuffed to a bed to sweat it out while Little Debbie crawls across my ceiling?

And at the same time, I hate exercise. I enjoy two things: riding my bike and taking a happy stroll. I love riding my bike at night when no one is out and about. The air is cool, my little headlamp catches the reflective eyes of critters, and I feel like I am flying. But also, no one has ever accused bicycles and strolls of being particularly intense forms of exercise. Boppin’ along on the street with a song in your heart, and riding through the bike path in the woods at night to look at possums while getting hit in the face with moths don’t actually build a beautiful body. Especially when you eat as much as I like to.

Otherwise, I am a lounger. I love being recumbent. I love a chaise lounge. I want to be fed grapes and served milkshakes on a chaise lounge. When I have milkshaked my way from my usual baseline, it is not physical activity that brings me back. It is the omission of excess calories— Less milkshakes, if you will. In fact, trying to offset my Milkshakes with physical activity just makes me hungrier.

It’s trying to defeat my baseline and dip below that weight where the skirmish becomes full-on war. Before I got married, I tried very valiantly to lose weight, and found that OMAD (One Meal a Day) was a very helpful way to cut calories. It’s actually really difficult to eat above your maintenance in one meal, and I’d found that my appetite was more in tune with what I needed versus what I wanted. But the whole being hungry the entire day was an absolute slog.

At the time, I was working in an office with enough other unrestrained fat women² that there were endless lunch deliveries, every birthday meant a box of Crumbl cookies appeared in the office, and to top it off, salespeople would drop off delicious snacks as tribute. It is very hard to not eat when there is no structure to mitigate temptations. When food came on days I was trying to have just that one meal, I usually failed.

Wagh! Wagh! I’m a big, fat failure! I will drown in my fat, greasy tears if you don’t subscribe. It is all I have left. Wagh! Wagh!

GLP-1, Part Two

In April, I tried to quit smoking and vaping (For the record, I lasted two months which is the longest I’d ever managed… WE’LL GET HER NEXT TIME, DUDES). But the initial cessation kicked my appetite into overdrive, and I found it was impossible to not keep things out of my mouth. Around this time, I had begun seeing advertisements for GLP-1 pills, and made the decision to hop on the train. If only because of a big fear I’d not be able to maintain my baseline and become a noble Obeast.

Maintaining my baseline has always been important to me. My body has settled there with little variance. Because losing weight has been a failure below that baseline, and I’m small enough to still kind of dress cool, it has always been important that I never get fatter than I already am. Beyond that point, one starts getting into melting candle body territory. You cease being a fat man or a fat woman. And just become a vague thing with little means of a tangible identity beyond the shapes of things on your big ol’ T-shirts.

The difference between the pill and the jab is that the pill is far more gradual. Side effects are lessened. You are expected to lose more slowly and lose less, based on the data. I didn’t think this was necessarily a bad thing. It allowed me to dip my toes into everything with less risk of those uncomfortable, farty side effects my ex-boss experienced. Nor did I run the risk of overshooting on a grey-market compound and skin-sacking myself in four months.

My initial dose began with a piddly 1.2-milligram pill, and every month the dosage would be approximately doubled. These early doses gave me no side effects and no tangible change in my appetite. When my dosage was upped to 4 milligrams, halfway through the bottle I got frustrated and started actually tracking calories to stay beneath my maintenance. I wasn’t consciously registering any change in behavior and the scale wasn’t moving. Only then did I notice that I actually had a clearer signal between wants and needs with food. It wasn’t crystal clear, but it was more tangible than I could ever remember it being.

The last two titration doses were that 4 milligram I mentioned before, then 9 milligrams. With calorie tracking, they shook out to an average loss of 1.5 lbs per week. That 9-milligram was the magic number, where gorging myself became an impossibility (though, snacking could be an issue). I began the actual therapeutic dosage and also hit my first short-term weight loss goal, effectively weighing less than I have since 2010. Today, my sister told me that my neck looks longer (Lance Corporal Long Neck Bukkake, over here~) and my wedding set wiggles a lot more than it used to (still fat though).

Is This Cheating?

This is generally the first line of criticism when it comes to leaning on peptides for weight loss. It’s that ceaseless finger-pointing at the word WILLPOWER. One’s weight, that number on the scale, is a powerful thing and it has the ability to make people insane.

One of my cousins, after her second child, gained some weight, as one often does. And she went on a social media diatribe about how she lost it without cheating like those Ozempic people. But, my cousin was never fat to begin with. And she never drank a milk-shake until throwing up, just to go back for seconds. I think this is the “willpower trap” for a lot of people who have relatively normal appetites. They think everyone is running the same software.

I cannot properly convey to you how foreign it feels to be on this drug. Let me remind you, I am 37 years old and have been this way about 30 of those years. I feel less like I am cheating, and more like I got a really well-needed software update. There is some program in people’s brains that, even though the cookie tastes really good, can let them have one of them and be okay not having the rest. My brain has literally never worked that way. And, just to make myself feel better, it didn’t just happen with cookies. I would take plates and plates of balanced portions multiple times if there was enough food. I just like eating, dawg.

High Status Bodies with Noble Operators

Thinness is status. If you can eke your composition past a certain body fat percentage you can don a crown of self-discipline others cannot. Luxury clothing brands rarely offer clothing in extended sizes. If you are a fat girlboss who makes enough hard cash to pay for a $3,000 Miu Miu dress? Tough titty. Their sizing stops at about a US XL (and probably not a Walmart XL). Designers don’t want fat people in their clothes, and if they do there’s a pretty big chance they’re just pandering.

Thinness is both effortless and the labor of hard work. Let’s harken back to the days where every bobbleheaded female celebrity somehow loved pizza while looking how they look. They had to be innately thin, but also relatable and fun. A guy with a poster of Sydney Sweeney probably doesn’t have a fantasy that includes her ten-minute allotment to eat 10 ounces of steamed salmon at 6 PM before a two-hour sesh with a trainer. If anyone has provided a service to that myth, it’s Rob McElhenney of Always Sunny fame:

To maintain an astounding physique, one must be propelled by an astounding level of psychosis about their body. People have been driving themselves crazy to stay thin for decades now. Almond Mom is just a nice way for people to say that one of their parents probably has a restrictive eating disorder. And let’s not try and untangle the implications with “Hot Girls have tummy troubles :(”

I think this is why most of the criticism from GLP-1 usage comes from people who are selling you something and people who have built their lives around maintaining astounding physiques. A ‘wellness coach’ on Instagram has a financial threat from a medication that reduces appetite³. A woman who can still wear the jeans she wore in high school has a direct threat to her ego.

One must suffer if they committed the sin of becoming fat. You could not sell Biggest Loser to a network if it involved a group of people maintaining a sensible calorie deficit and doing low-impact exercise. The point of the show wasn’t the losing. It was the suffering. If you mitigate the struggle, you did not pay the penance properly.

“What everyone seems to forget when the whole weight loss/will power thing comes up is that there are plenty of fat people who have done things that DO require tremendous amounts of willpower and effort. Unless you’re a complete shut in, you probably know at least one fat person who is good at something that takes a lot of work to master and yet they still remain fat.”

As much as we abhor hierarchies when they affect us, people love the ones that put them on top. Just as I enjoy being the coolest fat bitch, and the least retarded person who might have Prader-Willi Syndrome, someone I’m acquainted with might be thanking their lucky stars that they’re thinner than I am.

I am able to sew patterns into my own (mid, but passable) clothing. I am a competent enough artist that I have sold drawings and illustrations. I know how to do sculpted gel manicures that look like they came from a salon with no formal training. I have been trying to manage my weight for over two decades, and even in spite of knowing the formula I have not managed to make it work.

I look at it through a far more practical lens: I am getting to an age where my excess weight will start to cause me health problems. I would prefer to avoid those problems as long as I can. So I’m taking the pill because it helps me to achieve that far better than raw-dogging it with honor and willpower ever managed for the better part of my life. I don’t really care if that bothers anyone. It’s not about them.
