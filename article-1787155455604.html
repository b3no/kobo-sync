Memories of Woke 1.0

Lance Corporal Scott Bukkake
Aug 19
 
READ IN APP
 

The worst person you know read a screenshot from Tumblr that had been shared to Facebook and now they won’t shut up to you about racial bias at a party. You saw the same TEDTalk, too.

You’re zonked out on 2C-I, as far as you know, and you’re receptive and kinder than you’re inclined to be. But you also know that this broad lives in the suburbs with her rich parents. The prevailing feeling in your gut when you talk to any variety of these white girls at these house parties is the tacit knowledge that they’re not dissimilar to the girl from the song Common People. She’s a tourist.

You try to explain to this girl that doxxing someone who said something racist on social media isn’t advisable. You know that saying “Nigger” for clicks doesn’t mean someone deserves to have their life put at risk. You also have a rudimentary knowledge of how attention works on the internet and understand the struggle of being doxxed can be parlayed for more attention and funds. You try to explain that it’s simply something that shouldn’t be engaged with, but right now she’s all fight.

“Men are Trash.” But most of your trusted friends are actually men. Your friend who is a man has bailed you out of problems with nothing but a midnight phone call. He’s picked you up in his shitty car when you were stuck in the city after a fight left you without a ride. He brought his gun because it was a bad neighborhood. You don’t really know how you feel about guns, but you understand why he thought it was necessary and you appreciate it.

You absorb discourse about Thanksgiving being a holiday that celebrates colonizers. You wonder if teaching genocide to grade schoolers who are making hand turkeys is an advisable course of action. You shrug your shoulders and explain that most people just enjoy eating food, watching football and visiting their grandma. It’s not that deep. You know she doesn’t remember that we did learn about this stuff in school, because she wasn’t paying attention in class.

You try to tell the worst person at this party that she’s not going to change anybody’s mind. Telling people that they’re wrong about everything does not initiate a grand “Come to Woke Jesus” moment at the pit of someone’s soul. They just get angry because a girl without a job and a stupid outfit is telling them they’re wrong, but you have enough sense to not say that outright. You explain that even data doesn’t convince people. But she just wants to keep talking about all the new Theory she learned from social media.

You know that life is hard. You know that, to a point, ranking each data point about a person and weighing it against another’s has diminishing returns. And right now you’re trying to forget how hard life is, because it’s Saturday and Saturday is the day you get to take 2C-I and party. You want to sing along to the song on the stereo with your friends, and you’re trapped in a corner with a girl who says she’s an empath, yet can’t discern that you’ve been trying to escape for ten minutes… Or an hour. Your concept of time isn’t quite accurate right now.

She’s talks about slut-shaming. Its personal for her, because she has two kids out of wedlock with two different partners. The conversation drifts to how we shouldn’t judge Lady Gaga or Beyonce for their risqué clothing and how it has nothing to do with appealing to men. You try to explain that pop stars are owned by companies and nothing they do is in service of anything but the dollar. The 2C-I makes explaining this concept far more difficult verbally than it sounded in your head.

You hate this. You fundamentally agree with a lot of things she says. But she’s always a few steps off track. You could, if you weren’t on 2C-I, pluck her own biases out and play the Woke Game. You know how to do this, because you’ve seen the comment sections and how they devolve when privilege and lived experience are not properly minced, dissected and ranked according to whomever is in disagreement about the subject at hand. You don’t, because your tongue can’t stop noticing how smooth your own teeth feel.

You finally escape and stare at the wall tapestry in your buddy’s room. You maybe think about holding his tarantula, but you’re not sure if it’s gonna freak you out or not right now. You forget to mute the worst person at the party from your Facebook after you sleep it off.
