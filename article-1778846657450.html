My Best Outfits and Sewing Projects of April

My monthly fashion roundup, a bit late.

May 15

On a monthly basis, I release a roundup of my favorite fashion from the previous month. This one will be a little short, because I already covered what I wore and bought on my Paris trip, which took up half of April. But I still wanted to cover my “everyday” fashion at home, so without further ado…

Best Fits

I wore this to the park and looked a bit unnecessarily businesslike, but I had to find a use for my Aritzia standout blazer. To keep it slightly more casual, I paired it with distressed Abercrombie jeans and a fine knit J.Crew cardigan as a top. Those high vamp flats are an old Everlane model.

When I showed my husband this outfit, expecting him to think it looked really cool, he referred to it as “man-repellant.” I’m pretty sure he was just negging me, but anyway, it’s the Abercrombie Dylan midi dress with a vintage 2000s-era handbag and some Sam Edelman flats.

Similar outfit formula here. This one is a Reformation denim dress that I probably reach for more frequently than any other dress thanks to its ease, versatility and washability! I paired it with my trusty Osoi brot bag and some Vivaia flats.

This one was super comfy and practical—an oversized cropped sweater from J.Crew, the aforementioned Osoi brot bag, Abercrombie jeans, and my Weird Adidas of the Year. I’m also loving the idea of wearing a simple black cotton headband all the time, especially given that I love the look of headbands but I find structured ones extremely painful. I’m literally neurodivergent, telling me to wear a real headband is ableist.

I really loved wearing this outfit. I had actually been sitting on this Maje chenille sweater because I could never quite find a day with appropriate weather to wear it. I was planning to wait for summer and pair it with white shorts, but I quickly realized that would limit my options very easily (how many days are warm enough for shorts but cool enough for a long-sleeved chenille top?) Anyway, I stupidly didn’t consider jeans. (Bonus pic because the back of the sweater is obviously the best part.)

Technically, I’m cheating here—I didn’t actually wear this outside the home. I put it together, felt really good about it, and then my husband reminded me we were just going to the park with our kids.

Best Buys

I thrifted this really fun Vera Bradley Y2K-era shoulder bag, and it goes great with the vintage leather jacket that I bought in Paris earlier this month.

I also needed some dark brown accessories for summer, so voila! Thrifted in great condition, cheap, versatile, fantastic (something about buying Steve Madden at full price just feels…so very wrong.)

Sewing Projects

For a long time now, I’ve wanted a cobalt blue 100% cotton loungewear set, specifically with a non-longline, non-cropped, slouchy mock-neck top (think Parke-style neck). I also wanted the pants to have a relaxed but not oversized fit, and a high rise. The closest I could get was Aritzia, but it would have been fairly expensive and not precisely my vision. So naturally, I just got to sewing! I used some 100% cotton French terry from Lyrical Fabrics (I made sure to buy the matching ribbing!) and got to work! Genuinely the most versatile thing I’ve ever made.

I sewed my daughter an amazingly soft, high-quality pink T-shirt last year and she loved it, but then it got horrifically stained. I couldn’t bear to throw it out, especially since I knew it was oversized enough to last two years, so I just covered most of the stains with a patch I created from Spoonflower fabric—covered with cats, her newest favorite animal!

The best thing I made for my daughter all year is probably this two-piece set with wide-leg pants and a drop-waist top with a ruffle. The top is the Valerie Top by Clairey Lane Patterns—check her out on YouTube for the instructions and a link to the pattern!

I made these cool green gauze pants for my son, although I’m having a hard time explaining the concept of “beach pants” to a five-year-old who will most likely just be wearing Spongebob swim trunks to the beach.

My guilty pleasure for sewing is kids’ shorts, because they’re just so easy to finish and complete, and it’s really hard to make them too big or too small. I made my son two pairs of shorts, both using fabric from Lyrical Fabrics (one is a super lightweight cotton, the other is babywale corduroy.)
