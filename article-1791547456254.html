for those of you new subscribers: do not worry, you did not accidentally stumble upon a “fashion Substack.” Every month, I highlight my favorite outfits of the month and my best sewing projects of the month (because I sew a lot of my own clothes and my family’s clothes, and some of my readers look forward to this content). The people who enjoy fashion can enjoy it, and everyone else can wait a day or two for something they might like more. If you joined for the female body aesthetic survey, the male version is coming next week, and if you’re not into fashion, peruse some of my other recent works, such as the difference between “pandering to the male gaze” and simply being a straight woman wanting to look attractive, and the importance of looking like you have stuff going on so potential partners and friends don’t think you’re a loser.

Now, for the people who like fashion, read on. (My sewing projects will be covered in another post on another Friday, since this one is already pretty jam-packed.)

As you may have noticed from the August fashion roundup, I’m getting really into Damson Madder. I found this shirred Damson Madder cotton midi dress at a big Anthropologie sale for (I think) about $80 despite it now being on the official Damson Madder website for $240. I also got it in brown, and although that would have been perfect for September, I never managed to wear it. Holding out for a warm October day before it’s too late!

I wore this to a three-year-old girl’s birthday party. Those Vivaia ballet sneakers got absolutely filthy, but thank goodness they’re washable!

Another Anthropologie clearance find—this house brand black halter dress, which I paired with a vintage Coach soho shoulder bag and Vivaia mesh Mary Janes (not my first shoe choice but I was meeting up with my friend at the park so our kids could play—not an ideal place for kitten heel sandals.) Anyway, I quite like this dress, but it was much stiffer and more occasion-y than I had anticipated, and slightly too loose in the bust. Still keeping, but likely not wearing again until spring.

This jumpsuit from Balc (a French indie designer I stumbled upon in Paris this summer) has become a real MVP in my closet, in part because whenever I ask my husband what I should wear for a date night, he tells me “that jumpsuit” and I know he’s either talking about the slutty one that got me yelled at on Twitter or this one. The sun was still out for this particular date night, so I knew he meant the Balc.

This outfit was for my daughter’s birthday party, which was Western/horse themed. That’s not my usual aesthetic, but I wanted to stay on theme, and besides, I’ve seen so much Western influence on the runways courtesy of Ralph Lauren AW26!

Luckily I already had a pair of Miu Miu inspired moto boots from Gisel Moire (not exactly Western, but close enough.) You’d be shocked at how hard it is to find good moto boots like these that are not $2,000 or Steve Madden, and these were basically the only ones I could find. But so far, I really like them. The dress is a $40 Abercrombie sale buy, which is quite a bit shorter than what I like to wear, but I can see it working layered over tights or a turtleneck for cooler weather.

I wore this to the DMV to renew my ID, because I’ll be damned if I go all the way to the fucking DMV without serving cunt. Top is a basic Uniqlo baby tee, skirt is Abercrombie, and then the belt is a cool vintage piece I found at a random pop-up. The boots look deceptively like the Gisel Moire but are not and are actually a second hand pair of Aquatalias I got ages ago. They’re also waterproof, which came in handy because it started raining.

The Aquatalia boots made another appearance in this outfit, which I threw on to play with my kids outside. The jacket is an oversized funnel neck denim jacket I found from Reserved, an affordable Polish brand I found in London this summer. Pants are Abercrombie (of course) because I cannot imagine any denim fitting, feeling, or looking better. (This is not sponcon for Abercrombie, but I would not be opposed if it involved some free jeans.) These jeans are the ‘90s relaxed style.

This outfit is probably my favorite of the month. For a date night with my husband, I went for my MVP vintage soho bag, my all-time favorite Abercrombie jeans (high waisted flares they no longer sell online, booo) and two other vintage pieces—a fitted, black velvet Betsey Johnson jacket and a teal 100% silk blouse I found at a Paris vintage pop-up. Not entirely sure which decade the jacket, belt and blouse are, but I’m thinking all are likely to be ‘90s.

I wore the same jacket (along with a dress from who other than…Damson Madder) to dinner with my dad. Same Coach bag, and a pair of lace-up Vivaia boots from an old season which always feel a bit witchy to me. I’m sorry for saying “witchy,” I know that is r/femalefashionadvice 2017 behavior. But this outfit did feel a bit witchy.

For another dinner with my dad, my Aritzia Sidecar blazer (sadly out of stock) and the Enterprise skirt both in “birch.” The boots are second hand Stuart Weitzman, which I somehow snagged for $70. Bag is $15 and thrifted. I get compliments on this set whenever I wear it (I also have variations with the Standout blazer in both black and brown plaid) and honestly, I don’t get the anti-hype around Aritzia. Sure, a wardrobe that is 100% Aritzia-based will be boring, but you have no idea how many time I’ve had to say, “Actually, this is just Aritzia.”

The aforementioned Aritzia Standout blazer here over a button-down Damson Madder top, vintage Coach soho, Abercrombie ‘90s relaxed jeans and Vagabond Mary Janes. Feeling like a bit of a broken record with all my Holy Grail favorite items, but at least I know what works.

Now, the top without the blazer, because it’s really cool:

My son is really into pirates and pirates and pirate ships, which is probably what compelled me to buy the Tyler McGillivary wreckage T-shirt at their sample sale. I wore this for a casual outing with the kids, so I had to keep it practical: wide-leg Abercrombie jeans, Asics ugly-cute sneakers and a lightweight smoky purple jacket from Sessun, one of my favorite French brands.

For a casual dinner with two other families—vintage military-style denim jacket (00’s I think?) and a pair of wide leg Sezane trousers. I actually wasn’t a huge fan of this one—love the jacket, but feel like I should have paired it differently.

Close up of the jacket:

This top was another vintage pop-up find:

And to top it all off, this outfit has Stefon voice everything: Damson Madder, Abercrombie, and that crisp September sunny weather that you know is about to disappear for months!

I will feature my sewing projects in another post, but for one of the uncharacteristically warm days of the month, I was finally able to wear a blue and white striped cotton dress I sewed over the summer but never got to wear—and matching one for my daughter!
