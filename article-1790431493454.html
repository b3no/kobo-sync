The map is melting

A widening battlefield has Republicans bracing for a rough November. The party’s anxiety is no longer hidden behind closed doors.

Halina Bennet
Sep 26
∙
Paid
 
READ IN APP
 

At the end of September, the midterm battlefield is widening. States that have been safe for Republicans historically are drawing unanticipated money and attention. Republicans have been sounding the alarm for months about their chances to hold a majority in November, but the G.O.P.’s anxiety across the map is increasingly spilling into public view.

Texas is not a joke

Every public poll taken and released in September has Democratic State Representative James Talarico leading Republican Texas Attorney General Ken Paxton:

  • Texas Public Opinion Research, taken September 19 through September 22: Talarico +5

  • NPR/Marist, taken September 17 through September 20: Talarico +6

  • Barbara Jordan Public Policy Research and Survey Center at Texas Southern University, taken September 15 through September 19: Talarico +1

  • Trafalgar Group, a Republican pollster, taken September 15 through September 17: Talarico +1

  • Emerson College Polling/Nexstar Media, taken September 12 through September 14: Talarico +1

  • SoCal Strategies, taken September 12 through September 13: Talarico +5

  • ReconMR/Siena Research Institute, taken September 8 through September 11: Talarico +6

  • Telemundo/Mason-Dixon Polling & Research, taken September 8 through September 10: Talarico +3

  • InsiderAdvantage, taken September 8 through September 9: Talarico +1

  • N+ Univision News/YouGov, taken August 27 through September 4: Talarico +5

  • AARP/Fabrizio Ward/Impact Research, taken August 30 through September 1: Talarico +4

Polls are not gospel, and flipping Texas would be a tremendous feat. The last time the state elected a Democrat to the Senate was in 1988. And though every public poll has Talarico up, the margin varies depending on the pollster. That suggests uncertainty about turnout modeling, even if there is consensus on the direction of enthusiasm.

But even if polls should be read with skepticism, behind the scenes Republicans are panicked. Punchbowl News reports that Republican internal polling shows Paxton trailing Talarico and that as the G.O.P. nominee loses ground, the party is pouring additional millions into the race. Politico’s Playbook on Friday captured the mood inside the G.O.P. through an anonymous party operative in Texas who claimed that holding the seat would take a “Herculean effort” and predicted that the blowback will be “biblical,” with Paxton blaming incumbent Senator John Cornyn, who lost the primary, and President Donald Trump blaming Paxton. In the end, the operative said, Trump will deserve the blame.

“We’re gonna get crushed,” a House Republican told MS NOW of the nationwide outlook for Republicans in November. “The only question is by how much.”

Beyond the secret scramble, the G.O.P.’s cracks are showing publicly. A former Republican Texas state representative, Glenn Rogers, is featured in an ad by the pro-Talarico Lone Star Rising PAC vowing that he will never support Paxton. The former representative cited his vote to impeach Paxton on account of the Texas attorney general’s alleged corruption. “To me, character is more important than party,” Rogers said.

To keep up with the sudden inflow of cash on the Republican side, the Democrats are spending. Chuck Schumer’s Senate Majority PAC launched a new PAC, Texas Forever, specifically to boost Talarico.

Even the governor’s race is kind of close

The Marist poll that found Talarico ahead also has Democratic State Representative Gina Hinojosa leading the incumbent Governor Greg Abbott by three percentage points, 49 percent to 46 percent. But the more recent Texas Public Opinion Research poll that had Talarico up by five points found Hinojosa trailing Abbott by four.

The governor’s race, while tighter than anyone could have expected, has less leftward energy at this point.

CNN reporting identifies a combination of factors contributing to Texas Democrats’ unprecedented shot at a 21st-century victory. Shifting Latino voter preferences, data center backlash, and Republicans’ own problems with racism, high prices, and corruption are creating the perfect cocktail for Democratic success and Republican failure.

CNN gives some credit to former U.S. Representative Beto O’Rourke (something of a contemporary trailblazer in Texas Democratic politics) for his work building the Powered by People group, which recruited many of the Democrats running for state legislative seats this year.

“It’s nuts that this is happening in Texas,” O’Rourke told CNN. “But it’s happening for a reason.”

El-Sayed is building a real lead in Michigan

Unlike Texas, Michigan is a true swing state, where you’d expect Democrats to have a clear edge in a blue national political climate. Still, the race is tight, and progressive Dr. Abdul El-Sayed has faced skepticism about his alignment with the far-left wing of the Democratic Party.

But after a somewhat rocky summer, out of nine public polls taken in September, El-Sayed is leading former U.S. Representative Mike Rogers in seven:

  • co/efficient, taken September 21 through September 23: Even

  • New York Times/Siena, taken September 15 through September 22: El-Sayed +5

  • Suffolk University, taken September 16 through September 20: El-Sayed +7

  • InsiderAdvantage, taken September 16 through September 17: El-Sayed +2

  • Washington Post/Schar School, taken September 10 through September 14: El-Sayed +3

  • Emerson College Polling/Nexstar Media, taken September 12 through September 14: El-Sayed +2

  • Trafalgar Group, a Republican pollster, taken September 7 through September 9: El-Sayed +1

  • CNN/SSRS, taken August 31 through September 6: El-Sayed +3

  • Glengariff Group, taken August 31 through September 3: Rogers +2

Both the movement and range of the leads are similar to numbers from Texas, which perhaps says more about Talarico’s triumph than El-Sayed’s since the former’s race was never expected to even be close, but for a candidate whom many establishment Democrats warned would not succeed in a general election, the polling consensus in his favor is striking.

El-Sayed is gaining support from a few unlikely sources, boosting his credibility as someone able and willing to work with party centrists as he aims to gain support among both Black and moderate voters. While many Democratic candidates across the country are intentionally distancing themselves from former Vice President Kamala Harris, the Michigan Democrat is doing the opposite. He welcomed her this week to campaign on his behalf.

Another unlikely ally for El-Sayed is Tucker Carlson, who has publicly defended the candidate against attacks from his opponent. Carlson, the right-wing commentator and podcaster, left the Republican Party earlier this year over his objection to the war in Iran. He shares that objection, and a distaste for the Israeli government, with El-Sayed, the New York Times reports.

Taken together, these unlikely alliances show that El-Sayed is doing something that other progressives aren’t: building an unusually broad coalition through an energetic base and unconventional support.

The tossup in Ohio

A recent poll from Quantus Insights has former Democratic Senator Sherrod Brown and incumbent Republican Senator Jon Husted locked in a dead heat.

The race, perhaps more than others, will come down to turnout and spending. The New York Times reported on the crypto industry’s financial influence on the race, where the super PAC Fairshake plans to roll out a $30 million ad campaign against the Democrat.

In an amusing twist for Brown, 25 percent of Ohio voters reportedly believe that he is the incumbent. Unfortunately for him, the late-stage spending — the largest investment by the industry in any race this cycle — could clarify voter confusion.

Peltola is in hot water in a state known for its cold

Perhaps the hottest news of the week was about former U.S. Representative Mary Peltola, the Democratic nominee for Senate in Alaska. On Wednesday the New York Times published a profile of the candidate, whose staffers have alleged she created a hostile work environment, used slurs, and made them feed her pet chameleon. The report does not seem to be moving voters, according to the Times, and has drawn some serious discourse on X, where Hill staffers are posting in defense of the candidate, sharing their own experiences with tough bosses in Congress.

Alaska Senator Lisa Murkowski, a Republican who is not on the ballot, publicly defended Peltola after the report surfaced. She noted that Peltola faced unique pressures during her first term in Congress, when most of the alleged offenses took place, and that she had been thrown into a difficult campaign situation immediately after her special election and then was forced to cope with her husband’s death in a plane crash.

“That’s pressure that is hard for anybody to bear,” Murkowski, who previously supported Peltola for the House but is now supporting her opponent, Senator Dan Sullivan, in this race, told CNN.

In a red state, it is possible that Peltola’s churlish attitude toward Planned Parenthood — which did not endorse her — and her use of language that the left is quick to condemn might just help her chances with Republicans in November. They could also tank them. As with all races, only time will tell.

New England polling snapshot

Maine: In some tough news for Democrats, a new poll from the New York Times/Siena has Republican Senator Susan Collins leading Democrat Troy Jackson by three percentage points.

The poll was taken September 15 through 22, ending on the same day that ProPublica published its bombshell investigation into the F.B.I.’s dropped inquiry into Collins’s alleged history of illegally accepting huge donations from contractors in exchange for government contracts.

New Hampshire: New York Times/Siena also published a poll of New Hampshire’s Senate race that has U.S. Representative Chris Pappas, a Democrat, leading Republican John E. Sununu by five percentage points.

Kansas is taking everyone by surprise

Two recent polls out of Kansas show a shockingly tight race between Senator Roger Marshall, the Republican incumbent, and Adam Hamilton, a pastor who considered running as an independent before becoming the Democratic nominee.

An Emerson College Polling/Nexstar Media poll taken September 15 through 16 found Hamilton leading Marshall by two points. A Wedgewood Poll taken September 22 through 24 also has the Democrat leading by two.

Suddenly, both parties are paying attention. Politico reports that the Democrats’ main spending group, the Senate Majority PAC, is “actively thinking” about spending in Kansas and considering what role it ought to play in redder states that are looking increasingly competitive. Meanwhile, Vice President JD Vance was in Kansas last week to support the Republican.

Even as redder states grow more competitive, Trump is wading deeper into safe territory. The president will hold rallies in Durant, Okla., and Mobile, Ala., in the coming week. Neither state is competitive this cycle, so the stops, which are hosted by the Trump 47 Committee — the president’s joint fundraising operation with the Republican National Committee — might be more about fundraising and base-energizing ahead of a midterm that is forecast to be rough for his party.
