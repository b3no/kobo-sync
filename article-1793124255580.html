Makeup Is Like... Whatever.

Lance Corporal Scott Bukkake
Oct 27
 
READ IN APP
 

In the early 2010s there was what can only be described as a makeup boom. Thanks to enterprising creators on early YouTube, there was suddenly a treasure trove of savvy consumers and bona fide makeup artists showing off tricks of the trade, reviewing products and providing access to technique that might have otherwise required buying and studying a book or paying for a class.

Middling drag queens shared how they did their extravagant eye shadow and grew communities around it. The scene girls who watched those drag queens mimicked them and grew their own communities too, and soon enough there was a rampant ecosystem of women and gays of varying degrees of talent hawking 56-color eyeshadow palettes to their fans. This was also when beauty began being framed as self-expression and why in 2012, you might walk into a gas station and witness your clerk in a full-beat with falsies.

There was incentive to frame cosmetics from mere means from enhancement to a means of self-expression. The YouTuber Beauty Sphere was probably the first prominent example of The Monetized Internet. In exchange for kickbacks ranging from direct financial compensation to free lipsticks, a popular enough creator had an incentive to hawk whatever product to their legions of adoring fans. If a purple lipstick doesn’t technically look good on everyone, it behooves the person trying to sell it to convince them it does. Nothing reframes the matter better than switching the narrative from looking beautiful to expressing your creativity.

The fact of the matter, and why some people might figure cosmetics are a waste of money, is that there’s only so much you can do to polish a turd. These people can be captured by the market when you decenter the beauty aspect of it. A lot of people ask Why are women making themselves ugly with weird makeup, hair, tattoos and piercings? And these women are usually using cosmetics and accessories as a means of social signaling that isn’t dependent on traditional beauty standards. This can be a “cope” sometimes (ie. “I’m already ugly, so why not go hard”) or it can be presenting the aesthetics of a subculture that favors creativity over the appearance commercial beauty.

That doesn’t mean the functional claims of cosmetic products aren’t still duping people to this day. If you ever find the time to peruse a makeup-themed subreddit, it won’t be uncommon to find a post by a person whose unfortunate face is simply unfortunate, asking “What Can I Do?”¹ when the blush, contour and eyeliner don’t meet functional expectations. Because they do serve a functional purpose beyond spackling glitter to one’s eyelids for the hell of it. The product is still being sold as a function in tandem with a creative medium. It can make you look better than you do naturally, but there’s a limit. Hence the phrase Lipstick on a pig.

Makeup exists in this schism as a functional tool and an expressive tool that makes it a particularly easy thing to market in a predatory manner. An alt brand that offers crazy colors with provocative names like Bloody Diarrhea and Unicorn Jizz will usually carry products that provide those functional uses for enhancement as well, such as concealer and blush. At the end of the day, you can tell me you wear a shade of lipstick or eyeshadow for yourself, but when those products are used in tandem with a Kim K (failed lawyer) contour that seeks to conceal a double chin you’re existing in that schism too.

You are trying to look more beautiful. Whatever your own definition of “beautiful” might be.

Wearing Makeup For Yourself

Do you? Do you really?
Being good at makeup is hardly a flex against those who don’t really need to be. While someone might be impressed by your colorful smoky eye based on your ability to competently apply it, its not a particularly powerful social currency.

I balance on the razor’s fucking edge of self-expression and function. To look interesting, but socially acceptable. I’m not actually proud of this, but I have always felt it were a necessity. There are things I am trying to communicate and I am using every available aesthetic avenue to do so. I am presenting my best self and mitigating assumptions I find offensive… But I don’t really fuck with makeup too much these days.

I would like to say, like many others would claim, that I cut down on wearing and buying makeup because of the excessive consumption it encouraged by way of drops of limited edition eyeshadow palettes and what-not. But Honestly? HONESTLY?… I have strong features and wearing really colorful makeup kind of makes me look like a drag queen. Not that there’s anything wrong with that, but its not how I want to look. So, the “creative aspect” of applying makeup really lost a lot of novelty for me. It doesn’t serve me.

This extends to functional aspects too, but for a different reason. I don’t really fuck with contouring or overlining my lips. I can usually clock it on other people (even when well executed), and because I am not special or a savant, I figure other people can clock it too. Because it’s definitely clockable, and serves to try and restructure one’s face to look more beautiful… I would not seek to imply that I find anything wrong about my face enough to try and make it look too different in the first place. What are you? A baby? You’re sad about your nose so you draw on it? Get over yourself.

When I am wearing makeup, ultimately it is for other people. It has been for men, it has been for other women, and it has been for the general public itself. A well executed flick of eyeliner can be a novelty that appeals to a man, an assertion of status against women who can’t do eyeliner well and an assurance that the checkout clerk I am dealing with doesn’t think I am an insane person, so that they treat me nicer. It’s for me, but it’s also for all of them, so that I can reap the benefits a good image gives me.

What would doing anything aesthetic for yourself actually look like, is the bigger question. You can tell me that you wear purple lipstick for you, but this creates a cascade of follow-up questions for me. Humans have been coloring their lips in some fashion for thousands of years, and no one has actually been able to confirm the reason for why anyone started doing it in the first place.

For me, given that most modern cosmetic products seek to imbue the user with features associated with youth and health, I ultimately see them as tools of sexual signaling and social advantage. Bright eyes, full lips, dense lashes, and healthy skin.

So, lets break down this purple lipstick example: The purple Lipstick is for you. But for the sake of argument, you very carefully apply it, using your accompanying purple lip liner to ensure your mouth appears fuller and symmetrical. And then, also for the sake of argument, this application accompanies a full face of makeup, mascara, maybe even eyeliner. The end product is no less polished than someone using a traditional pink. The only subversion is the wacky color, but the overall effect is still a polished aesthetic that implies youth, health, and maybe one’s questionable music tastes.

It’s for you in hue alone. If it wasn’t about the conceivable social benefits, it would just be purple lipstick. But it’s seldom just a strange color preference when paired with those other accoutrements.

I understand this completely because cracking open a fresh duochrome eyeshadow is a thrilling experience. Some of these products are gorgeous. The colors are gorgeous, and we love them whether or not purple lips and green lids occur in nature.

But when I put that duochrome shadow on my eyes, you best bet it’s going to be arranged in a way that makes my eyes look prettier. If it can’t achieve prettier eyes, I’m chucking it in the bin.

Results Subject to User

If you ever find the time to peruse a makeup themed subreddit, it won’t be uncommon to find a post by a person whose unfortunate face is simply unfortunate, asking “What Can I Do?” when the blush, contour and eyeliner don’t meet functional expectations.

Shamefully, there was a time that I was the member of a small number of makeup shaming groups. Groups that would critique and roast bad makeup application techniques. Due to the social climate online, each of these groups had prevailing rule: Shame the makeup, not the person!

Not that I’d say I’m a pattern recognizer, but eventually all these groups would implode with in-fighting. Usually based around claims of body-shaming. Because when it comes to make-up… Be it avant garde or not, the photos being posted were of people who could be considered unattractive. Having known of plenty of women who were considered attractive in real life who had questionable makeup choices and application, I am pretty confident to say that (as with all things) when a sexy person fucks their shit up no one actually notices or cares… And we’ll find any reason to make fun of someone ugly (even if we’re not allowed to say that it’s because they’re ugly).

If you’re an attractive person with purple lipstick? Its not going to be particularly detrimental nor is it going to be an improvement. And if you love purple? Fuck it! You don’t need someone who hates purple in your life anyway. If you’re a lil Uggo?… Same thing, really. No one’s actually judging your purple lips. Not really.

You are forever chained to what you are naturally, so…

Aw, Fuck… None of This Shit Matters!

We’re all just going to get lip filler and deep-plane facelifts anyway.
