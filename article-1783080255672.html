What I Wore in Paris This June

My monthly fashion roundup, now with extra French!

Cartoons Hate Her
Jul 3
∙

READ IN APP
 

Oh no, the fashion roundup! I’ve gotten some feedback on these monthly fashion articles, where some people just really hate them, subscribed for social commentary and personal essays only, and really do not want to see them. On the flip side, I’ve had other subscribers say, “Hey, why aren’t you doing more fashion? We miss the fashion!” (I promise you the latter group of people actually does exist and is not a group of big strong manly men with tears in their eyes begging and crying like dogs.)

So far, it seems like most people don’t have strong opinions either way, but for now I will keep doing monthly fashion roundups (that’s right, monthly, maybe twice a month, it’s not that much) for the people who enjoy them. If you don’t enjoy them, rest assured there will be other content throughout the rest of the month—typically around than 15-20 articles) which will appeal to you more.

Anyway, this summer, we are spending a lot of time in Europe. Most of this time has been spent in Paris, although we just got back from visiting my husband’s relatives in Greece along with his parents and brother’s family, to celebrate his parents’ fiftieth wedding anniversary. (And, by a sheer stroke of luck, we left for Greece right when the heat wave hit Paris.) So, for fun, I thought I’d make the fashion roundup this month oriented around our trip and around (as always) some cool brands I discovered! Today will cover Paris, Greece coming soon!

My Favorite Outfits

I randomly discovered this super cool French brand called Balc in a pop-up space—lots of natural fibers (mostly cotton) and really fun designs at somewhat affordable prices (I’d say it’s somewhat more expensive than Aritzia, and in some cases on par, but much better quality and cooler maximalist designs. And it’s great to support independent artists!

These pants were a great find given that they include three of my favorite colors to wear. Flats are (per usual) Vivaia.

I also got this really cool jumpsuit from the same brand. A very fashion-forward waiter asked me if it was Zimmermann, and I was very happy to announce it was about 1/10th of the price.

Cool back details!

One of my favorite outfits on the trip so far: a homemade dress that I sewed with some brown and pastel blue polka dot cotton jersey fabric from Lyrical Fabrics (no longer sold online) alongside my Sezane raffia tote that I got second hand.

Two particularly well-loved pieces on my trip were homemade and from the same fabric—this cotton stretch poplin from Lyrical Fabrics in light yellow with dark brown polka dots. Honestly, I wish I had bought 10 yards of this, because I used up what I did buy almost immediately. I made this skirt for myself using my go-to favorite skirt pattern, and then designed a matching dress for my daughter:

And of course, I made a dress for myself too, using this pattern (I added a good 3-4 inches on the hem though, because the original pattern is so short it barely qualifies as a dress.) Here I paired it with Vivaia white mesh flats:

And then here, I paired it with brown wedge flip flops from Steve Madden and a thrifted brown woven shoulder bag:

Slightly closer up:

But wait, there’s more!

One of my closest friends was actually on the trip with us, along with her husband and children. Our daughter also happen to be close (they crowned each other “best friends” on the trip) and to top it all off…she sews?? (The perfect woman doesn’t exi—) I’m still not sure what she thinks of the Trump impression. We’ve been friends for almost two years but I’ve kept it at a minimum.

Anyway, we did a challenge to make ourselves matching dresses (same pattern, different fabric) and our daughters. Ta da!

On a random walks around the Marais, I came across a store called Comptiour Des Cotonniers which had a lot of high-quality affordable cotton clothing (mostly “basics” or casualwear) alongside some pajamas from Princesse Tam Tam, another French brand. For reference, this cotton pajama set (which totally doubles as a regular co-ord) was only about forty euro. Guest appearance from my Baggu seashell bag.

Speaking of Comptiour Des Cotonniers, I also found this really fun jacket (which honestly is a bit out of my comfort zone, given the extremely boxy fit) which I really liked, plus it was deeply on sale. Just for the purpose of trying it on, I paired it with some black wide-leg pants that I also wanted to buy, but would not pair with this jacket. I really struggle with loose-on-loose silhouettes, which I believe has more to do with my freakishly small head than anything weird about my body, but now I’m wondering if I’m just not used to it. That said, if I wore this around my husband it would definitely show up in my marital PIP.

Also, I visited the Marimekko store- I’ve never actually bought anything there but I can’t resist the bright colors and patterns, and I mostly just treat it like a museum. And what a treat, because this time they appeared to have a CHH logo themed co-ord set! You might be wondering why I didn’t buy this, and the answers are threefold: 1.) too expensive, 2.) loose-on-loose and 3.) too much white for someone with ketchup-loving young children, and who will probably be wearing it in their presence!

For a double date with our friends, I wore this yellow silky Damson Madder skirt which I absolutely love but—warning—it seemed to snag on my chair a bit, and I don’t recall sitting on a particularly spiky chair. Still a pretty skirt! The top is an eyelet tank top with a built-in bra from the French casual wear brand Oysho, the bag is a second hand Sezane number, and the shoes (do I even need to say second hand anymore or do you all know by now that I’m TheRealReal’s number one customer?) are Cult Gaia.

Another homemade item (or well, a two-piece set):


Plus, not an outfit, but I was super thirsty one day and randomly picked up this bright green soda (turns out the soda is clear and the bottle is bright green, which was both cool and made me feel less guilty about food dye.) Anyway, if you’re ever in France, pick one of these up, it was very good.

Back in America…

I feel like I should at least include the June outfits from America, less exciting as they may be.

I’ve been pairing a lot of red and blue lately, and this month was no exception with this outfit entirely made up of thrifted items (minus the Adidas)

I love this ‘90s vintage Betsey Johnson dress. This month I paired it with dark brown accessories, because as I’m sure you can tell, I love pastels with dark brown. Also, The Elder spotted:

I wore this outfit for a double date night with some friends and to watch Obsession with my husband in theaters. It was probably the first time we saw a movie in theaters together without the kids in…actually, since we had kids! The real MVP of this outfit is definitely the 2010s “vintage” Alice McCall top, which unfortunately is no longer sold online and only comes in this one color (otherwise I’d collect it in every shade of the rainbow.) The bag was another sweet second hand find, Simon Miller for just $40! (Downside, I still cannot figure out how to hold a bag non-awkwardly. Listening and learning.)

I wore this for a date night with my husband. It’s a STAUD dress that I wore in England last year but haven’t worn in nearly a year just because there aren’t too many occasions where the weather is cool enough and the dress is appropriate. But finally, it happened. The strappy shoes are from JW Anderson and have a really cool gold architectural heel but you can’t see it in the photo sadly:
