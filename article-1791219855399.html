Ira.

Lance Corporal Scott Bukkake
Oct 5
 
READ IN APP
 

This morning there were three dead mice, all decapitated, down the walkway to my backdoor. Three separate mice. They were not there yesterday. It has become so frequent that I have officially made a decree to my sister that she’s in charge of picking up the mice. I’m tired of shooing flies and wasps from their corpses and chucking them in the trash bin every morning. And the cat who kills them is hers. Ira. A fifteen-year-old tortie who couldn’t weigh five pounds.

While there were three outside, I can’t entirely be sure of how many mice Ira kills at night. Neighbors have reported that she leaves them in their yards too. Or, worse, gets them inside of our house by way of the basement window where she comes and goes if the weather is permitting, and there’s the possibility of the mice simply being inside the house to begin with. A mouse trying to live inside of my house, is basically biding time before Ira crushes its little spine between her teeth.

The dead mice inside the house are worse. In the mornings, when I’m groggy and my eyes aren’t really ready to work yet, there’s a hefty risk of stepping on a dead mouse. Most of the time they’re missing at least a head. Intact mice are rare, but far more pleasant to step on because I don’t need to hobble to the bathroom and wash blood off of my foot. I grab the paper towel. I scoop it up. I walk it outside. Dead animals shouldn’t go in the inside trash unless you were going to take it out anyway.

Ira is fifteen years old. I don’t even think she weighs even five pounds. The only time she will let you pet her is if she is outside. The best you’ll get from her when she’s inside is that she’ll sit on your lap if she’s cold. If you touch her, she’ll leave. Fuck you. But if you’re outside and she’s outside, she’ll roll around on her back and chirp at you. I don’t understand why the setting makes it okay, but that’s just how it works. Cats have weird rules.

A friend of mine once told me that cats must think we’re god and that dead animals are a tribute to convey how much they love us.

Ira catches birds live and brings them inside— If I let her in at the door without noticing, or she comes in from the basement window, she makes adorable mewling noises which I have learned mean she definitely has a live bird. It’s the only time she makes noises like that. Her destination with every live bird is my niece’s bedroom. My niece is also fifteen and verifiably less useful than Ira. Ira can at least boast that she controls vermin. My niece is mostly good at forgetting cereal bowls in her room until uneaten Cinnamon Toast Crunch becomes concrete, and playing Minecraft. Ira does this with mice too, but the birds seem to come into the house preserved more often.

It’s better at this point, if the bird dies. Trying to get a live bird out of your house is a pain in the ass, and apparently when cats catch things, the unfortunate victims can get sepsis pretty easily from the cat’s gross butthole licking lips. Dying in Ira’s jaws would honestly be more of a mercy at this point. But Ira is intent to get it to my niece. Not as tribute, mind you…

When I was my niece’s age, we had two cats. My mom has never made a cat stay inside in her entire life, in spite of multiple catching FIV and dying early deaths that all made her cry. Hugo and Pope. They were brothers. And they were killers too.

Every summer, unrestrained from the power of not having to wake up for school, I would go nocturnal. Nocturnal Life was my favorite life and always has been. Staying up from afternoon to sunrise also rewarded me with a unique experience: Frequently, Hugo and Pope would stand on the front porch and meow with either a live chipmunk or baby rabbit (it was always baby rabbits) between them. When I opened the door, they would not want to come inside. If they unfortunate catch tried to escape, one of them would corral it back and stare up expectantly at me with their pupils blown wide and black. That excited murder-stare cats get.

The two cats were trying to teach me how to kill.

Cats don’t understand that the hands which open the cans pate and gravy they love so much do not require a skill for killing small rodents. The two acts are completely separate things. To them, murder is simply a necessary skill someone they love should have. When they don’t see the human being they love killing something, they feel duty-bound to make sure you know how.

I find that very comforting, in spite of the carnage. That something can love you so much that they’re invested in ensuring your survival, even if it’s only within a context of their own limited understanding.

Love you, Ira.
Even though you’re kind of mean.
