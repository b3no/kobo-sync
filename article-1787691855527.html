Reaction to the CA/US Trade Breakdown: A Little Nuance

Canadians aren’t happy, they’re resolved.

Kyla Ronellenfitsch
Aug 25
 
READ IN APP
 

This weekend is constituted as a BFD – politically, economically, historically. The Prime Minister’s announcement that Canada is withdrawing from trade talks with the US has woken Canadians out of their summer slumber. According to Google Trends, recent events are on track to be the PM's 2nd biggest political event in 2026, after the Davos speech.

I’m sure we’ll see a deluge of polls in the coming days about how Canadians are responding. We’ve already seen some numbers from the Angus Reid Institute showing 76% of Canadians think Carney was right to back out of trade talks. 3 in 4 might feel like carte blanche territory, a signal that things are definitively on the right track. But that’s not a full read.

Instead of hopping into the field with a survey, I decided to gather qualitative insights. I wanted to understand the texture of opinion, not just a thumbs-up/thumbs-down. I conducted 48 interviews using Relay’s proprietary Text Snapshot methodology, which lets us do qualitative research via text message. The study was launched within a couple of hours of the PM’s press conference on Saturday and wrapped up last night to capture opinions as they gelled. Participants were asked about their feelings about life in Canada, awareness of and reaction to the collapsed trade negotiations with the US, impression of how Pierre Poilievre might have handled these negotiations, and what advice they would give the PM if they had a chance.

Often, *exhales* still, we talk about Canadian voters as if there are two kinds: cost-of-living voters and anti-Trump voters. These interviews make it very clear that these two apparently distinct voters are often one and the same. They’re cross-pressured.

About 2 in 3 participants supported the Prime Minister’s decision, most of whom strongly support it and see it as an assertion of Canadian autonomy and a source of national pride.

  • Strongly support: 27 respondents

  • Lean support: 4 respondents

  • Neutral/Unsure: 6 respondents

  • Lean opposed: 3 respondents

  • Strongly opposed: 8 respondents

This shouldn’t be read as joy or “a win.” It’s resolve. Because at the same time that many of these respondents expressed support for the government’s handling of the Trump administration, they also expressed significant concern about how things are going in the country currently. Some of this is because of healthcare. Some of it is about politics. Most of it is about the cost of living, particularly the cost of food.

In this context, Carney telling Trump to shove it isn’t exactly a cause for celebration. It signals that things that are already hard are going to get harder, but there is no other choice. There’s also a significant stream of righteousness – the feeling that Canada is standing up to a bully (or worse).

Participants’ advice to the PM was really demonstrative of how they’re feeling in this moment:

  • I would tell him that I’m proud of him and feel that he’s the right man to be PM in the absurd situation in which our country finds itself. The whole thing about Trump being the head of the most powerful country in the world is insane, as is the man himself. - Woman, 78, LPC Voter

  • To keep calm and grind away. - Man, 39, LPC Voter

  • I would tell him to stand firm and be transparent with the Canadian people and the provinces to keep everyone on the same side. - Man, 78, LPC Voter

  • I would tell him to actually look at an average grocery receipt before making these decisions. Stop worrying about political standoffs with the Americans and start worrying about what regular people can actually afford. My advice is to make a deal that protects our cost of living, because everyday Canadians can’t afford to keep paying the price for these trade wars - Man, 25, LPC Voter

  • Do what’s best for Canadians and do not give in, we are a strong country and we have enough resources to be able to stand on our own - Woman, 45, CPC Voter

  • Continue to be firm and patient, we are not strong enough to withstand a powerful US country but should tariff the items that the US needs very badly. Hopefully US citizens will put pressure on their own leaders - Man, 54, CPC Voter

Of course, not every respondent followed this pattern. Conservative voters in this sample were almost evenly divided. The most common reasons for opposing the PM’s decision include lack of trust in the PM/Liberals, assumption of political benefit, and fear of the economic toll.

  • I’ve only heard what you just mentioned but do not believe anything coming from this government. If the negotiations failed I believe that there is much more to the story than what we are being told. - Man, 77, CPC voter

  • I heard some, first there was a deal, then there wasn’t. Honestly, I think Carney suspects an election is looking and he pulled out of negotiations to have Trump to blame the economy on in the event there is an election - Woman, 55, CPC voter

  • It’s a bad idea our economy has taken a toll. It was wrong we need to trade with the us to stabilize our economy. - Man, 32, CPC voter

Politics is all about contrasts. How do voters think Poilievre would have fared if he were Prime Minister? 30/48 participants feel like Canada would be in a worse position.

  • P.P would have done nothing for a trade agreement, Because he is incapable of doing anything for Canada, all he has ever done is live off the backs of the taxpayer for the last 24 years! - Man, 75, NDP Voter

  • No, I don’t see him as a strong leader and I think he is not able to stand up to trump . PP would have given away everything and laid the groundwork for Canada to become the 51st state. The man scares me. - Woman, 78, LPC Voter

  • Pierre won’t even get his security clearance. He’s never had another job besides this one and he can barely do that. It would be horrendous - Woman, 57, LPC Voter

9/48 respondents felt like Canada would be better positioned. For context, 16 of the respondents in this study were Conservative voters.

  • Much better | He is for canadiens not American interests - Man, 41, CPC Voter

  • So far, from what I have seen Pierre Poilievre seems to focus on the people. Do I think he could have done a better job, I would have to say yes. No we would have been in a way better spot. - Woman, 56, CPC Voter

  • I think conservatives would have accepted but on different terms, Pierre is more of a risk taker and willing to gamble for a better future, alcohol would be on shelves for sure! - Woman, 56, CPC Voter

But an equal number (9) felt like there would have been no difference either way.

  • I honestly think neither would do a good job, the problem is Trump. I think whoever in Carney’s position it’d be the same outcome. - Woman, 27, NDP Voter

  • Probably about the same. Poilievre may have taken a different approach, but the U.S. demands were the main obstacle, so I don’t think Canada would necessarily be in a significantly better position. I think we probably would’ve ended up in a pretty similar spot. Poilievre might have handled the negotiations differently, but I don’t think that necessarily would’ve resulted in a better deal for Canada. - Man, 23, CPC Voter

This data points to why survey questions, especially forced-choice ones, can be incomplete. They create a story that’s far too tidy. Here we find Canadians, not necessarily in conflict with themselves, but having two sources of anxiety, which at times overlap. They’re worried about what life costs today, and what Trump could cost us (in every sense) tomorrow. The misread is to feel like Carney is just crushing it - that voters are over-the-moon happy. It’s not really happiness. It’s something like faith. Or confidence. Or hope. A guide through the storm.
