Where Are All The Middle-Aged Tradwives?

Clue one: nobody is "hiding" them.

Cartoons Hate Her
Jun 25
∙
Paid
 
READ IN APP
 

This week, writer Jessica Valenti took to social media to ask: where are all the 47-year-old tradwives? Her answer? They’re all living on the streets LIKE DOGS because they’ve been traded up for newer models and, without a career on which to fall back, were left destitute. Take that, Ballerina Farm! Mic drop.

The Internet replied in basically two ways: either confirming that tradwives are gullible losers (who presumably deserve everything bad that happens to them) and are usually if not always abandoned by their husbands in middle age, or that all the older tradwives are living such joyous, perfect lives that they don’t have “time” to talk about it online. (Get into some immodest-bikini-at-the-splash pad discourse on Twitter, however, and suddenly the free time materializes!)

In order to understand who is correct here (and spoiler: it’s neither) we should understand what a tradwife is. I don’t think Valenti actually knows (heck, I don’t think anyone knows) because if your definition of a “tradwife” is someone who makes tradwife content, and calls themselves a tradwife, then a lot of tradwives are basically actresses playing a role, and the easy answer for why there aren’t any older ones is because younger actresses get more work. Would so many people tune into Ballerina Farm’s content if she was dowdy and over fifty? I’m sure some people would, but she wouldn’t be as popular. For that matter, where are all the happy childless, single, fifty-year-old ladies of NYC? I haven’t seen a single popular influencer who fit this profile—only hot and young ones, curiously!—therefore, their mature counterparts must all be wearing grippy socks in the psych ward and stroking animatronic cats, right?

But I don’t think that’s what Valenti means. She is too smart to believe that there is no good explanation for the dearth of older tradwife influencers. I think she means “conservative stay-at-home moms who did not prioritize a career.” And it’s odd to so confidently declare that “they” are “hiding” these women (Mitt Romney’s “binders full of women” line comes to mind) because there is plenty of data available about them, and how often they are actually being abandoned.

Before I dig into the data, let me remind you that “divorce” doesn’t necessarily mean “man abandoning his wife.” In fact, it usually doesn’t mean that. A woman who gets divorced is more likely to be the one who initiated it, and while her husband’s infidelity is sometimes the cause, this is not a universal rule (infidelity is a common cause for divorces, but these days men and women cheat at fairly similar rates, so we can’t assume this is unique to men.) In any case, most data about divorce doesn’t back up the idea that “tradwives are all left on their asses for a newer model,” but it’s messy, because the majority of divorces are initiated by women. Seeing that a particular factor is associated with increased divorce risk does not mean “men are more likely to leave women who do this.”

Marrying younger (and by “younger” I mean very young, often early twenties or late teens) significantly increases divorce risk, with no specifics about whether men or women are more likely to pull the trigger. While some divorced couples specifically site age at marriage as the biggest contributor to divorce, this finding is more about correlation than causation. Divorce is also associated with financial instability, and while marriage is increasingly becoming a privilege for the upper classes, the people who marry under the age of twenty-two tend not to be financially stable. And when a divorce is initiated after the marriage of two eighteen-year-olds, when is that divorce happening? I’m sure it varies, but many of these marriages may end just a few years later, not necessarily decades into the future when the wife “expires.” (The average length of a first marriage that ends in divorce is just eight years.)

But if I had to guess, Jessica Valenti was more interested in the wife’s working status, not age at marriage, when she used the label “tradwives.” So…is it true that a woman who doesn’t work outside the home is more likely to be abandoned by her husband for a newer model than any other married woman? The answer isn’t complicated. The answer is “no.”

Employment is relevant when it comes to divorce risk, but there is no correlation with a man being more likely to leave his wife based on her career status in either direction. According to a 2011 observational, longitudinal study by the American Journal of Sociology, a woman’s career can affect the likelihood that she will leave (because presumably such a backup plan softens her landing) but her career or lack thereof has zero effect on whether or not her husband will leave. Instead, his own career status can influence divorce from both parties. A man who is out of work, or who makes far less than his wife, is more likely to get divorced, and not only because his wife left him—he’s also more likely to initiate divorce himself, even if he doesn’t cite marital instability! Perhaps in those cases, it’s a hit to his masculinity that reduces attraction to his wife, or maybe all that spare time leaves more opportunities for scrolling Tinder.

But either way, none of this has anything to do with “tradwives.”

You can make a feminist argument that men shouldn’t be threatened by an economic role-reversal, but you cannot look at this data and say that women put themselves at a higher risk of divorce by being stay-at-home moms. What you can say is that being a “tradwife” (not in the influencer sense, because that is a career, but in the “homemaker as only career” sense) puts you at a higher financial risk if you were to get divorced because you will have no safety net outside of alimony and perhaps, helpful relatives. It is genuinely very hard to start a career at the age of forty-five, especially if you have no degree and/or no career history.

I also believe tradwife propaganda—not to be confused with actual women who happen to be doing “tradwife” things—is centralized around a desire to limit women’s options so that they become “collateral,” not partners. When I wrote about this phenomenon, the argument was not that staying home with children made abandonment more likely, but that a certain echelon of right-wing edgelord wannabe-patriarch does not actually value domesticity in a woman and rather, wants a live-in maid without her own options so he can gallivant around with a bunch of Eva Brauns with lip filler. But even in that article, I pointed out that this was not how most religious or conservative men behave.

But the fact that “tradwives” are taking a financial risk in the event of divorce by deprioritizing a career doesn’t mean the outcome of middle-age abandonment is likely to happen, let alone the most common outcome. There is a word of warning—perhaps something we’d like to share with the viral 19-year-old self-described tradwife who smugly bragged about not having a degree and marrying her much older husband after just a few months of dating (will she definitely have a bad outcome? No, and I hope not! Just… there are red flags!) But a word of warning need not be the same as a revenge fantasy.

Throughout America, there are millions of conservative, religious stay-at-home moms in their forties and beyond who are still married and whose husbands will not trade them up for an eighteen-year-old (never taken into account is whether not an eighteen-year-old would even want these guys.) Are they all living heavenly lives of domestic bliss? Probably not, because most people are not happy all the time.

You could easily make the same conclusions about “childless cat ladies.” I have no doubt that there are plenty of single and childless-by-choice older women who are not living sad, gray lives of regret. Perhaps some are, but many aren’t. There is probably a lot of nuance—maybe they wish they had met the right person, but are happier single than with the wrong person. Maybe they wanted kids but were fine without them, or maybe they never wanted them. Maybe they’re totally satisfied with all their choices. I don’t know! But it would be just as untrue and ridiculous to say that “you never see happy middle-aged childless single women.” Where would you expect to “see” them if they aren’t part of your social circle, and if you live in a place where this type of person—happy or not—is uncommon? It would be like asking, “Where are all the Mongolian people? Clearly they’re being hidden away somewhere,” while I’m standing in the middle of Idaho farmland.

Older women in general are not prominent influencers. That doesn’t mean anyone is “hiding” them. And at least with regard to middle-aged Catholic moms I can tell you where they’re hiding: Substack.

Other people’s misfortune (imagined or real) is a double edged sword. It can be a prompt for empathy, or for a big fat “haw haw.” Look no further than conversations around SSRI use and depression. Right-wing men online often jeer at older women using SSRIs (a commonly-prescribed class of antidepressants) at higher rates than men or younger women because presumably, this is a “gotcha” that proves these women should have gotten married and had kids. Never mind that married people, including married women, are actually more likely to use SSRIs than those who aren’t (not for any scandalous reason either- probably a boring socioeconomic class correlation) or that older women are, curiously, the only group experiencing menopause. And these same men will point toward men’s increased rates of suicide as evidence that men are oppressed, presumably by the very women whose depression is meant to be funny. The women’s depression says something about their own “bad choices” (which isn’t even backed up by the data of who uses SSRIs) meanwhile, men’s depression says something about an unfair society rigged against men.

The same argument is frequently applied to tradwives of the past. The irony is not lost on me that many of the outspoken feminist writers who believe that antidepressants are wonderful inventions that saved their lives due to unpreventable “chemical imbalances in their brains” will leverage the use of “pills” as evidence that 1950s housewives were miserable—decidedly not because of these “chemical imbalances” but because they were living objectively horrible lives. When people ask how we know 1950s housewives were so unhappy with their lives, these women will immediately clap back, “they were all on pills.” Girl, so are you.

Perhaps the biggest predictor for whether a woman—or anyone—will be happy with their lives is whether or not they are living a life they wanted for themselves. I am reminded of a woman I met at a children’s museum who was happy to stop at one child and told me she loved going into the office because she got restless at home with her son. This woman was about as different from me as you can get, assuming you aren’t including groypers, tankies, and people who enjoy Greek yogurt outside of a cucumber soup formulation in the equation. And yet, I’m sure she was very happy with a life that would have had me overcome with anxiety—I vowed never to work in an office again after having my first child. And the feeling was mutual! When she discovered I am home with both kids all summer, with no day camp and no nanny, she blurted out, “I would go crazy.” But this was the life I chose for myself, it’s the life I enjoy, and even if I am crazy it’s unrelated to time spent with my children. And what better example of feminism is there than two women living different lives, each happy with what they’ve chosen, each with the ability to choose it?

That’s not to say that “every choice a woman makes is feminist,” because I don’t actually care if my choices are feminist and I doubt “tradwives” care either. Only that the true predictor of happiness and stability is that our life choices are made with free will and reflect what we enjoy. And I have no doubt that among “tradwives” and “cat ladies,” many are perfectly happy with what they’ve chosen, while others would have preferred another path. And while that’s not nearly as exciting as “all the tradwives will be destitute after forty,” it’s the truth.
