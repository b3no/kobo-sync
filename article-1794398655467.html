Kyla Ronellenfitsch on Canada's Breakup With America

The Ottawa pollster on Trump, Carney, and fresh ways of asking Canadians about all of it

Paul Wells
Nov 11
 
READ IN APP
 

Kyla Ronellenfitsch runs Relay Strategies, a relatively recent arrival in Canadian public-opinion research. I’ve found she consistently finds fresh new angles on our big debates, and the podcast she co-hosts, Culture Lab, is solid new content for political animals.

So I asked her to come on my own podcast to talk about her research and what she’s seeing in public opinion lately.

First, stop worrying about the long name. It’s Roan ELLEN Fitch. Easy to say. With that out of the way, it’s a great conversation. Kyla begins by talking a bit about how you become a pollster, or how she did. She discusses some of her methodological quirks. She combines conventional polling with an unusual technique: one-on-one interviews by text message, designed to discover not just what Canadians think, but why. I’ll let her tell you more.

Then we discuss some of what she’s finding lately. (This interview was recorded before I travelled to Europe for two weeks, so there are no cutting-edge revelations here. But the trends we discuss have been robust over the last year or so.)

Ronellenfitsch’s findings suggest many Canadians no longer see Trump as a temporary disruption. They think something has gone seriously wrong with American democracy. They’re not at all sure a different president would change much.

Partly because of that, a lot of Canadians are willing to cut Mark Carney a lot of slack. But there is one warning in the data for Carney: admiration for his international leadership won’t erase concern, and impatience, about housing, groceries and the cost of living.

The whole talk was such a blast that I’ve invited Kyla Ronellenfitsch to come present more of her research to the audience at my Holiday Show in December. Tickets go on sale to paid subscribers on Thursday; more about that here.
