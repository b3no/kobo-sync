Bedbugs

IT COULD HAPPEN TO YOU.

Lance Corporal Scott Bukkake
Aug 18
 
READ IN APP
 

The night I broke up with my Bad Boyfriend, I opted to sleep on the couch. I am a short person, and in my attempt to be magnanimous I took the L on sleeping arrangements. And if I’m being honest if I had wanted to sleep in our bed alone, he would have fought me on that and I didn’t have much fight left in me.

I had no where to go. My sister, her husband and four children had all moved into my parent’s house and there was basically no space left for an additional person with things and stuff. Until a place to stay could be secured, we opted to cohabitate. So, I slept on the couch. Or, at least I tried to. Shortly after settling with my little blanket and pillow, I made a discovery lurking inside the cushions of our couch that no person ever wants to make: Our apartment had bedbugs.

Bed bugs are a disease you get from your boyfriend’s weed dealer, or by staying at Great Wolf Lodge.

In my case, I’m pretty sure it was his weed dealer¹, but I can never truly be sure. Our apartment, which I chose exclusively because it was cheap and I could build rental history with it easily, housed old people and pill-heads exclusively. And the pill-heads are definitely in the ‘Getting Bed Bugs’ demographic. We had one bedroom, a kitchenette, a small bathroom and a little living room space.

The place had already felt like a prison once my very good job let me go, and my Bad Boyfriend could never really manage to pick up the slack. So we were always struggling.

When I did get a new job, it was not as good as the last one. I had always been the one making more money and suddenly he was the breadwinner. I was more financially literate than him and had been the one with an actual credit score, but we were relying on his check for the bulk of bills and he could not manage money to save his life. Instead of remembering we had bills and a budget he was blowing funds on weed, video games and for some reason knives and swords. It became clear we were never leaving this one-bedroom apartment because no money could be saved.

When we discovered the bedbugs I was quite literally stuck even if there might have been options to simply couch surf or stay with a friend. Bedbugs can travel on clothing. I was radioactive. Any excursion somewhere involved pulling my shoes out of the freezer and checking the seams of my purse and clothing. Giving someone else an infestation of bed bugs would have made me want to end my life. All I wanted to do was abscond into the arms of my mother and sister to lick my wounds, but now I was stuck as roommates with a fat idiot in Bedbug prison.

Losing Everything

A few months before, in some weird attempt to make my apartment feel more like home. I had moved my beautiful, wooden antique bedroom set (That I’d had since I was a child) into our apartment. Apartments suck because you can never really make the place yours without risking your security deposit. You can’t replace the carpet, you just gotta get a shitty rug. If you’re even allowed to paint your space, it’ll have to be whitewashed before you leave. Anchoring anything on the walls is a recipe for disaster. I was never escaping, so I may as well have nice furniture, right?

Bedbugs like to live in wood.

After weeks of fighting with the property management company who owned the building, we finally got them to send over an exterminator. Unfortunately they were only willing to shell out the money for chemical treatments. That’s another problem with bedbugs. The best way to kill them is with heat treatment. Just as the body will raise its temperature to destroy an infection, you must do the same with your domicile to destroy the bedbug.

Otherwise, they’re hardy fucking things. If there’s no blood to suck? They go dormant. They can survive cold longer than you think. The eggs they lay can survive chemical treatments and are glued into hard to locate places. Months can pass and they will emerge, ready to start the cycle again. A proper heat treatment, kills living bugs, and is one of the few things that can destroy their eggs. A chemical treatment has to be performed multiple times to cover generations to come, and meanwhile you’re still in bed getting snacked on…

The other concern was that chemical treatment would merely push the bugs through the thin walls of our apartment building to other occupants, only to send them back again².

Thankfully, my sisters husband (who is crazy) was kicked out of the house for being crazy. And I was finally able to escape my shitty apartment… After processing some of my clothes in the high heat dryers of a laundromat. No furniture. No electronics. No books. Not even all my clothing.

Bedbug PTSD

I remove my arm hair now. I don’t have problematic Robin Williams fur on them (at least I don’t think I do…). It’s just that a slight breeze through the little wispy strands gives me a brief and intense anxiety, because it feels like bugs crawling on me.

This shit happened a decade ago, and a fallen hair slipping down my back still makes me freak out. When someone tells me they picked up a piece of used furniture from Facebook marketplace, I get palpitations on their behalf.

The worst part about an infestation is the social stigma of havin’ bugs. There’s always an implication of personal filth. But bedbugs don’t care how clean or dirty your domicile is³. They are flat little creatures that can tuck themselves in the cracks of your baseboards, and between the heavy seams of your mattress. They come out at night when you’re asleep to feed on you, and go back to hiding where your eyes don’t see them. Your vacuum cleaner cannot suck their eggs up. By the time you see a bedbug, you have many, many bedbugs.

And when you know you have many, many bedbugs, laying down to sleep at night becomes something else. And even after you escape the bugs… Every itch. Every tickle. Every breeze that brushes through your arm hair. Those sensations become signals of an unseen enemy living in your walls and trigger a visceral reaction that persists through the years. When the dry skin on your elbow feels itchy at night, you can’t help but wonder… What if they’ve come back?⁴
