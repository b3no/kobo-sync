Cool Outfits and Trends I Spotted in Paris and London

Some of the outfits that caught my eye on our summer in Europe

My summer in Europe has come to a close (I am sad, but very excited to drink Spindrift again.) I spent most of my time in Paris, with a trip to Greece to see family, another one to London to see different family, and a variety of shorter day trips around France just for fun. If you’re curious about what I wore on these trips, here’s my roundup for Paris in July and here’s my roundup for Greece.

But today is about other people. I can’t confirm that everyone pictured below actually is French, but given that many fashionable people congregate in Paris, I still enjoyed looking at their clothes. This is not a “how to dress like a French girl” article, because I don’t find that goal particularly helpful or even concrete (French women all dress differently, and not every woman in Paris is French or even lives there!) I also had no plans to uncover particularly edgy or high-fashion trends (honestly, I did not see much of that anyway) but rather things that most people could find at a reasonable price and wear.

Anyway, without further ado, these are some trends I observed, plus some miscellaneous cool outfits, and some shopping suggestions (with zero affiliate links):

Ethereal White Dresses and Skirts

I saw a lot of women wearing lightweight, flowy, often textured long white dresses and maxi skirts. Beautiful as it was, I did not want to hop on this trend because I have two little kids who would destroy such a dress instantly. However, these women were braver:

I also went to some kind of free fog exhibit at a nearby museum, and apparently did not get the memo that everyone was supposed to be wearing long white dresses. Apparently, a great deal of the women attending this exhibit were going for the Instagram photo op, which naturally made a lot of sense with white gowns. Seriously, it looked like I was waiting in a courthouse for a bunch of people to have casual weddings:

Two especially nice outfits from the fog exhibit:

This gorgeous midi dress spotted in Antibes, in the South of France:

This incredibly chic woman in Bruges, Belgium:

Another person wearing this trend: my friend and fellow Substacker Phoebe Maltz Bovy, with whom I hung out in Antibes with our families! I was so inanely excited to see her, and we had a great time! Her white dress can be found at M&F Linens in Toronto.

The closest I came was this pastel yellow Audella Paris set (small indie designer at a pop-up).

Some options for “stealing” this look from the US, from left to right:

  • Urban Outfitters, $79 (Before you scoff at the retailer or price, it’s 100% cotton.)

Moto Boots, Mini Dress

For a long time, I’ve admired the iconic Miu Miu leather moto boots but frankly, did not have $2,000 to spend on them. At this point, there are dupes everywhere and I am simply not refined enough to be able to tell the difference. I noticed a couple women in Paris contrasting these edgy boots with girlish mini dresses, and instantly I was inspired.

This first woman was on her way to the aforementioned fog exhibit, clearly unaware of the DRESS CODE but still looking great:

This outfit is an instant classic that I hope to replicate myself:

And, some shopping suggestions for boots:

  • Gisel Moire, $251 (on sale from $375)

  • Vagabond Shoemakers, $450

  • Steve Madden, $159

Capris and Slim Black Heels

I noticed several women in Paris sporting a sexy, slim-all-around look with fitted black tops, matching black pedal pushers, and minimalistic black heels—often strappy heels or heeled flip-flops.

And this is a trend I was able to do, courtesy of this cool asymmetrical vintage Moschino crop top I found!

For the pants:

  • Abercrombie, $95

  • Reformation, $128

  • Norma Kamali x Revolve, $95

For the shoes:

  • Larroude, $138 (on sale from $275)

  • Coach, $175

  • For Love and Lemons, $169

Balloon Pants

So, I’ve been following Fashion Substack’s coverage of balloon pants, and my first thought was that they were just too weird for me. Not hideous, not tacky, but just…not my thing. However, I saw them everywhere in Paris, and eventually I started to come around.

This was, by far, my favorite balloon pants execution. The headband! The heels!

Also spotted in London:

I even saw a man wearing them (I know what you’re thinking, and yes, he definitely had a mustache and mullet hairstyle, and it worked.)

Eventually, I purchased what I thought were balloon pants from the affordable Spanish brand Oysho, but they actually fit a bit more like harem pants due to the lightweight cotton gauze material, but without the drop waist. True balloon pants, as far as I can tell, are a bit stiffer. I prefer more of a drapey look, and I’m very in love with these! (In case you’re wondering, the frog closure athletic crop top is also from Oysho—very sad to leave this brand behind, as they do not have stores in the US!)

Anyway, as soon as my son saw me in these pants, he asked me if I wanted to play Aladdin.

Shopping ideas:

  • Le Bon Shoppe, $115

  • Chan Luu, $195

  • Cos, $129

  • Pistola, $84 (on sale from $168)

Bermuda Shorts

This is an item I will probably never wear, because I fear it would be catastrophic with my legendary cankles, but I can admire it from afar. Throughout Paris, I saw a variety of women wearing knee or mid-calf length bermuda shorts, often in a businesswear formulation (pleated, typically black or khaki.) Sometimes, they were paired with heels, but I actually saw them with flats more than anything else. I actually saw multiple women wearing them with bright scarlet flats specifically, which must be some Parisian uniform of the summer:

Culottes in a more casual, effortless style:

This ‘70s-inspired striped coord set with a belted waist:

This monochrome bubblegum pink coord set with matching hat and shoes. Very CHH-coded, I must say:

You can find it here, although the skirt appears to be out of stock. I also love that she paired it with sleek red flip-flops.

Colder Weather Trends

Thinking ahead to fall? Done with the white cotton and gingham? Well, I also visited Paris in spring, and noticed some cooler-weather trends which will probably still work for fall in the US this year.

First, I noticed lots of long trench coats, mostly leather, and typically in very dark shades as opposed to khaki: black, dark brown, deep burgundy, etc.

This woman paired rich burgundy tights with plaid shorts, which I really love for transitional weather:

Another trend I noticed—and regrettably, I only got one photo of it—was thin red socks visible underneath black shoes. I saw this done with both loafers and flats:
