You might think that this rhetoric is a response to popular backlash against technology companies. And yet, Big Tech firms are popular. In The Argument’s most recent national survey, fielded from May 29 to June 3, most Americans had a favorable opinion of four large tech companies: Google, Amazon, Microsoft, and Apple.

Far fewer Americans had opinions about the leading AI labs. About 87% of respondents had some opinion about OpenAI, but around 4 in 10 had never heard of Anthropic or Elon Musk’s xAI.

Progressives (and some conservatives) are increasingly critical of Big Tech companies and, by extension, artificial intelligence.³ Indeed, Big Tech companies often engage in anticompetitive behavior that harms consumers. But anti-tech populism is poorly suited to serve as the foundation for mass politics.

Americans love Amazon

What is the most popular institution in America? It’s not a government agency — it’s Amazon, followed closely by the military, Google, and the police.

That’s according to a 2023 survey from the Harris Poll and the Center for American Political Studies at Harvard. And it’s not a one-off: Harris and CAPS found that Amazon was the second-most popular institution (after the military) in a 2021 survey. Morning Consult’s 2023 polling found that Amazon was the third-most-trusted brand in the United States and the single most trusted tech or e-commerce brand.

In other words, our finding isn’t an outlier. And if you look under the hood at our data, you’ll see that Amazon is popular with almost everyone.

It’s popular across all races, but especially among Black people.

People under 30 are the least favorable on Amazon, but it still has a +16 favorability rating with that group. And older people really like the platform.

What’s really interesting is the relationship between having a negative opinion of Amazon and measures of socioeconomic status, such as income or educational attainment. Because our May poll had a sample size of over 3,000, we can look at relatively small subgroups (such as people who earn over $200,000 per year) and still have enough resolution to get usable results.

In our data, higher-income voters were more likely to hold unfavorable views of Amazon — though majorities of all income categories saw the company favorably.

Is hating Amazon a luxury belief?
Amazon is more popular with poorer and less educated voters

Among the poorest voters — those earning less than $25,000 per year — 61% held a favorable opinion of Amazon, while 14% had an unfavorable opinion. Among the richest voters, the gap was narrower: Fifty-six percent of those earning $200,000 per year or more had a favorable opinion of Amazon, while 26% had an unfavorable view.

Nearly two-thirds of voters without a college degree had a positive view of Amazon, compared to 57% of those with a college degree.

But the most striking split is between liberals and conservatives.

People like successful businesses

Our polling showed Amazon was more popular than any tech company besides Google, which was tied in overall favorability. There’s a pretty simple explanation for this: Amazon (and Google) make products that a lot of people use and enjoy.

In the Morning Consult polling, trust in Amazon is consistently about 10 percentage points higher among Amazon Prime subscribers than among the general public. In 2024, just under 73% of American adults were Amazon Prime users (and that share has been rising over time). You’re probably one of them, and I’m almost certain you’d agree that Prime is a very useful and convenient service.

Amazon is unique among major American institutions in that most people interact with it quite regularly and almost always leave with a positive impression. The other day, I had to buy some doorstops and a can opener for my apartment. It took me about five minutes to find suitable products on Amazon at a very reasonable price, and they were on my doorstep the next morning. I couldn’t find a way to stream The Life of Birds anywhere until I realized I could just buy the series on Prime Video for $8.99.

This is how almost all of my experiences with Amazon have been. I need or want to buy a specific product, so I order it on Amazon, and it arrives very quickly. One time, I bought an iPad case that didn’t work, but it was very easy to return it and get a refund.

It’s a very legible business model: You give Amazon your money, and Amazon sends you items in the mail.

If you work at an Amazon warehouse or as an Amazon driver, you might have legitimate complaints about your working conditions. Maybe the hours are too long or the pay is too low or the company is using new surveillance technologies that you dislike. But most people who interact with Amazon do so as customers, not employees.

Google is similar. The way its business model works is that you can search up anything for free, and Google will collect data about you and use that data to sell ads. This turns out to be an extremely lucrative business model.

Online publishers and ad-sellers often complain that Google uses its market dominance to treat them in unfair or anticompetitive ways. But most people aren’t running a digital news platform or trying to advertise on Google; they just want to look things up.

Crucially, neither Amazon nor Google is in the business of operating social media platforms. We didn’t poll the favorability of social media companies like Meta, Twitter, or TikTok. But Harris/CAPS did, and their data showed that these social media platforms are markedly less popular than Google or Amazon.

Social media platforms have to make content moderation decisions. As a former comments section moderator, I can tell you that these decisions can be controversial, especially when you’re dealing with edge cases. Before Elon Musk bought Twitter, conservatives liked to complain that its content moderation policies were biased against the right. Post-Musk, it’s people on the left who claim that the site’s content moderation is biased against them.

This sort of dynamic — plus the fact that AI-generated fruit infidelity videos are objectively less useful than being able to buy almost anything or having all of human knowledge at your fingertips — makes it hard for social media platforms to be as widely liked as Google or Amazon.

The data shows that most Americans like Big Tech companies. That does not mean that these companies don’t do bad things (like behave in anticompetitive ways or treat their workers badly). But it does mean that virulently anti-Big Tech attitudes are a niche attitude among the public.
