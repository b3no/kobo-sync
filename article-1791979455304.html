Muscles or Height? Dad Bod or Skinny? The Male Bodies Most Loved By Women (And Gay Men)

The results of the male body survey are in!

The wait is over! Last week, I published the results of my female body survey, but today is all about the men. This isn’t the first time I’ve done a survey on men’s bodies, but it is a far superior iteration of my initial survey. My first findings were pretty common sense: women liked slim, fit men. But I was unable to accurately show height, and because the body generator I used couldn’t make “unnatural” physiques, the most fit body in the survey was nowhere near gym bro tier. It was also unclear exactly how fit they liked the men. Would a small amount of muscle be enough, or would they want you in the gym four times a week?

Anyway, I was able to fix all of that with my new survey! This one showed men at five heights: 6’5”, 6’1”, 5’10”, 5’7” and 5’4”. I showed the height by using a 5’5” woman for scale. I also illustrated a variety of body types, meaning to illustrate the different variety of muscle and body fat percentage. For reference, all body types at the 6’5” height:

Now, I am fully aware that the muscular body type is generally unattainable without steroids, but that was the point. I also had female bodies that were unattainable without steroids or plastic surgery, and since my first survey caught heat for lack of extremes, I made sure to include some extremes here. I also know that “average” is not what the average American man looks like (the average American man is significantly overweight.) In this survey, “average” meant someone who was not fat, not thin, and had some muscle but not a lot, similar to the “average” woman from my female body examples.

And now, without further ado, the top ten bodies for straight women, with one surprising dark horse:

That’s right—while height was unsurprisingly an asset, and while most of the top bodies were 6’5” or 6’1”, a 5’4” guy managed to get on the top ten. I’m still not totally sure why the 5’4” average-build guy was there but not the 5’7” one (ratings between 5’4” and 5’7” weren’t that different across the board) but either way I doubt it was a mistake. Here was the more specific rundown of his rankings among straight and bi women.

Keep in mind that I deduced women’s binary yes/no for dating based on a question posed before they saw any bodies: what attractiveness rating from 1/10 was their “floor?” Basically, I wanted to get the idea of what it meant, practically, for someone to be a 6. If you rate someone a 6, would you still date them? As you can see below, a woman’s own self-rating correlated with her dating floor, and straight women’s dating floors were actually very similar to that of straight men’s.

Height Matters, but Build Matters More

I long suspected this one was true, but now I have confirmation. For men rating women, overall weight mattered much more than curve ratios. For women rating men, body composition (fat and muscle percentage) mattered far more than height. This isn’t to say height didn’t matter at all—the top-rated bodies were disproportionately tall—but if height was the main deciding factor for women, I would expect to see untoned or overweight tall men performing better than fit short men. This was not the case. Most women also still considered shorter men dateable, as long as they had conventionally attractive builds.

Looking at a woman’s yes/no binary, it doesn’t change that much as a man gets taller. A man with a slim, average or fit body type has pretty good dating odds even at 5’4”, whereas an obese guy still struggles at 6’5”.

One weird thing here was that the skinny body type performed worse as he got taller. I don’t think this can be extrapolated to real life, I think that most likely the image generator made the skinny guy look increasingly ghoulish as he got taller and he verged into Slenderman territory, which was not the case with the ordinary slim man.

But of course, height (especially 6’1” or over) was an asset. To put things into perspective, an untoned 6’5” guy scored roughly the same, if not slightly better, than a fit 5’7” guy:

Now, what if the 5’7” guy isn’t fit? A chubby 5’7” guy performed about as well as a 6’5” husky-overweight guy. Keep in mind that the taller man received more polarizing ratings (more 1 and more 8+ ratings) whereas the shorter guy just did kind of okay. But their overall dateability was similar:

I was also wondering which female characteristics correlated with a stronger preference for tall men. Some patterns emerged. Women who rated themselves higher on the 1-10 scale, politically right-leaning women, younger women, and higher-earning women were more likely to see height as a bigger asset. Height preference had nothing to do with a woman’s orientation (bi vs. straight), her own body size or fitness, or her relationship status. I anticipated that single women would be pickier about height because having a strong height preference might make it harder for you to pair off, but not so!

This chart illustrates what I mean. Comparing single versus partnered women in their thirties, you would expect to see the single ones with stronger height preferences, because the partnered ones were less picky and found someone. But no!

I also wondered if perhaps we would see a delta between single women in their twenties versus thirties, in case women who were still single past a certain age were more likely to ease up on the height preference. Instead, I saw the same general pattern, but single women in their twenties rated everyone a bit lower. (This was a common pattern when it came to age—generally, older women of all relationship statuses gave higher ratings, with women over 40 the most forgiving.)

Gay Men Care Less About Height

Tall men were also popular among gay and bi men, but height mattered even less for them than it did for women.. For gay men, the most conventionally attractive body types saw basically no penalty or reward based on height, while overweight or untoned bodies benefited a bit more from increased height. As you might notice here, gay men were also more open to overweight men then straight women, especially when tall, and pretty forgiving of slim short men. Perhaps this is the whole twink/bear thing I keep hearing about.

Ultra-Muscular Men Do Better with Gays

It’s a stereotype that men who take steroids are “appealing to the male gaze” but it seems the stereotype is true for a reason. Of course, the “fit” body type favored by women was still very muscular, but the extreme muscular body took a significant penalty with women. This was not an issue for gay men.

Gay men were more likely to rate ultra-muscular bodies high, and also more likely to want to date them:

Now, you might wonder if gay men were just less picky across the board. This is somewhat true, but with an important caveat. Their dating “floor” was about the same as women’s—around 5/10. But on average, gay men rated male bodies a full point higher than straight women did, so more men passed their yes/no binary. Straight and bi women found 56% of the bodies dateable, gay and bi men found 68% dateable.

As you can see, gay men still favored fit men over ultra-muscular, but the very beefy guy still made it to the top ten. Height was all over the place. Contrast this with women’s results, which featured more average builds and leaned toward taller men.

Muscular bodies were by far the most polarizing for women. This ties into my longstanding theory that while most women do not want a “bodybuilder” type, there are so few bodybuilders out there that the men who actually do look like this probably get a decent amount of female attention.

As you can see here, the muscular body type had ratings all across the board—about 16% of women thought he was an 8+, which means a guy who looks like this will certainly have plenty of options in real life, but more women found him unattractive.

Meanwhile, the average body type just performed better overall, with significantly more women rating him higher. This doesn’t mean, however, that zero women would be into the ultra-muscular guy, just that he would need to find his niche.

A Boost from Skinny to Slim

The difference between my skinny male model and slim was fairly subtle, but made a big difference in how women rated them. Gaining just a big of fat and muscle can make a super-skinny man much more attractive.

Perhaps interestingly, women rated the skinny man similarly across the board regardless of height, but the slim man benefited more from height. My suspicion is that the skinny man was a turnoff no matter his height for some women, whereas the slim man was more likely to pass through women’s filters at a taller height because they were more open to that body type. It’s also possible that women who like really skinny men would be open to men with that shape no matter their height, or, as I said before, that the skinny man just rendered weirdly at taller heights.

The Benefit of Adding Muscle for Fat Guys

Not everyone has the lifestyle or genetics to be slim or fit. But if a guy naturally skews big, I wanted to see what kind of boost could be gained from adding muscle while still at an overweight BMI, going from a straightforward obese body type to a still-overweight body type that reads more “husky” because muscle is layered underneath fat. (Likely he would still lose some fat too, but not a lot.)

While neither of these body types are the conventional favorite, adding muscle to an already-overweight body type makes a significant impact for dating. Assuming 5’10” height remains constant, a husky-overweight guy was considered dateable by 30% of female respondents, compared to 9% of respondents who saw the obese body type. This change also significantly increases the percentage of women who would rate a guy as above-average.

This change actually helps even more for gay guys than for straight guys. If you are interested in attracting men, adding muscle to an already-overweight body increases dateability from 15% to 52%!

Women’s Own Looks Matter

A woman’s preference or lack thereof for tall men did not change very much depending on her own self-rating of attractiveness or degree of physical fitness. But women who considered themselves visibly physically fit were more likely to rate the fit and muscular body types higher, while women who were overweight were more interested in chubby or obese men than other women were. Meanwhile, visibly fit women were harsher on overweight bodies.

This was somewhat similar to how men rated women—no matter the man’s body size, he was more interested in slim women than overweight women, but overweight men were less harsh on overweight women than the slim or average men were. Meanwhile, overweight men were harsher on skinny women than other men who took the survey, and you can see that effect replicated here with overweight women rating fit and muscular men slightly lower than other women did.

All that said, overweight people of all genders seem to prefer fit, slim and average-sized partners on average, even if they are more open to dating other overweight people than a slim person would be.

As you can see here, losing weight (but not too much) and gaining muscle (but not too much) results in a significant increase in the amount of women who would date you, although men seem to reach a point of diminishing returns once an average, slightly muscled physique has been achieved (cutting after this point won’t help that much, at least as far as all women in the aggregate are concerned.)

Just as I explored for female bodies, I wanted to know if filtering for the most in-demand women changed the beauty standards at all. If you are interested in single, skinny, slim or fit women in their twenties, would you benefit more from being fitter? As it turns out, these women are just harsher raters across the board (not surprising) but their overall pattern of attraction is pretty similar. The only difference is that they are more interested in slim and fit men as opposed to average men. For “women” in general, having an average build with some subtle muscle is really all you need. For the most conventionally attractive young woman, cutting body fat and going to the gym a lot has measurable effects, with slim and fit men outperforming everyone else.

That said, overly skinny and ‘roided out men still face a penalty with these pickier women.
