Europe’s hour

The prime ministers of Britain and Canada cozy up to the old continent. Their opposition parties, and the White House, are spoiling for precisely this fight

Paul Wells
Sep 30
∙
Preview
 
READ IN APP
 

“No fatties, no trannies, no beardos, no weirdos, no wimps, no radicals,” Pete Hegseth told about 600 junior officers and enlisted members at the Marine Base in Quantico, VA on Wednesday, outlining his latest frenzied roid-rage vision of a fighting force fit for whatever he sees when he stares longingly into the mirror.

Donald Trump’s handpicked “war” secretary then told the crowd he has appointed Elon Musk, Palmer Luckey and Newt Gingrich — each of whom could check off at least two items on Hegseth’s own checklist of undesirable traits — to ponder the future of combat. And that he will bring some large number of US soldiers home from overseas to serve in a newly-built “fortress” on American soil. The news that a self-styled war secretary wants to complement his siege mentality with siege infrastructure sounds ominous, as does Hegseth’s assertion that reporters whose coverage of the Iran war displeases him are committing “treason,” a capital crime.

I believe it’s no coincidence that, while this sort of stuff goes on in the DC suburbs, the prime ministers of two countries long allied with the United States — Canada and the United Kingdom — are brainstorming closer relations with Europe. Even though the leading opposition party in each country is spoiling for precisely this fight. ...
