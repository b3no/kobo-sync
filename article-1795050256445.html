Good Enough for a Tuesday Night

An affair with an economist exposed the calculus of modern dating.

By Merritt Tierce

We met on OkCupid. He was a handsome, accomplished economics professor, and I was in New York for the shoot of an episode of the final season of “Orange Is the New Black.” For our first date, we decided on Buvette, a cute French bistro in the West Village. He was sitting on a bench outside the restaurant, and I could tell as I walked up that he wasn’t blown away by my beauty. We had dinner, and he told me that he’d read my book, in preparation for our date. I didn’t understand yet that he was from that élite class of European men for whom it’s nothing to read two novels a week, for pleasure and erudition, in addition to going about high-level professional work, raising children, and practicing some intense sport. I was flattered that he’d read my book, a novel about a self-destructive waitress that had helped me land a writing job on “Orange.” And I was surprised when he took my arm outside the restaurant and invited me to go home with him. Although I was willing to go to bed with him, I hadn’t expected him to see me that way. I knew I wasn’t as attractive as he was, and I felt a distasteful conflict between the delusion of taking his interest personally and the humiliation of opting for an orientation of gratitude. I grasped about my interior for whatever dark magic makes a plain woman irresistible to people for other reasons, but I didn’t have it. In any case, I went up to his apartment, which was on an upper floor in a high-rise. His kids were with their mother, in Brooklyn, and I was good enough for a Tuesday night. The Europeans love a novelist.

He had a chiselled body because he was a serious rock climber. His penis was nothing special, his thin lips were not ideal for cunnilingus, but his torso was sculpted like a Polykleitos. I wanted to laugh every time I saw him without his shirt on. He could grab any pole out in the world and make himself perpendicular.

We had sex, and then, at 1 A.M., I went back to the furnished corporate apartment that the show had rented for me in Chelsea. My call time for pickup by the Teamster van that would take me to set was 6:15 A.M. I slept the few hours and was downstairs two minutes late, for which the Teamster driver reprimanded me. I didn’t mind. I loved the grand, intricate machine of production. I loved that two minutes mattered. The days on set were long, and that day I was tired but also relaxed, calm, invigorated. Being tired because you were up late fucking a beautiful man is a kind of deliquescent, acceptable tired, not unenjoyable, nothing like the hard, barren tired of depression or loneliness.
