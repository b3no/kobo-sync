From the bodies we prefer to our actual sex lives in practice, politics affects our sexuality.

No but seriously—from the looks we prefer in a partner, to our level of pickiness and openness, to the actual sex we are having, there are clear differences by political alignment, and not just the things you might suspect.

I will publish these findings in two parts. This half will focus on our physical preferences, because spare for the scenario of a glory hole, physical attraction comes into play before any of the sex really happens. The second part (which I will publish next week) will focus on the actual sex that people are having—down to the specific acts and more.

I am drawing from data from two separate surveys I did—for part one, a survey where I asked people to rate digitally-generated headless bodies in a binary yes/no fashion (with a third option to single a body out as “their type”) and for part two next week, another one where I asked people about their most recent sexual encounter. These were two different surveys with presumably some overlap in their respondents. The body rating survey had about 3,000 respondents while the sexual experience survey had about 4,000 respondents. Put together, I discovered some interesting things about how our politics distinguishes us on the dating apps and in the bedroom.

Right-Wing People are Pickier

When it comes to bodies alone, right-wingers are pickier than left-wingers, and men are pickier than women.

This might come as a surprise to you given that the stereotype is that women are pickier than men. And when I did my separate survey that intended to mimic a fake dating app, women were the choosier sex.

This could be twofold. First of all, the fake dating app simulator (which didn’t ask about politics) used real photos of real people, whereas the rating survey used digitally-generated (and honestly, kind of uncanny) headless body renderings of men and women for the sake of consistency. People complained about how weird the bodies were, but I deliberately prioritized consistency over the variables of facial beauty, race, pose, background, etc. I understand that perhaps a better generator should have been used, but what’s done is done. It’s possible that men were more put-off by the fake female bodies than women were of the fake male bodies. But even with all fake bodies of one gender as the denominator, right-wing people were pickier than left-wing people.

It’s also possible that women’s more sensitive “ick” was triggered by other cues in the real photos, like facial expression (or faces in general) alongside posture, clothing, etc. I’ve long suspected that the very act of being photographed ding’s men’s attractiveness in a way that it doesn’t ding women’s. Alternatively, women are pickier about faces, while men are pickier about bodies.

So, what can we glean from this? Indisputably, right-wing politics makes one pickier about body size and shape, whether you are a man or a woman. As for the difference between men and women, this could be a factor of women’s bodies showing up more oddly in the generator, but I also think the infamous OKCupid study showing women rating “80% of men below average” is just as flawed. (Did you know that OkCupid had a Tinder-esque function where rating someone a 4 or above automatically created a “match” and invited the person to message you? It stands to reason that women might be more cautious about handing out those high ratings!)

But anyway, we can quibble about whether men or women are pickier all day and I don’t think we’ll ever come to a fair conclusion. But right-wing people are pickier about bodies, whether they are male or female.

The Top Bodies Are Bipartisan

Below we have the two most popular bodies in the dataset (filtered for heterosexual ratings and bisexual people rating the opposite gender.) Both are pretty much what you’d expect—slim (but not stick-skinny) and fit (or in the woman’s case, fit/with curves.)

(In case you are wondering, I will publish findings on gay and lesbian preferences at a later date, including bisexual people rating the same gender.)

There were some slight variations in the “acceptance rate” for these bodies, but it was mostly just statistical noise. For example, 98% of liberal men considered the female body acceptable for dating, while “only” 92% of right-leaning men agreed (I had a good number of conservative respondents, but not many respondents who considered themselves “far right,” so when I say “right-leaning” I am including both conservative and far-right people. I had enough data to distinguish liberals from the far left, but when I group them together, I will say “left-leaning.”)

Similarly, the top male body was the most common preference for basically all women across age and political alignment.

Right-Wing Women Prefer Husky Men

While slim/fit male bodies and slim/curvy (curvy meaning ratios, not overall size) female bodies were the most popular in the dataset, things diverged politically once we were past the first and second place winners.

For one, right-leaning women are more attracted to a “husky” male body type (shown below, on the right) than left-leaning women. In fact, far left, liberal and centrist women all prefer the man on the left, while right-leaning women prefer the man on the right.

Left-Leaning Men Are More Open to Muscular Women

Men’s top preferences diverged less than women’s. From left to right, these were the most popular female bodies with all men regardless of political alignment. When it came to the top 4-5 bodies, men didn’t differ very much politically.

While left-leaning men were generally less picky than right-leaning men, I noticed a few patterns. For one, left-leaning men were meaningfully more accepting of muscular/athletic women. I created the two bodies below, one (on the right) with a more defined waist. Both were more popular with far-left men than with anyone else, but right-leaning men were the most likely to reject. 63% and 84% of far-left men accepted the straight-shaped and nipped-waist athletic bodies respectively, compared to right-wing men, who saw no difference between these bodies as far as attractiveness went and accepted both 56% of the time.

Far-Left Men Like Chubby Women More

One fairly unsurprising trend was that men on the left (especially the far-left, as opposed to liberal) were more open to women who were overweight, from chubby to obese. The acceptance rates for women who were mildly chubby were much higher than for those who were obese, but the pattern was linear across the political spectrum—as men became more right-wing, they became more likely to reject women for their weight.

To put things into perspective, the body on the left was accepted by 75% of far-left men and 56% of right-leaning men, with that slope in acceptance continuing through liberal → centrist men. In fact, the body on the left was the fifth top “ideal type” for far-left men. The body on the right was accepted by 49% of far-left men and 29% of right-leaning men.

The heaviest female body in the dataset was less popular, but still performed better with men on the left. 16% of far-left men accepted this body, compared with just 6% of right-leaning men.

Far-Left Women Like Skinny Men More

Just as far-left men are more accepting of larger women, far-left women are more accepting of both heavier and skinnier men. I covered this in my earlier rundown of women’s preferences for male bodies, but the skinniest male body in the dataset (see below) performed worse than muscular, slim, average, husky, and mildly overweight bodies, but performed better than obese bodies.

More specifically, this body followed a linear political pattern, considered acceptable by 72% of women on the far-left, and only 50% of right-leaning women. It was also the ideal “type” for 7% of women on the far left but only 4% of women on the right.

One confounding variable here was that this body performed pretty well with women who were at the extreme ends of body size themselves—it was the ideal “type” for 10% of self-described “skinny” (not just slim) women, and the ideal “type” for 12% of women who considered themselves significantly overweight. It also so happened that women on the far-left were more likely to be at these extremes in body size than women of other political alignments.

But Right-Wing Men Like Super Skinny Women More

The female equivalent of the super skinny man—a super skinny woman without much in the way of curves—performed very differently. In fact, this was one of the only female bodies where right-wing men were more accepting than anyone else. The men most likely to reject this body type were on the far left! Far-left men accepted this body type 63% of the time, while right-leaning men accepted 73% of the time.

Some Female Bodies Are Normie Politics Coded

The overall pattern of averages favor the thesis of right-wing men being pickier than left-wing men (and the pattern of rejection being linear, with men becoming pickier as they become more right-wing) but certain bodies appealed most to centrists and liberals, while less popular with men on the far left or the right.

There was less of a pattern here, but basically, any body that wasn’t chubby or obese, and also wasn’t super skinny, was more popular with centrists and liberals than men on the right or men on the far left. None of the below bodies were especially polarizing, but the pattern was U-shaped, with liberal and centrist men having more positive reactions than far-left or right-leaning men.
