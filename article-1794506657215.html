AI will probably kill most of us, a top AI researcher said in Toronto on Tuesday

Paul Wells
Nov 12
∙
Preview
 
READ IN APP
 

It’s hard to believe I’m writing this.

I’ve been distractedly reading the same things you have about AI acceleration and its attendant, very large risks. But this week in Toronto two of the world’s top AI experts made it feel more real and urgent.

The speaker for the second annual Hinton Lectures, organized by the University of Toronto’s Geoffrey Hinton, was Paul Christiano from San Francisco, who’s just joined the OpenAI Foundation board.

I might as well start the way Christiano did. He delivered his argument across three consecutive nights at the Metro Toronto Convention Centre. I’m going to pull the most important statements from each night out in indented pull quotes. There will be a lot of those.

Here’s how the first lecture, on Monday night, began:

“I’m going to start with the most important sentence. I think there is a high risk of an AI takeover within the next 3 years.

“What I mean by an AI takeover is a situation where there are AI systems pursuing goals, and those systems call the shots — in the same sense that humans today call the shots and what happens on Earth — and where if, say, you or me or Donald Trump wanted to stop them, they would be as ineffective as if you or me was trying to stop the US military.

“I think this is a terrible situation. I think it’s reasonably likely many people would die during that transition. Or that many people would die shortly thereafter if we were unable to maintain a habitable environment. I think even if we don’t die I think a takeover is sort of the end of human sovereignty and self-determination. And it is something we should be working incredibly hard to avoid.”

What’s a high risk? Hard to say, Christiano said. But if AI development continues as rapidly as possible, the takeover he described is “something like a coin flip” in the next three years. He hopes it’s lower because he hopes governments slam on the brakes of AI development. But even then, “the risk remains exceptionally high.”

“I think for most people in this room, the most likely reason they will die is because of an AI accident.”

And this will take three years? Maybe. Maybe less.

“I think there’s meaningful risk in the next six to 12 months. The trajectory in the field has been extremely alarming over the last three to six months to people who have been tracking closely. I think we have seen roughly the things we might expect to see shortly before something really bad were to happen.

“So I’m saying ‘Within the next three years,’ and indeed much of the risk is further out. But I think there is also material risk in the immediate future. This is not an issue we should be treating as an abstract discussion. This is something we should be trying to react to immediately.”...
