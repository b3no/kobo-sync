The Panel: Time for a Carney-Trump deal?

And how seriously should the Liberals take Will Greaves’ tiny perfect rebellion?

Paul Wells
Nov 27
 
READ IN APP
 

This week’s visit from The Panel quickly devolves into a brazen plug for my Holiday Overflow Show at Ottawa’s Overflow Brewery on Dec. 9, because that’ll be the next time The Panel gets together in person. Tickets are available here, and this post tells you what’s on offer.

Then we discuss the prospects of a Trump-Carney trade deal. That’s “very close,” said the US President this week. To which I answer: What? Why? I don’t know about all y’all, but it sounds to me like The Panel is altogether more persuaded of the necessity of reaching quick agreement to minimize US tariffs on Canadian goods than I am. I don’t normally go into these discussions with my friends looking for a reason to disagree, but I found one here. With James Moore, I ask: what good’s a deal with big Don Trump? And what does Carney give up in trying to reach one?

Upgrade to paid

Next up, we discuss Victoria Liberal MP Will Greaves’ Toronto Star article in which he worries — publicly, with his name on it, as though he had a right to an opinion like anybody — that the government with which he sits is blowing Canada’s climate credibility in its quest for business deals. Here I was struck by how concerned Marci Surkes, our panel’s Liberal, is at Greaves’ polite, contained gesture of rebellion. Not despondent or panicky, but concerned. Garry Keller, our Conservative, resists the urge to gloat. It’s a nice Panel.

Then we discuss AI catastrophe, because we may never get another chance to question our machine overlords. And that’s that for one of our longest and, I’d argue, best Panel discussions.
