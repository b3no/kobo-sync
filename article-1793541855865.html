Initially, I agreed with this criticism. I believe once kids are old enough to have opinions about their costumes, they should get the first pick, and the parents should either dress as something different, or center their costumes around the children. That means that if a child wants to be Cinderella, the mom can be a different Disney princess, or another character from Cinderella, or something else entirely, but she can’t say, “No, honey, I’m going to be Cinderella and you can be the broom.”

However, a few things that made me diverge a bit from my original feeling on this topic:

1.) Paris insists her daughter wanted to be Chip. This could be a convenient fib (especially since it’s unlikely her daughter and son both wanted to be supporting characters) but my kids have definitely chosen bizarre costumes before (this year my son almost went as “a tube”) so it’s entirely possible:

2.) It didn’t even occur to me that these costumes weren’t the family’s actual costumes for Halloween and were just a photoshoot for Paris’s social media specifically. For all we know, the kids trick-or-treated as Pikachu and Spongebob. Social media is not real life.

3.) Some of the criticism (albeit not mine) verged into bizarre fanfiction where Paris’s choice to be a glamorous princess was indicative of psychosexual resentment of her toddler daughter which is, frankly, insane. I do not want to be associated with that, so I’ve been negatively polarized into supporting Paris Hilton.
