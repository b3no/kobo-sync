The Line Podcast: Conservatives can’t cope as Carney racks up wins

On the budget, the midterms, and why our opposition is struggling to adapt to the PM.

Line Editor
Nov 6
 
READ IN APP
 

In the latest episode of The Line Podcast, recorded between November 5th and 6th, 2026, Matt Gurney and Jen Gerson are joined by special guest Stephanie Levitz of The Globe and Mail.

This episode is also brought to you by Cameco. Building Canada’s energy future means creating jobs for Canadian workers. That’s why the AP1000 reactor is the right choice for Canada: it is already operating and ready to deliver the power we need. Choosing the AP1000 means a reactor built by Canadians, creating skilled jobs in construction, fabrication and the trades while keeping more investment and economic benefits here at home. If we are serious about creating Canadian jobs and strengthening our economy, the choice is clear. To learn more, visit ap1000.cameco.com.

First up, a brief explainer: due to Jen being at a conference, part of this episode was recorded earlier in the week, after the midterms, but before the Canadian federal budget had been released. To avoid ignoring one of the huge stories of the week, Matt recorded a special segment of the podcast on Friday, after Jen had left for her event. In that segment, Stephanie Levitz joins Matt for a quick discussion of the federal budget. What’s in it, what’s missing, and what does it tell us about the Carney government’s priorities? They take stock of what Ottawa is promising, what it’s actually prepared to spend money on, and what Canadians should take away from it all.

This episode is also brought to you by BioCanRx. We’ve been partnering with federally-funded Canadian not-for-profit BioCanRx to learn how made-in-Canada immunotherapies offer new ways to beat cancer. This month we’re addressing common questions, like: are immunotherapy technologies dual-use?While many immunotherapies are personalized using a patient’s own cells, the research and manufacturing infrastructure behind them can produce other important products, like vaccines. The people behind those research, manufacturing, and clinical delivery processes are also extremely capable and versatile. Greater support for translational research and clinical trials would help grow this workforce and build capabilities that extend well beyond cancer. In a health emergency, that capacity could also help Canada respond quickly to urgent needs for new treatments and vaccines .Thanks to BioCanRx for continuing to partner with The Line to share this information. Visit BioCanRx.com to learn more.

And then, as recorded earlier in the week, Matt and Jen have a lengthy discussion about the U.S. midterm elections and what they mean for Canada. They consider the political forces shaping the results, what the elections tell us about Donald Trump’s America, and what Canadians should expect from Washington in the years ahead. Whatever happens in American domestic politics, Canada still has to figure out how to deal with a neighbour whose political priorities and relationship with the rest of the world have changed dramatically.

Finally, your hosts pivot from the American midterms to how they’re being received here in Canada. Jen breaks her own rule about not talking about the Conservatives and talks about the Conservatives. Matt, meanwhile, has a warning for some Canadian conservatives: he thinks they’re having a hard time coming to grips with the fact that they’re Liberals now. And the sooner they figure that out, the better.

All that and more in the latest episode of The Line Podcast.
