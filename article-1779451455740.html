I have been the Twitter Main Character twice this year, and somehow both times involved sexy metallic clubwear.

The first time, I posted a somewhat silly, sleep-deprived question about the appeal of a tight, short silver Gucci dress featured on the runway. Baddie Twitter wound up roasting me for being a dumpy mom who should keep her middle-aged nose out of fashion and leave it to the fashion girlies.

This encounter left me wondering if maybe I, cringe millennial mom as I am, had misjudged the dress. I love to sew, and I sew a good portion of my own clothes, so what if I sewed my own metallic, bodycon, Gucci-inspired dress? Would I dare? As I perused some slinky metallic knits on Mood Fabrics, I realized that perhaps the correct move would be a metallic wide-leg infinity jumpsuit—think Cher, disco Diane von Furstenberg, you see the vision—which would still qualify as sexy nightlife attire but would be modest enough for a lounge or restaurant and a little more appropriate for my thirty-six years of life. And unlike the dress, it would carry zero risk of exposing my literal ass cheeks to unsuspecting restaurant patrons. (I have a sizable rear and a long waist, which is really a recipe for becoming an accidental flasher.)

I’m afraid some of my male readers may have drifted off or started thinking about the NBA playoffs. Please come back. The cuck stuff is coming. I am laying the groundwork, which unfortunately for you involves fashion. It’s almost over, you can do this.

Anyway, one day I carved out some time to design, cut and sew the perfect nightlife jumpsuit in material inspired by the Gucci dress. And, voila:

I sent this photo to all my female friends, but was so excited about it (it’s literally the best thing I’ve ever made for myself) that I posted it to Substack and Twitter too. And although I’m not exactly surprised by the reactions, I became the Main Character again for an entirely different reason—being so indecent and immodest to post such vulgar filth when I am a married woman with children. Does your husband know you dress this way?! people incredulously asked, as if the desired occasion for an outfit like this is not, quite literally, “to wear with my husband.” Why no, my husband has no idea I dress this way! I have my clandestine sexy wardrobe for my secret husband-free excursions, like going to the dentist to get scammed into another “deep cleaning.”

While most people were complimentary, a sizable minority were fairly convinced that any man who “allows” his wife to wear something like this (my husband, in fact, asks me to wear stuff like this) is either a clueless chump whose wife is getting off on Internet creeps jacking off to her, or has a cuck fetish himself. The Twitter mind cannot conceive of a man who wants his wife to look hot and either doesn’t care if other people see, or deliberately wants people to know (The words “arm candy” and “trophy wife” used to mean something. RETVRN.)

As much as objectification has its problems, it’s far preferable and healthier than the growing trend I’ve noticed (far beyond me and my jumpsuit post) where even the most mainstream, safe-for-work depictions of “sexuality” are twisted and contorted until they can resemble someone cuckoldry. No matter what, someone is being wronged—the person’s partner, the people seeing the image, whoever they can imagine first. Either the viewer is being wronged because the woman is sleeping with everyone but him, or the woman’s partner is being wronged because she’s trying to sleep with everyone but him. Either way—an attractive woman is no laughing matter! Can’t you see there’s a person is theoretically getting cucked right now, in my imagination?

In case you are wondering, yes, my husband saw the tweet. I was playing with our toddler and said “I saw the thread about you on Twitter.” I said “You’re going to have to be more specific.” He said, “The one about you being hot.” I said, “Again, please narrow it down.” He laughed and said, “Well, it’s a great jumpsuit and you do look hot in it.” At this point, I informed him that some people were angry, and he had no idea why. “Because it’s too revealing for a married mother,” I clarified. His response? A confused look, and a single statement: “That’s not even revealing.” Truly, built different. It’s called being a Mediterranean millennial, losers.

But as I mentioned, my jumpsuit was hardly the only time this type of discourse happened. Earlier this week, a 57-year-old professor was accused of nefarious thirst-trapping (and presumably, cucking her husband) for posting a photo of her blue painted toenails matching the cover of her book. When an attractive college girl posted an innocent video of her dorm room design, she was flooded with comments fantasizing about all the men she probably fucked on that bed, none of which were self-inserts and all of which were tainted with bitterness. Another viral post mentioned a woman picking up the phone from her boyfriend when she sounded as if she had been running, with the confident verdict that she had picked the phone up in the middle of getting railed by her side piece (one must ask: why would she pick up the phone at all?) Hilariously spoofed on season two of Beef, every Reddit post where a man asks for relationship advice is inundated with comments insisting his girlfriend is cheating on him. People called Alex Cooper’s husband a cuck for marrying a woman who had previously enjoyed casual dating, something he likely did too. And then there’s my favorite one—a post I wish I had bookmarked (you’ll just have to believe me on this one) which involved a man cheerfully announcing his new relationship, and the majority of his comments were like, “She’s fucked big, strong guys with bigger dicks than you. The dicks slipped out and she begged them to put it back in.” Are you guys okay?

Although this trend is far more likely to involve men than women, women are not immune either. A viral TikTok video with over 300,000 likes depicts a woman correctly using “POV,” acting as the viewer’s husband’s receptionist in ten years, with whom he is likely cheating. The comments range from “I’m not gonna bother with marriage at this point” to “I get triggered every time and I don’t even have a husband.”

Increasingly, it seems like terminally online people are microdosing a masochistic cuck fetish by fantasizing about partners they don’t even have cheating on them.

Now, I understand some of you may think I must have posted that photo with one singular goal in mind: to receive horny comments from men. Look, I don’t want to be misandrist here, but men are not so discerning that they require ten hours of sewing a custom garment in order to say something horny. If I wanted to get male attention, I could have done it without turning my husband’s home office into a one-woman sweatshop. But yes, I expected horny comments, not as the purpose for posting but as an occupational hazard. If I could post only to women, gay men and respectful straight men I would, but for some reason nobody has shipped this feature. That said, horny comments are an absolute delight compared to the cuck stuff. This type of unwarranted, miserable, hateful cuck-fantasizing goes a step beyond horny, or perhaps never fully gets there. Certain men (and perhaps some women) seem absolutely hell-bent on turning every mild depiction of sexuality as a sign of duplicitous evil.

I feel ridiculous using this word, but it’s almost a literal case of sexphobia, where even something as vanilla and mainstream as a man liking to show off his attractive wife (or a single woman posting a photo where she looks good) must be perverted and wrong. Either the viewer is being cucked, or the husband is being cucked, or imaginary guys she knows are being cucked. Someone is being cucked as long as an attractive woman is present, doing anything at all. Sex is no longer about the sex, it’s about whoever isn’t having it, whoever is being tricked, and whoever is being wronged. A happy couple isn’t just a happy couple—if they seem too happy, too eager to tell everyone that they’re happy—they must secretly be miserable.

The best-case scenario here is, unfortunately, that a lot of these people actually just have cuck fetishes. I always thought cuck fetishes were niche and unusual because my husband and I find this scenario deeply unappealing, but psychologist Justin Lehmiller reported in his 2018 book Tell Me What You Want, that in a survey of over 4,000 respondents (classic CHH sample size, btw) cuck fantasies were reasonably common in a slight majority of men (58%) and nearly a third of women, even if they didn’t act on it. (This may seem high, but the bar was “have fantasized about before.”) Honestly? I kind of hope that’s all this is. I can handle people having a kink. I only wish they were aware enough of it not to lash out at total strangers, but what can you do?

Also, I find it funny that people can look at me and my husband, decide we have a kink, and then not pick up on the obvious clues as to what that’s likely to be, instead settling on cuckoldry. They’re not sending their best!

But moreover, if 58% of men have fantasized about their partner actually being with other men, shouldn’t we assume that something far more vanilla—gaining satisfaction from other people seeing that you scored an attractive wife, and wanting your wife to wear clothes that accentuate her body—is even more common? Perhaps so common that the majority of men outside of theocracies feel this way, and it’s not even worth mentioning? Of course, it can veer into kinky territory, like when Kanye West had his wife, Bianca Censori (misleading last name) basically walk around naked. But I don’t think you could accuse Kanye of having a cuck fetish. He wanted to show off his wife. Maybe he was acting out some dominance kink where he purposefully humiliated her, or maybe it was her kink, maybe she spent too much time in Taylor Lorenz’s apartment and is dangerously overheated. I have no idea, but nothing about that arrangement shouts “cuck” to me.

While I’m sure some men genuinely prefer modesty (and while I can assure you my husband prefers modesty for occasions that warrant it) it seems like the age-old male desire to show off your attractive partner, and to have your partner’s hotness reflect well on you, is being pathologized by cuckold-obsessed weirdos projecting their own fantasies and/or anxiety onto normal people. Is Jeff Bezos acting out a cuck fetish when he “allows” his wife Lauren Sanchez to display copious cleavage and an insanely snatched waist at every formal event? Or—no, she’s cucking him against his will and Jeff Bezos, one of the most successful business moguls in history, is just too dumb and oblivious to notice! Was Trump acting out a cuck fetish (or being obliviously cucked) when Melania modeled nude for British GQ after they started dating? Are millions of European man secretly cucks for “allowing” their wives to take off their tops at the beach? Are their children irredeemably traumatized by witnessing such indecency?

No judgment either way, but some guys want their partner’s body to be shrouded in mystery to the general public. Other guys want everyone to see, the same way you might be proud to show off your new car, but don’t want anyone to touch. Neither of these groups are doing anything that weird! There could even be cultural elements too—even within secular society, certain cultures seem to value modesty more than others. As I hinted before, it’s long been a stereotype that Mediterranean men (like my husband) enjoy displaying their wives’ beauty instead of concealing it. (See the Italian philosophy of bella figura.) When my husband first took me to Greece to meet his extended relatives, he even instructed me to look my best as soon as we arrived, warning me that Greek people are very focused on women’s appearances and would likely appraise my looks without my consent. I did as he told me, and wound up getting groped by his sixty-something-year-old female cousin before she declared that he “made a good choice.” (More on that story here.)

But the point remains that many men not only allow, but want their wives to walk around wearing things far more revealing than my jumpsuit (the commonly-worn uniform of bike shorts and a sports bra are significantly more revealing) and are not acting out a cuck fetish. In fact, it’s the exact opposite. A cuck fetish revolves getting off on humiliation and loss of status. Showing off your attractive wife to the masses is about gaining status (or at least feeling like you’ve gained status) by showing what you’ve bagged. The worst thing you can say about it is that it’s objectification—but again, I’ll take 2003 hot-woman-burger-commercial objectification over the angry cuck anti-fantasies any day.

There is another explanation for the mass cuck psychosis, though, and it extends far beyond me. It relates to my earlier theory of highfivesexuality, which can best be described as a transient state of sexual development where mocking and antagonizing the desired sex is more appealing (and scary) than actually having sex with them. The reason highfivesexuality has started to persist beyond its intended end (middle school) is because young straight people aren’t pushed to interact with the opposite sex via overwhelming horniness and boredom—they have the option to get validation on social media and dating apps, goon to nonstop streams of porn, and basically enjoy a “lite” sugar-free version of sexuality without having to really participate in the risky, sometimes embarrassing, often difficult process of having relationships. We are now at peak virgin in the United States, and a sharp rise in sexlessness for both men and women. The more distant these young people are from sex, the easier it can be to view any display of sexuality, especially among people who “shouldn’t” be sexy or sexual (parents, older people, whatever) as perverted or dishonest.

Another side effect of this social shift is that sex negativity is on the rise. This rhetoric comes from several places. First, “baby radfems,” who declare that blowjobs are always abusive, while lashing out at “sex-obsessed,” cringe millennial women for enjoying (gasp) having sex with men. Meanwhile, on the other end of the spectrum, lots of disaffected men (and some women) have decided to unilaterally impose theocratic modesty laws onto a Western, secular populace, to the point that an attractive woman, completely fully-clothed but sexily dressed, begins to look like hardcore pornography. I knew we had lost the plot when someone said, “How would your children feel if they saw this photo of you on the Internet?” as if I had posted not a photo of a homemade jumpsuit, but a close-up of my open anus. (Sorry for anyone who followed, hopeful for such content—my churn isn’t quite bad enough for the anus pivot.)

Given that so many people are sexually stunted—not only in experience but in how they identify with their own sexuality—it’s not entirely surprising that people would have a kneejerk angry reaction to relatively tame displays of sexuality that used to be “normal.” Sexually appealing attire from a young-looking pop star (or even just a short woman) is “baiting pedophiles.” Normal sex acts, like fellatio, are “abusive and coercive.” A girl being excited about her dorm room is clearly sending a secret message about how many guys she’s fucked there who aren’t you. A married woman continuing to dress in a figure-conscious way must be intentionally humiliating her husband because they cannot conceive of why else a man would enjoy this. They are experiencing sex entirely through the lens of Internet-poisoned grievance, and the longer they go in this state, the further from reality sex and sexuality becomes. Condemning it is simpler, and less scary, than engaging with it like a normal person. And while I’d be the first to admit that dating apps don’t give men a fair shake, so sexlessness is not entirely their fault, some men who identify as incels openly admit they’ve never even tried to meet up with a woman in real life before.

As the writer Jaimee Marshall said on Twitter the other day, society has become so sex-negative that even people who weren’t a fan of 90’s-00’s era raunch culture and hookup culture (as I wasn’t) have been forced to take the stance of sex-positivity. While I’m no fan of extremes, I will take “cringe” millennial sex positivity over whatever the hell this secular Sharia stuff is.
