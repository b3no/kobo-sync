On The Line: Are data centres the new moral panic?

Jen Gerson interviews Mitch Heimpel.

Line Editor
Nov 24
 
READ IN APP
 

In this episode of On The Line, host Jen Gerson speaks with Line contributor Mitch Heimpel about his latest piece on AI Data centres, and the impact on local politics.

This episode is brought to you by BioCanRx, a federally-funded Canadian not-for-profit, to learn how made-in-Canada immunotherapies offer new ways to beat cancer. If you want to meet their team, where can you find them? BioCanRx staff and researchers regularly participate in scientific and policy conferences around Canada. This week, President and CEO Stephanie Michaud (mee-show) is moderating a research, government and industry panel at the Canadian Science and Innovation Policy Conference in Ottawa, along with a few other events. BioCanRx also runs its own conference, the Summit for Cancer Immunotherapy. This year it was in Vancouver, and in October 2027 it will take place in Calgary. They’ve just announced that the CEO of Canada’s Drug Agency, Suzanne McGurn, will give a keynote. Registration opens in the new year. For updates, or if your organization wants to support the conference, visit cancersummit.ca. Thanks to BioCanRx for its continued partnership with The Line.

Will it become all but impossible for data centres to get built in an era of harsh opposition? Heimpel recalls the trauma he experienced working as a political staffer during the days of Ontario’s Green Energy Act - when trying to get a wind turbine built meant opposing self organizing activists who were convinced turbines would kill bats and birds en masse.

This episode is also brought to you by the Forest Products Association of Canada. For years, the Forest Products Association of Canada has called for action to reduce regulatory duplication and create greater certainty for mills, factories, and investment. We’re encouraged to see the federal government taking action to make Canada’s regulatory system more coordinated and predictable, removing made-in-Canada barriers that have held the sector back. This is an important step for forestry and for communities across the country that depend on the sector. Now, we need to move from promises to production. Canadian jobs, stronger supply chains, and new opportunities for communities from coast to coast to coast depend on it. Canada’s forest sector is ready to do its part. Learn more at FPAC.ca.

Mitch goes on to talk about “rope ladder capitalism,” or the tendency of larger companies to seek out greater regulation in order to protect themselves from competition. We have a bad case of it here in Canada. They also discuss whether or not the furor over data centres constitutes a moral panic, with Gerson laying out her test for whether political outrage is simply out of hand, or a truly dangerous social phenomenon. In this case, Mitch notes that many of the objections to data centres originate with water concerns that are largely local to the southeastern U.S. Canada, by comparison, has a relatively friendly climate for these kinds of investments.

This episode is also brought to you by Cameco. Building Canada’s energy future means creating jobs for Canadian workers. That’s why the AP1000 reactor is the right choice for Canada: It is already operating globally and ready to build now, with Canadian ownership and strong participation from Canadian suppliers. AP1000 technology can support thousands of skilled jobs in the trades, help build energy security at home and a global export market for Canadian expertise – all with investment staying here in Canada. A proven design built by Canadian workers. That is how we build Canada. To learn more, visit ap1000.cameco.com.

Further, what will the long term consequences of becoming reliant on a technology that we increasingly depend on non-democratic countries to supply be? If we’re going to use AI, there’s a compelling case for ensuring we’re depending on data centres that are subject to democratic norms and the rule of law.
