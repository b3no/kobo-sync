Women Be Mad about Boobs On the TV

Lance Corporal Scott Bukkake
Sep 19
 
READ IN APP
 

I stated in my most previous essay that I am very unplugged from pop culture and seldom understand why anyone is famous or being talked about. As a Tubi Enjoyer™, the only commercials I usually see are for eczema medication and slot machine apps. So I only know about the Sydney Sweeney commercials from logging into Substack.

Where do I stand on the gambling ad? I think gambling is bad mostly. But that style of advertising is nothing I haven’t seen before. Bear in mind before I had my sweet, sweet Tubi, I was growing up during the era of Carl’s Junior’s Sexy Ladies Eating Sandwiches ads.

The best fast food experience of my life once happened at a Carl’s Junior. The man in front of me in line put in a ginormous order, and then while he waited for his food I ordered a single cheeseburger and curly fries. Due to the quantity of my order, it was made before the man’s, but when the cashier extended the bag for my sad little order he grabbed it and walked out before anyone could stop him. They gave me his order. Tit for tat, right? I returned home with it like a conquering hero because I’d essentially gotten three times the food for the price of one meal (and not even a premium meal).

But back on track here… Advertising…

I always thought the Carl’s Junior ads were kind of tongue-in-cheek. Everyone knows that fast food is garbage and you’d be hard-pressed to find defenders of it. We’re in on the joke of the Cheeseburger ad and we all know that baby EmRata (who is in the above ad) would sooner cut her own tongue out than eat at Carl’s Junior. I can guarantee that bite she took for the camera got spit straight into a bucket.

I’m not going to talk about the cultural significance of Sydney Sweeney’s titties as they relate to feminism. I want to talk about Advertising…

Death of Some Salesmen

Death of Some Salesmen is an episode of Tales From The Crypt which aired on HBO in 1993. It stars Ed Begley Jr. as Judd Campbell, a criminal who sells fake burial plots under the guise of a salesman. Like all Tales From the Crypt episodes, Judd gets his comeuppance after fucking over innocent people when he runs afoul of the Brackett Family (Ma, Pa and Daughter Winona all played Tim Curry). The Brackett Clan essentially murders any salesman who dares to set foot on their property, and Judd’s fate is sealed the moment he knocks on that door and makes his offer.

Personally, like the Brackett Clan, I operate on the intuition that anyone trying to sell me something is probably trying to fuck me over. I can, with great confidence, claim that institutions that turn a profit with gambling are most definitely trying to fuck a person over. Sydney Sweeney’s titties are serving as the same Tongue-In-Cheek function as the Sexy Hamburger Ladies. I get it. It’s a tale as old as time.

Criticism to Women: You Need to Explore Why Something Actually Makes You Uncomfortable and Pick More Salient Hills to Die On

This might make me sound a little presumptuous, and I might be reaching… But sometimes I don’t think everyone online who has a ‘take’ on something knows how to communicate their thoughts well, nor do they seem entirely aware of why they are feeling the way that they feel.

Advertisements for products geared toward men, like those provided above, have always relied on bodies of sexy women. I can only surmise that a calendar with artfully composed photos of drill bits and auto parts won’t get eyes on the brand. Because the main demographic of people buying tools and automotive accessories is usually men, it stands to reason that these companies would use another thing men in these fields enjoy: Sexy Ladies.

If you are a woman who enjoys tools and automotive parts, hold your tongue. We’re not playing that game.

Most women are not like the women in these advertisements. When you are not like the women in advertisements but still have to see them, it can make you feel very yucky about yourself. And it can make you feel very yucky about the men you know and even love.

I do not believe it uncommon for some women to worry that a man is not telling her that she doesn’t measure up in some manner while waiting for someone more like the Snap-On Tool Pin Up of his dreams. We fear he is biding time while we help pay the bills. If you’re on the internet or even overhear conversations, there will be men who confirm this insecurity. Frequently. We see examples of this in real life when men divorce their wives for someone younger and sexier.

At the same time, especially for the Have-Not’s, women will go through life witnessing our better looking counterparts receiving preferential treatment. Promotions. Free shit. We might have watched our male friends and family endure egregious behavior from a partner who was particularly beautiful. And it feels yucky to know that the world operates that way. Even if you do your best to make your peace with it, and take it as an undeniable fact it will always feel a little yucky.

Finally, beautiful women are used to advertise things to us too. Very often things that leave us disappointed or things we never needed in the first place. On all fronts, it’s other women who are used as the means to exploit our worst insecurities so that those insecurities can be weaponized to line someone else’s pockets.

I think this is what needs to be explored before a conversation about how something disrespects women’s sports or unfeministly appeals to the male gaze. This is the problem with that discourse: I don’t really know what the rules are. When we have conversations about which famous person is naked feministly or unfeminestly, it kind of seems to be mostly arbitrary depending on who you ask, their level of insecurity, politics, and blah blah blah… You can only cut a pie so many ways, alright?

I could argue any of these are either Yass Queening or Pick Me-ing if I wanted to

When it comes to Sydney’s Novig ad, I feel like if any man I knew well enough to care about saw that ad and downloaded that app, I’d just be disappointed. First and foremost, gambling is loser behavior. Like, Bro, don’t waste your money… Spend it on model trains or literally anything else… Secondly, if it was on the back of an ad with a sexy lady? It feels like a little confirmation that every insecurity I want proven wrong is actually true.

And maybe it is true. What a fucking bummer, in that case. These are not high-status feelings to share, but at least they’re more defensible than… Disrespecting women’s sports.

Criticism To Men: This Is Not the Gender War You Think It Is

As a Sympathetic Take Enjoyer, It’s not as if I don’t understand the draw of never being sympathetic about where another person is coming from. I want to do it constantly, yet I rein myself in… Even though it’s not as fun. It would be easy to classify all men as prosaic gooners and wipe my hands of it. But as a man knower and man enjoyer, I know that’s not true.

So, the Sydney Sweeney Novig ad made some women angry… And as stated above, since many of those women (in my opinion) can’t work through and verbalize why they’re angry about it, many men are flapping their paws like happy seals who just got tossed a fish. Maybe in another context defending the half-naked celebrity of your dreams would be fine. But if you’re defending the half-naked celebrity of your dreams who owns equity in the gambling platform that they are advertising to you (the demographic that suffers most from gambling)… You’re not really dying on the correct hill either, are you?

I am an old, ugly broad who is married. I don’t have any skin in the game to prove anything. I’m not trying to prove anything. But being old has given me time to witness that this cycle of conversation tends to boil down to: Women are trying to take away my toys. Depending on the toy, this is not always a fair statement.

A prime example for me is when clothing companies employed larger models to show off pieces of clothing that came in larger sizes. The function of doing this might have been in part a means to showcase a brand’s inclusivity. It was probably also because if you make a piece of clothing in a larger size with intent to sell it, showing what it looks like on a person who is that size helps sell the clothing. Men are not buying the bra and panty set (usually), even if they might enjoy the copy ad with a sexy lady wearing it. It’s not their toy (usually).

I don’t think I am off base by saying that if you wish to see sexy ladies, there is no shortage of them. They are still the predominant figures in film, television and music. There are still Michael Bay movies. So in the context of a changing media landscape, I just don’t think there’s actually anything to complain about. Nothing has been taken away.

Even if a woman you don’t like says Sweeney is doing a bad feminism (which I could counter with: she’s actually the most feminist by propagating the very systems that drive young men to suicide and decrease the number of low-value males in the dating ecosystem), defending an advertisement is no better in the battlefield of thought.

Final Thingie: Instead of a Gender Warrior, Be a Brackett

Sydney Sweeney and Novig (which she owns equity in) are trying to fuck you over. That’s what gambling is. You, getting fucked over. And maybe you get lucky, but over a long enough period of time, most gamblers lose money.

And it’s not just Novig. Everything about our existence seems to be peppered with endless, unfulfilling and predatory ways to separate us from our money. Be it a gadget we never needed, a phone that flips, or an app that lets you bet on whether or not nuclear war will break out. That’s very gross, isn’t it?

These things only exist because enough people are willing to play the game. And cultural significance is dictated by how much you talk about those things. Instead of talking about it, it would probably be a better idea to collectively pump our shotguns (in Minecraft) at the salesman knocking on our door.
