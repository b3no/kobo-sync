The “Trying To Make Friends as an Adult” Fall Field Report

Seven events I attended (and threw) to try and make friends as a socially awkward adult this fall

Cartoons Hate Her
Oct 29
∙
Paid
 
READ IN APP
 

One of the first topics I covered on this Substack was my longstanding difficulties making friends. The irony is not lost on me that I’ve made a career analyzing social dynamics while not being socially gifted myself, but this actually shouldn’t be surprising at all. People naturally born with social skills never have to think much about them, and have very little to analyze. Me, on the other hand? I have been analyzing what’s wrong with my personality and social skills for years, and (I like to think) finally taught myself how to make friends in my late twenties. My childhood and most of my young adulthood was spent throwing parties nobody attended, wondering why women didn’t call me back after “friend dates,” and regrettably, breaking into musical impressions at other people’s birthday parties.

I’ve come a long way. Sure, plenty of people probably still find me annoying (the Internet is not shy about this), but one of my resolutions for 2025 was to see friends…wait for it…once a month. After sandbagging that goal, I now see friends about once a week, which is a really great frequency for me. I still have yet to throw a well-attended party that was not for my child’s school class but…perhaps that’s a better resolution for 2027.

So in the interim, I’m keeping track of my attempts to socialize, build my social skills and make new friends, and I will be documenting these experiences on a semi-regular basis (Quarterly? You tell me how much you want this!) along with what I wore to each event, because why not enjoy some fashion with the social awkwardness?

If you are an IRL friend of mine, don’t worry—I’m not secretly taking notes during our hangouts to make fun of you. If I plan on making fun of anyone, it’s myself.

The Happy Hour

When my son started at his school this year, there was a happy hour for parents at a nearby bar. Because we couldn’t find a sitter (tale as old as time) I had to go alone and leave my husband at home with the kids. Being out by myself at night can be a little anxiety-provoking (I know this is “giving zoomer” but I have OCD and am actively working on it!) and I decided to face this exposure head-on. I also don’t really drink, so I couldn’t rely on alcohol to calm my nerves or make me more sociable. To top it off, about fifteen minutes before I had to leave, my toddler stepped on a wasp and began screaming for me, right as I was about to leave.

I felt like the worst mother in the world, leaving my wasp sting survivor child to go to this stupid happy hour. I held her, hugged her, and asked my husband to go instead so I could stay home with her, but by then she had stopped crying and was happily watching Bluey. Even though she was feeling fine, I was too worried about her suddenly collapsing from a previously unknown wasp allergy. I began Googling “child got stung by wasp how long before allergic reaction” and I realized that if she suddenly swelled up like Violet Beauregarde I would discover this information via text from my husband in the middle of a public place thirty minutes away. There was approximately a zero percent chance that I was going to have a good time.

The happy hour was at a bar, so when I arrived, I had no idea which people were parents from the school versus just random adults between the ages of 30 and 45. Eventually I ran into a woman who looked equally lost, who turned out to be another mom of a new student. She introduced me to her friends, who all seemed very nice, and we got a booth together.

Now, I have a long history of, in moments of social panic, glomming onto a group (whether they’re the right fit for me or not) and then missing out on the opportunity to connect with other people. I did not want that to happen, and as I shimmied into the booth with these women, I had a feeling I was going to be in that booth all night. It didn’t help that they were already friends with each other, so they probably had no real urge to go out and meet new people, but I did.

It also turned out that all these women were expats from Europe and Asia, and the vast majority of them were planning on leaving the United States within the next year. I couldn’t tell if they were serious, or if they were doing the expat version of the “I’m totally moving to Canada if Trump gets elected again” thing, but even if they were, they actually had a much more viable path to do this than, say, Lena Dunham, so I took it seriously.

Normally, I would relish any opportunity to have some chit-chat even if I know I’ll never see the people again, but in this case I had to think about the ROI of my time and attention during a relatively brief (yet fairly important!) happy hour, and I was concerned that if I spent all my time talking to people who were about to leave the country, I might be missing some more reliable long-term friend opportunities. My efforts would be futile at this table—or at the very least, staying there for the whole night would be a bad idea—but I didn’t want to be rude because these women were genuinely very nice.

I did what I hoped was a sneaky move: realizing that nobody was coming by to take our order, I announced that I’d be grabbing a drink from the bar, and encouraged them to join me. Nobody did. Then I went to the bar and got a seltzer water.

At that point, I saw a woman who I vaguely recognized from an orientation event and we had a great time talking. She was extremely dynamic and funny. At this point, I realized she was carrying a distinctive metal water bottle which looked identical to the one my husband reported losing at pickup the other day. I asked her where she got it and she said it was “a gift.” I would very much like to befriend her, but I will have to make sure not to let my husband see the water bottle to avoid a real Curb Your Enthusiasm moment.

As for the earlier table of women, I did wind up befriending one of them via a playdate a few weeks later. As far as I can tell, at least she is not planning on leaving the country.

The Parent Picnic

On a nice sunny day, a family from my son’s school class hosted a picnic at a nearby park. We were eager to go, because we are always looking for new ways to socialize and meet new potential friends. We showed up a bit late, so we were worried we had missed all the fun, but as it turns out we were the only other family there, aside from the (very nice and affable) family who was hosting. This experience partially inspired my earlier article about how many parents make zero effort to meet anyone new, because the couple hosting the picnic validated this theory—they had hosted other parent events and found that a very small percentage of parents from the class were interested in going.

Anyway, we had a really amazing time—a rare scenario where we like both the husband and the wife equally—and we’ve since seen them again several times. Very nice people! (I read that in the Trump voice, and while I meant it genuinely, I’m not revising it.)

A few setbacks though, none of which were their fault.

First, our six-year-old son was bored, and he was not subtle about signaling this. His go-to move was theatrically groaning, “I’m so bored.” At times, he would say it like more of a warning, rapidly chanting, “About to be bored, about to be bored, about to be bored.”

Then, I saw a couple show up with a gigantic and scary-looking pit bull and then plant their asses about twenty feet away to just…sit there. Technically, they weren’t doing anything wrong, but you can bet your ass my eyes were fixed on that dog, treating it more or less like a tiger on a leash. I cringed as my toddler and a few of the other kids started dancing and waving colorful streamers and playing whistles, doing their best to resemble living, breathing squeaky toys.

I’ve seen the statistics on pit bulls, and I’m fully pilled to the gills on this. Typically, when I see a pit bull I just vacate the premises but I was still trying to make friends with this lovely couple, so I tried my best to push forward and ignore my fears. But man, did that dog throw me off my game. I was unable to make eye contact, not because of neurodivergence but because I was staring at the dog the entire time. Finally, I just said “Sorry guys, that dog over there is freaking me out.”

As it turned out, this couple had never heard of pit bulls being especially dangerous, and the husband kindly asked me if I had experienced dog-related trauma as a child. (The funny thing is, I had, but it was at the paws of a tiny terrier and the story makes me look like a massive pussy.) So I stammered for a bit and said, “Well, a few close calls!” and then proceeded to tell him stories about times that we spotted unleashed pit bulls many feet away and fled, which just made me look more insane. I was very close to pulling up my phone and attempting to radicalize him on this topic but instead I changed the subject.

Luckily, I don’t think I fucked this one up too badly, as they still wanted to trade numbers and have asked to hang out again.

The Trio Dinner

One of my longest-term local friends, Rebecca, is a fabulous host and extrovert, and is always introducing people and bringing people together. A while back, she had introduced me to a friend of hers, Julia, whose son made fast friends with mine at a summer program. We had one playdate with Julia, but then lost touch (I assumed she didn’t want to hang out again, but I had also failed to text her, so she could have assumed the same thing.) We got back in touch over the summer, and decided to do something that also included Rebecca since she had introduced us. Julia offered to host Rebecca’s family and mine for dinner.

This is basically my dream scenario: it’s someone else’s house (I don’t know why, but I love going to other people’s houses), both a familiar and a new friend, kids roughly the same age who get along, and no risk of an errant pit bull showing up to friendblock me.

The only setback was that most of the dinner took place outside in the apartment courtyard, where there was a large fountain. My three-year-old daughter kept trying to climb the fountain, insisting she “wasn’t going to fall in.” Sure, you would say that! Unreliable narrator alert! Rebecca’s lovely eleven-year-old daughter offered to babysit my toddler so that the adults could enjoy dinner, but I found myself hovering to make sure the kids didn’t do anything dangerous, especially after I saw them walking around with cups of water I didn’t recognize, which I fear they had procured from some dirty bucket. I was the only adult who was hovering over the kids, which I chalked up to being the only adult with a three-year-old (aside from my husband who was not terribly concerned) but also, I can’t tell if maybe I was just being crazy. Many such cases. I was able to calm down significantly when we all went inside, although I did glance at the large picture windows to make sure they were locked.

The School Family Event

I don’t even have a photo of my outfit here because I showed up in lounge clothes. Our son’s school was having a game night and my son was desperate to win a Target gift card so he could buy a long-coveted Aladdin play set. Initially, my plan for this event was to socialize (Rebecca was going to be there) but instead, I wound up getting way too intense about the game because I wanted to get him the Aladdin playset just as badly as he wanted to get it, but to avoid spoiling him I couldn’t just buy it. We proceeded to lose games all night and my son was frustrated to tears, but by the end of the night he won a raffle and the prize was none other than a Target gift card. The Aladdin play set was shipped the very next day.

Did I have a good time? No, not really. It was extremely loud and I was about to have a full-blown meltdown because I couldn’t hear anything and I was afraid we were missing important details about the game, and then I’d have to come up with some other scheme to make my son “earn” the Aladdin play set before a holiday. At one point before the raffle, my husband suggested we leave and I found myself earnestly growling at him, “But we haven’t even won anything yet.” My eyes were on the prize and I was unable to socialize, even with Rebecca. But we got the gift card, and by now the Aladdin play set is prominently featured in our home and a favorite toy already.

The Friend’s Birthday Party

Rebecca had her fortieth birthday party, and my husband and I had been working tirelessly to find a sitter leading up to the big night. Unfortunately, his parents were out of town, and on top of that, every other sitter we knew (even ones we only knew through friends and word of mouth) was unavailable. NOBODY WANTS TO WORK ANYMORE! Ultimately, I had to go alone. Again.

I had really been looking forward to this party, because we don’t get invited to many, but without my husband I was kind of dreading it. I knew that Rebecca would likely be tied up with friends all night, I couldn’t expect to stick to her the whole time, and I would have to just meet new people all by myself. But who was I kidding? I’m literally writing a book on the importance of socializing even when you’re nervous, anxious or tired. I did a cold plunge and told myself to stop being such a pussy. I promised myself I would stay for at least an hour, even if I felt uncomfortable and even if nobody wanted to talk to me.

When I first arrived at the party, Rebecca introduced me to a friend of hers who also happened to be in a creative industry. I realized quickly that this peron, while also a creative, was much more serious and intellectual than I was. She was explaining her work to me, which all sounded painfully intimidating and legitimate, and then I tried to explain what I did and for some reason I wound up showing her this comic:

She said something along the lines of, “Okay, I think I get it. Some Atlantic piece comes out about women swearing off men, and then you need to issue a response?” And like, yeah. She completely got what I’m all about. (In case you were wondering, I did explain the KN95 lesbian bar situation to her.)

I chatted with that woman for a while, and later the mom from the parent picnic and the mom from the trio dinner (Julia) showed. A crossover episode! The three of us wound up chatting for so long that I was a full hour late coming home.

My Halloween Party

We hosted a Halloween party for my son’s class, because Halloween is my son’s favorite holiday. Unlike other parties I’ve thrown, this one had great turnout. However, two important points: first, none of these people came because they liked me—they didn’t know me at all and they came because they were invited to an all-class party. Second, only about ten families of the class RSVP’ed at all and the class is made up of almost thirty kids! So, even when there’s a zero percent chance of my personality being the problem, only 30% of a list will even RSVP at all.

But, as it turns out, “just” seven families can make a great party, especially since we included siblings. The weather was nice, we brought out the bouncy castle, and supplied the kids with a bottomless trough of candy. Terrible day for any parents who give out pretzels on Halloween, but I didn’t want their demonic presence at my house anyway.

We also had a big picnic blanket with a variety of books for kids of different ages. This turned out to be a great party decision, because while most of the kids in attendance were between three and six, there were some older kids and babies who were either too young or too old for bouncy castle mayhem. My son really loves the Bunny versus Monkey and Dog Man comic books, so we laid out a bunch of those for older kids, plus some fun Halloween board books for babies. Next time we throw a backyard party, we will be sure to do this again!

I definitely found myself not deliberately focusing on my social skills, because I was having a good time and feeling less self-conscious. At one point, I found myself telling the story about my son’s genie lamp fiasco, which is arguably one of the funnier stories in my arsenal, but I worried I dragged on a bit too long. On the bright side, no Trump impressions were performed.

The Backyard Gathering

(This outfit looked better in person)

To wrap off this round of social events (and to thank Julia for hosting recently) I invited Rebecca and Julia over to my house for some apps and drinks. Of course, it was kid-friendly. At this point, the event was less about meeting new people and more about hanging out with people I already know, but it was a good exercise in hosting.

Six kids were in attendance, including my own. We brought out the bouncy castle because we were blessed with some good weather, and my son was locked into one of his builder subscription boxes, which he was able to complete with the help of Rebecca and Julia’s eldest children. Later, the kids took turns doing various performances—Julia’s son sang karaoke with Rebecca’s daughter performing backup dance moves, and my son performed a magic show with, of course, The Elder.

I do have to come clean about one thing though. Rebecca ribbed me toward the end of the event and congratulated me for resisting the urge to do a Trump impression. At this point, Julia, who has never heard (or even been informed of) the Trump impression, said, “Wait, you do a Trump impression? I want to hear it!”

At this point, I was giving:

Anyway, I want to say no Trump impressions were performed all season, but this one was in fact requested, so I can’t be blamed. Also, I was getting over a cold and my voice was very raspy so it was probably one of my best Trump impressions from the standpoint of technique.

All in all: seven events, only one Trump impression. That’s progress.
