A Tale of two Ericas

From grade school through high school, there were two girls named Erica. They were both relatively popular girls, and into middle school and high school they did not struggle with dates. In spite of bearing no relation to one another they both had very similar faces. This was always a bit of a novelty for me as an outside observer since they were both named Erica. The similar face they shared had always reminded me of, not to be a mean girl, the Treasure Troll dolls that were popular in the early 1990’s.

It is not a face anyone would associate with beauty, even if you shellac it with some Covergirl. They both had wide, flat noses and broad mouths that lacked definition. As a child who was too online, I found it peculiar that they didn’t seem to suffer for these features. If they’d posted their photos online, surely anyone would have roasted them with the Troll doll comparison. Two women with the same name, and the same face that I felt was an ugly face were doing just fine socially and it drove me bananas.

What I didn’t understand at the time was that I was being given the first of two very important lessons. The first one being that how people behave online is a far cry from how they interact in real life. Most people hold their ugly thoughts in and a lot of them don’t even have ugly thoughts in the first place.

The second lesson was that men don’t actually care about the face card.

The big lie they tell every little fat girl is that she has a pretty face. There is a moment where you realize that you are different, and similar to a descending angel, some well-meaning aunt or god forbid your fucking mom flutters down at your darkest moment and declares to you: Well, you have a beautiful face. And you hold onto that honor like a dog with a bone. You clutch the status of your perfect, beautiful face.

Until you see that so many boys have crushes on the troll doll girls. Don’t they understand that they look like troll dolls and those are not cute? It was the most frustrating realization of my life! My face! My beautiful face didn’t matter at all!

This meant that the only thing that mattered to men was the body. Everything was fair game, so long as that game looked good in a bikini. And that’s what it legitimately felt like as I watched people hook up and gossip about who was into who with friends. Miss Skeksis had plenty of suitors, so long as she had dem collarbones. They didn’t care at all about pretty faces as a whole. They just cared if the body looked good.

I absorbed a great deal of information and did my best to contextualize it as a young person. But from my perspective as a social outsider, all I could conclude was the most important thing is the body. I also determined that style and aesthetics were a superficial layer that men passed off as depth — A man who says he’s not into girls like that just likes girls like that with bad dye jobs and a different playlist — It’s all about da body.

Imagine being envious of a butterface. Butterfaces could still get boyfriends, and I wanted to make out and wear someone else’s hoodie, goddamnit. Eventually, to add insult to injury, I also learned that my Mommy lied to me and my face isn’t really that beautiful either. I have no upper lip, my nose is big and my eyes are too close together. I have brown hair and brown eyes which is boring. I got nothin! There was nothing special about my fucking face. Fuck you, mom. Lyin’ ass hoe.

Still, it was an endless pervasive thought. I would always notice when a cute guy was dating a girl who’s face was, in my opinion, lacking. At this time, I was dealing with my biggest crush never noticing me, in spite of (what I assumed were) very important similarities, in favor of mooning over a tall, thin blond who had a nice face. So I put it in the bucket of things men didn’t care about. They also didn’t care about your personality. The face didn’t matter (except sometimes???). The personality didn’t matter. No one was ever going to like me because I was fat.

Online didn’t help either. Online, at the time and in the spaces I frequented, people could be brutal about a person’s appearance. Anyone was open to criticism about their supposed flaws. The internet is still like this, and it’s annoying.

Mid: Margot Robbie, Zendaya, Sydney Sweeney.

Men online have been calling conventionally attractive celebrities ugly ever since they could. I do not know the exact reason why this is. The fact of the matter is that they don’t usually put ugly people on your television and in your music. You’ll also notice that there has never been a lot of noise about character actors being ugly. I figure this is because they aren’t putting Kathy Bates and Margo Martindale on billboards and forcing us to look at them. And no one cares that Brittany Howard from Alabama Shakes isn’t hot because people who care about hot people performing their music generally don’t listen to Alabama Shakes.

Venture into the profile of a man declaring the midness of a modern celebutante and chances are that if he has the “in a relationship with…” on his profile, the woman in question is going to be… Well…
She certainly ain’t gonna be no Margot Robbie.

So what the fuck is happening here? Why are all these ugly broads finding boyfriends and husbands. And why are all these objectively gorgeous women being called ugly? What the fuck is going on? It makes it all so confusing! Imagine being a girl and trying to make sense of this information. You’re gonna go crazy.

The guys who dated Troll Face, in all likelihood have fired off on twitter about how Famous Female Celebrity is actually not that hot. So what the hell do men actually find attractive (and why isn’t it ME!?!)?

The first I can decode is that being a celebrity carries with it the unfortunate issue of people just getting sick of you. We didn’t really hit peak “Robbie is Mid” until Barbie kind of left everyone oversaturated. Same with Sweeney and same with Zendaya. You can probably find people explaining why Britney Spears isn’t attractive twenty years ago on countless abandoned forums. I’ve said it before, but I’m too lazy to link it: People are dumb and don’t know how to explain the complexities of what they’re feeling. When it comes to being tired of seeing someone’s face, they’ll just go with “Ugly.”

Men Don’t Care About the Face Card, Thank God. (But they do care if you annoy them)

Men don’t care about the face card. Which is probably a good thing. If a guy doesn’t like a woman, he’ll notice all the things that are wrong with her face. If he doesn’t, or even really enjoys her company, then those supposed flaws might become endearing. Suddenly, he’ll be on his social media platform of choice espousing how women with horsefaces and big gums are actually the most beautiful women of all, if he loves his horsefaced broad enough.

That same man, when he gets sick of [any flavor of the week female celebrity] will be on his social media platform of choice, talking about how she’s not actually that pretty, and if he’s particularly enterprising he will find a graph that explains why the angle of her eyelids is bad or something. It’s not about what she looks like. It’s that she’s starting to annoy him — Maybe because she ruined Spider-Man or said something in an interview that he thought was stupid.

My husband also proclaims that women on the television are ugly, when they are prettier than I am. He also says I am pretty when I’m in my luteal phase and have greasy hair. Earlier in our relationship, this incongruity confused me until I figured out he just thought those women were annoying and I am not annoying (to him). But without understanding the subtleties of that, it’s confusing to witness.

Most people aren’t actually ugly. Most people aren’t sideshow freaks. Culturally, we have optimized and homogenized appearance within popular media to be very a narrow spectrum. Social media apps have built-in default face filters which further fucks our collective perception of self… But it’s a minuscule amount of people who could actually be considered ugly. Most everyone is going to be fine. Someone is going to see your face and think “Wow! What a cute face!” unless things are absolutely ruinous (apologies to any burn victims reading this).

I learned this in my mid twenties. Even though my face doesn’t line up with the “perfect face chart", it’s still a face I can work with. Even though I am fat, I’ve always been fat in a way that is unmistakably feminine and can be enhanced well enough to draw some eyes to it in a positive appraisal. It was a gradual demystification on the nature of attractiveness. None of the men I dated were freaked out that my eyes are a little close together. Most of them thought my lips were cute even though I don’t have a top one.

Younger people are more prominent online, and they also take this shit seriously because they don’t really have enough experience to see beyond these very superficial things. They’re kids and they’re not that interesting. Being attractive is a more tangible concept than one’s own interiority. Of course this means that when it comes to the subject of what’s attractive? those online young people are going to have hard, loud opinions. They’ll have manifestos about ratios. They’ll throw out the perfect face chart.

Within this mess, you’ll also find that man who writes a manifesto about the regal nature of women with Horse Faces. Within the noise, he is simply reconciling with the fact that a woman with a horseface does it for him, and he is defending what he loves to a cruel society that might belittle her. He is grappling with the predetermined rules of society telling him one thing, while he is feeling the actual truth of another. He knows he shouldn’t think his Horse Face is beautiful, but he does and must defend her. How can she be ugly if he thinks she is beautiful?

This is usually part of the article, where I’d throw a bunch of pictures of unconventional looking female celebrities at you while screaming “IT DIDN’T USED TO BE THAT WAY.” Instead, I am going to show you a photo of the actress Martha Plimpton.

Yeah, yeah, yeah. You know my game by now. Martha doesn’t look anything like the female celebrities that I mentioned above, and contextually she wasn’t making it on any sexy lady lists in the early 90’s either. The important thing about Martha is that she used to date Tragically Deceased Hollywood Heart-Throb, River Phoenix. When people talk about River, it’s usually in the same context as James Dean. If he were young and alive today, he’d be dating a Kardashian. He was a pretty boy, and cool. And he dated goofy looking Martha Plimpton. He didn’t care about her face card. If he could, he would have probably gone online to explain why women with heavy brows and no top lip are the most beautiful kind of women.

The self can’t be trusted to self-report, when it doesn’t know itself. And most people don’t really know themselves. Nor does data collection provide enough depth to determine every single detail that comes together when one is determining their attraction to another. When you give a man the option to select what he believes are the most beautiful faces from a list, he’s going to pick the ones that are the most technically beautiful. If a woman with the one of the same faces he chose says something stupid to him or is generally unpleasant, he’s going to notice her negative canthal tilt and move on… Unless he just wants to sleep with her.
