Fiona the Hippo

In a story that feels right out of Parks and Recreation, a beloved and famous hippo in the Cincinnati Zoo, died after swallowing a knit hat that was in her enclosure. According to the zoo officials, the knit hat belonged to an anonymous child (age unclear, rumors abound) who threw the hat into the air, only for the hat to hit a ceiling fan and accidentally land in the hippo enclosure through a one-foot gap in the glass.

Now, I had no idea who Fiona the hippo was before this incident, and after reading up on her and her unlikely survival (she was born severely premature and became a local icon) I was pretty darn sad about her dying in a freak accident. In fact, I don’t think I can hear about any elephant, rhino or hippo dying without feeling a little choked up, and I can’t even really explain why. But the denizens of Twitter had a very hard time processing the idea of a random, non-malicious and unpredictable incident causing harm. If it was an accident, WHOSE BLOOD CAN WE BE AFTER?!

For those of you wondering why eating a knit hat would kill a hippo, I had to look this up myself too. Apparently, the digestive tract of a hippo is extremely narrow and nearly anything can cause a bowel obstruction. However, despite this being basically the definition of a “weird fact,” the Fiona fandom on Twitter was quick to blame the child’s anonymous parents for failing to prevent such an obvious tragedy. After all, we all know it’s important to give your kids the safety talks: stop, drop and roll, don’t do drugs, and never throw a hat anywhere near a hippo exhibit.
