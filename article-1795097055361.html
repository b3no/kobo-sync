Matt Gurney: Can we please give our political class an emergency gonad transplant?

My precious, anonymous senior Tory. My fellow voyager on this journey we call life. You could just say something.

Matt Gurney
Nov 19
 
READ IN APP
 

By: Matt Gurney

You may have missed it, especially if you’re one of those unlucky folks who happens to live outside this wonderful province of Ontario. But just a few days ago, the Toronto Star published an absolutely delightful story about some dissension in the ranks of Premier Doug Ford’s government.

The story is interesting on its own merits. But I think it works better as part of a meta-analysis of what is going wrong with our politics right across the continent. Namely, can we please do some emergency gonad transplants for our leaders’ fellow officials, senior staff and key advisers? Because a distressing number of them seem to have misplaced the set that God gave them at birth.

Let me tell you a bit about the Ontario story so you can fit it into the bigger picture. Ford has got it into his head that Ontario’s side of Niagara Falls needs some big, shiny new casinos, as part of an economic revitalization effort.

There are some real reasons to question the wisdom of a casino-based plan. First of all, Ontario has already rolled out a pretty robust ecosystem of online gambling. (Maybe too robust, but we’ll shelve that for another day.) Would more casinos in Niagara generate new gambling revenue? Or would they just cannibalize the existing online revenue?

The second objection, and it’s very much related to the first, is that Niagara Falls already has old-school brick-and-mortar casinos. And Ontario’s casinos are already reporting declining revenue, probably in part because of the aforementioned pull of online gambling, but also potentially, in Niagara’s case, due to competition from casinos across the border in the United States. The case for more supply given available demand is, to put it mildly, unproven.

And finally, as the Star article notes, the infrastructure connecting Niagara Falls to the rest of Ontario is already overwhelmed. It’s all well and good to talk about drawing tens of millions of additional visitors to Niagara Falls every year. But as someone who’s already an occasional visitor, let me tell you, the traffic ain’t fun. Trying to shoehorn more people in before you address the infrastructure that allows them to get there seems like a bad idea.

So that’s the crux of the Star article. But what really jumped out at me was an absolutely delightful line about what it’s been like trying to get Ford to realize that his plan might actually be terrible. Here’s what a Tory insider told the Star:

“This is like the private plane all over again. Everyone knows it’s a dumb idea but no one wants to tell him until after everything goes south.”

That caught my attention because this seems like a much more solvable problem than the social consequences of gambling, the needs of the economy in the Niagara Falls region, or the transportation infrastructure linking Niagara Falls to the Greater Toronto Area and points beyond. Those are genuinely complicated issues.

Looking your boss dead in the eyes and telling him what he’s proposing is stupid, in contrast, isn’t complicated at all. It could be career-limiting. It would certainly be socially awkward. But among all the problems that could face senior operatives within the Ontario government, this has got to be one of the easier ones to handle. All that’s required is a teeny-tiny bit of conviction and, like I said, some trace remnants of your gonads.

Like, my dude. My precious, anonymous senior Tory. My fellow voyager on this journey we call life.

You could just say something.

That’s it. You could just say something. You don’t even have to think of something new to say. You could just slightly repurpose the words you already uttered or in some way electronically transmitted to my colleagues at the Star. Watch. I’ll demonstrate:

Hey, Doug? Here is something that some of your people would like to tell you, but for some inexplicable reason, apparently cannot. Your casino plan for Niagara Falls? They aren’t a fan. To wit: “This is like the private plane all over again. Everyone knows it’s a dumb idea but no one wants to tell [you] until after everything goes south.” Thank you, Premier Ford.

See? Was that so hard?

Alas, it apparently is indeed so hard. It seems like conviction and gonads are in short supply in and around the leader of Canada’s most populous province. Which is why you have concerned insiders talking to the Toronto Star instead of the premier.

I wish this was a problem confined to Queen’s Park. But as I was reading the absolutely, delightfully self-devastating quote someone unwittingly gave the Star’s intrepid reporters, I was also recalling a series of press conferences last week in Washington, D.C., after the nationwide ground stop at U.S. airports.

The exact cause of the disruption has yet to be fully established, but every indicator to date points to some kind of telecommunications disruption. Okay, whatever. Not optimal but, sure. Stuff happens. But what really struck me from these press conferences, and it had a lot in common with other video we often see coming out of Washington, is how palpably conscious the officials before the cameras are of their need to inject obsequious praise for President Donald Trump into their remarks at a fairly steady pace.

My favourite moment? The Homeland Security secretary, Markwayne Mullen, standing behind a White House lectern after the U.S. air-traffic-control system crashed and saying, with a straight face and as a compliment, that “This is what the leadership of President Trump looks like.”

I mean, gosh, Mr. Secretary. If you insist.

Kidding aside, these guys are clearly well aware that the boss is going to watch clips of their press conference and are sure to pepper their remarks about the actual problem at hand with compliments for Trump. We all know what that is. If there’s ever a moment to go out and laud the visionary leadership of the president du jour, it’s probably not in the middle of a nationwide debacle that exists solely under the jurisdiction of the federal government. But these guys know that their job security hinges on how effectively they feed the president’s notoriously gigantic ego.

These are grown men who are grovelling with a straight face in the hopes that their boss catches a clip of it on the news. They’ve concluded that this is their only path to success, or at least their only way to avoid termination, and they don’t have the pride and self-respect to find that problematic. So they kiss ass. For them, I only wish two things: an eventual rediscovery of some sliver of self-respect, and, if that proves too much, at least shoes that fit.

Other examples abound. One could easily point to the absolutely pathetic performance of the Liberal caucus during the dying days of the former prime minister’s tenure. On our podcast, I began collectively referring to them as gormless weasels because of how utterly incapable of articulating an independent thought or acting in their own political best interests they proved themselves to be. Recall that in the end it took only a single letter from Chrystia Freeland, who was not among that government’s best political operatives, to bring down Trudeau. But nobody had the guts to actually do it until Freeland did it in her own self-defence.

So there you go. Three examples, covering two countries and the left and right in Canada. A little something for everyone. So now let’s ask the real question: What are we doing? What the hell is wrong with these people? What the hell is wrong with us for tolerating it?

In other parts of the world, political accountability is still a thing that people can impose on their bosses. Australians love purging their PMs; if anything, the British seem to enjoy it too much. The Brits probably ought to tone it down a bit, but I’d certainly welcome a little bit more of that kind of pluck over here in North America.

Justin Trudeau ended up being a house of cards. Trump is effectively already a lame duck who has just suffered a massive defeat at the hands of the American people. Ford is a marshmallow notorious for backing down when actually confronted with anything. Indeed, other than his enduring dream of putting casinos into cities, that’s probably what he’s best known for.

I don’t think it would be controversial for me to say that we probably need to punch up the overall quality of our political leadership. But I’m starting to think the problem is as much in the concentric rings of fellow politicians, staffers and advisers who surround our leaders. Calling people out for their stupidity is something we need to see more of, and it doesn’t kill you. Everyone needs someone they trust who’s got the guts enough to say, you know what? This is dumb. Don’t do this.

So please, all of you, rediscover whatever pride and self-respect and courage you were theoretically born with. Your primary responsibility is to the people who elected you (or your boss). Act like it.
