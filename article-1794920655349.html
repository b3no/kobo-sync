Latvia: REASSURANCE // 26-02

Last week Canadian soldiers and their allies travelled across Latvia to sharpen the defence of NATO’s eastern edge. Photographer Mikolaj Nowacki was with them.

Paul Wells
Nov 17
 
READ IN APP
 

Most Canadian soldiers’ rotation through an overseas assignment lasts six months. These photos show the second rotation of the year — roto 26-02 — of Operation REASSURANCE, the largest overseas deployment of the Canadian Armed Forces. These photos were taken for me — or rather for you at my request — by Mikolaj Nowacki, an award-winning Polish photojournalist. Readers can follow his work on Instagram at @mikolajnowacki.

Most of the soldiers in these photos are from 5 Canadian Mechanized Brigade Group in Valcartier, outside Quebec City. Stationed at Military Base Ādaži outside Riga, which has tripled in size since I last saw it in 2017, the Canadians in Latvia are there to lead forces from several other NATO countries in ensuring NATO’s front line of defence against any potential Russian attack.

Later this week I’ll write at greater length about how the CAF’s approach to potential combat is changing, quickly, to reflect the drone-driven upheaval in military doctrine driven by wars in Ukraine, the Persian Gulf and the African Sahel. But I’m always so fascinated to watch soldiers at work, and one thing I wanted to do with this trip is to share some of those images with you.

A couple of years ago I made plans to cover Canadian soldiers training Ukrainians in Poland, but those plans fell through. That was when I became aware of Nowacki’s work, which has also been published in The Sunday Times Magazine, Le Monde, Stern, Neue Zürcher Zeitung, National Geographic (Poland), and elsewhere. When my plans for this Latvia trip came together, I asked him to come take photos. His first question was whether he could stay with the soldiers he’d be photographing, sleeping in their camps so he could cover full days with them. The work you see here is a product of that dedication. The last photo in this post shows him with some of our soldiers. On a laptop, you can click on any of these photos to enlarge.
