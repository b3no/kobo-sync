Do You Want An Enormous Man, or Do You Just Want to Feel Tiny?

Sometimes, the turn-on is the height gap, not the height.

I feel a bit weird writing about women’s preference for tall men. On the one hand, it’s a bit self-limiting if you take it to the extremes or view it as a dealbreaker. Only 15% of men are over six feet, and that’s not even accounting for all the other variables which might disqualify that already-small group. But on the other hand, I feel weird demonizing the Main Shallow Preference for women when the current zeitgeist is repeatedly informing women of the supposedly-unknown “hard truths” about men’s biological preferences for youth and big boobs, and concluding that any prickling over these lectures is merely the shrieking of coping harridans who can’t accept biology. Can women not have a crumb of red-blooded shallowness too?

I also have to take into account an area of bias: I don’t care much about a man’s height myself. All things created equal, I suppose I’d prefer a taller man over a short man, but most of the time, all things are not created equal, and height has always been last on my list of priorities, kind of like how a guy might feel about a woman with really pretty shoulders.

But it’s not hard to see why a woman’s preference for very tall men can be perfectly natural, the same way a man’s preference for young and curvy women is just accepted as biological reality, whether the man in question stands much of a chance with one. Tall men likely aren’t actually healthier than short men (in fact, there are plenty of health risks that come along with ultra-tall physiques) but from a cavewoman perspective, a really enormous guy (both tall and muscular) would presumably be the most effective protector and clearly sexually dimorphic (ie: more masculine on a sliding scale from feminine to masculine). Because men start to shrink around forty, height can also be a very rough, lizard-brain proxy for prime reproductive age and high-quality sperm.

But I think there’s another motivation at play, which interestingly doesn’t seem to exist in reverse for men: the desire for a really big man so you can feel as small as possible, effectively seeking out a partner with high sexual dimorphism as a way of boosting your own. It’s less about the tall man himself, and more about acquiring as enormous of a “height gap” as possible, which is why this height preference is prevalent in short women, who should in theory consider a 5’8” guy more than tall enough.

While most women won’t be able to marry a 6’4” guy (there are only so many to go around!) fantasy and erotica give us a clue into what women want in the abstract: absolute fucking units. Substacker EK Carlisle also theorizes that a great deal of the desire not only for tall men, but for extremely tall men, is about women wanting to feel smaller. I agree. Many women will just admit this to you without you having to wonder.

I had this conversation with a female friend a while back. She’s 5’10” and had a very strict “only over 6’2” dating policy. In a way I hoped was helpful (but ultimately wasn’t) I encouraged her to expand her horizons to all men taller than she was, including men who were “only” 5’11”. She told me that this would be the equivalent of asking me to date a man who was morbidly obese. She said any man that even came close to her height would make her feel “huge” and sexual attraction would be impossible.

I thought this was pretty odd, since she wasn’t exactly Ms. Trunchbull. She was tall, yes, but pretty, slender and very feminine. But she said being so tall her whole life left her feeling big and manly, and she wanted to find a man who would make her feel more feminine, in the form of feeling small. She also pointed out that at 5’5”, I wouldn’t understand.

Consider my average height privilege checked. She was right that although I’ve had my own fair share of issues with femininity, I don’t relate to this particular one. But what I find interesting is that this feeling is not limited to the tall women who, of anyone, “deserve” to feel this way. Plenty of ultra-short women—shorter than me!—want their men as tall as possible, even though one must imagine the mechanics of the whole thing start to turn against them at a certain point.

In fact, TikTok is rife with “height gap relationship” videos showcasing all the various ways that a man can out-size his female partner, not only in height but in hand size, shoe size, and the ability to pick her up and put her on his shoulder like a parrot.

Then, let us not forget that someone thought it was important to write an entire article on a couple who “didn’t realize” they had a two-foot height gap. This revelation, I assume, happened right around the time they figured out how tall they were.

(Of course, this is not the case for every height gap relationship, many of which I assume are just two normal people who fell in love. I am just noticing a trend here.)

I have sympathy for my 5’10” friend (who, by the way, eventually married a very tall man.) But most of the women above are not anywhere close to 5’10”! In fact, many of them are shorter than I am. It almost seems like the more extreme the height difference, the more entranced they are by the whole thing. Because it’s not even about wanting a tall man—it’s about wanting to accentuate just how tiny you are. It’s not the mondo-man, it’s the height gap. It’s not the big, strong guy. It’s the fact that you are tiny enough to fit on his shoulder. One influencer, who almost exclusively posts content about her marital height gap and how young she looks, has even used her enormous baby to show off how tiny she is.

It feels almost like a height version of the “body-checking” phenomenon in the eating disorder community, wherein people (often women) suffering from eating disorders find subtle ways to show how small they are next to various objects. Many people suspected Ariana Grande was doing this when she posted a photo of herself nestled inside a guitar case. I saw another woman on Twitter take part in this behavior when she put her over-ear headphones on either side of her waist to show how little space she took up.

It might seem confusing that women who are already small would be obsessed with appearing even smaller. I don’t blame them; loads of people have “illogical” body obsessions—and the fact that they’re obsessing over something they already have is pretty common. While some people obsess over traits they don’t have but wish they did, other people will obsess over a trait they do have, but then go overboard in optimizing it because it’s never good enough for them. I wrote about this myself when I became obsessed with waist-hip ratio in my early twenties. I actually had a decent waist-hip ratio to begin with. Arguably, if I wanted to focus on my biggest “flaws,” I had other things that were more objectively short of the feminine ideal. But instead, I focused on something that was more of an asset, but then convinced myself that only the most extreme form of that trait would be acceptable.

You see this sometimes when certain short women (#notallshortwomen) accentuate their shortness in situations where it isn’t even relevant. How many times have I watched Chopped only to see a 5’2” female chef say something like, “I may be teensy, but great things come in small packages!” Girl, there’s no stereotype that short people can’t cook.

I also suspect that a great deal of the monster-fucking fetish (yes, it’s a thing) is related to the fact that a monster can be even taller compared to you, on a scale unmatched by any human man. Admittedly, that’s not my thing! I like my men humanoid! But I don’t think the “mythical creature” thing is the whole story. Nobody is throwing her panties at Gollum or Dobby. Count Orlok of Nosferatu gets bitches, but this feels contingent on him being 6’7” and, in turn, making you feel extra tiny and frail.

This is, notably, not a thing for men. When I ran a study evaluating men’s reactions to different female bodies, testing out variables related to weight, height and different shapes, height didn’t matter at all. Men have plenty of shallow sexual preferences, but wanting an extremely short woman so they can feel taller just isn’t one of them. (Protip: a great way to make your penis feel bigger is to date a girl with a really small penis.) In fact, some female bodies (like the very skinny one) performed better at 5’10”. Most saw no impact from height at all.

Some theorists have chalked this height gap obsession up to the patriarchy and women’s desire to minimize themselves. Less plausibly, others have accused straight women of “autopedophilia.” But I find these explanations a bit silly and farfetched. First of all, women’s desire to be short (or emphasize their shortness) is not something that tracks with especially patriarchal times. Plenty of women want to emphasize being tall and willowy too, and not for especially feminist reasons. I also feel a bit weird saying, “Women hold unrealistic standard for men; women most affected, men to blame.”

Instead, I’d say there must be some innate desire (at least in some women) to maximize their own sexual dimorphism by choosing a partner who shows greater contrast. In a way, many people perform variations of this that are unrelated to height. Naturally submissive people feel more submissive in the presence of a dominant partner, and vice versa. People like partners who make them feel good about themselves, and while “attractive” is usually enough to check that box, for a woman who has a complex about being as small as possible, an enormous man is the goal.

Now, I’m not here to condemn this preference, as illogical as it may be. I know it angers tall men and short women alike, but people can’t choose what they desire. Men also can’t control their attraction to big boobs or youth—the only thing standing in their way is how many young women with big boobs are interested in them. As I’ve said before, the market will sort itself out. And if a short woman has no trouble attracting tall men, well, that’s the market speaking.

Also, plenty of women don’t care all that much about men’s height, nor do they have any particular aspirations to resemble Plankton themselves. Too often, the generalization “Women prefer tall men” is distilled into “women will only date tall men” in a game of incel telephone, and we all know that’s not true. Average-height and short men get married all the time. And per my survey on men’s bodies, while the most attractive man in the survey was exactly who you’re imagining, height was not the primary defining factor of the top ten bodies, and women rated fit and average BMI men under 6’ higher than untoned or overweight men over 6’. Hardly a “height doesn’t matter at all” white pill, but then again, women don’t get a “boobs don’t matter at all” white pill either, and thus is life.

And in case that’s still discouraging, men, never fear: Jensen Huang is only 5’7.” Now you just need to become the next CEO of Nvidia.
