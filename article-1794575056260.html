My sewing roundup for October 2026

Sewing?! Yes, sewing. While my Substack is not terribly sewing-forward, I do like to highlight my sewing projects and creations on a monthly basis for the niche group of followers who really like fashion and sewing. For those of you (I imagine, 90% of you, if not more) who don’t care about sewing or fashion, feel free to ignore and wait for another article you like better in a day or two—Many Such Takes returns this Sunday! For the sewists and sew-curious among us, read on.

First, up, something simple: I whipped up a bunch of basic long sleeve tees for my daughter in some fun patterns!

First, ice cream (I forget where I initially bought this fabric but it is on Etsy.)

Close up:

Then, some tea pots to celebrate her love of tea parties—and in a dark, busy pattern too so she doesn’t stain it! This one is from Spoonflower.

Another Spoonflower find: some whimsical fall foxes!

I also made one honoring pasta, one of her favorite foods! Unfortunately I don’t remember the exact name of this fabric, but I got it on Spoonflower. It’s giving Lisa Says Gah!

And a simple fall dress using some stretch floral twill from Lyrical Fabrics:

For my son, some fun with harem pants to support his obsession with genies. My husband has informed me he cannot wear these to school because they’re too goofy, but what else was I supposed to do with all this leftover velour that I bought for his Halloween costume? (No, the genie pants aren’t the costume. A costume, but not the costume.)

Here’s how the pants fit along with an amazing genie lamp print sweatshirt with a fleece fabric I customized and printed. All hail The Elder! Look at the drape!

A crushed velvet orange “genie costume” that can double as a sweatsuit:

Speaking of crushed velvet, I used a different one to make my own secular wimple jumpsuit, a costume I created solely for the amusement of the Internet since we aren’t going to any adult Halloween parties, and nobody would get it anyway:

A fun sewing project for my husband, although out of season. A while ago he got a stain on a Sandro T-shirt he loved, which had a graphic of a dog and duck on it. He didn’t want to get rid of the T-shirt but it was pretty unwearable. To save the graphic, I cut it out and sewed it to a new T-shirt I made using navy and white stripe knit viscose cotton blend from Mood Fabrics. Not a perfect sewing job, but it’s rustic.

And I finally finished this dress, on which I had been working for ages. It was honestly a huge pain in the ass to make, and by the time I finished I feared it would be too cold to wear it for more than a day. Here I used some textured gingham from Lyrical. The dress itself is a very altered version of the Audrina mini dress, which I’ve already made a few times. It’s lined, so it took a while but I am happy with how it turned out!

I also made a really cute mini skirt using the pattern I always use for skirts. The fabric is a really affordable cotton suiting houndstooth from Lyrical, which has a bit more structure and which I’m using to make a very extravagant Thanksgiving dress. Guest star: my teal vintage Coach suede bag, which seems to go perfectly with every outfit!

I also made these insane purple satin pants. I was inspired by these Reformation pants which are nearly $200. Instead I decided to use some cheaper purple satin from Mood Fabrics to make them (only $8 a yard!) They’re a little outlandish, but they work pretty well with a witchy vintage Betsey Johnson jacket.

Now, the most exciting moment of the post: our Halloween costumes!

This year, I made everyone’s costume, which was a super fun challenge. I gave myself a good 1-2 month runway by forcing my kids to pick something and not change their minds. Ultimately, they were surprisingly compliant. My daughter wanted to be Belle from Beauty and the Beast and my son wanted to be Astrobot (the character from the Playstation game.) We decided to divide and conquer: my husband was a PS5 to match our son, and I was Snow White to match our daughter:

Better photo of my daughter’s costume. That little brooch in the middle is actually a spare Self Portrait button I had lying around! Always save your buttons!

My Snow White costume was extremely fun to make! I used parts of this cosplay pattern but I wound up altering it a lot because it runs really big and I was using stretch fabric. This was my first time doing this type of trim, and I’m really happy with how it turned out:

And the Playstation/Astro costumes:

Until next Friday, fashion and sewing lovers. See everyone else for Many Such Takes on Sunday!
