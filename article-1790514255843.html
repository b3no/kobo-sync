KN95 Lesbian Bar

I could not in good faith do a Many Such Takes without talking about the KN95 lesbian bar story from the Boston Globe. Basically, a small town lesbian bar in western Massachusetts had a requirement for patrons to wear an KN95 “or better” mask well into 2026. When some customers complained about this requirement, the owners attempted to compromise by removing the mask mandate for one day a week. (That’s right, one day.) The more vocal detractors from this new policy accused the bar owners of eugenics, genocide, ableism, and of wanting to host a “hate and discrimination night.”

First of all, I already wrote a deep dive on this situation (and why I believe it resonated so much with left-lib Twitter) which you can find here:

What you need to understand about this story was that there are two antagonists. The primary antagonist is a not-very-frequent patron of the bar who lives about an hour away named Soe Noire. (Noire is a Black artist and performer who uses he/him pronouns and identifies as “gender expansive.” People have been confused about how one can be a lesbian and also use he/him pronouns, and obviously this is not my area of expertise, although I should point out that Noire appears to be AFAB, not a cis man) Noire seems to have led the opposition to the removal of the mask mandate. He was present for a 3.5-hour Zoom call to complain about the removal of this mandate, where he said it was “no longer a safe space for revolutionary praxis.” When the call finished up he said there was more he wanted to share, but told the mediator that it was racist for him to be asked to join any kind of followup call because that was unpaid labor.

Asking people to pay him appears to be a big part of his schtick—his Instagram shows demands to be paid, not for any particular good or service, but “especially if you’re white.” Somehow, he even manipulated one owner of the bar into pushing people to pay him, even as she is homeless herself. And in case you’re wondering, yeah, he’s still mad.
