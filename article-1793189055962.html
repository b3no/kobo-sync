The Eternal Battle Between a Man's Dignity and His Halloween Costume

Why are you, as a man, wearing an inflatable chicken costume?

Cartoons Hate Her
Oct 28
 
READ IN APP
 

My husband’s relationship with Halloween is complicated. We’ve been together since we were nineteen and twenty-one, and despite the fact that he loves Halloween, he has always harbored some deep shame and embarrassment about the act of actually wearing a costume.

He didn’t feel this way as a child, but as he transitioned from a boy to a man, he acquired a particular neurosis about looking like he was trying too hard. Any costume he could think of suddenly felt embarrassing. So every Halloween, he would phone it in. For multiple years in a row he went as “Ironman” and he literally just wore a T-shirt with a light-up Ironman logo on it. (He wore this for multiple years. I threw it away against his wishes because it started acquiring mold in the closet.)

Arguably the most I’ve ever seen him “try” with a Halloween costume was our first Halloween together, before we were even officially dating. He went to Goodwill and picked out some child-sized camo print clothing to go as GI Joe, egged on by his roommate. Perhaps understandably, he is still embarrassed about this costume as it was a shameless display of “look at how hot and jacked I am,” but he was twenty-one and single, so it kind of made sense, and also, it did get him laid. Since then, however, he has been completely opposed to any Halloween costume that shows even a modicum of effort. And then, without fail, every Halloween, we show up at some kind of party, he sees a guy doing an amazing high-effort Game of Thrones whitewalker costume and makes me promise I will “push him to try” next year.

The next year comes. I urge him to start thinking about costumes in September. He tells me Halloween is “months away.” I continue to remind him and he says all my costume ideas are silly and embarrassing. Halloween comes and we have to hobble together a last-minute costume for him, usually a variant of “some guy.” We go to a party and he sees a guy in a really cool King Arthur costume. “Make me try next year,” he says.

Clearly, your average straight man feels differently about Halloween costumes from your average woman. But perhaps we have one thing in common: an internal struggle. After all, many women have long dealt with the struggle of, “is my costume too slutty or not flattering enough?” Perhaps men have another internal struggle: why am I, as a man, wearing a costume at all?

I remember seeing a post on the red pill subreddit years ago about how wearing a Halloween costume was the ultimate act of being a “beta cuck.” Obviously, this was ridiculous, but the comments on the post turned out to be more interesting than I thought.

The OP’s idea was that wearing a costume, whether it was scary, flattering, or goofy, was demeaning to men because it made them look like they were putting on a degrading show to appeal women—you know, putting the pussy on the pedestal. (Note for next year: “pussy on the pedestal” would be a great costume, if you could manage to keep the flaps out of your face). In particular, the OP singled out the humiliating ritual of walking around a party dressed like a giant banana, “begging for a crumb of pussy.” (Okay, new couple’s costume idea: pussy on the pedestal and crumb of pussy).

Many of the comments pushed back, indicating that a guy who is able to show up to a Halloween party dressed like a banana is showing nonchalant alpha energy. He’s having fun and he’s not afraid to take himself too seriously. A guy who shows up belligerently refusing to wear a costume to avoid looking like a cuck is, ironically, the ultimate cuck. That’s kind of how I felt about Jim Halpert of The Office whose hatred of Halloween costumes became a costume in and of itself. Like, dude, you’re goofy enough to set a stapler in Jell-O but you can’t wear a costume?! Grow up!

So in general, I believe men (including my husband) should get over it and just wear a fun costume, but I also understood where the OP of the “costumes are cucked” reddit post was coming from. While women are subject to many double standards, there’s one in particular that makes life a bit harder for men, which is that men should be physically attractive and appealing to women, but they have to do it in a way that looks completely accidental, because trying, especially with anything related to your appearance, is coded as a bit feminine. Even the act of having your photo taken, as I’ve written about before, is vaguely embarrassing. We generally give women a pass to partake in Slutty Halloween, but a straight man attempting to do this would, at the very, least, raise eyebrows. And for a guy who has a tendency toward self-consciousness and overthinking, any costume might seem a bit cringe.

So what’s a Halloween-loving man to do, especially one who doesn’t have a wife who is willing to pull his chestnuts out of the fire at the last minute with a low-effort couple’s costume? If a banana costume is too silly, and no costume at all (or something ridiculously low-effort, like a sign around your neck that reads “Nudist on Strike”) reads that you take yourself too seriously, and a high-effort costume reads “trying too hard,” what exactly are your options?

It’s Halloween, and Halloween is scary. Here’s the spookiest fright of all: being cringe. There are no safe options if you want to be 100% certain nobody will think you’re cringe. I get that women are also not immune from being mocked for their unoriginal angel/devil best friend costume, but the costumes feel like much more of a minefield for men. And ultimately, you will just never be safe. Even playing it safe by doing nothing is not actually safe.

I also feel like this phobia of costumes extends to a bizarre tendency for men to hinge their masculinity on actually being less attractive. Call it laziness, call it intrasexual competition, call it whatever you want, but men are very quick to judge appearance maintenance in other men as “gay,” or otherwise beta/cucked/lame. It’s not just costumes. I recall a bunch of chuds on Twitter telling me there’s no way my husband had a masculine personality, because at 5’10” and 160 lb, he’s too thin (you heard it here first, fellas: if you lose that extra weight, you’ll turn gay!)

While women may not want a guy who meticulously waxes his eyebrows, spends all day in the gym, refuses to eat bread, and has perfectly manicured nails, this type of straight men is so vanishingly uncommon that it’s pointless to even mention it. As I’ve said before, even men who are “too muscular” to appeal to women are so rare that signaling your lack of attraction to them means nothing—you will likely never encounter a competitive bodybuilder in the wild, and no man will ever become that muscular by accident. Somehow, the combination of women attempting to seem down-to-earth by discounting the value of male effort, and men telling each other that any effort is gay, has led to an epidemic of straight men who think they’re trying their best, but really aren’t even topping out at 20%, and then if you notice this people just remind you that Clavicular exists so actually we’re living in an epidemic of excess male effort. That’s not to say you need to show up in a dazzlingly creative Halloween costume every year to attract women, but I think most men are over-indexing on the negative effects of looking like you care. Most men don’t care nearly enough.

However, I’m not going to just shrug and say “it all depends.” I’m not going to deliver a sort of Lost in Translation type verdict that doesn’t exist at all but presumably, if you were smart you’d get it. I’m going to make a real, explicit verdict about men’s Halloween costumes, and my verdict is that if you are afraid of looking like you’re trying too hard, and it’s too late to actually put together a cool costume, you should try to be as funny as possible. While I know it can be unappealing to shout “look at me” with your costume, nothing is less appealing than being afraid to have fun.

I will add one caveat: those gigantic inflatable dinosaur costumes will make conversation very difficult. We recently went to a Halloween party where a guy tried to talk to my husband while wearing one of those, and my husband couldn’t tell where his face was supposed to be, nor could he really hear anything he was saying.

But everything else funny is probably fine for your average self-conscious costume noob. Go ahead, be your favorite SNL or Tim Robinson character. Be a banana. Be a hotdog. Be whatever goofy thing is still available at Spirit Halloween on the afternoon of October 31.

Obviously, this advice doesn’t apply to men who have no qualms caring about Halloween and have already put together something that took real work and creativity. If you’re a guy who has spent weeks crafting the perfect Phantom of the Opera costume, genuinely good for you. Honestly, as embarrassed as some men are to do stuff like that, I actually think it’s cool and wouldn’t evoke too much judgment. if anything, women would probably applaud you for the creativity. But this article is for the guys who are painfully self-conscious about costumes, like my husband, and I don’t anticipate that many of them could become Costume Guys overnight.

So good luck out there, gentlemen. And remember: it’s Halloween. It’s okay to be perceived for one night out of the year.
