Does Every Man Really Think His Wife is Out of His League?

And if not, why are so many "how we met" stories about women reluctantly agreeing to tolerate a less attractive man who won't leave her alone?

Earlier, I reviewed Phoebe Maltz Bovy’s amazing book, The Last Straight Woman. While there are several really interesting themes in Phoebe’s book—primarily the shocking fact that straight women are A.) exclusively attracted to men, B.) cannot easily rid their lives of men without being very lonely (and perhaps a bit horny), there was another theme I did not cover in my review: pushing back against a culture that insists that men are ga-ga for women—especially their bodies—while women just sort of…tolerate men and find men’s bodies vaguely gross and clownish.

I had always noticed this (and it always bugged me) but I never really identified it until I read Phoebe’s work. Like the false assumption that all women receive much more male attention than they want, or that women don’t actually enjoy sex for anything other than emotional intimacy, there is an insidious belief that a woman’s attraction to a man (especially physical attraction) either doesn’t exist at all, or isn’t factored into her relationships. A man dates a woman when he thinks she’s hot, a woman dates a man when she finally gets worn down enough to put up with him, or if she needs a free meal. Or so they say.

In particular, Phoebe highlights something I previously referred to as a “land acknowledgement for wife guys” where every wedding announcement between a straight couple includes some obligatory line about how the woman was way out of the man’s league, and her desire for him was limited to “eventually came around to it after he refused to go away.” One cited wedding announcement in her book even included a man claiming that his wife “did not know” they were dating at first, and he proceeded to date her, apparently without her knowledge, until she reluctantly came around.

We are supposed to look at the state of marriage and take it at face value that the only man a woman can expect to marry is someone far beneath her in attractiveness who she deigns to tolerate, and that the only woman a man would ever consider marrying is one far more attractive than he is. Assuming that’s true, what a sad state of affairs!

I guess the one problem is I don’t think this is true. I think everyone is lying.

First of all, people feel really weird about this dynamic when the genders are reversed, which should tell us something. It’s funny and affable when a man claims to be this lovable, doofy fuggo next to his goddess of a wife. But when I wrote about how my husband is out of my league—not because I’m a disgusting freak but because he’s just really hot and I’m only mildly attractive—people expressed concern for my mental wellbeing. Some people thought it was pathetic for me to view us in these terms at all, especially at my age. And most people said it’s very unlikely that a man would marry a woman he didn’t think was attractive—so why do we assume women (who can very well make their own money and provide for themselves) consistently marry men they don’t find attractive?

Despite the mixed reaction to my essay on the topic, men all over the country say this exact same thing, in much stronger terms than I did! I even saw a successful Substack essay—widely praised and not even once criticized for “putting himself down”—where a man advised other men on how to woo the ladies of their dreams, assuring them they did not need to be that attractive to pull a top-tier hottie because his girlfriend was super hot and he was a “potato man.” (It was a good essay, to be clear, but I couldn’t help but notice a double standard. Mr. Potato exists, yes, but he is married to Mrs. Potato.)

I don’t think this many men actually believe their partner is far better-looking than they are. When I tried to investigate the question of whether women were just better-looking than men, I had people rate their own looks, and also their partners’ looks, to evaluate whether or not most relationships were actually between a good-looking woman and a “meh” man who made up for it by being occasionally charming.

My theory is that your average person believes they got slightly lucky with their partner (and isn’t that cute!) but our culture demands this hyperbolized “she’s out of my league” narrative of men while finding it unbecoming in women. But why? Why is it necessary for men to debase themselves like this, but “concerning” when a woman does it?

First of all, men see scoring a hot wife as a bragging point, but for women, being the “ugly one” is kind of embarrassing. If I found out that my husband wasn’t that physically attracted to me at first, but eventually warmed up to me because I was funny and he suspected that one day I would have a very successful Substack, I think I might actually throw up. I joke that he’s out of my league, because I truly believe he is, but I’d be upset if he agreed with that assessment.

Meanwhile, many men might hear the “she’s out of your league” statement and say, “Hell yeah, I won her over!” This is one of the many reasons that men hire sex workers more frequently than women do. It’s not that single women don’t get horny and wouldn’t enjoy safe, consensual sex with an attractive man. It’s in part because a single horny woman can probably find free sex, but also, a single horny woman might prefer free sex with a man she considers her equal, as opposed to a man she knows is only there because he’s being paid. The reason Nancy Pelosi or other powerful older women don’t have secret young boytoys isn’t because there are zero young men willing to fill the role (I imagine there are quite a few, actually) but because a woman is typically turned off by scenarios where they feel like they are buying sex. The man genuinely finding her attractive is very important to her own arousal. That doesn’t mean the man’s looks are irrelevant though!

For example, in the great movie, Good Luck to You, Leo Grande, Emma Thompson plays Nancy, an elderly widow who never had an orgasm with her late husband. She nervously hires a hot, young male sex worker named Leo Grande (played by Daryl McCormack) to give her what she never experienced. But there isn’t much sex in first half of the movie, because she spends a great deal of time worrying about whether or not he’s actually into it, and apologizing for how old and unattractive she is. But she is attracted to him, and I believe many older women are attracted to young men, but in a self-conscious, guilty, “is this allowed” way. Nancy explains that she knows she’s attractive enough to find a man her age for sex, but doesn’t want men her age and wants to experience someone young and conventionally attractive, about which she has quite a bit of embarrassment. This mindset is taken for granted with men, but shocking when it comes from a woman. What makes it believable, however, is that she feels so bad about it. In a particularly memorable scene, she peppers the sex worker with questions about other clients, finally reassured when she discovers she isn’t the oldest woman who paid him for sex (he claims to have slept with an eighty-year-old.)

In this sense, women are just as much perpetrators of the “she’s out of my league” delusion as men. Women believe that their main asset in attracting partners is their looks (not to be confused with their main asset in a relationship, which would be something less shallow) while men seem a bit more indifferent to their looks as their main initial selling point, or even a selling point at all. I can’t tell if people feel this way because it’s actually true, or because we’ve been told it’s true so many times that we feel it should be true, and we don’t want to buck the trend by being the ugly woman who still pulls hot men because she’s funny. In Lena Dunham’s bestselling memoir, Famesick, she admits to having a great deal of anxiety about her relationship with Jack Antonoff, specifically on the basis that he was too conventionally attractive for her. This perceived imbalance was embarrassing for her, not something of which to be proud.

So yes, there have been women who scored men out of their leagues, but it doesn’t have the same bragging rights as when a man does it, and it tends to conjure up questions like, “Is he secretly gay? Does he need his rent paid?” However, for a man to score a much-hotter woman, he would have to be extremely grotesque, or multiple decades older than her, for anyone to assume her attraction wasn’t totally genuine.

It makes me wonder if a man would be flattered or insulted if a woman joked that she was only with him for his looks, and that she won him over by being funny and successful. I have a feeling most men would simply be very confused because they can’t conceive of any woman feeling this way.

In fact, a while ago, I made some comment on Substack about how “attraction” based on a transactional basis of providing sex for resources isn’t really attraction, and if it were, sex workers would be “attracted” to all their clients. I was shocked at how many men either disagreed, or didn’t understand the concept at all. They believed this was the only way that straight women experienced anything resembling attraction, and that wanting a man’s money and offering sex to obtain it was no different than finding a man hot. Many doubled down by claiming that “most men are ugly anyway” and assuming that because they don’t want to fuck men, most women probably have no genuine interest in it either.

It’s not that I believe men and women experience attraction the exact same way, but even if men are “more visual” on average, this tendency exists along a spectrum, and women (at least the ones with eyes) can be visual too, often in a very conventional way. While many women claim that they don’t like muscles on a guy, or don’t like “pretty boys,” they are typically talking about freakish, bodybuilder, steroid-induced, over-the-top muscles, not muscles generated from high-effort fitness. My male body survey showed that slim/fit male bodies performed the best (although women were open to a variety of other bodies) and Aella’s nude looksmatching survey basically showed the same thing in much more graphic detail, peens and all. Perhaps some women are virtue-signaling about “not liking muscles,” or perhaps the ones who do like muscles are staying silent (not I!) but it’s pretty obvious that men aren’t the only ones with predictable, conventional taste in hotness. They just feel less embarrassed to talk about it.

For some reason, our culture is steeped in a misogynistic belief that straight women—unlike any other orientation or gender combination—aren’t actually sexual beings. Per this misconception, women do not enjoy looking at attractive men (or perhaps women can’t even tell when a man is attractive—they just like when guys are funny and nice, right?) women do not enjoy having sex or even care if they have an orgasm, women do not watch porn, women do not enjoy any male attention, and women would generally rather just be left alone unless they need something material from a man. I hate to “hand it to them,” as there’s plenty they get wrong, but at least red pill content acknowledges that straight women have amoral sexual urges and that straight women’s sexuality is based on something other than wanting cuddles and safety.

But as Phoebe covers, this belief system is present in both misogynistic environments (she and I have both seen right-wing men claim that women don’t enjoy sex and it’s just a marital duty—major tell!) and, weirdly, in feminist environments, such as in #BanMen feminism which repeatedly insists that even if women have some sexual attraction to men, we find them altogether too bothersome and gross to want them in our lives, and we’d rather segregate ourselves indefinitely in “woman-only spaces.” A classic example is the woman-only gym, which makes sense if you’re already in a relationship or truly uninterested in meeting any men, but I maintain that plenty of single straight women (albeit not all) would enjoy meeting a man at the gym! Do we not have literal singles events at gyms? My issue with the call for women-only gyms isn’t that zero women would enjoy them (obviously some would- and they can exist for those women) but the generalizations that all or even most single women would enjoy them.

Taken to a less extreme place, we generally assume that in every relationship or even every interaction, the man is the desirer and the woman is the desired. Unlike Phoebe herself (or at least how I assume Phoebe feels—she does a great job of writing about sexuality so intensely without oversharing, a feat which I cannot claim) this dynamic of wanting to be desired more is very central to my own sexuality. Any initiative or aggression on my part makes me feel gross and genuinely turns me off to the point that sex no longer feels worth it. I understand this sounds like I am a data point against Phoebe’s thesis, but my feelings do not indicate a lack of desire for men (or, more specifically, my husband.) I do desire him—sex is not a chore I perform so that he continues to provide for me and stops bugging me with ass grabs while I do the dishes (him: “what dishes?”)—I just need him to desire me more, or at least display desire more, by taking more action than I do. (It’s not exactly “fair” to demand this, as many men have yelled at me in my comments, but all parties are consenting! If you’re so worried about him, ask him how it’s going!) And for what it’s worth, my husband has never self-effacingly joked about me being out of his league, not because he doesn’t think I’m hot, but because he’s aware of how hot I find him.

This is very different from the idea of a woman’s sexuality being purely reactive—that she is only getting off on the idea of the man getting off to her. That can certainly be part of it, but women do, in fact, get turned on by attractive men! Perhaps women are not quite as visual as men and need more psychological context (I am not aroused by a context-free photo of a good-looking man, but I have gotten aroused watching non-pornographic movies starring attractive actors.) I know it’s unfashionable to say because everyone hated it, but I did find Wuthering Heights kind of hot, and I can’t claim that I’d feel the same way if the brooding, sultry (and somehow constantly wet) Heathcliff was portrayed not by Jacob Elordi but by Carrot Top.

It’s no surprise that many women agreed with me when I said that I felt I needed permission to be horny, or that I did not want to make decisions or take initiative in the bedroom. In fact, when I wrote that piece I thought people would be like, “Okay, lady, whatever!” but in fact, many women said I had articulated something they didn’t realize was true about themselves. One woman even credited the article for transforming her sex life with her husband. But “I want this person to make all the first moves,” is not the same thing as “I don’t find this person attractive.” I—and perhaps many other women—just don’t like acting on desire, which is an entirely different thing. A great deal of sexually-driven women’s media (ie: Bridgerton) unsurprisingly involves Victorian or otherwise repressed settings where a woman acting on her desire would be taboo. “Bodice rippers” are a thing because for someone to have to rip a bodice—as opposed to just taking off your Aritzia T-shirt—there has to be some resistance, which in most cases is basically fake resistance, where the woman does really want the man but can’t for various cultural or external reasons (hence, Victorian era). Moreover, at least for me, acting on my own desire implies nobody else wanted to take action first. If he wanted to, they would, as they say. But this is not the same thing as not experiencing desire in the first place, or only feeling reactive desire.

This is another reason why wedding announcements and meetcute stories so frequently feature an obligatory line about the woman being out of the man’s league. Maybe hearing this is actually very important to the woman, even if it’s not true. Because what women want isn’t truly to be with someone far less attractive than they are—they want to be with someone equally as attractive as they are who believes that they got lucky. Trust me, modern women have plenty of options and nobody is forcing them to marry. I find it hard to believe that an attractive woman in 2026 would marry the first doughy schmuck who came along for no reason other than “he wouldn’t go away.” No matter what wedding announcements claim.
