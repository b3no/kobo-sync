Announcing the Paul Wells Holiday Overflow Show!

I sold out my Holiday Show so fast I’m going to do another show the next night. Tickets go on sale Friday. I am not even making this up.

Paul Wells
Nov 18
 
READ IN APP
 

As many of you noticed, tickets for my fourth annual Holiday Show sold out in about 10 minutes. That’s 300 seats, gone way too fast. In a way that’s exciting and gratifying. I really appreciate how much interest there is in Ottawa about joining the community we’ve been building here. But on the other hand, it’s genuinely frustrating when readers try to join the fun and can’t.

So today I’m announcing that I’ll do another show.

On Wednesday, Dec. 9 at 7:30 p.m. — the night after the Holiday Show at the NAC — I’ll be joined by a bunch of friends to present the First Ever Paul Wells Holiday Overflow Show at Overflow Brewing Company, a genuinely impressive taproom and live music venue near Bank and Heron Road in Ottawa’s Alta Vista neighbourhood.

The Wednesday show will not be completely different from the original Tuesday show. But it’ll be fantastic. I’ll be joined by the regular members of The Panel — Allison Gifford, Garry Keller and Marci Surkes — to discuss the politics of 2026 (whew!) and 2027 (uh-oh). I’ll open that panel by delivering my own extended remarks, as I do at every Holiday Show and Road Show.

I’ll take a bunch of questions from the audience. And I do mean a bunch: I want to beta-test making audience questions a big part of these shows, as some of the leading US podcasts do. And since I actually don’t know the answers to everything despite my best attempts to fake it, I’ve invited reinforcements: Catherine Cullen, the host of CBC Radio’s The House, and Christopher Nardi, a senior parliamentary reporter for The National Post, will join me for Audience Questions. Catherine is brilliant, as everyone knows. Nardi speaks three languages — four if you count sartorial brilliance.

And I’m bringing the young singer-songwriter Gavin McLeod up from Toronto to perform several tunes. Gavin was a huge hit at my Toronto Road Show in June, where he played an acoustic set with no drummer. This time I’ve told him to bring his band with a drummer, hot off a Saturday-night gig at the Rivoli in Toronto, to play a longer set for you. Here’s how that sort of thing goes:

Tickets for this show will cost a little less than for the NAC show, at $50. I’m not going to do subscriber pre-sale because honestly, that stuff is really stressful for me. Here’s the link to buy tickets, which you can visit now, but it won’t actually let you buy any tickets until 9 a.m. Friday morning, Nov. 20.

Once again, here’s that link. It will go live for ticket purchases at 9 a.m. Friday.

So that’s the plan. Second night, different venue (250 places, and yes there’ll be seating for everybody). Cash bar. The Panel, Cullen & Nardi and your questions, my monologue, and Gavin McLeod rocking a house built to rock. All will be merry and bright.

I want to be clear why I’m doing it. I always wish there were seats for everybody at all of my shows. We didn’t come close this year. I still want to meet as many of you as I can, and thank you for your support. I haven’t asked my sponsors for extra funding for Wednesday night, and I’ll send all proceeds after expenses to a prominent Ottawa charity.

I’m glad you let me do fun stuff like this. I’ll see some of you on the 8th, and others on the 9th. Thank you, always.
