Data centers, AI, and the 2026 elections. Ft. Paris Marx.

Plus, three things we should be watching as Americans head to the polls on Tuesday.

Brian Merchant
Oct 30
 
READ IN APP
 

If you’ve heard anything at all about the midterm elections, you’ve heard at least two things: it’s going to be a ‘blue wave’ in which Democrats are poised for major electoral gains, and everyone involved hates data centers.

We’re all used to hearing about that data center hate now, it’s almost a given; “the single most bipartisan issue in politics,” and so on and so forth, but I do want to pause to underline how remarkable this is. This time last year, data centers were a niche issue in a handful of local political fights. Now they’re one of America’s most visible, universal and undeniable political battlegrounds.

Despite that, a lot of pundits will try to minimize data center politics. They’ll argue it’s a reactionary movement, or relegate it to a set of Not-in-my-backyard concerns. But it makes a lot more sense to put the backlash in the context of everything else Americans are going through, and angry at, right now. Data centers are a stand-in for so much of what’s wrong with the American economy and American democracy right now. They’re monuments to what we don’t want our future to look like.

Plus, I dig into three things I’ll be watching during the midterm elections next week; data centers, sure, but also the impact of tech money in politics, ambient AI hate, and California’s pioneering, tech titan-opposed billionaire tax.
