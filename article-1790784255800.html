Dating Columns from the Order of High Elven Society

My sister is an artist. She is an artist who does not have her GED or high school diploma and four children (three of whom are legal adults) because she ran away from home when she was fourteen and ended up getting pregnant. I’m not going to tell the whole story because it’s not mine to tell. But I can assure you, my sister was not living with our parents and being supported by them. After about fifteen years of intermittent factory labor, odd jobs and hustling, my sister settled into (trying to) making a living doing [Artist stuff].

She is much older than many of the people in her circle who do similar work. She also lacks parents who can financially support her, which a lot of these younger kids have. That’s the thing about any artistic endeavor. Most people who succeed have an element of bankrolling happening behind the scenes. A husband. A mom and dad who believe in them.

The “crash outs” I have witnessed regarding this! She would come home filled with anger because part of being an artist is networking with other artists. Mid-twenties people who still have parents paying their rent! People who suck at their craft but have the charisma or art-hoe good looks to persist! And you have to grin through it, because it’s still beneficial to work with some of these people. It gets the word out. It expands your reach. You can’t burn a bridge, even if it’s a coddled, annoying one.

This was why, when Former Plus Size Comedian Tom Segura had his subsequent “fuck the poors” arc in the wake of his airline tantrum (but it wasn’t a tantrum. He was laughing, OK?) there were so many people very eager to point out he was actually a Teaspoon Baby¹ with a Money Daddy². No one is denying that Segura is funny. He’s had multiple Netflix specials, but it’s a hell of a lot easier keep trying and honing a craft when there’s a benefactor to pad your corners and slide a mattress under you when you fall.

The one comedian who has actually acknowledged this dynamic with any honesty (that I know of) is Pete Holmes. His autobiographical show, Crashing, begins with his wife divorcing him because she is tired of supporting him. Even after that, he makes it a very common theme that the support from others is what permitted him to pursue comedy (even if that support was as simple as getting to sleep on Artie Lange’s couch).

This is why I very often disregard a lot of advice from anyone with affluent parents. To have successful parents is kind of like stacking multipliers through your lifetime. If you fuck up, you were real bad at the game. And if things are going well for them, I cannot trust any advice or even the thoughts of that kind of person in relation to what assets I have to work with. Even for someone who has a relatively mundane career… They might also have someone paying for their phone bill.

One more thing about Former Plus Size Comedian Tom Segura. The advice he does give out about being successful is: “Work hard! :)”
This is also similar to Failed Lawyer Kim Kardashian’s advice, which is also “Get off your ass and work!” Neither of these proclamations touches base on the fact that both Tom and Kim are playing different games than a prole like you or me, and the definitions of success are going to be very different between us.

The Elven Dating Game

If you play video games or dabble with TTRPGs you might agree that life is surprisingly similar to a point-buy system for skills, only we don’t always get to directly choose or influence where the points slot into. Even though people are very flexible and can do astounding things, we’re all built different. Skills can be built from the ground up, but some things are inherent and can be optimized.

While it would be ideal to have a character who is good at everything, the player is not often provided with enough points to do so in a game that is actually balanced. You have to lean into specialization. An Orc Barbarian doesn’t need to be intelligent. It needs to be good at hitting things, so you stack the skill points in categories that make it excel at hitting things.

In real life, we can’t be good at everything either. Even when we really want to be. Unless there are external forces that permit you to spend every waking bit of free time to work on yourself… To read the books, to do the exercises, to take the class, to practice the hobby… You’ll never have it all, and instead optimizing what you’ve already got an edge on is probably your best avenue.

For this reason, I find the realm of relationship advice and content very interesting, particularly what source that content originates from. If I am not specced for it, I cannot attack the world as an Elven Bard. My methods have to be different. Which is why I have never been quick to take advice from an Elven Bard explaining the best method to be successful in dating. When I put out my essay about Love vs. Status, a lot of people assumed, defensively, that I was asking sexy, sexy men to date ugly women³.

I once saw a humorous post somewhere that was something along the lines of: Why don’t incels just sleep with fat girls? It was tongue in cheek, of course. But like all things that are subtly funny on the internet, it went over a lot of people’s heads if the comment section had anything to say about it.

I wasn’t necessarily implying that, but a lot of my opinions on the matter are empirical. I am totally a Dwarf who has managed to enthrall a few Elves in my day, is all I’m gonna say (for now, anyway).

This is my problem when Elves give dating advice. They’re running the campaign with a certain set of stats and stacked multipliers that a lot of people don’t have. And while some of them are Dwarves or Orcs who might have been able to transmutate into an Elf, that transmutation is still following the conventions of Elf strategy. They Elf-maxxed.

An elf who has a natural affinity towards charisma and being lithe and sexy has no basis to tell an Orc how to fuck if that Orc is struggling. However, elves dominate the domain of relationship advice, if only because their elven traits give them a bigger pool of options. More people want to date elves so they get to go on more dates. Then they get to tell you what they think they’ve learned about all those experiences.

You’ll notice that many Elven Dating Columnists aren’t necessarily in long term relationships… Or they’re coupled with another Elf (or perhaps a very rich Orc). The Elf will offer the strategies that worked for them. A Transmutated Elf will tell you how to Elf-Max your stats, but if you aren’t capable of elf-maxxing or willing, no one is really telling you anything helpful. If you aren’t already an elf, that is.

Natural Elves carry with them a sometimes willful ignorance about their elven traits that can feel pretty irritating. I don’t think there’s necessarily anything intentionally insidious about what they say. They’re just dumb, or trying to lessen their obvious status to not alienate an audience or hurt feefees. Or they had an awkward phase in elven adolescence that made them obsess over their waist-to-hip ratio… Relatable!⁴

But Transmutaters are the worst. They’re the kind of elves who will tell you all else is hopeless unless you Elf-Maxx. That you’re doomed, unless you look like a perfect elf. They have a narrow window of what constitutes success and happiness and are often joyless grifters.

General Relationship Advice is Actually Really Hard to Give

Earlier I mentioned that I, a Dwarf, have been able to date elves here and there. But someone who is not me probably isn’t going to be working with the same specs and multipliers I’m working with either. I can’t properly explain to you how to ensure you had enough childhood trauma and what movies to watch to hone your personality and personal style to attract an elf.

Those little personal things can’t be replicated. And it requires me to understand myself perfectly, which is a tall order. Am I really a dwarf? Maybe I’m actually a hobbit or a tiefling? Even the most beautiful elves feel like dwarves sometimes. The things I like about myself might not be what someone else is actually attracted to either.

Since there’s nothing more boner-shrinking and vaginer-drying then asking why someone is into you in the first place, that’s not something most people do. Beyond that, I have to take another person’s word on the qualities they found attractive about me. That’s also not reliable.

And speaking of the Dwarf-Elf pairings I’ve been part of? They didn’t work out. My ability to attract an Elf did not yield a long-term relationship with one. Maybe it was my lack of elven traits. Maybe it was that I’m kind of a little butthole and difficult to get along with. I don’t know. Any kind person, when asked, is not going to tell you that they stopped dating you because you were an ugly dwarf.

I have read the data. I know that when you survey people and present them with featureless pictures of bodies they tend to like the ones that align with Elven traits. I have no reason to take this information to heart. Everyone already knows that and it doesn’t do anything for them. It might help a person elf-maxx, but it can also just become an added anxiety or obsession for someone who could probably improve something that doesn’t involve recomping their body or saving enough gold for Elf Surgery and grey market potions.

What I can infer about having been in Elf-Dwarf Mixed Relationships is that attraction has a great deal of fluidity. More than anyone assumes. Everyone has walked down the street and passed a mismatched couple making love-eyes at each other and wondered Why Those Two?

Well, its because love blooms in mysterious places for mysterious reasons, you unromantic fuck.

The only prerequisite is that you have to be open to it, and the more you close yourself off, the harder it is to find (also, yeah… You have to shower and actually talk to people. Sorry! That’s the only piece of advice that remains evergreen).

Even if you’re stinky, I’ll still kiss you if you become a paid subscriber.
