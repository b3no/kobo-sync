Low-Class Boppin’

Lance Corporal Scott Bukkake
Oct 30
 
READ IN APP
 

Yesterday I had a job interview. As a non-driver this meant I had to get up earlier, leave earlier, and do a lot of walking. Which is fine. It was a nice day. I texted my husband, because I was unsure when I’d be home, and told him I was gonna bop around and maybe visit my mom.

The connecting bus stop was just on the street outside of a Burlington Coat Factory. I haven’t been inside the Burlington Coat Factory for over twenty years. They’re a discount retailer that offer’s some pretty hefty discounts on brands that fall under the umbrella of low-tier luxury, a la Calvin Klein and Tommy Hilfiger. The last time I’d been inside of one, they had a lot of clothing from the brand Baby Phat. I remember a lot of jackets with the logo sewn on the back.

The store was in good order back then, like any regular department store, if my memory serves. It was just basically another TJ Maxx or Marshalls. A place for poor people to buy a Michael Kors bag made of vinyl. The function is the same now, but it resembled a badly kept Goodwill when I entered those sliding doors. Dingy fluorescents and overstuffed metal racks, labeled by size but badly organized within. Not exactly a great shopping experience (though, as a fat, I seldom would say I have collected a number of good shopping experiences).

I texted some friends: “I’m at Burlington Coat Factory. This place SUUUUUUUUUCKS” / “This is poor person Backrooms” / etc…

I bought a three-pack of exfoliating towels, because they were cheaper than the ones I usually buy at CVS. The single check-out clerk and I had a brief, happy conversation about how much we liked those rough exfoliating cloths as I made my purchase, I shoved the exfoliating towels into my unbranded (Because I tore the XYQQU Branded label from the Amazon vendor off) corduroy purse/convertible backpack (“Have a nice day!” “Thanks! You too!”), and walked back to the bus stop to smoke a cig until the bus came. Cigarettes are magical things, because if you want to smoke a whole one, and you’re waiting for the bus, it always comes before you finish. This was the case for me.

I can get to my mom’s pretty easily from where I live. It’s basically a straight-shot and a short walk. From Burlington Coat Factory, I had to catch a connecting bus, which is also not that complicated. It gave me enough of a wait to finish that cigarette. I stared at the Allen Bradley Clock Tower, checked my phone and smoked. And then a man in an old jeep stopped next to me. He asked me a question that I didn’t hear the first time, thanks to my deaf ear. I made the international sign of holding my hand up to my good ear to let him know I didn’t catch his query.

He wanted a cigarette. Anytime someone asks a stranger for a cigarette, they do it in a very pitiful, apologetic manner. Sometimes they’ll trade you a dollar for one, but this guy had no dollars. I gave him a cigarette, he lit it and clasped his hands together in thanks before he drove off. The gifting of a cigarette is always met with proper reverence.

The bus came shortly thereafter, and I hopped on and got as far to my Mom’s apartment as it could get me and walked. My parents used to have a house. A two-story in the UMC Suburbs that boasted four bedrooms and two baths. There was even a large room with a Jacuzzi and a fireplace (party room!), but later when my dad got serious about performing music it was converted into a practice space for him and his Middle Aged Jam Band buddies.

After years of not paying taxes, and a whirling storm of my father’s health and addiction problems, my parents had to declare bankruptcy. They sold the house and downgraded to an apartment. My father, who was never good at money, used some of the meager profits of the house to purchase a Saturn Sky (?????), after Saturn vehicles had stopped being manufactured. When he died in 2025, that was sold as well, and my mom moved to a different apartment in the city where she could walk more. She lives off his social security and is on a budget.

My mom spends her days cooking, watching horror stories and paranormal content on youtube, and making paper mache art. She’s a very good baker, and a very talented artist (in my opinion). We watched grainy ghost footage, and she had me try a piece of her Chicken Pot Pie so I could grade her on the quality of a new crust that she’d tried. It was delightful, albeit a bit crumbly. There’s really nothing like my mom’s cooking.

I helped her with a batch of cookies, and helped her avoid a lunch date with some of the friends she’s made at the apartment complex that she didn’t really want to go to. Instead, we caught up about whatever. I told her I had to pick up my dog’s pills, and she said she wanted to come with for a stop at the local liquor store and maybe the Walmart. My Walmart is newer, so its not too scummy. The older a Walmart is the more scummy it becomes.

She didn’t come with me into the CVS, and I don’t blame her for that. I really regret using it as my pharmacy, but the headache of switching the medication my dog has to take to a different pharmacy is more of a headache than enduring fifteen minutes inside of it. All of my dog’s medications are controlled substances, so if there was any delay or trouble in getting the meds straightened out (And I’m already on the phone with the vet and pharmacy more than I want to be about this shit) I’m boned, and so is my dog. So CVS it is. I got the pills, while she sat in the car and to Walmart we went.

When it comes to Walmart, I say “A port in any storm.” The Walmart is a short bus ride from my house and isn’t crazy expensive like the other nearby grocery stores. Once, when my husband and I were driving around the neighborhood, I said “Everyone around here is ugly.” I live in an area (and if you’re familiar with Wisconsin this isn’t surprising) where there are small bars on every corner, and toothless drunks toddling to and from those little bars at all hours of the day.

Recently, we’ve started seeing more homeless people too. In the summer, for the first time in my life, I had to tell someone that I was smoking my final cigarette of my pack (even though I wasn’t) because they were the last of four homeless people who asked me for one and I was not willing to give up anymore. One such homeless person approached us in the parking lot of the Walmart, as if he could sense my mother’s generous spirit while we were still in the vehicle, and she gave him some spare change (of course). I threw in a dollar, out of social obligation.

I drink my coffee with heavy cream (goated), and had to re-up on that. And I needed a sealable container for rice, because our last bag got buggy (gross!). I grabbed box of Honey Bunches of Oats (which I have hidden in the cupboard because I am not sharing) and also re-upped on underwear.

The final leg of this shopping spree involved Halloween candy. My mother chastised me for buying a big bag of Fun Size candies, because this is not the way for the people of our family. When my parents had the house in the UMC suburbs, they passed out full-sized candy bars, which was a big fucking deal. A normal sized candy bar in a sea of bite-sized, individually wrapped candies is a prize for a kid. I conceded and bought packs of Fun Dip also, because they were full-sized portions. I cannot justify the cost of handing out full-sized candy bars to an entire neighborhood of children.

My day of boppin’ about concluded. I said goodbye to mom, and made dinner.

When I walk to the corner gas station, I pass a very nice house. A nice house for my neighborhood, which means there is a two-car garage and it has a flagpole, and no peeling paint. The houses in my area aren’t bad, but they tend to be small and have cramped, little yards. But I pass this house, almost every day, and whoever owns it has a camera on the outside. Everyone has ‘cameras’ thanks to Amazon Ring and Google Nest cameras around to make sure no one steals packages. But this camera, it talks.

A robotic voice calls out with a tinny: “YOU ARE BEING RECORDED.” And I turn my head and mug at the camera every time, for interrupting my walk. When I see them, I also mug at cameras with “Smile, you’re on camera!” signs. Fuck you. Those smile signs always felt so smug to me, and I hate them. A “Recording in progress” would suffice.

The neighborhood I live in is filled with toothless drunks and there is a bar on every corner. And there’s more homeless people here than there were when we moved. I ride the bus with these people. I shop with these people. When they ask me for cigarettes, I let them have one (unless its over three people, apparently). I am alike, but different. We both have family members who are endearing and frustrating. Walmart is scummy. Burlington Coat Factory is the Poor Person Backrooms. I’m not special and I’ll smile at your stupid talking camera. I’m not stuck here with you. You’re stuck here with me, and I smile back.
