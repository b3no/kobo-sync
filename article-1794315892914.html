I will attempt to explain the recent Jensen Huang news to CHH readers.

Just imagine if the Lindy West drama happened again, except it wasn’t Lindy West’s partner Aham, it was Jensen Huang, the CEO of Nvidia, and it wasn’t a polycule under suspiciously manipulative circumstances, it was an affair, and imagine that instead of Aham’s lover Roya being a normal woman, she was a honeypot Chinese spy who worked at Megaspeed and was leveraging the affair to evade US probes into her country and procure Nvidia chips for Chinese AI.

Don’t worry, I won’t spend too much time (or really any time, aside from what I just wrote) talking about Jensen Huang’s corrupt affair. But details about this story reflect a fear that many women share, even if only in the abstract or subconscious: being the “starter wife.” Dedicating your youth and time to a man who has nothing, helping him build success, and being tossed aside as soon as that success is realized because you committed the sin of still being alive after forty.

Jensen Huang met his wife Lori in college when both of them were nerds with no money. As is the case for many successful men, his wife believed in him and supported him as he earned his riches and fame over time. But because they met when both of them were poor, she is not a “trophy wife,” and is not physically out of his league the way some other billionaires’ wives are. They’re both in their early sixties, and the look like two people in their early sixties.

But when you’re a billionaire man—even a normal-looking one—women will throw themselves at you. Some might be genuinely attracted, and others will have very specific financial (or in Jensen’s case, geopolitical) motivations for pursuing you. And even successful and powerful men who aren’t multibillionaires will cheat. Not all of them, but some of them. Some of their wives may find infidelity alone intolerable; others will look the other way as long as the affairs are fleeting and emotionally insignificant. But then, a significant proportion of those powerful male cheaters will “trade up” for a younger, better-looking spouse, discarding the woman who loved him when he had nothing—and it’s hard to “look the other way” when you have to tolerate your kids being partially raised by the woman for whom your husband left you, who is young enough to be your daughter. I shudder at the thought.

This is not the case with Jensen Huang (this affair is unlikely to materialize into a new marriage, given the obvious fact that the other woman is a CCP spy) but like I said, I’m talking about a broader phenomenon. And, in Lori Huang’s case, her (presumed) humiliation is compounded by denizens of the Internet looking at her, looking at the attractive affair partner, and saying, “Well, who wouldn’t?”

This is where a primal fear is activated for women. I would liken the fear of becoming a “starter wife,” or wasting your prime years on someone who will later discard you, to the male-coded fear of being tricked into raising another man’s children. Nobody likes to be cheated on or betrayed, but certain scenarios activate something that feels a bit more visceral and lizard-brainy. For men afraid of cuckoldry and paternity fraud, it’s not hard to understand why: nearly every male mammal is opposed to raising another male’s children, and almost all of what we call “the patriarchy” and policing of women’s sexual behaviors is based around this precise anxiety. Yes, it’s unlikely any woman you date will actually do this, but the underlying, subconscious fear of it drives quite a bit of heterosexual male behavior.

But nobody talks about the female equivalent (Trump voice: they don’t let you talk about the female equivalent!) And I would say that the female equivalent is not the fear of dedicating your time and resources to another person’s child, but the fear of dedicating your youth to someone who will endanger your emotional safety in old age: the fear of being a “starter wife.”

When evaluating “female equivalents” of things, it’s common to just replace sex with resources and call it a day, because, duh, women provide sex (and want resources) while men provide resources (and want sex.) I disagree here—it’s a major oversimplification which ignores a bunch of nuance about female sexuality (women also want sex with attractive partners) and the reality of survival versus sexual desire (a woman is more likely to “want resources” if she needs a man to survive, and will weight it less heavily, and perhaps weight looks and charisma a bit heavier if she doesn’t).

The reality is a bit more complicated and involves the factor of time. If a woman supports her husband for twenty, thirty or forty years, and then he leaves her for a younger woman but pays out bigly because they had no prenup, most women would still consider this scenario abysmal. Surely, if women only cared about resources, this would be a massive success! You get to lose the annoying man (so you don’t have to put out anymore) and walk away with billions of dollars to buy purses or whatever item for which women be shopping. And yes, this scenario is certainly better than one where the woman is left with no money, but the money is not the primary concern—it’s the emotional betrayal of being left at a time when they are less “valuable” than when they came into the marriage, and more specifically, the wasting of their prime years.

(I don’t say this to imply that older women have no intrinsic value, but objectively speaking, a sixty-year-old woman has a much harder time remarrying to a man she finds attractive than she would have at thirty.)

It’s also not so much that sex and resources are polar opposites for men and women, but rather that both men and women are scared of wasting something valuable on someone who will take advantage of it and drop their end of the bargain. For women, that “something valuable” is often their youth (represented as “wasting their time”) and for men, that valuable thing is their resources.

So while most women would be deeply betrayed by any kind of affair (as would most men, for that matter) the worst affairs are ones where something very valuable is wasted under deception. For men, that fear is realized in the scenario of cuckoldry and paternity fraud, and for women, it’s being a starter wife.

Obviously, neither of these scenarios are the status quo, and most people do not consciously worry about these worst case scenarios, but both are ingrained in how many people process fears about dating and relationships. When it comes to the starter wife fear, it’s “common” enough that when a man has the option to make it a reality, we can see clear patterns of this behavior, even though most will never have the opportunity and plenty won’t have the desire. I used Claude to gather data about billionaire CEOs on the Forbes list within America. Of those men, the vast majority aren’t divorced, so the starter wife is hardly a foregone conclusion. But of the men who are divorced and remarried, there is a clear pattern of selecting a second wife significantly younger than the first wife:

Of course, there’s another factor here: these men may have had the first marriage fall apart for totally kosher reasons, and then, newly single (and rich!) they are able to score a much younger woman than would have been possible before their monetary gains. If so, no harm, no foul. So no, this is not a pattern of, “Look at all these cheating scumbags.” In fact, as I’ve said before, I don’t agree with the cynical view that men are only as faithful as their options, and I think the majority of men care too much about their wives (and are too fearful of the consequences) to cheat, even if they could.

But the pattern is still there. It’s not hard to pull up examples of men who casually tossed aside their first wife (who helped them build their empire, and loved them when they had nothing) for a younger model who only wants them for their money, without a care in the world for what the first wife sacrificed for them. It’s not a stereotype because every man secretly wants to do this, but it’s a stereotype because it happens often enough for people to notice. Often enough for SNL to formulate this infamous sketch about it, wherein married men in their thirties enter a game show to meet the women they will marry in ten years after divorcing their wives. All of the second wives are children at the time of the game show.

As I pointed out earlier, most women will not suffer this fate. Most men (even ones without much of a moral center) aren’t super-wealthy and don’t have many opportunities to cheat anyway. And even plenty of super-wealthy men don’t want to cheat. But many women are more familiar with a watered-down version of the starter wife, a fate which has probably befallen them or other women they know closely: the sunk-cost girlfriend phenomenon.

I wrote about the sunk-cost girlfriend before. Essentially, this is what happens when a couple enters a long-term relationship that persists for years while the woman waits for a proposal, and the man repeatedly says he’s going to propose, while repeatedly putting it off or coming up with fake excuses for why he hasn’t done it yet. The woman knows these years are valuable (especially if she wants kids, which she often does) and while she technically could leave at any point, she’s not sure whether or not to believe her boyfriend. If she wants marriage and kids, and this guy is actually going to propose soon, wouldn’t it be better to stick around and wait a few more months for him (it’s just around the corner!) than start over, several years older? The longer this dance goes on, the harder it is to leave.

These relationships tend to end the same way. Once it’s clear the proposal isn’t actually going to happen, they break up. Then, almost without fail, the man marries the next woman he meets, usually in a very short timeframe. The ex-girlfriend has often lost her most valuable window for marriage and children—sometimes up to ten years or more!—while the man doesn’t experience the same negative impact to his own future. In fact, the previous girlfriend may have helped him mold himself into a more eligible partner. After a seven-year relationship, he may have started dressing better, gotten into shape, made more money, or anything else that might happen to a man who has a supportive girlfriend helping him out.

Of course, that’s not to say “men age like wine,” because plenty of men let themselves go or otherwise become less desirable as they get older, but for whatever reason, that tends not to be the case with the sunk-cost boyfriends. (Selection bias, perhaps. The ones who get married shortly after the relationship—the ones we hear about and who infuriate us—are probably the ones who improved themselves over that period of time.) In fact, a woman who wrote an infamous article for The Cut advocating for age gap relationships made the case that if she married an older man, she could bypass the labor of building him up (that would be taken care of, she assumed, by his previous girlfriend) and reap the rewards, with no worry about being traded up, since she’s the trade-up.

Unless you are in such a situation (or have a friend going through it) most women don’t think about this very often provided they stay off femcel forums. But the fear of being the starter wife (or sunk-cost girlfriend) seeps into how we think and act, even if we’re not consciously worrying about it. The same is true for men and their own subconscious fear of being a cuckold (yes, some men openly obsess over a cuck anti-fantasy but for others it’s more subtle.)

For men, the fear is more pronounced and explicitly named throughout social media. Look no further than the “dual mating strategy,” which is essentially the cornerstone of most red pill and manosphere thought. This is the belief that while most women would prefer to secure commitment from a top-tier man (Chad!) most can’t, so they adopt a dual mating strategy of having wild sex with the top tier man and then settling down with a reliable schmuck who doesn’t make her horny, but who can at least be trusted to pick the kids up on time. The most extreme version of this narrative involves the “beta cuck” eventually realizing that he’s been caring for the “alpha Chad’s” kids this whole time.

I don’t think most marriages are actually like this, nor do I think most men walk around thinking like this, but it taps into a primal fear. The same is true for women and the fear of being a starter wife. While men may be extra wary of a woman using them for their money (think, going on a date with you just for a free meal) women are especially wary of men wasting their time, regardless of whether or not sex was involved. Nobody wants to be used for their money, and nobody wants to have their time wasted, but these two things seem to especially trigger men and women respectively.

Perhaps the worst part of the starter wife fear is that there is seemingly no foolproof way to avoid this fate. If you attempt to cash in and leverage your beauty for money, you could wind up with a man who explicitly only wants you for your beauty and will see no issue dropping you once you get older. But, as we learned from the Jensen Huang scandal, marrying a nerdy guy who has nothing to his name could also go wrong, provided he later becomes successful. And, on the flip side, unemployed men cheat as much as high-powered men, so you better avoid the bums too, just to be safe. Perhaps the “safest” bet is making sure your husband only ever has a mediocre job, but that’s a quite demoralizing for everyone involved.

Of course, the answer is that aside from the basic things one would do to keep the romance alive in their relationship, there’s really no surefire way to avoid being betrayed, no matter your gender or the gender of your partner. Perhaps you find that scary, but it’s not blackpilling enough to sour me off marriage or romance, and I don’t think it should be for you either. Because for every billionaire who cheats on his wife, there are many other men who don’t, and many other couples who are happily trucking along in their normal, uncontroversial, spy-free marriages. But, for better or worse, “Man Doesn’t Cheat On Wife” doesn’t really do numbers in the California Post.
