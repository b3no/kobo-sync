The midterms, 100 days out

Which is worse in a volatile midterm landscape: no money or no enthusiasm?

Halina Bennet
Jul 27
∙
Paid
 
READ IN APP
 

With fewer than 100 days left until Election Day, both parties are navigating real weaknesses. A cash-poor D.N.C. and an unmotivated G.O.P. base are trying to keep their candidates on course while primary season continues putting points on the map.

National picture

Democrats are favored for the House, underdogs in the Senate
Decision Desk HQ’s forecast 100 days out from November 3 gives Democrats a 60 percent chance of winning the House and projects a 225-to-210 majority while projecting a 50-50 Senate, which Republicans would control through Vice President JD Vance’s tiebreaking vote. Decision Desk’s chief analyst acknowledged to The Hill that the war with Iran and rising gas prices are complicating expectations for both parties on the generic ballot. Both issues are generally bad for Trump’s approval ratings and thus for Republicans — pollsters and party-aligned groups are warning that enthusiasm has disappeared among core G.O.P. voters — but when the conflict goes through cool periods and gas prices level, Democrats’ advantage tends to slip.

Newsweek’s review of prediction markets determined that Democrats have a path to a Senate majority but that their room for error is zero, with the party needing to sweep most G.O.P.-held battlegrounds and hold Michigan to come out on top.

D.N.C.’s financial crisis deepens
The D.N.C. is having money trouble. The New York Times reports that the committee is asking vendors to delay bills until after the midterms, and new reporting by NOTUS shows the D.N.C. recently took out its largest-ever off-year loan (a $15 million credit line) and put its partially owned Washington headquarters up as collateral, a practice the D.N.C. maintains is not atypical but that has concerned members given the scale of the party’s debt. As of June 30, the D.N.C. reported $16.3 million cash on hand and more than $18.5 million in debt, compared with the debtless R.N.C.’s roughly $128 million war chest.

On top of his empty wallet, D.N.C. Chair Ken Martin is facing scrutiny after the New York Times reported he threw a phone toward a young aide’s desk, which led to an internal H.R. complaint.

Mail-in-voting fight continues
A federal appeals court on Saturday upheld a lower-court block on parts of Trump’s executive order restricting mail-in voting. It is the latest legal setback for the president’s top stated policy priority. The administration says it might now appeal the ruling to the Supreme Court, the New York Times reports.

Races to watch

Maine
State Democrats on Friday officially nominated Troy Jackson, Maine’s former State Senate president, who won the primary in a near-unanimous vote of 566 to 5 over Saundra Pelletier at the state convention.

Jackson, who has hit the ground running against Susan Collins, has raised $2 million, according to his campaign. The Senate Leadership Fund is spending tens of millions to defend Collins, who has a cumulative $19 million between her campaign account and a super PAC, Politico reports. Despite Jackson’s 11th-hour entrance into the fray and the yawning gap between the candidates’ bank accounts, the enthusiasm that the Democrats drummed up early in the election to unseat Collins is likely to help elevate Jackson — and the relief at having a candidate who does not, at least publicly, carry the same baggage that Graham Platner, the Democrats’ previous candidate in the race, had is welcome for the state’s left.

Michigan
The Democratic primary between Abdul El-Sayed and Representative Haley Stevens is in a dead heat. Governor Gretchen Whitmer endorsed Stevens late last week, joining Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer, while Representative Bonnie Watson Coleman joined Representative Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez and Senators Bernie Sanders and Elizabeth Warren in endorsing El-Sayed.

Stevens is leading among Black voters, Punchbowl News reports, and the visible demographic differences between the candidates’ bases are raising issues for some.

“I saw a picture that concerned me coming out of Michigan. I saw the rally,” Representative Jim Clyburn, a Democrat from South Carolina who is Black, told Punchbowl about an El-Sayed rally. “I didn’t see a single person that looks like me. That tells me all I need to know.”

Meanwhile, Representative Ilhan Omar publicly called on Stevens to personally disavow anti-Arab comments made by a supporter who is also a school board member. The Stevens campaign had previously repudiated the comments on Stevens’s behalf.

The two will debate on Monday, July 27, on Fox 2 Detroit’s “The Pulse” ahead of next week’s primary.

South Carolina
The filing window closes Tuesday for candidates running in the special election following Senator Lindsey Graham’s death earlier this month. Governor Henry McMaster appointed Graham’s sister, Darline Graham (who has since dropped her last name, Nordone, in official campaign materials), to serve out the remainder of the term. She was sworn in on July 14 as the state’s first female senator and officially launched her campaign last week after Trump endorsed her.

On July 23, former Governor Mark Sanford entered the primary, running a campaign centered on the national debt. He joins Graham, Representative Russell Fry, and Representative Ralph Norman in a crowded G.O.P. field that Representative Nancy Mace is not entering. She has instead broken with Trump to endorse Norman. Democrat Annie Andrews, a pediatrician running a public health-focused campaign, is the Democratic nominee.

Texas
Tensions are running high in Texas, where Democratic nominee James Talarico is facing mounting pressure from Black Democrats in the state after he was criticized during a Houston town hall for his campaign’s failure to do adequate outreach to Black voters. He has since said that he supports governments “repairing past harms” and expressed his support for the (failed) George Floyd Act, Texas Scorecard reports. He also increased his outreach to Black voters with a $25 million pledge in an effort to prove that he is serious about winning their vote, which will be crucial to his chances of winning the Senate seat.

Separately, Talarico has gone on offense against his opponent, Texas Attorney General Ken Paxton, accusing him of the very same kind of voter fraud Paxton has built his career fighting. Talarico’s claims come from reporting by ProPublica and the Texas Tribune that found Paxton voted in Collin County elections using an address he had apparently moved out of in 2024 amid his divorce. ProPublica and the Texas Tribune report that Paxton may have violated state law according to election law experts, and the Collin County Democratic Party has filed a fraud complaint with the Texas secretary of state. Paxton has declined to answer questions about his registration.

Wisconsin
The August 11 Democratic primary for retiring Governor Tony Evers’s seat is only getting more chaotic. State Representative Francesca Hong, who is backed by multiple chapters of the Democratic Socialists of America, currently leads a five-way Marquette poll among Democratic candidates. The survey of 838 registered voters was taken July 8 to 16 — ending the day before Lieutenant Governor Sara Rodriguez exited the race — and included former Lieutenant Governor Mandela Barnes, who at the time was no longer in the race but has now stepped back in after Rodriguez pulled out. Despite Hong leading in the poll among Democrats, Barnes is the only Democrat still in the race who led likely G.O.P. nominee Representative Tom Tiffany in general-election polling (44 to 40 percent). Hong trails Tiffany 40 to 43 percent.

This dynamic has resulted in the right-leaning outlet Wisconsin Right Now and Brady Penfield, a Turning Point USA-aligned operative, both publicly stating their support for Hong in the primary with the explicit goal of handing Tiffany an easier general election.

“Little does Fran know that I’m her worst nightmare,” Penfield wrote on X after he and his roommates received Hong’s mailers. “However, I’ll be voting for her in the primary to ensure Tom Tiffany has an easy win in November.”

A Republican Governors Association-linked super PAC has reportedly spent more than $2 million boosting Hong-related ads, which the Barnes campaign has called out as G.O.P. meddling, the New York Times reports. But while the Democrats focus their attention on party infighting, Tiffany has outraised the entire field combined.
