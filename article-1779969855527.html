The Most Problematic Age Gap: Two 18-Year-Olds

I never thought I'd live to see an age gap shamed for being too small, but here we are.

I wrote about age gap relationship shaming a few weeks ago—mostly the fact that it exists to protect an individual’s desired dating market by enforcing a consequence for failing to settle down young. Most commonly, we see this with women shaming older men who date younger, but that’s probably because those relationships are just more common than the reverse. As I covered, older women dating younger men are subject to the exact same treatment, with many critics calling these relationships “hagmaxxing” and accusing the women of failing to get married at more acceptable ages because they were too busy “competing with men on the job market.” (Show me on the doll where the employed woman hurt you.)

Age gap relationships between consenting adults (I’m talking 10-15+ years, not 5) have their own challenges and are not the desired scenario for your average young person, but they are typically harmless, some turn out great, and it’s stupid to moralize about them. However, I never thought I’d see the day when people attempted to shame relationships for the participants—in these recent cases, two teenagers—being too close in age.

I won’t post photos of the children involved because, well, it’s weird, but all week I have been repeatedly bombarded with the same prom photo of four good-looking teenagers with the caption:

The stark physical difference between young men and young women of the same age is severely understated. Relationships with significant age gaps are very obviously biologically advantageous.

Big asterisk on “significant age gaps” because the guy who posted it is in his mid-twenties, not his forties (I am laughing at the idea of trying to convince an eighteen-year-old girl to ditch “boys her age” and settle down with a “real man,” and that real man is a 24-year-old named Aiden.) However, several other men who actually were much older agreed with him. In their minds, the boys looked obviously underdeveloped compared to the girls, therefore, something was wrong with the girls wanting to date these boys instead of men old enough to be their dads. Notably, nobody could explain what sinister force made these “unnatural” relationships happen.

The “biological advantage” of breaking up these teen relationships so the girls can date older men is nonexistent, really, unless it’s a euphemism for “that kind of relationship would make my penis happy, personally.” And while shaming relationships with large age gaps is a strategy to protect one’s desired dating pool, the same is true of shaming relationships between two teenagers who are too close in age. If your desired dating pool is “teenage girls,” that is.

Perpetuating the myth that teenage girls don’t find boys around their own age attractive, or that young men are “worthless” and shouldn’t bother even attempting relationships until they get older, is a very obvious form of intrasexual competition. #MenInWomensFields.

The thing about women is that we have a superpower. No, not the superpower of giving life through the miracle of childbirth, although as Trump might say, that’s “really quite something.” No—our most remarkable power is the superhuman capacity to generally find guys our own age attractive no matter how young or old we are.

As a thirty-six-year-old woman, I do not find high school aged boys attractive. But did I, when I was seventeen? Yes, In fact, I dated boys far less aesthetically pleasing or developed than the ones in the prom photo. There were a variety of fedoras and retainers in my dating history, and I have dated guys who weighed less than 100 pounds, okay? When I was fourteen, I spent almost the entire year heartbroken over a sixteen-year-old boy who was 5’4” and had no facial hair, but did have a full wardrobe of kids’ size Hawaiian T-shirts.

My parents got divorced when I was fifteen, which meant I witnessed my mother dating in her forties. I definitely got more of a VIP front row seat to these antics than I would have liked, so I learned a lot of details about the guys she was dating, who ranged in age from thirty-two to fifty-something. And even the “younger guys” looked hilariously old to me. In fact, sometimes I worried about my own capacity for someday finding grown men attractive when my mom would describe a forty-five-year-old guy as “really good-looking” and then she’d show me a photo that might as well have been of Prince fucking Philip.

But alas, now I’m thirty-six, and I am married to a guy who is almost thirty-nine, and I consider him objectively better looking than men in their twenties. I even consider him better-looking now than in photos from twenty years ago, and it’s not because he had some kind of weight loss transformation or clothing makeover—he was a pretty hot college guy whose fraternity nicknamed him “The Bod.”

This is why I am skeptical of any discussions attempting to identify men’s “sexual peak.” We all know that most men, no matter their own age, find women in their twenties the most physically attractive. But there is no universal “golden age” for men’s attractiveness, despite how many completely data-free sexual market value graphs are made, because women’s attraction is directly dependent on their own ages. It’s kind of like saying “what the best temperature?” but failing to specify whether you mean for the weather or for a roast chicken.

A man will always be most desired by women about 2-5 years younger than he is. Arguably, this means a man’s real peak is in his nineties because, provided he lives that long, there is a huge surplus of single women who specifically prefer him at his age. But nobody says that, because on some level we know that “men’s peak” happens whenever the women he finds most attractive prefer men his age in the greatest quantities. So assuming all men find twenty-year-olds the hottest, men’s “peak” would be in their mid-twenties.

Yes, there are mitigating factors that might push a man’s ideal age a bit higher, like the ability to own a home or take a girl to a date that isn’t a Jacob Elordi lookalike contest in the park, full of random mustachioed ne’er-do-wells who look nothing like Jacob Elordi. Perhaps we can adjust men’s peak age up to thirty, but even then, the vast majority of twenty-year-old girls aren’t dating guys ten years older (let me remind you that the average age at first marriage is still just two years apart) and it’s not because feminist harridans are “shaming” them out of it. And any discussions about men hitting their peak at forty? I mean, that’s definitely true if you’re primarily looking to attract women my age, but I doubt it!

Yes, wealthy and famous (and often also good-looking) men can date women young enough to be their granddaughters until the day they die like a dog, but these men are obvious outliers. Your average older man is not Brad Pitt (currently dating a thirty-year-old) the same way your average older woman is not Rachel Weisz (married to the hunky Daniel Craig, had a baby at forty-eight.) The most attractive people of any age group will be able to live their dreams. As we can see with hot older celebrities, this usually means (for men) dating much younger or (for women) marrying a top-tier man their own age. Many such cases.

How does any of this relate to the teenage boys, joyfully preparing for their prom and pinning corsages, blissfully unaware of the fact that their photos would be ripped apart by the likes of AureliusConquest1488? Because as strange as it looks to adults who see babyfaced teenage boys and can’t fathom how anyone could find them attractive, teenage girls absolutely do. That’s just how your average heterosexual woman works. We find twenty-year-olds hot when we’re eighteen, and by the time we’re forty, we think forty-five-year-olds are hot. Some of us have wider ranges than others, and some of us have different preferences, but there is a reason that, on average, first marriages in the Western world dating back hundreds of years are always between a young woman and a man just 2-5 years older.

Provided everyone is consenting and no laws are being broken, shaming any age gap directly exposes your own insecurities and attempts at sexual arbitrage, while attempting to pass these feelings off as a form of moral superiority. This is always dumb, but especially dumb when the “gap” you’re shaming is just the way most relationships happen for most people.

But moreover, perhaps due to wishful thinking or a very bad understanding of history, certain men insist that actually, two eighteen-year-olds dating is the perverted thing, because the “norm” is cross-age breeding (yes, the term “cross-age breeding” was actually used, I regret to inform you.)

And sure, if by “cross-age” you mean a few years, you’d be right on the money, backed up by census data going back centuries. But generally, in societies where people marry for love, and where young people can choose who they marry, couples follow an incredibly predictable age gap pattern. If this was so unnatural and undesirable, why would everyone have been doing it in England in the 1500s? Probably all the XOJane millennial feminists forcing young women to pretend to find men their own age attractive.

There is no real “biological advantage” to larger age gaps except for the fact that they make some people horny. Enjoying these relationships, provided the younger person is of legal age, is fine. Pretending we have a righteous moral imperative to normalize and promote them is another thing. It’s “Leonardo DiCaprio can’t handle a woman his own age” in reverse. If teenage girls are happy to go to date teenage boys, perhaps we should acknowledge this is a normal part of being a teenage girl, no matter how unappealing teenage boys look to adults.
