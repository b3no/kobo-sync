Casual Dating Before Marriage Isn't a Luxury Belief

Many casual daters ultimately want marriage--and still obtain it. Alex Cooper is no exception.

In case you missed it, Alex Cooper of sex-positive podcast Call Her Daddy fame has announced her pregnancy, or according to one highly publicized accusation on Twitter from Brad Wilcox of the Institute for Family Studies, “quietly got married and had a baby” despite hypocritically preaching the gospel of casual sex. (I won’t spend this article dunking on him specifically—he’s deleted the tweet so best not to pile on.)

But just to get one thing out of the way, none of this was done “quietly.” Cooper’s 2023 wedding was covered in Vogue, and her pregnancy wasn’t incidentally spotted by paparazzi, but announced by a PR team with professional maternity photos. She has openly mentioned her husband multiple times on her podcast. One must wonder if Brad Wilcox also considers Gilbert Gottfried “softspoken.”

But anyway, Wilcox wasn’t the only one to react with surprise or even anger at Alex Cooper’s baby announcement, given that she has made a public persona out of being a sex-positive woman who runs a podcast that basically consists of women’s locker room talk. Famously, she gets famous women to admit naughty details of their lives, such as Katy Perry saying that if her then-beau Orlando Bloom had washed the dishes, she would announce, “Get ready to get your dick sucked.” Well, at least we know Justin Trudeau is having fun!

The main accusation is that Cooper was “hypocritical” to create content that presented casual dating in a positive light while getting married and having a baby. By secretly choosing a traditional path for herself, she has apparently been living a double life and lying to all the impressionable women who consume her content.

But the people angry about Alex Cooper’s life decisions are not genuinely concerned for her fans. They want Cooper (and women like her) to suffer the consequences of playing the field and failing to settle down as young as possible—which, by the way, is the same revenge fantasy often used to shame age-gap relationships. But Cooper’s timeline is far from unusual, and her decision to date around and then get married to a man she met at the absolutely decrepit age of gasp twenty-six, is actually incredibly common. Many women who date casually, or who sleep around, still get married—some on a relatively typical timeline—not to “beta chumps” who don’t know any better, but to men who have lived similar lives, who had similar timelines in mind.

For many casual daters, often upper middle class educated young people in cities, marriage is still the goal, but they view their young adulthood as the time to date around—not instead of getting married, but before it. It’s not a luxury belief to enjoy casual sex and then get married. It’s just what lots of people do.

Personally, I am not a die-hard Alex Cooper fan, nor do I have any personal interest in defending her or her life choices. We probably disagree on plenty of things. I am, famously, not a fan of casual sex. I have written before about how hookup culture was inappropriately pushed on teenagers in the 2000s, especially teenage girls who felt pressure to be adequately “chill” for a cohort of boys whose ideal date involved nothing more than a blowjob behind a dumpster whilst quoting South Park. (As for whether these girls are listening to Call Her Daddy, I do not think teenage girls are all that jazzed to hear about Gwyneth Paltrow’s sex life, but I could be wrong.)

Another reason that my defense of Alex Cooper is not personally motivated or “cope,” as the kids say: I also got married in my mid-twenties to a man I met at nineteen, and I have written about how advantageous it is to meet your spouse in college. If a twenty-year-old girl who strongly wanted marriage asked me if she should focus on finding a serious partner or juggling several rotating dick appointments for eight years, I would absolutely tell her to prioritize monogamy. Not because casual sex is wrong, or because it will fuck you up forever, but because the pool of desirable partners is biggest when you’re all young and single.

But what traditionally-minded people like me often get wrong about casual dating is that they assume anyone who engages in it is rejecting marriage. This is why they believe Alex Cooper is peddling lies—surely, if she makes lighthearted jokes about casual sex, she is anti-marriage! But even though I was never a fan of hookup culture, it’s always been obvious to me that people who engage in casual dating are still interested in marriage, and often find it eventually. The reason for this is that casual daters—the type of people discussed on Call Her Daddy—tend to exist in milieus where everyone is casually dating in their twenties. A man living in this world typically expects his female peers to be casual daters too, and asking about someone’s “body count” is deeply embarrassing behavior.

I have plenty of observational experiences to back up this theory. I spent the first half of my twenties in San Francisco, and I was the youngest of my friend group by about six years. I was also the only one who was engaged or married. (I got engaged at twenty-four, and married within ten months.) My friends—all thirty or almost thirty—wanted to get married eventually, but found it very silly that I was so focused on it at such a young age. They weren’t wildly promiscuous, per se, but they had a variety of casual and serious relationships. At one point, they suggested we create a group blog of all our “dating disasters” but I was told to step back from the blog when all of my stories were about handjobs and involved one of our moms picking us up at the end of the date. But my friends had loads of stories—real ones—and they weren’t ashamed. At thirty, they were starting to think seriously about finding husbands, but not overly stressed about it. At the time, I remember feeling bad for them, convinced that all of them would remain unhappily single until they died because they had failed to capitalize on their youth when they had the chance (yeah yeah, I know, take me outside and shoot me like a dog for that one.)

I’m not really in touch with most of these women anymore, but they are now all in their early forties and searchable on Facebook. Here’s what they’re up to now:

  • Woman A: Married at 40

  • Woman B: Engaged in late 30s, seems to be off Facebook but I assume the wedding happened

  • Woman C: Married in late 30s, now has a toddler with her husband (She met her husband around age 30)

  • Woman D: Married in late 30s, now has a toddler with her husband (She met her husband in her late thirties)

Even the drunken party girls I met spontaneously at at SantaCon or Bay to Breakers, who I friended on Facebook on a whim, are all now living incredibly conventional lives. They are almost all around forty and married with kids, to men I’d say are “in their leagues.”

It’s also not surprising that these women got married, in part because they were social butterflies with lots of friends, always going places and meeting people, and knew how to make conversation. Yes, I’m aware “men only care about youth and fertility,” but what can I say, obviously the men in San Francisco have different priorities from guys with Roman Statue avatars on Twitter.

I also know some naysayers will insist these women simply gave up when they realized they couldn’t bag Chad and married some loser because he was the only guy willing to date them in their post-wall state. Perhaps this would be the case if these women were living in a culture where casual dating was unusual and stigmatized, and where they needed to apologize for it. But they were living in the San Francisco tech world in the 2010s. Everyone was dating casually. We even had a startup (actually a cult) that was all about fingering, for crying out loud. (The CEO famously got fingered in front of a group of investors—talk about a disruptor!) Women who date casually in their twenties often marry men who also dated casually in their twenties.

Would I switch places with them, so that I got married at forty instead of twenty-four? Do I wish I had dated casually for the first ten years of my adulthood? Would I speak at a college commencement and advise thousands of young women to date casually? No. I chose my life path because getting married young for the purpose of having multiple children was very important to me. I’m assuming that my San Francisco friends—the ones who were still single at thirty—did not feel quite so strongly about the importance of having children by a certain age. And when it comes to Alex Cooper, her “casual dating” phase lasted less than a decade. She met her husband at twenty-six, married him at twenty-nine, and got pregnant at thirty-one. While my single San Francisco friends took a very long time to get married, lots of casual daters hang up their hats while still in their twenties. And again, that’s normal.

Statistics (including my own) will generally show that people with higher body counts are less likely to be married by a certain age. But this is pretty obviously a correlation problem. If someone in her thirties has only had 1-3 partners, by definition she’s likely to be married because that outcome is a hell of a lot more likely than remaining celibate for most of a 10-20 year period. A woman who remains single for any reason will likely have many opportunities to have sex, even if she is not hellbent on racking up a high body count. It’s less about more sexual partners making you undesirable for marriage, and more about being single for longer correlating with more opportunities for sexual partners.

So no, Alex Cooper is not living a double life or peddling luxury beliefs. She’s just doing what lots of people in her milieu do, men and women combined. If her podcast changes its tune, that’s also expected. People grow—Taylor Swift’s music sounds different than it did in 2011, and in just a few years, Euphoria went from a show about horny teenagers to a D-tier Quentin Tarantino movie. And if Alex Cooper wants to have me on as a guest as a thank you for this defense, I would love to tell her all my tenth grade handjob stories.
