Don't Be The Girl Who Dates On Her Birthday

The other week, when I wrote about women making the first move, an interesting sub-conversation happened within the 300-plus comments. Assuming a man met the bar of “attractive enough” in the first place, women preferred men who took initiative, and in some cases, aggressively pursued them. And yet, another seemingly contradictory stereotype exists about female sexuality: that we want what we can’t have, and are actually turned on by mysterious aloof men who do not make their interest obvious.

Part of this contradiction is that women are different, or that a man who is less interested in a woman, by default, is probably just more attractive to begin with (he’s less interested because he’s out of her league, many such cases!) But I actually think this contradiction exists within men as well. How many men say, “I just wish women would tell me they like me and stop playing games!” yet call a woman “clingy” when she texts him too often? (Don’t tell me this never happens—it’s happened to me multiple times, often with a guy who was initially very interested!) This is a less gendered phenomenon than we might think. People enjoy being pursued (or at least having the other party show clear interest) and some people even enjoy very aggressive pursuits, but if a person seems eager or anxious, it’s a turnoff.

This phenomenon is also not limited to dating! People enjoy when potential friends ask to hang out, and when there’s reciprocal effort to meet up and maintain the friendship. But sometimes, a friend will be…just a little too available, a little too eager, and some degree of platonic ick emerges.

So what the hell is going on? Where is the line? After studying this phenomenon for many years of my life, I am prepared to make a generalization: people like when people make them feel good about themselves, whether it’s for dating or friendship. And if someone seems like they really want to hang out with you because they have nothing else going on, it’s not flattering.

People want to feel like the first choice for a winner. They do not want to feel like the last resort for a loser.

Making friends or dating is actually a bit like interviewing for jobs—something at which I’m surprisingly good. If you’re learning how to interview, you immediately realize that showing enthusiasm about the job and the company is paramount. Most of the time, this enthusiasm will be completely fake. If you’re applying for some mid-level individual contributor role, there’s about a 0% chance that this is your “dream job,” but you have to claim it is, and make up a convincing reason why. Then you have to pretend that you’re more enthusiastic about that company than any other company on your job search. You know how they always ask you, “Why do you want to work here?” Like, what the fuck are you supposed to say? They want to be your first choice, even though statistically, every job search involves many companies and it’s highly unlikely that your company will be everyone’s first choice. But every time I got a job, I had told the recruiter and hiring manager that this job was, by far, my first choice. It’s the “haha I’ve never done this before with any other guys” of job interviewing.

I realize some of you may not realize the amount of duplicitous fake enthusiasm that is required to get a job. But it works. You’re welcome.

But eventually, I realized that another key part of interviewing is appearing important, in-demand and not easily accessible. I figured out the enthusiasm piece very quickly, but it took me years to realize that getting on my knees and begging for a job won’t get me anywhere—well, aside from a job as some pervert’s secretary. You need to create a sense of urgency and high demand around you. This doesn’t mean purposefully missing recruiter calls, but for example, when an interviewer asks you “where you are with other companies?” you are absolutely not supposed to say that you just started the job search and this is one of the first interviews you’ve done. You are especially not supposed to say, “I’ve applied to a bunch of companies, but nobody has even called me in for an interview…until you guys.” Instead, you’re supposed to vaguely insinuate that you are in later stages with other companies and could get a job offer at any time, and then the company interviewing you will try harder to lock you down.

In case anyone is thinking, “Hmmm…sounds familiar,” well…you’re right.

Socializing with other people is almost exactly like this. You need to balance enthusiasm that feels extremely personalized (“Wow, this person specifically thinks I am really interesting!”) with the idea that you are not actually that available or desperate—that you could be socializing with just about anyone but have chosen to socialize with that person. Yes, even if this isn’t actually true.

A friend of mine recently came to this conclusion too after witnessing the dynamics of his wife’s friend group. He surmised that one of the women in the group was the “alpha” of the group. I asked if he meant she was the prettiest or most successful. He said, “Neither—she’s the least available.” This woman, by virtue of having the fullest life with the most things to do, had secured her position as the top dog in her friend group. (I’m aware this could also be a correlation, not only a causation.) He mentioned that all the other women were eager to hang out with her because they never knew when they’d get the chance next.

Most of us don’t need to be the alpha of their friend group, but the same strategy is relevant for making friends or finding a romantic partner. If people think you took a break from all your other options for friends, partners or activities to talk to them, and you really enjoyed them in spite of your myriad of other options, they will like you, even if you didn’t really dazzle them by being exceptionally funny, charming or smart. For most of my life, I’ve made the mistake of assuming that being exceptionally funny or charming is important, but what’s more important is making other people feel special. You can do this with conversational skills, of course, but you also need to appear in-demand.

It’s very easy to take this advice and use it to brag about how important and busy you are, but people really hate braggarts, so that will backfire. In fact, I’ve noticed that of all the female influencers and bloggers dragged most often on Reddit snark subs, the most common reason is that they seem full of themselves. Projecting a lifestyle of importance doesn’t mean awkwardly boasting about all your accomplishments and how many people want to befriend or date you. I once eavesdropped on a man on a first date who had opened Google Maps and was showing a very bored-to-tears woman how many countries he had visited. Don’t do that. It’s as simple as, if someone asks what you did last weekend, making sure that answer include social engagements or activities. Ideally, your answer should be true, which is why actually having a full social life helps the most, but I understand it’s hard to have a full social life if you don’t know anyone to begin with. You have to start somewhere. That said, there are answers between “lying about a fake party” and “admitting I was at home all weekend watching Pop The Balloon dating show clips on Twitter.” Join a volunteering organization, running club, or something else, not only for fun and to meet people, but so if someone asks you what you were doing all weekend, you have a good answer.

I’m reminded of a boy I knew in high school who basically did the opposite of this. He believed that his best bet for securing friends or girlfriends was making everyone feel bad about themselves while also pitying him, doubling down that nobody involved had anything better to be doing. I, unsurprisingly, did not like him very much. When my ninth grade boyfriend dumped me, he assured me that I was “damaged goods” and he was now the only person who would have me. Clearly, he did not mind socialization arising from desperation—he sought it out! But he made the mistake of thinking other people wanted this as well. When it was time for the winter formal, he asked me to be his date. For no particular reason, he added, “Jane wouldn’t go with me, so I’m asking you.” I was already planning on saying no, but this sure as hell didn’t help!

He was not the best-looking person in the world, but there were boys who looked like him, or even boys less physically attractive, who had loads of friends and who dated (my high school had a very mismatched male-female ratio, so even weird-looking boys had lots of options.) His looks weren’t the problem. The problem was that he made everyone around him feel like a loser—and not just a loser, but the last resort for a loser—the biggest loser possible.

Nobody wants to feel like the last resort for a desperate person who has no other options. I have a feeling some men—especially romantically unsuccessful or inexperienced men—find this hard to believe, because they don’t consider the way such a hypothetical woman would make him feel. They insist, repeatedly, that a woman with no friends and no options, who is desperate for them, would be really hot. It’s easy to understand why they think this would appeal to them. They’ve never gotten much female attention, so they imagine they’d like it no matter how they got it, bonus points if it was easy. Plus, every time I notice anything about attraction dynamics which is unrelated to boobs, there’s some guy waiting in the wings to inform me that men are noble savages who are strictly interested in boobs and any attempt to humanize them with depth and desire beyond mammary glands is “cope” and projecting a feminine mindset onto a man’s brain. But anyway, I’ve had plenty of guys say that they would love it if a woman who had absolutely nothing else going on, no friends, no incoming male attention from anyone else, aggressively pursued them. (The unspoken part of this fantasy is that this woman is hot, of course, so it’s particularly implausible that she’d have no other options.) I also have a feeling that this scenario is appealing to them because they have social anxiety, often paired with laziness, and an extremely forward, desperate woman requires nothing of them.

I notice this confusion arising online when women and incels argue over whether or not unattractive women are justified in being sad about being single. Incels will point out that “men will fuck anything,” and therefore ugly women should keep their chin(s) up because there’s probably some guy out there who can’t score anyone hot and will deign to use them as a fleshlight for a minute or two—a scenario which will almost definitely not end in orgasm or a relationship for the woman. But women can still enjoy sex without an orgasm, and the main reason is that they still feel “desired.” A woman does not feel desired if a man is horny for anything—ideally a woman, but a piece of soft leather furniture if necessary—and has chosen her as a second-to-last resort.

While I don’t think most men feel this way, the “no other options, desperate, yet still hot” scenario is what makes mail order brides so appealing to a minority of men. Presumably, a man who travels to impoverished countries to find a wife knows that the woman who goes home with him will be doing it out of monetary desperation, not attraction. Perhaps he doesn’t think women can differentiate between those things, or he has sold himself the lie that straight women aren’t physically attracted to men anyway, so needing a man’s money and citizenship is the same thing as wanting him. But I think the vast majority of men find this arrangement unappealing, for the simple reason that you want your partner to want you, not to need you (or, frankly, anyone) in order to survive.

Put into practice, this is why constantly being the one to text first (and right away) turns a lot of people off. It doesn’t just read “enthusiastic,” at least not in the earliest stages. It reads “has nothing else going on.” It’s kind of like the Seinfeld episode where Jerry goes on a date with an attractive woman, but is overcome with the ick when he realizes it’s her birthday and she didn’t have plans with anyone else.

This can obviously go too far in the other direction too. A while ago, I started befriending this woman who, at first, seemed like someone who could become a close friend. She was a great conversationalist and did an amazing job showing interest in other people. But quickly, I realized she was just…never available. I don’t think she was making this up to avoid me, but he had an older child involved in travel sports, so when she wasn’t partaking in travel sports she was going out of town to her second property. When she saw me, she was enthusiastic! She genuinely seemed interested in me! But after a while, I stopped inviting her to things because she was almost guaranteed to be busy, and she never once made the first move.

Almost everyone will say that they find “playing hard to get” very unappealing, and I would agree with that, but if you are playing hard to get well, nobody will know that’s what you’re doing. You will just look like someone who has a lot going on, with golden windows of availability. When I first met my husband, I had very little going on, but I didn’t want him to know that, so I would deliberately delay responses to his texts and frequently invent fake stories about pre-games with my friends before we met up, when in fact I was drinking blue raspberry vodka by myself in my dorm room. Most people will not have to lie like this because most people are not in the echelon of loser that I was in college. But anyway, it worked.

If you’re mastered appearing slightly unavailable, you also need to have the enthusiasm piece for any of this to work—otherwise, you just don’t seem interested. In practice, showing enthusiasm for any gender will involve asking lots of open-ended questions, first and foremost, and then asking followup questions. You should make eye contact and actually listen to the other person. Remembering small details about someone’s life is a big power move. I used to keep my coworkers’ personal details in a spreadsheet so I could effortlessly bring them up later, when I would frequently be complimented on being such a good listener.

For women, especially young women, having “big” reactions to people’s answers to your questions (especially if you are platonically socializing with another woman) is really important. Obviously, not every woman is like this, but a while ago I realized that I was not responding correctly to things other women said. Even if I thought a woman said something interesting, I wouldn’t show any clear enthusiasm, I’d just tell her it was interesting. I started observing how other women acted with each other and I noticed much more hyperbole in their enthusiasm. If a friend showed me a new dress, I might say “Oh wow, that looks great on you” but I noticed other friends, when showing off their new clothing, might say “Girl. STOP. That is GORGEOUS.” I couldn’t deny that the latter would also make me feel really good if I heard it! I began experimenting with having these big reactions until it came naturally to me.

Now, for men, you don’t want to say that, because the woman will simply code you as a gay friend. Having outsized, flowery responses is not required, or even helpful, if you are a man. I have approximately zero experience making male friends as a man, so I have less perspective here, but I have noticed that men have their own version of, “Girl. STOP. That is GORGEOUS.” Male friend groups seem to thrive on inside jokes. My husband and his friend group call these “gimmicks,” to the point that even the use of the term “gimmick” has become a gimmick, especially after one of the men in the group was caught at a bar chatting up a woman by informing her that he’s “really good at gimmicks.” A British guy might refer to this as “banter,” or better yet, “top bant,” and while I can identify it, I don’t have many tips for being good at it.

Dating is obviously a bit more challenging than making platonic friends, if only because unless you’re polyamorous, you can only have a relationship with one person at a time so there are fewer people available to date, and the standards for dating are much higher. Can you imagine how difficult friendship would be if you were only allowed to have one friend at a time? Presumably, you’d be friend-ghosted constantly. Anyone looking to use these tips for dating should be aware that the bar to hit for dating is just higher than for friendship. Very few people would refuse to befriend someone over their looks, but refusing to date someone you don’t find physically attractive is very common. If you are just really not someone’s type, mastering the perfect combination of enthusaistic/unavailable is not going to turn things around. But it certainly beats looking desperate and bored.

And if someone asks you on a date, and it’s your birthday, maybe do it the day after.
