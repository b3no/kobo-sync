I have never had any particular interest in playing GTA or any similar game, and I had a feeling the GTA 6 launch would never fully register in my mind, just like whatever is going on with sports at any given time.

But, as it turns out, GTA 6 isn’t what I (or anyone) expected. Yes, you get to steal cars, but consider that more of a side-theme compared to the real meat of the game, every man’s favorite activity: building furniture with your girlfriend.

It’s almost as if someone purposefully trojan horsed GTA 6 to trick young men into developing emotional intelligence and relationship skills. Oh, you think you’re stealing cars? Well, you are, but you know what comes first: your partner’s mental load and how you can release the burden.

Moreover, when you play as Lucia, the woman, you still have to put effort into maintaining the relationship, but mostly in the form of “fun” activities like buying gifts and sending selfies. Lucia is under no obligation to paint walls, unclog sinks, or clean up kitchens. They weren’t kidding about Woke 4! What are her pronouns?

But, despite the complaints, guys are playing the game! The reactions range from scathing, to glowing, to liking the game but finding it amusing and puzzling that so much of it involves domestic chores. And despite jokes about the game being a relationship simulator, it seems that the very men who would be targets for something like that (incels) are immediately put off by how big of a “simp” or “cuck” they have to be in order to progress. After all, if you’re going to have a fantasy relationship to replace the unpleasantness of real-life, surely it doesn’t involve unclogging toilets and picking up Midol on the way home.

Perhaps there’s another less conspiratorial explanation for all the chores. Some of the most popular games around have involved ridiculous degrees of drudgery. This is actually an area where my expertise is valuable, because a great deal of The Sims (or really, any simulator game) involve lots of annoying to-do tasks that most people already do in real life, and yet, for some reason, they’re choosing to do fake versions of these things for hours a day on a screen. Sometimes, this even takes an absurd and tragic turn, like when a couple in South Korea spent so much time raising a virtual baby in a video game that their actual, real-life baby died of neglect. The vast majority of gamers won’t do anything like that, but I’d be the first to admit I’ve neglected the dishes to make my Sim wash the dishes.

Back when I played The Sims (I haven’t played it in years) it had a reputation for being about dramatic love triangles, romantic weddings and untold riches, but most of it was actually spent on the menial labor and organization tasks which made those achievements more exciting. Your Sim can’t have that scandalous affair if he hasn’t showered and needs to pee. Your Sim can’t walk down the aisle if it’s snowing and you didn’t consider the weather when picking the day. Your Sim can’t get rich instantly (unless you cheat) and actually, you need to spend multiple days making her read books and practice speeches in front of the mirror.

The Sims was so focused on the mundanity of life that we even got an expansion pack about (and I’m not making this up) dust. And when users generate their own content and mods, some are thrilling and fantastical, but others are just more boring realism. There is a mod that enables your Sim to have dental insurance. There’s another mod all about HVAC systems.

This pattern is also seen across any kind of farm simulator, where the entire game is the menial labor: finding seeds, planting them, watering them, and so on. Farmville was a Zynga Facebook game that had people in a chokehold, despite revolving entirely around watering plans.

Now, Sims (or Farmville for that matter) is a simulator which mostly appeals to women. But are women that much more interested in the mundanity of life than men? Are women itching to do the laundry and vacuum the dust? If anything, women are more likely to do these things in real life and therefore more likely to want to escape from them. But somehow, the realism mods appeal to them. And despite most of us probably being bored to tears working on an actual farm, farm-oriented games (Farmville, Animal Crossing, The Sims Cottage Living) are remarkably popular and addictive, despite most of the gameplay consisting of watering plants and harvesting crops. The sense of achievement (no matter how fake) along with the brief and exciting thrills, make for a really good game, even if on the surface it looks boring as hell.

Perhaps something similar is happening with the men who enjoy GTA 6. Yes, they want the thrills and crimes, but getting to that point after quite literally watching paint dry could be more satisfying—or at least satisfying enough for them to want to keep playing.

Plus, there are plenty of drudgery-simulation games that appeal primarily to a male audience, they’re just not focused on things like cooking and cleaning. Flight and train simulator games are slow with long cruising timelines and annoying checklists, but are mostly played by boys and men. I spent a great deal of my youth playing Sid Meier’s Civilization with my younger brother, which involved a tremendous amount of planning and waiting for monuments to built, and not as many battle-time as one might expect. And, per my research, in Red Dead Redemption 2, a great deal of time is spent taking care of your horse, cleaning gus, and getting food. My husband used to play Legend of Zelda, and while watching him play I was astonished by how much time was spent cooking and buying ingredients for meals.

So maybe all the chores are worth it after all. Or at the very least, they’re tolerable. On a YouTube tutorial about cleaning and repairing Jason’s house, the comments are full of men graciously thanking the creator for breaking it down, zero complaints about the task itself:

On a different tutorial, a user applauds the game for teaching him some useful handyman skills, with one user saying, “I Love that GTA 6 includes these quaint, everyday moments too.”

So maybe the right-wing influencers of Twitter are in the minority. A lot of people really are enjoying the drudgery of GTA 6.
