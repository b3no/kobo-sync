The Cold Pizza Theory of Sex

Women are more sexually discerning than men—but that doesn’t mean they enjoy sex less.

A few years ago, I made a sweeping statement on Twitter which I now regret, and not because any kind of mob demanded an apology. I declared, very confidently, that men are much hornier than women.

I don’t think I was entirely wrong. There is not, last I checked, a booming industry of women paying for men’s used underwear. If a man wants to make money as an OnlyFans performer, his best bet would be to market himself to gay men, not women. Women do not seek out casual sex the way men do. To lure men onto apps and websites meant for casual hookups, a large number of female bots must be deployed. And famously, when social scientists ran an experiment by having an attractive person proposition random college students for sex, the woman had a 75% success rate and the man had a 0% success rate. (I can’t stop thinking about that study without imagining what the decoy told the men after they said yes—did Ashton Kutcher suddenly appear to punk them?)

It’s tempting to look at all this evidence and deduce that men desire women, and women don’t really desire men. Or at the very least, women desire men but only Glenn Powell as a sadistic aristocratic vampire, so their desire never winds up sparked by anything in real life. Ergo, the everyday experience of heterosexuality is men lusting after women, and women recoiling and fleeing into the forest to locate the nearest bear (and they would, a bear has got to be at least seven feet tall. Grizzlymogged oncemore!)

But perhaps the most commonly-used argument to defend the idea that women don’t really find men sexually attractive, or experience any degree of the sexual desire men do, is the cold pizza theory. Basically, men will take any woman, in any scenario, because they see woman like pizza and all pizza is basically fine, even if it’s cold. (More proof that I am not a man: I do not like cold pizza, even in the literal sense.) Meanwhile, women require that specific conditions be met for them to be aroused. Using the pizza analogy, women might not even want hot pizza…if it’s the wrong crust thickness, the wrong sauce, or if it’s Domino’s.

Ergo: men like “pizza” more than women do.

The problem with this theory is that, even examining it as a pizza-only phenomenon, it’s simply not true that a person who eats crappy cold pizza enjoys pizza more than someone who is specific about their favorite crust thickness and toppings. My brother is perhaps the biggest pizza enthusiast I’ve ever met in my life (remember, I am still, quite literally, talking about pizza) and yet he will flat-out refuse to eat many types of pizza. He only likes thin crust pizza, he has a strong preference for the classic pizza margherita, and he has a specific cheese-to-sauce ratio he likes. I have teased him for being a “pizza snob” more times than I can count.

But my brother fucking adores pizza. It’s his favorite food. He would eat pizza every day if he could. His specificity about the pizza means nothing about how much he likes it. This is why the cold pizza theory of men’s and women’s sexuality doesn’t stack up quite as neatly as a box of frozen DiGiorno’s. Specificity and discernment does not equal a lack of desire.

The cold pizza theory is broadly correct that most straight women would not enjoy having sex with a cold pizza man—say, a smelly man they found physically unattractive on a damp mattress, although I also have the feeling that when men refer to “cold pizza” women, they’re still generally referring to women who are young, clean, pleasant and not-fat, which is…not really cold pizza, if you catch my drift. I don’t doubt that men cast a wider net for sex and have fewer stipulations around it, but again, this means nothing about how much they like sex.

As Louis CK said, in a joke that now feels all-too-self-aware, women are tourists in sexual perversion and men are prisoners. And it’s true, there are no records of female comedians blocking a door to bean-flick in front of horrified captive men. But, funny as the joke may be, I don’t know that it’s so universally true. Men may believe their “sexual perversion” is more significant because our society revolves around it, while repeatedly pretending women don’t feel physical or sexual desire for men, just a drive for intimacy and resources. And while a lot of men propagate this theory, straight women themselves often do as well—although I would venture that many of the “straight” women who truly almost never find men attractive might want to explore this great new thing called “women.”

There is no greater misunderstanding of women’s sexuality than the idea that women only experience attraction as the bargaining of sex for the acquisition of resources—and lots of seemingly well-adjusted men believe this. I once spoke to a man who said it was distressing to imagine that women’s physical attraction was more genuine than “attraction” that exists primarily as a sexual barter to extract resources because he was more confident in his salary than his looks. But if this were the case, strippers and sex workers would experience genuine attraction for all their clients, but we all know they usually don’t. Yes, a man’s ambition and power can make him more attractive to women, but there are vanishingly few women out there who do not experience sexual desire, at least initially, as a function of physical attraction (this is seen as a brutal “black pill” for men, even though women have known from day one that men experience sexual attraction this way—and worse, often only this way, with very few levers for adjustment!)

This doesn’t mean women are insanely picky about looks. Some men hear that women care about looks even a little, and are so incensed by the idea that they assume women must be pickier than a casting agent looking for the latest Calvin Klein model. But while women may be somewhat aligned on what an ideal male body looks like (again: not unlike how men feel about women) there is a much wider variety of men they would consider attractive outside of the ideal. While I don’t share this particular preference, there are plenty of women who genuinely do find Jack Black and the late James Gandolfini sexy. Do women experience attraction exactly as men do? No, but insisting that they find all men kind of ugly and gross and only sleep with them as a bartering tool isn’t any more accurate.

But yes, women and men have very different reactions to the idea of casual sex, and it’s worth getting into that, because “men are more open to casual sex” is the strongest argument against the idea that women and men have remotely comparable desires.

There are a bunch of reasons that casual sex with an “average” woman (and again, I have major doubts that these men are actually imagining an average woman when they say this) might appeal to men more than the reverse would appeal to women. First of all, safety. Women compromise their safety when they sleep with a new man, and some degree of risk is inevitable (even a man you marry, who seems like a great guy, could one day annihilate the entire family) but it’s worthwhile to filter the men who seem obviously messed up from the start, or even just situations that feel risky. Yes, I’m aware plenty of dangerous men still manage to hoodwink women into dating them, and I’m aware not all women have a strong risk-aversion tendency, but I’m speaking in averages here, and risk that seems evident from the initial encounter. This is one reason that the random man propositioning female college students for sex had a 0% success rate. You would have to be insane to have sex with a random guy who stopped you on the street asking to fuck, even if you really wanted to fuck him.

Not to mention the risks of pregnancy (falls entirely on the woman) and STIs (higher risk for the woman.)

So there’s a risk to sex, but there’s also a reward—sex is, as I’m told, really quite something—and women have less of a reward than men when it comes to casual sex. A man’s orgasm is more or less guaranteed during penetrative sex, but a woman’s orgasm is not, and that is doubly true for casual encounters. When I asked people about their most recent sexual encounter, men’s orgasm rates neared 90% or higher across the board (although married and monogamous sex was superior) while women’s orgasm rates varied dramatically depending on who the sex was with—and were always lower than the men’s:

Not to get all sex education 101 on you, but this “orgasm gap” is not necessarily about anyone being especially dogshit at sex. It’s genuinely just harder for women to have orgasms. Heterosexual sex revolves around vaginal intercourse even if other activities are involved, and while many women can’t orgasm from penetrative sex alone, the orgasm is not guaranteed with foreplay either. The issue can be physical, but it can also be psychological, and completely irrelevant to whatever her partner is doing. I made this comic years ago to illustrate what I mean. The slightest hint of an unsexy intrusive thought—from anxiety about work to a random memory of your eighth grade chorus teacher spilling coffee on her skirt—can kill a woman’s journey to climax. This once happened to me when I randomly remembered a sad scene from a documentary about an elderly brontosaurus dying on a beach. None of this means, however, that women don’t get truly horny, or that our sexuality is purely responsive and does not involve any desire for men.

And yes, men and women are different. I once saw a tweet (wish I had the link) which pointed out that when sexting, men prefer obvious, straightforward photos of women’s bodies without any particularly flattering lighting, outfits or anything glamorous, while women would probably prefer some well-articulated dirty talk over a dick pic. I think this generalization is broadly true, and it speaks to men’s and women’s sexuality being differently orchestrated, but not the idea that women don’t really like sex that much.

I suspect that there is also an element of hunger at play. If women have more to lose from choosing a partner who ends up on the wrong side of the risk/reward equation, they will be more selective even before their sex drive comes into play. On top of that, women are just pickier than men, although slightly less picky about certain things like weight. As a result, there are a lot of single men out there who are genuinely very “hungry.” And you know how much food you imagine you could eat when you’re hungry.

I notice this dynamic playing out a lot on Reddit specifically. Single men on Reddit often spin elaborate fiction about the type of sex they’d be having if only they had a girlfriend. They do not imagine they’d ever be “full.” They feel basically how I feel when I get to an Indian buffet after skipping breakfast (and this actually once happened to me at a work meeting, and then I had to keep pitching the product to the client with a giant plate of butter chicken in front of me that I was clearly not going to finish). These men are convinced that if a woman was up for it, they’d want sex every day, all day, they’d luxuriate over every minute of foreplay and never once find themselves “not in the mood.” But my theory is that most of these men would change their tune after actually obtaining that arrangement in real life.

In fact, Reddit is also full of another type of sexual post from men, wherein men who have girlfriend with high sex drives complain about being tired of all the sex. I’ve seen multiple men complain more specifically about their girlfriends wanting a “free use” dynamic (a BDSM kink where he has full access to her body 24/7 and typically involves some degree of forceful interruption of daily tasks). These men aren’t asexual. They want to have sex. But as it turns out, three times a week is just fine for them.

These aren’t necessarily men with low sex drives, but when it comes down to reality, most men do not actually want to have sex twice a day, or even every day, especially as they get older. When asking partnered heterosexual men and women about their desired sex frequency, the number is strikingly similar. The actual average preference for sexual frequency between people already in a relationship is a few times a week. Men want sex slightly more than women do, but not by much. When both people have their ideal type of “pizza,” they want to eat similar amounts.

Your average man does not actually want sex all the time, with any woman, in any scenario. This is how sexually frustrated men talk because they’re starving and can’t imagine ever turning down a “meal,” but once men are actually in relationships (or if they have options in general) you will notice their sexual appetites aren’t very different from those of women. Perhaps slightly more elevated depending on their age and life stage, but not a dramatic mismatch on average. And when I ran my survey on dead bedrooms (releasing soon) it turns out that about 30% of heterosexual dead bedrooms involve a woman who wants more sex than the man—and that’s including all the external variables that specifically reduce a woman’s sex drive, from postpartum changes to menopause.

And what about in practice? Well, married heterosexual men and women have relatively similar levels of satisfaction with both sexual frequency and sex quality. When I first ran this survey, I anticipated that men would be far less satisfied with frequency than women, and women would be far less satisfied with quality, but neither were true.

While one might think this is good news for men, I actually think most men view the reality about women’s desire (higher than we think, clear physical component, unambiguously sexual as opposed to strictly transactional) as bad news, because they don’t see this desire directed at themselves, personally, at least not as often as they’d like. If women aren’t displaying desire toward them because women just aren’t that into men and think penises are kind of a yucky occupational hazard of getting a free dinner, then it’s no big deal. But if women do have sexual desire—not exactly the same as men’s but similar in some ways—it’s more of an indictment on a man’s intrinsic value if a woman is not displaying interest the way a man would, or at least that’s the way he sees it. This is why, when it comes to discussions of women’s sexuality, men are often the first to proclaim that women don’t really enjoy sex, don’t find men physically attractive unless they’re movie stars, and just tolerate men’s pawing for the sake of resources and companionship. And while some aspects of male and female sexuality are pretty similar, others are different, such as the fact that even if a woman does feel sexual desire toward a man, she’s unlikely to make aggressive first moves, furthering the idea that she just doesn’t want men at all. (As I’ve written about before, for many women, displaying any kind of aggression or initiative is a bit of a ladyboner-killer—but that doesn’t mean they don’t enjoy sex.)

It’s true that women are more specific (yet varied) and psychological about their sexual desires than men are. In some ways, men should be relieved by this fact. If women can access sexual desire by finding out exactly what they’re into—which is likely partly physical, but not 100% tied to a man’s looks—that gives couples a lot of room to unleash some great sex, even if getting there involves trial and error. But women having a strong preference for a particular type of hen of the woods mushroom topping on a thin-crust artisanal pizza does not mean they “don’t really like pizza.” And a man inundated with pizza—especially crappy pizza—will slowly realize he doesn’t like pizza as much as he thought.
