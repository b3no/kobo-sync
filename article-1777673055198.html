Plus recommendations

Halina Bennet

Happy Friday! I have a longer piece about young adults’ attitudes toward marriage coming on Sunday, so stay tuned for that. In the meantime, I am feeling kind of spicy, so I thought I’d share some takes and, as always, some weekend recommendations.

Without further ado:

  • It is okay to not want children. This take comes from the discourse around the New York Times article published this week, “These Couples Wanted to Have Children. Rising Costs Are Stopping Them.” While I do believe that there are couples who wanted to have kids and cannot afford to give them the lives they would like to on account of the rising costs of child care, groceries, and housing, those are not the couples who are represented in this article. This article is actually about couples who like their DINK (dual income, no kids) lifestyles and prioritize that over having children. I think it is perfectly fine to decide that you would rather spend your time and money on other priorities than on raising children. If someone does not want a child, they should not have one. This is a financial negotiation that people have always made with themselves — children are time-consuming and expensive and require sacrifice. What this article is ultimately pointing to is a tendency of people who don’t want kids to try to avoid being shamed for that choice by blaming factors outside of their control, like the economy or climate change.

  • You should invest in mending your clothing and cobbling your shoes rather than buying new garments to replace old ones.

  • If you can read instructions, you can cook and bake. Saying you can’t reflects a lack of patience, interest, or willingness to try. It’s okay to say you don’t cook or bake. But you certainly can.

  • Italian food is rarely worth going out for. It’s usually overpriced and no better than what you could make at home. There are exceptions: Sometimes the vibe is great (Noodle Pudding in Brooklyn), and sometimes the pasta is just that good.

  • It is better for TMZ to be investigating politicians than celebrities. No one should be harassed and gossiped about, but public, elected representatives should be subject to more scrutiny and held to a higher standard than celebrities.

  • Dog parks are good, actually. Sorry, boss, but while I concede that dogs aren’t people, I do think that dog parks are good precisely for that reason. It’s good for dog owners to be able to take their dogs to spaces where other dog owners are consenting to being around unleashed dogs. By the same token, dog owners should not be allowed to have their dogs unleashed in public environments around people who don’t want to engage with dogs.

  • Botox and cosmetic surgery should be disqualifying for actors. Unless it is an earned stylistic choice or a character would genuinely be a person with that kind of intervention, I don’t want to watch lip filler in a period piece or frozen foreheads on middle-class, Midwestern mothers. They’re distracting and take away from the content.

Weekend letter of recommendation:

  • If you haven’t seen “Project Hail Mary” yet, do.

  • Maibritt Henkel recommends not watching the third season of “Euphoria” and if you want drug content to instead watch “Trainspotting.” Kobe Yank-Jacobs says he hated “Trainspotting,” so tread at your own risk.

  • My mom says that “In Colorado, this would be an awesome weekend to read in a hammock at the park.”

  • She also recommends “Theo of Golden” by Allen Levi. Perhaps I’ll give it a read this weekend.

  • I just read “The Frozen River” by Ariel Lawhon on my friend Lizzy’s recommendation. It is a historical-fiction crime mystery set in Maine, and I thought it was totally immersive and deliciously escapist.

  • I’m newly addicted to Eadem lip balm, which I received as a gift. It is stupidly expensive ($24 for a tube) and I would not initially have purchased it myself, but it really is good. I’ll probably pony up for a new tube when I run out.

    • If you are in the market for more reasonably priced lip balm, then I can recommend Portland Bee Balm (especially for S.P.F. coverage), Bag Balm, Aquaphor, and Weleda Skin Food. Wow — maybe I should become a beauty writer.

  • If you are in the D.M.V. this weekend, you should attend Adams Morgan PorchFest. Maybe I’ll see you there.

  • Listen to Robyn’s new album.

    • While you’re at it, listen to Noah Kahan’s new album and to anything by the pop artist Devon Again. Devon and I went to middle and high school together, and she is fantastically talented. So talented, in fact, that she is opening for Olivia Rodrigo on tour.
